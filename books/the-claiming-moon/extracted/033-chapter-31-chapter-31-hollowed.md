Hoseok made sure all three of them were inside and safe and all the gates were locked before he left them on their own. He stood at the entrance for a second, watching Namjoon steer Jungkook and Taehyung down the corridor, and then he turned around and went the other way, towards the east wing.

His legs were hurting so bad right now. His whole body was tired; honestly, it had been building for two straight days, and now whatever the hell just happened added up more to his body’s misery. He needed to sleep soon or he might fall dead soon.

That wasn’t something to ponder upon for him, he thought, but he did need to ponder upon it eventually, soon even, he was aware, just not presently when his pack needed him.

He pushed open the door to his room and stopped and exhaled to look for him.

Bon Woo was still there. Of course he was.

Hoseok left him here before Taehyung's scream tore through the mind link and yanked him out of this very room; he had told him to stay put and rest, and the boy apparently interpreted "stay put" as falling sideways across the bed in his bloodied uniform with his arm still wrapped in the cloth Hoseok pressed to it nearly two hours ago.

The cloth was soaked through now; the excess blood kept leaking. The boy was so dumb, always getting hurt somewhere or other.

He was asleep, and even asleep he was making small whimpers and his breath hitching ever once in a while and little sniffles too, and his brows were pulled together like the pain was still reaching him through the unconsciousness.

The beta soldier was so fucking adorable.

Hoseok pulled a chair over and sat down and started peeling the cloth back carefully.

There was something about Bon Woo that Hoseok had never quite been able to explain, even to himself.

Red Moon was rich in alphas and omegas, always had been; that was just the nature of the lineage, but betas existed in the pack too, a few hundred of them. They were necessary as armies.

Bon Woo was one of them. A soldier.

A hard-fought one apparently, because Hoseok had heard the stories from the other men about how relentlessly the boy trained and how he never asked to be let off early and how he pushed until people twice his size told him to stop.

But then he would end up in Hoseok's room like this, arm torn open, making whimpering sounds that no one who had seen him charging at a bear would ever believe.

Every time Hoseok looked at him, he thought the same thing, that there was something in Bon Woo that reminded him more of an omega than anything else, this softness underneath all the fight.

He had never said that out loud because Bon Woo would hate hearing it, but it was there every time.

He had healed him over a dozen times at this point. Fourteen, actually, if he were counting, and he was.

The wound under the cloth was a long, deep gash starting just below the elbow. It was clear that he fought a bear again. The boy never listened. Even alphas preferred to stay away from patrolling in regions where bears crowded, but this boy would volunteer to work there and keep the pack protected.

Hoseok started cleaning it, and the boy stirred and then jerked awake hard, his eyes flying open, and he made this strangled sound and shoved back against the headboard like something in the room was about to hurt him.

Hoseok kept his hands where they were and said nothing.

Bon Woo blinked open his eyes from slumber. His eyes tracked slowly to Hoseok's face and then to his hands and then back up, the sleep clearing out of him slowly.

"Oh," he said, his voice rough. "Your majesty."

"What did I tell you about calling me that?" Hoseok said, not looking up from the wound.

Bon Woo's mouth moved. "Hyung-nim," he corrected himself, quietly.

"Better."

Hoseok kept working, and Bon Woo sat there watching him with the dazed look, he was very dizzy.

Then his face shifted, and he tried to sit up straighter and said, "I heard screaming earlier. I tried to get up, and I kept falling back down; my legs weren't working right, but I heard it, and I thought, Was that the prince's mate? Is everyone okay? What happened?"

"Everyone's inside and safe," Hoseok said. "Stop moving your arm."

"But—"

"Bon Woo. Stop moving your arm."

He stopped. He pressed his mouth together and looked down at what Hoseok was doing and did not ask anything else for a minute, which took visible effort.

Because he always wanted to ask about things. He was a curious beta.

"What did I tell you about going home when I tell you to go home?" Hoseok looked angrily at him.

Bon Woo looked up at him. His eyes were already going red from unshed tears and exhaustion, which happened every single time and which Hoseok had started to not comment on. He does not want Bon Woo to feel bad for crying.

"I know," he said.

"Then why are you still here?"

"Because I don't want to go," Bon Woo said honestly. He wanted to stay here.

"You all take care of us. You always have. Forever. My whole life you've all looked after this pack, and I want to fight for you, your majesty, hyung nim. I want to do something useful. I don't want to be sent home."

Hoseok didn't say anything to that. He started putting the herbs in, grinding them between his fingers first, and Bon Woo made a sound like something had bitten him and grabbed Hoseok's wrist with his free hand. Not pulling away, just holding on, knuckles white.

"Owww." Bon woo whimpered. His big eyes looked more like puppy eyes.

"I know," Hoseok said. "Hold still."

"It hurts," Bon Woo reported, very urgently, like Hoseok might not be aware of this.

"You have a twelve-centimeter wound you left untreated for two hours. Of course it hurts."

"You didn't have to say the number."

"Hold still."

Bon Woo held still. He made several more sounds of protest, and his big eyes went red and tears  started streaming down silently, and two tracks ran down his face, and he didn't make any noise about it and didn't wipe them either.

He just sat there crying quietly while Hoseok worked. It happened every time, and by now Hoseok had stopped mentioning it.

He felt the pull start in his sternum, the slow drain of energy moving down through his hands and into the wound.

The edges started pulling together. Bon Woo's grip on his wrist slowly released. Hoseok finished and sat back and looked at him.

Bon Woo was already drooping. The relief of the pain leaving had taken the last of whatever was keeping him upright, and he fell sideways against the pillow, blinking slowly, his eyes barely staying open.

"Okay, hyung nim," he mumbled. His eyes closed. "Okay."

Hoseok watched him drift off, his face relaxing the moment he fell asleep. He stayed there for another second before grabbing a fresh set of clothes from the shelf and leaving them over the back of the chair for Bon Woo. Then he moved the lamp to the shelf above the bed so the light wouldn't shine right in his face.

He ruffled the boy's hair once, quickly, before he left.

He pulled the door mostly closed and stood in the corridor and pressed the heels of his hands against his eyes.

Sara and Jee.

He breathed out slowly through his nose and kept his hands where they were for a long moment. Then he dropped them and moved down the hall and did not let himself think about it.

—

Yoongi stood at the borderline as he tried his best to not cross it.

He stood there in the drizzle looking out through the trees, but there was nothing visible out there, no movement, nothing he could point at and name, but that didn't mean anything, and he was very aware of that.

It wasn’t even twenty minutes ago when Taehyung stepped barely a foot past this exact line, and two dead women had grabbed his ankle before he could pull back.

Whatever was out there did not advertise itself.

Something about the place made the hairs on the back of his neck stand up. He didn't know why. He just knew he needed to stay alert.

He remembered something his father had told him years ago. They'd been sitting by the fire when he'd suddenly turned serious. "If you ever come across one, Yoongi, you run. You do not try to understand it. You do not try to reason with it. You run.”

Well.

He took one long last look at the trees. Still nothing. Still that heavy, watchful nothing.

"Better luck next time, you creepy white ladies," he said, just said it to whatever was standing out there in the dark, and then he turned around and walked back toward the palace.

He walked fast. He was not going to pretend he walked slow. Yoongi was scared shitless, and he was not about to put his life in danger when he had a pregnant mate waiting at home.

The library was lit when he got there. Namjoon was at the long table with his glasses on and a book open in front of him, and he looked up the second he heard footsteps, that immediate alert thing he did when he'd been waiting.

"You're safe?" Namjoon said.

Yoongi nodded and came and sat down across from him. "Where is everyone?"

"Jungkook's upstairs with Taehyung. He came up with a fever after what happened; Jungkook's trying to bring it down. Jimin was already asleep before any of it; he didn't hear anything. I locked them in." Namjoon set his pen down.

"Jin knows. I told him before I came here. He went back to sleep with the gates closed."

The two of them let out a short snort at that at the exact same time.

"And Hoseok?" Yoongi asked. "Jin?"

Namjoon exhaled. "Hobi's in his room healing that soldier, the one from the east border. He can handle himself. And Jin's sleeping, I already told you; he went back with the gates closed."

Yoongi nodded slowly. "Good," he said, mostly to himself. "Good."

The rain was still going outside, the same steady drizzle it had been all day. The clouds looked all grey and mopey.

The library was warm and quiet and completely at odds with everything that was sitting in Yoongi's chest right now.

"Hyung," Namjoon said, his voice sounding a little scared and difficult.

Yoongi looked at him.

"It was Sara and Jee," Namjoon said. "Taehyung recognized them. He was certain. The mating marks on their shoulders, their features. He said he knew them the second he looked."

Yoongi stared at him. The room was very quiet.

"The fuck?" Yoongi said.

"Yeah," Namjoon said. "That's where we are."

Yoongi sat back. He looked at the table and then at the ceiling and then at Namjoon again. Namjoon was looking back at him steadily, letting him process it, not rushing.

This changed everything. Not just because of what was waiting beyond the border, but because of what came next.

Jungkook couldn't be the one standing in front of Jee.

That wasn't even up for discussion. The second he saw her, he'd fall apart, and they couldn't afford that right now.

Yoongi was going to have to figure out how to keep him back from that without making him feel like he was being stripped of something, and that was going to be a whole separate problem for a different hour.

"Are we still standing on the apocalypse Appa told us stories about?" Yoongi asked.

Namjoon looked at him for a second and then shrugged. "I'm not sure about anything right now. You named this thing. I just supported your diagnosis."

Yoongi scraped his chair back and stood up as he looked at the walls around him. Floor-to-ceiling shelves on three sides, hundreds of books, their ancestors' entire collected knowledge of every creature and curse and reality that had ever crossed paths with their lineage. It was all in here somewhere. It had to be.

"Then what are we doing sitting here?" he said. He was already moving toward the shelves. "Let's pull some out."

"What exactly are we looking for?" Namjoon said.

"Anything about something that used to be alive and isn't anymore but won't sit down," Yoongi said, and he reached up and pulled the first book off the shelf.

"Appa called them something. One word. I heard it once when I was twelve and never again, and I cannot remember what it was. But it's in here. Everything's in here."

Namjoon got up from his chair and went to the shelves and started moving along them slowly, head tilted, reading the spines. There were a lot of them.

An absurd number of them, hundreds of books packed in tight from floor to ceiling on every wall, and Yoongi watched him from the table as he started at the left side and worked his way across.

He pulled out a few and put them back. Pulled out another and looked at the spine properly and shook his head and slid it back in.

At some point he grabbed the ladder that ran along the rail at the top of the shelves and climbed up to the higher rows, one hand holding the rail, the other pulling books out a few inches to read them.

"Romance," Namjoon said, from up on the ladder. "Mystery. Another romance." He pushed one back in.

"Three more mysteries." He moved the ladder along the rail a little and climbed further. "Appa wrote this one. And this one. And apparently this one too."

"Not what we need," Yoongi said.

"I know that, hyung."

He kept going.

The ladder scraped along the rail as he pushed it to the other end of the room. The shelves there held the oldest books in the library. Most of the titles had worn away years ago.

Namjoon climbed up and pulled out more of them.

Then he stopped.

He stood up there on the ladder for a second, not moving, just reading whatever was in front of him.

"Got it?" Yoongi called up.

Namjoon climbed back down with a book under his arm. He set it on the table, and Yoongi looked at it.

The book had seen better days. The cover was faded and rough beneath his fingers, the corners rounded from years of use, and the spine had cracked in two places. It was hard to imagine what it had looked like when it was new.

"Zombie apocalypse," Namjoon said. "Your diagnosis."

"Who wrote it?" Yoongi said.

Namjoon turned it over and looked at the inside of the front cover. "Great-grandfather."

Yoongi nodded and looked at the book and then pushed it across the table toward Namjoon.

He coughed once as the dust lifted off the cover and filled his lungs, and then again and pressed his sleeve to his mouth. "You read. You're better at this language."

Namjoon took it without argument and opened it.

The first page caught him off guard. The paper had yellowed with age, but the ink was still a rich black, every letter as sharp as if it'd been written yesterday. Elders had taken their time to create this.

Zombies.

The word was the only thing mentioned in the middle of the page.

Neither of them said anything. They just looked at it for a second before Namjoon turned the page.

The pages had darkened, and Namjoon would like to think it’s because of how old these books are; the pages were a little curled up from the edges. The corners felt almost velvety from years of being handled, and every page made the same dry, delicate sound when he turned it. Careless hands would've torn it in seconds.

But the text inside was still readable. Still intact. Whoever had stored these books had stored them right.

Namjoon started reading aloud, slowly, because the language needed slowing down.

"Those who perish under the affliction of the bitten do so swiftly and without remedy. The body faileth at once upon receiving the wound of one already taken, and there is no returning from such a passage."

Yoongi looked at Namjoon. Namjoon looked back at him.

That matched. That matched exactly what they knew about Sara and Jee, who were both dead by every account, who had been buried, and who were not supposed to be walking around in the trees outside their pack borders.

Namjoon turned the page and kept going.

"The afflicted do spread their condition through the act of biting one another, and thus the disease moveth through a community as fire through dry wood, each new victim becoming in turn the instrument of another's doom."

Again they both nodded, not saying anything, just taking it in.

"Upon receiving such a bite, death cometh swiftly to the host, and what riseth thereafter weareth the body as a garment only. The eyes of such creatures do become fixed and dilated, empty of the soul that once inhabited them. Their hearts beat no longer. No part of the living person remains within. They are gone and wholly done with this world, though their bodies do persist."

Namjoon stopped reading. He looked up at Yoongi over the top of the page.

"Hyung," he said. "They're dead."

Yoongi was already looking at the page. "I told you," he said.

They kept turning. A few more pages of description, more symptoms, and more accounts from whatever era this had been written in, and then Namjoon's finger slowed on a paragraph near the bottom of a page, and he read it out.

"There existeth no method by which these creatures may be destroyed, no weapon nor wound nor force yet known to man. The great plague of the eighteenth century, which didst claim three parts of four of the werewolf population before its conclusion were observed to diminish in the presence of neem. Though this cannot be stated with full certainty as a weapon or a cure, those afflicted were seen to recoil from it. Let it be noted, however, that the use of neem against them is not to be relied upon as a permanent measure, for the reliability of this reaction cannot be guaranteed and may in time cease to hold."

Namjoon closed the book. It landed on the table with a heavy thud that sent up another small cloud of dust.

He sat back in his chair. Yoongi too sat down beside him, both of them in deep thoughts.

The library was very quiet.

"Now I'm even more confused," Namjoon said.

Yoongi could not deny that. He looked at the closed book and kept thinking about everything.

Namjoon states. "How the fuck did they end up here?" he said. "And out of all the people, why Sara and Jee? It could have been anyone. Our soldiers have been at those borders for days, and none of them saw anything; none of them were touched. Something's weird in here."

Yoongi shook his head. "Something isn't weird, Joon. Everything is fucked up. I don't get it at all."

Namjoon had gone quiet. He kept staring at the same page, barely moving except to absently rub his thumb along the edge of the paper. Yoongi had seen that look enough times to know he was putting something together.

He was looking at the table but not seeing it, fingers tapping once slowly against the cover of the closed book.

"I think you've diagnosed this wrong," Namjoon said.

Yoongi looked at him. "Yeah, genius? Tell us what the right diagnosis is. Don't get too high on power."

Namjoon smiled at that, small and brief, the dimples coming out for just a second. "It will never happen."

He looked at Yoongi, his hands moving as he talked. "Think deeply, hyung. Taehyung said "eyes" with a black dot. Nothing like that was mentioned in the book. And the white face, the bones showing through the skin. None of that was in there either. And that's before we even get to the main part, which is if they were dead the way this book describes, how did Sara and Jee manage to find their way here? Dead things don't navigate. Dead things don't travel."

Yoongi looked at him and said nothing. Namjoon did have a point, but Yoongi couldn’t argue with logic over this.

Namjoon stood up and went back to the shelves.

He went up the ladder again, back to the same wall, and worked along the higher row more carefully this time, reading each spine slowly. It took a few minutes.

Yoongi stayed at the table, listening to books being pulled from the shelves and the steady rain tapping against the windows.

He didn't let himself think about Jimin.

About his mate asleep upstairs, probably curled up under the blankets with one hand over his stomach. How long it'd been since Yoongi had climbed into bed beside him without having somewhere else to be. Just holding him. Kissing his forehead. Falling asleep together.

Lately, it'd been Taehyung taking care of Jimin more often than not. Sitting with him, making sure he ate, keeping him company whenever Yoongi couldn't.

Yoongi owed him more than he could ever put into words. He'd make it up to him someday.

But first, they had to get through this.

Namjoon came back down.

The second book looked like it'd spent just as many years collecting dust on those shelves. The leather had darkened, the corners were soft, and the edges looked ready to flake apart if someone handled it carelessly. It wasn't as thick as the first one. Across the front, someone had carved a single word into the cover.

HOLLOWED

Namjoon opened it. The second page stopped both of them.

Someone had drawn a picture of the person with the same disease. The drawing looked so neat and amazing that Namjoon was stuck in awe until he remembered what he was actually looking at.

The figure was standing, though it looked like it could collapse at any second. Its head leaned slightly to one side. The skin over its hands was pulled so tight he could make out every knuckle and every bone beneath it, and there was blood dried at the corners of its mouth. Its eyes were almost completely white. Just a tiny dark pupil presemt in the middle.

It was exactly what Taehyung had described. Every detail of it. Down to the proportions, down to the hands, down to those eyes.

"Found it," Namjoon said, sounding more certain than he had in the last half hour. He straightened up and started flipping through the pages, reading faster now, his eyes moving from one paragraph to the next.

"The disease doth not pass from one of the afflicted to another, nor from the afflicted to the healthy by means of touch or bite or breath. Its origin lieth not in the bodies of those already taken but in the common wolf, those wolves that are of the natural world and not of our werewolf kin. When such a wolf, running wild and carrying within it the particular bacteria of its wilderness, doth bite a werewolf, a war beginneth in the blood of the one thus bitten. The blood of the werewolf and the parasite brought by the wolf's teeth do fight against each other, and in this fighting the parasite doth gain dominance and doth take full control of the host body. However, and herein lieth the great difference, both do continue to live within that same body."

Namjoon's mouth had gone dry. He licked his lips and kept reading.

Hollowed didn't come from werewolves at all.

It came from wild wolves. Somewhere out there, one carrying the wrong bacteria bit a werewolf, and everything that followed started with that one moment.

Sara and Jee must have been bitten by one. And if both of them were still alive somewhere inside those bodies, if the parasite had taken over but the host was still in there, then...

He kept reading.

"The one who is taken doth begin to lose themselves from the moment of the bite. The human part of them doth slip away gradually and without return until there remaineth in them only one thing, one last surviving piece of who they were. All else is consumed and all else is gone. But this one thing, this final remnant, doth persist above all others within the body. And that thing is..."

Namjoon stopped. He kept on reading over and over again, trying to make sure he didn't misinterpret any single word.

"And that's what?" Yoongi said, watching him.

Namjoon lifted his head from the book and just looked at Yoongi for a second. There was no panic on his face, just surprise. Real surprise. Whatever he'd found, he hadn't been expecting it.

"Love," Namjoon said. He said it quietly, because, what the actual fuck!

The library was completely silent.

Yoongi stared at him, and Namjoon just stared back because there was nothing else they could do.

What the actual fuck? Jee and Sara had come here for love? Who, exactly?

Namjoon put the book down and stood up and started pacing, moving around the table, one hand pressing against his mouth as he thought. His feet were going fast even if the rest of him looked still.

"Hyung, they're still alive," he said, stopping and turning to face Yoongi. "Somewhere in there, they're still alive."

Yoongi looked at him. "How are you so sure?"

"Because if they were dead, the way the first book described, completely gone, then what is keeping them out?" Namjoon said.

"If they were truly dead and the parasite was all that was left, it wouldn't care about our borders. It wouldn't be stopped by anything that requires a living thing to obey it. But they're still out there, still outside the line. Something in them is still obeying the boundary." He looked at Yoongi straight.

"Because something in them is still alive."

Yoongi sat there for a second, scratching his head. He wasn’t the brightest person out there, but Namjoon definitely was. "You're fucking smart."

Namjoon's dimples appeared briefly. He took a deep breath and let it out and sat back down, and the relief of the diagnosis lasted about thirty seconds before the weight of the rest of it came back down on both of them.

"Who do you think they came after?" Yoongi said, sighing. He looked at the table. He wanted nothing more than to go upstairs and lie down next to Jimin and put his hand on his stomach and feel the baby move and not think about any of this for six consecutive hours. He was going to do none of that.

Namjoon had gone still again. That pulling-at-a-thread look.

"Wait," he said. "Where did you bury Kihyun's body?"

Yoongi started to answer, and then his mouth just stopped.

Fuck!!

The answer was there fully formed in his head, and he looked at Namjoon and felt the late realization hit him square in the chest like something cold.

"On the fucking border," he said.

Namjoon looked at him and wanted to fucking laugh at Yoongi’s face. "You can be so dumb sometimes."

"Sara smelled him," Yoongi said, following it through. Kihyun was Sara's first mate, after all.

His body was buried at the border. Sara, with whatever was left of her love still burning inside that taken body, had followed the scent straight here.

"She followed her mate's scent all the way here." He nodded.

"Okay. But then why is Jee here? Jee's mate is Sara, but it doesn’t feel like they had a good relationship after that night. Or maybe they did? Jee just followed Sara?"

Namjoon shook his head slowly. "I think you're being unreasonable about that one, hyung. Jee wasn't here for her mate." He looked at Yoongi carefully.

"I think she's here for Jungkook. Why do you think she grabbed Taehyung by the ankle? She smelled Jungkook all over him. That's what set her off."

Yoongi looked at him for a long moment.

Then he let out a low whistle. "Bingo," he said. "You're a fucking genius, Namjoon-ah."

Namjoon leaned back in his chair and looked between the two books spread across the table. For once, he didn't look very pleased with himself for figuring something out. If he was right, then Sara was still out there somewhere beyond the border, walking through the rain because whatever was left of her was following the scent of her dead mate. Jee was doing the same, except she was following Jungkook. Her brother. The one she'd loved, the one she'd lost, and the one she maybe never stopped looking for. They weren't alive anymore. They weren't dead either. Somehow they'd been left with one instinct so strong it had survived everything else, and none of them had the slightest idea how to fight something like that.

The rain kept going outside. The library was warm and quiet.

Neither of them said anything for a while.

Yoongi reached across the table and pulled a blank piece of paper toward him and uncapped his pen.

"Dumb everything down for me, Joon-ah," he said.

Namjoon nodded and pulled both books closer and sat up straight and started going through them properly, reordering everything they had pieced together into something that actually made sense laid out in sequence.

"Sara and Jee are alive," he started. "They're not zombies. They're affected by a disease called "hollowed," which hollows their bodies from within and leaves bones over time."

Yoongi was already writing, pen moving fast across the paper in his quick, cramped hand, getting it down in points before anything slipped.

"The parasite takes over their body and makes them lose themselves bit by bit until there's no other emotion left in them except the strongest one," Namjoon continued. "And that's love."

Yoongi nodded and kept writing.

"Any behavior patterns?" he asked, not looking up from the paper.

Namjoon flipped back through the hollowed book, scanning pages quickly, running his finger down the text. He went through three pages before he found the section he was looking for and started reading it over fast, pulling out the relevant parts.

"They can stutter or not have perfect memory. They most likely don't have any real memory at all, actually, not in the way we do. They just sniff around their most loved one, trying to find them, trying to see them one last time. And sometimes," he paused, reading the line again, "sometimes they die after seeing that person."

Yoongi's pen kept moving.

"Name the symptoms again," he said. "All of them."

Namjoon went back through both books this time, cross-referencing, making sure he wasn't missing anything. "Boney bodies. White eyes with black dots. They feed on raw animals. Their teeth can rot badly and their bodies can start to smell." He turned another page.

"They might show small pieces of human emotion. Brief things. And they're not cannibals, at least as far as anything recorded here goes. They do eat raw animals, though; that part's confirmed."

Yoongi finished writing it all down and looked at the list.

"Any way to bring them back?" he asked. "Since they're not dead."

Namjoon was already looking. He went through the remaining pages of the hollowed book slowly, then went back to the beginning and came through again more carefully, checking every paragraph. He took his time with it. Yoongi waited and said nothing and let him look properly.

Namjoon shook his head. "It's not mentioned."

They both exhaled at the same time.

"Silver," Yoongi said after a moment. "Does it still affect them?"

Namjoon nodded, and something in his posture shifted slightly, like this was at least one solid thing to hold onto.

"It does actually. They're still werewolves underneath all of it. Silver weakens them the same way it would any of us."

"Perfect," Yoongi said.

"How to kill them?" he asked next.

Namjoon turned straight to the last few pages without saying a word. Yoongi wasn't surprised. The remedies were always at the back. It didn't matter what the book was about, that was where you'd find what you actually needed. They were torn.

Both of them stared at the torn pages for a second. Yoongi's pen hit the table.

Namjoon scratched the back of his head slowly. Then he looked up and said, "Well, we don't really need it though, do we? If they're still alive somewhere in there, they can be killed the way any living person can. Standard rules should apply."

Yoongi looked at him. "I don't fucking know at this point," he said, and he meant it, because nothing about tonight had gone the way anything was supposed to go and his confidence in standard rules applying to anything was sitting pretty low right now.

He folded the paper and pressed it flat and tucked it into his pocket. He looked across the table at Namjoon.

"Let's both of us deal with this, yeah?" he said. "Just the two of us."

Namjoon nodded without hesitation.

It was the only way it made sense. Jungkook was out entirely, not because he wasn't capable, not even close, but because putting him in front of Jee right now when he had no idea what he was walking into and no time to prepare himself was not something either of them were willing to do.

He was their little brother. He was Jee’s little brother too.

And they will do anything to keep him safe and alive. It does not matter that Jungkook was ten times stronger than them all combined. As an elder brother, Yoongi always took responsibility for taking care of his brothers.

Hoseok needed to stay clean and healthy because if anyone got hurt in the next three days, they would need him functional, and a healer who had been out fighting hollowed women in the rain was not a healer who could do his job.
