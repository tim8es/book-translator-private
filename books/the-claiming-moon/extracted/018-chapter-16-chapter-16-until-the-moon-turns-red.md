Chapter 16

Taehyung adjusted the thin frames sitting on the bridge of his nose and blinked to clear the blur. The glasses were still new that he forgot he was wearing them sometimes. Jungkook had brought them back three weeks ago after a supply run to a neighboring pack, wrapped carefully in soft cloth like they were made of something precious.

Taehyung tried to act casual about it, like it wasn't a big deal, but his hands shivered in excitement when he first put them on and realized he could actually read the small print in the old herbal medicine book he had been squinting at for days.

Now he sat cross-legged on the thick rug in the library section of the pack house, surrounded by four different books he had pulled from the shelves. The library was nothing too grand, but still such a cozy place.

He had been here for nearly two hours now, reading through a collection of essays on lunar cycles and their effects on wolf behavior. It was so freaking fascinating to him. Before coming to Jee's pack, Taehyung hadn't touched a book in a while. Kihyun had made sure of that.

It was a bitter memory that he does not wish to recall. He just turned the page and kept reading, letting the words fill the empty spaces that used to ache. The door to the library opened, and Taehyung glanced up. It was one of the younger omegas, a girl named Harin. She waved at him and whispered something about needing a book on preserving fruit before disappearing into the back shelves. Taehyung waved back and returned to his reading.

Life here had been good. Better than good. Jee's pack was smaller than Moon Village, but it felt more alive somehow. People laughed and smiled with him; they even hugged him on a daily basis, and Taehyung absolutely loves hugging. There was less formality and more warmth. Taehyung had made friends with a handful of omegas who liked to knit and crochet in the afternoons.

They invited him to join them after the first week, and now it was a regular thing. He showed up with his yarn, and they showed up with tea and gossip, and it felt normal. It felt like something he could keep. The alphas were friendly too, though some of them had been wary at first. Jungkook's reputation had traveled faster than they had. People knew who he was.

They knew what he had done. But Jungkook had worked hard to prove himself useful, and after a month of him helping with hunts and patrols and repairs around the village, most of the suspicion had faded. Now when Jungkook walked through the main square, people greeted him by name. They asked him for advice. They treated him like he belonged.

Taehyung closed the book he had been reading and stretched his arms above his head until his shoulders popped. His back was stiff from sitting in the same position for too long, and his neck ached a little. He rolled it slowly from side to side and sighed.

The glasses slipped down his nose, and he pushed them back up with one finger. He glanced at the small clock sitting on the table near the window. It was almost evening. Jungkook would be back soon from whatever he had been doing today. Probably helping Jee with something. He always was nowadays.

Taehyung stood and gathered the books into a neat stack, carrying them back to their shelves one by one. He made sure to put them exactly where he had found them because the pack librarian, an older beta named Minho, got grumpy if things were out of place. Taehyung had learned that the hard way.

Once the books were put away, he took off his glasses and folded them carefully before tucking them into the small pouch Jungkook had given him to keep them safe. He slung the pouch over his shoulder and headed out of the library, closing the door quietly behind him.

The hallway outside was empty and smelled like something aromatic. Someone was cooking already. Taehyung's stomach grumbled at the thought, and he realized he hadn't eaten since the small bowl of soup he had for lunch. That had been hours ago.

He made his way down the hall and through the main corridor of the pack house. It was a large building, much bigger than the one back in Moon Village, with wide-open rooms and very high ceilings.

Jee and Sara lived in the upper section, but the lower floors were communal. There was a dining hall, a meeting room, the library, and a massive kitchen that everyone used. Taehyung had spent a lot of time in that kitchen lately.

He pushed open the door and stepped inside. There were two long counters on either side of the room and a huge stone in the center with multiple cooking pots hanging above it. Shelves lined the walls, stocked with jars of dried herbs, spices, grains, and preserved vegetables.

It smelled like rosemary and garlic and something sweet he couldn't quite place. Two of the kitchen maids were there, chatting quietly as they wiped down the counters. They looked up when Taehyung entered, and both of them smiled.

"Luna," one of them greeted, bowing her head slightly.

Taehyung still wasn't used to that title, but he had stopped correcting people after the first few weeks. Everyone heard Jungkook calling him Luna, and apparently they decided that Taehyung would be called that too. It felt easier to just accept it. "Hiii," he said, offering a small smile in return. "I'm going to cook tonight. Do you mind if I use the space?"

The older of the two maids, a kind woman named Eunji, shook her head immediately. "Of course not. Do you need help with anything?" Taehyung thought about it for a second. Cutting vegetables still took him forever because his knife skills were terrible and he had a bad habit of cutting things unevenly. "Could you help me chop some vegetables? I'm making goat meat fry."

Eunji's face lit up. "That sounds wonderful. Of course I'll help." The other maid, named Somi, joined in without being asked, and the three of them worked together for the next twenty minutes.

Eunji chopped onions and garlic while Somi handled the carrots and potatoes. Taehyung pulled out the goat meat from the cold storage and set it on the counter, inspecting it carefully. It looked fresh. Good, Taheung could only cook when things were simple; any complication and he might give up.

The maids finished chopping and set everything in small bowls, lining them up on the counter. They bowed again before leaving, and Taehyung thanked them, watching as they disappeared through the side door. Once he was alone, he rolled up his sleeves and got to work.

Cooking had become something of a project for him over the last month. He had always been terrible at it. Embarrassingly bad.

He had burned rice more times than he could count. He had oversalted soup to the point where even Jungkook, who would eat anything Taehyung made, had struggled to finish it. He had cut himself twice with a knife and burned his hand on a hot pan at least five times. But he kept trying.

At first it had been out of stubbornness. He hated the idea of being completely dependent on Jungkook for every meal. It made him feel useless in a way that gnawed at him. Jungkook never complained. He cooked every single day without being asked, and he did it with a smile on his face like it was the easiest thing in the world. But Taehyung wanted to contribute.

He wanted to do something that felt like he was taking care of his mate instead of always being the one taken care of.

So he eventually started learning. The omegas in the pack had been more than willing to teach him. They walked him through the basics. How to properly season meat and how to tell when oil is hot enough and how to layer flavors so everything doesn't taste flat. Taehyung listened carefully and took mental notes and practiced whenever he had the chance.

And somewhere along the way, he realized something that made him feel a little foolish for not seeing it sooner. Cooking wasn't just an omega thing. It wasn't about fitting into some traditional role or proving he could be domestic.

It was a survival skill.

If he wanted to live, he needed to know how to feed himself. It was as simple as that. He had spent so long rejecting anything that felt like it was being forced on him that he had missed the fact that some things were just practical and necessary to survive.

Now he actually enjoyed it.

He liked the peace of chopping and stirring. He liked the satisfaction of tasting something he made and realizing it was actually good.

He heated oil in a large pan and waited until it heated before adding the chopped onions. They sizzled immediately, and the smell filled the kitchen. He stirred them slowly, letting them soften and turn golden before adding the garlic and ginger paste he had prepared earlier. The scent deepened, and Taehyung felt a flicker of pride.

He added the goat meat next, piece by piece, and listened to the satisfying sizzle as it hit the pan. He stirred everything together, making sure the meat was coated evenly in the spices. Turmeric, chili powder, coriander, and cumin. He had measured everything carefully this time. No guessing, because measuring from heart was something he couldn’t be trusted with. (My granny’s recipe, btw)

As the meat browned, he hummed softly to himself. His hands moved on autopilot, adding the chopped tomatoes and a splash of water to help everything cook down into a thick, fragrant sauce.

Taehyung pushed his sleeves up higher. A bead of sweat rolled down the side of his face, and he wiped it away with the back of his hand. He was so focused on stirring the pan that he didn't hear the door open behind him.

Arms wrapped around his waist suddenly, and Taehyung jolted, letting out a small yelp before he realized who it was. Jungkook's cedar smell surrounded him immediately. Then came the warmth of his chest pressed against Taehyung's back and the soft brush of lips against the side of his neck.

"Someone's cooking," Jungkook murmured, his voice low and amused.

Taehyung relaxed into the hold, his heartbeat slowing back to normal. "Yeah," he said, tilting his head slightly to give Jungkook better access to his neck. "I really wanted to cook today."

Jungkook hummed, the sound vibrating against Taehyung's skin. He pressed another kiss to the curve of Taehyung's neck, kissing him like he had all the time in the world. His hands pressed  flat against Taehyung's stomach, warm even through the fabric of his shirt.

Taehyung tried to focus on stirring the pan, but it was difficult when Jungkook was being so distracting. "Stop distracting me," he said, though there was no real bite to it.

"I'm not distracting you," Jungkook replied immediately, his lips still brushing against Taehyung's neck.

"You are, dear alpha." Jungkook laughed; Taehyung felt it all the way down to his toes. He turned his head just enough to catch a glimpse of Jungkook's face. His mate looked happy. There was a  sheen of sweat on his forehead, and his hair was a little messy, probably from whatever work he had been doing outside.

"Fine," Jungkook said, pulling back just enough to give Taehyung space. "I'll help instead of distracting." He moved to the other side of the kitchen and started pulling out a pot to cook rice. Taehyung watched him for a moment, smiling to himself, before turning back to his pan.

Taehyung stirred the goat meat and adjusted the heat while Jungkook measured out rice and water. The sounds of cooking filled the kitchen.

"How was your day?" Taehyung asked eventually, glancing over at Jungkook. Jungkook sighed long and dramatically. "Some of the teenagers gave me a hard time today."

Taehyung raised an eyebrow. "Oh?"

"Yeah. They were supposed to be helping with patrol training, but they kept messing around. I had to use my alpha voice just to get them to take me seriously."

Taehyung snorted. "Stop terrorizing the pups," Jungkook grinned, shameless. "I terrorized you too during the Claiming Moon, didn't I?"

Taehyung laughed despite himself, shaking his head. "You're terrible."

"You love me anyway, dear Luna."

"Unfortunately." Jungkook moved closer again, abandoning the rice for a moment. He reached out and turned Taehyung around gently, hands settling on his waist.

Before Taehyung could ask what he was doing, Jungkook leaned down and kissed him. It was soft and sweet and tasted faintly like the mint tea Jungkook must have had earlier. Taehyung melted into it, his hands coming up to rest on Jungkook's chest. When they pulled apart, he was smiling so wide his cheeks hurt. "What's gotten into you?" he asked, a little breathless.

Jungkook's eyes were bright, almost giddy. "I get to mate you tomorrow. Can you believe it?"

Taehyung's heart did something complicated in his chest. He stared at Jungkook for a long moment, taking in the way his mate looked at him like he was the most important thing in the world.

Tomorrow. Tomorrow they would finally be mated. Officially. Fuck yes!!

They had talked to the elders weeks ago, along with Jee and Sara. They had told them their intentions, and the elders had asked them to wait for the perfect day. Tomorrow the moon would turn red for four hours, and that was the ideal time for a mating bond to take hold. The bond would be strongest then, they said.

Taehyung didn't really believe in all of that. He thought it sounded a little superstitious, a little too old-fashioned. But Jungkook believed in it. He believed in doing things properly, in honoring tradition when it mattered. So Taehyung had agreed to wait.

They had waited so long already. A few more days hadn't hurt.

But now that tomorrow was actually here, Taehyung felt the weight of it settling over him. Tomorrow he would be Jungkook's. Not just in name or in practice but in every way that mattered. Their souls would be tied together. Their lives would be bound.

And Taehyung wanted it. He wanted it so badly it scared him sometimes. "I can't believe it," Taehyung said quietly, his hands curling into the fabric of Jungkook's shirt.

Jungkook smiled and pressed another kiss to Taehyung's forehead. "Me neither."

They stood there for a moment, just holding each other in the middle of the kitchen while the goat meat sizzled in the background and the rice bubbled on the stove. Taehyung rested his head against Jungkook's chest and listened to his heartbeat.

"We should finish cooking," Taehyung said eventually, though he didn't move.

He washed his hands and then came up behind Taehyung again, resting his chin on Taehyung's shoulder. "Smells amazing, pup."
