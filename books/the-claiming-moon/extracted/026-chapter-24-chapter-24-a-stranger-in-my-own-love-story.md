Taehyung quickly reached Sunghi’s home finally.

My alpha didn't actually go through with it that night; he helped me instead. But eventually the pack found out about us, and they banished me for it. My mate just followed with me."

"Aiigooo", she said finally, shaking her head slowly, the syllables stretching. "Poor boy. Couldn't even find his own mate in peace because of some stupid old tradition." She clicked her tongue.

"Most of them agreed," Taehyung said quietly.

"Aigoo," she said again. "Aigoo, aigoo." She looked at him. "But your alpha, your mate now. You love him?"

"Oh, I did," she said, and something in her voice shifted, reminiscing about the good old days. "A very good one. Very hard-working, very steady, that man. We loved each other so much, child, so much." She smiled again, but it was a little sad too.

Tending to others didn’t come naturally to him, but he was learning from the best.The scent that filled the air from the pot was rich and thick, making his stomach growl.

"I hope you like venison, child," she said, watching him.

"I love venison," Taehyung said honestly, already sitting back down on the floor with his plate, crossing his legs and settling in.

She laughed a little at that, pleased. "Good. Good boy."

They ate in comfortable silence for a while.

"I do," she said, nodding steadily. "Two sons. Both mated; both have pups of their own who are mated too, now. I have great grandpups running around somewhere.” She said it with obvious satisfaction.

"Where are they?"

"Far off, past the river. They have their own lives there." She waved a hand loosely. "Good lives."

"Did they leave you alone, or did you want to live like this?"

She looked at him like that was a very sensible question.

"I wanted it, child. When I was young I enjoyed my life without my elders living under the same roof, you understand? I wanted the same freedom for my sons. Let them live as they like; that is what I always believed."

She picked up another piece of meat with her spoon. "And I love living alone. There is so much peace in it. So much quiet." She smiled to herself.

"Besides, you never know when the Moon Goddess decides to take you. I just want to live happily until then. No fuss."

He looked at her, the very old woman sitting in her colourful hut with her sharpness and optimism and her wonderful food and her quiet happiness, and felt something warm nestle inside his chest.

After she finished her meal, he took her bowl without saying anything and placed a basin of water before her, kneeling down beside her chair so that she could wash her hands properly, and she allowed him to do so, and she smiled big.

"You are a good boy," she said, drying her hands on the cloth he offered. "A very good boy."

"Thank you, Halmoni."

"I called you here for a reason, you know," she said then, her tone shifting into something a little more eager now. "It wasn't only to feed you, although that too," she said, giving him a look. "I heard you do crochet. And that you teach the little pups how to do it."

Taehyung grinned before he could help it. "You know about that?"

"I heard about it among the members," she said, pleased with herself. "Word travels, child, even to old women in small huts."

"I love to crochet," Taehyung said, and he meant it genuinely. "And I love teaching people about it too. I love watching people learn stitches and believe in themselves to know they can create something beautiful. It's just very satisfying."

"Aiigooo, I know exactly what you mean," she said, nodding vigorously. "I felt that my whole life. Not crochet; I knit. Have been knitting since I was younger than you are now."

She looked at her own hands for a moment, turning them over slowly. "These hands have made so many things. Scarves, blankets, and little shoes for my grandpups." She smiled. "The hands remember even when the mind starts to forget."

"Do you still knit, Halmoni?"

"Slowly," she said. "More slowly than before. But yes, still." She reached beside her chair then, pulling up a worn tote bag that had been sitting against the leg of it the whole time, and she opened it with unhurried hands and drew out a small, folded packet.

She held it for just a moment and then looked up at him. "I wanted to bring you this, child. Come here."

Taehyung leaned forward, and she placed the packet in his hands, and he unfolded it carefully.

It was a handkerchief. Small and square, knitted in colours that ran into each other nicely, a little uneven in places, but that was common for handmade things, and a little crooked at one corner, but beautiful. So, so beautiful.

"You make things for others," she said simply. "I wanted to gift you something knitted. From one maker to another."

Taehyung's throat closed without any warning. He sat there holding this small, crooked, beautiful handkerchief and felt his eyes go wet; he hadn't expected it at all and hadn't braced for it, and he had to press his lips together very hard for a moment to keep himself from actually crying again because he had already cried so much today, and he was not going to cry in front of Halmoni over a handkerchief even though it was the most thoughtful thing anyone had done for him in recent memory.

"Thank you, Halmoni," he managed, and his voice only wobbled a little. "I will make something for you too, okay? I promise. The best and softest wool I can find."

Her whole face lit up. "I will be so happy, child. So happy."

"What do you need? I can make you anything. A blanket, a shawl, little things for your grandbabies—"

"Surprise me," she said, waving a hand. "You seem like a boy with good taste. I trust you."

He laughed at that, tucking the handkerchief carefully into his pocket. "Okay, halmoni. I'll surprise you."

"Now," she said, settling back a little, "tell me. How long have you been crocheting? Who taught you?"

"My mother did. When I was a small pup." He tilted his head. "Did someone teach you to knit?"

"My mother too," she said. "Who learnt from her mother. And so on and so on, going back further than I can remember." She looked at him thoughtfully. "You know the best way to learn tension control in knitting?"

"I always struggle with tension," Taehyung admitted.

"Everybody does at one point. Here is what you do." She held up her hands, her fingers moving in the air like she was holding invisible needles.

"You hold the yarn here, over this finger, and you breathe out when you pull the stitch through. Not before, not after. With the stitch. Your breathing and your hands have to agree with each other."

Taehyung watched her fingers and tried to imagine it. "That's actually really helpful."

"Of course it is," she said pleasantly. "I have been doing this for seventy years, child; I have learnt a few things."

He laughed again, and she looked satisfied with herself.

It was while she was settling back in her chair, adjusting her shawl around her shoulders, that her eyes caught on the scarf around his neck and stopped there. She stared at it for a moment, her sharp eyes moving over the fabric with an expression; she recognised it.

"I know about that," she said.

Taehyung blinked and looked down, then reached up to unwind the scarf from around his neck, holding it out toward her. "Yeah? My mate made it for me. Back in Moon Village. I used to wear it to hide my neck."

She nodded slowly, still looking at it. "I know about this, dear. The material gives it away. There is only one place this kind of weave comes from, only one boy who makes this particular blend." She looked up at him.

"And only your mate has a habit of making scarves in his free time. He has been doing it since he was around fourteen years old."

Taehyung smiled at her gently. "Halmoni, I think you might be mixing something up. He grew up in Moon Village. That's where he's from."

Taehyung laughed a little, because what else was there to do? "Halmoni, he lives in Moon Village. He belongs there."

She opened the cupboard and moved a few things around with unhurried hands and then brought something back and held it out to him. "See."

It was a small woven ribbon, used to tie back hair, made from the same material as the scarf in his hands. Well, wasn’t it common to use silk?

"Your mate gifted me that," she said. "To tie in my hair."

Taehyung took it slowly and held it next to the scarf and looked at them both.

His hands felt very still suddenly.

He lifted the ribbon without really knowing why and touched it to his nose, breathing deeply, searching beneath the scent of age and laundry and the past, until he found something very faint beneath it all, a trace of cedar that was hard to detect.

His heart thudded against his chest.

There was some noisy and complicated thinking happening inside his head, a process that seemed to be developing from the bottom up, and it must have shown on his face, for the grandmother was looking at him with a cautious expression.

"Are you all right, child? You look pale."

"I'm fine, Halmoni. I just..." He stopped. "I need to go."

She nodded slowly, not pushing. "Okay, child. It was very nice to have you here."

"It was nice to be here," he said, and he meant it even through everything that was suddenly crowding his head. He folded the handkerchief in his pocket very carefully. "I'll bring you something beautiful, I promise."

"I will be waiting," she said warmly.

He walked faster than he'd come.

Why? Why would Jungkook try to hide this? It was just a tiny thing, nothing big, and he could tell anyone, but for some reason he hadn't told Taehyung.

Taehyung watched Jungkook's face do something he had never seen it do before, watched the laugh die somewhere before it reached his mouth, and watched something freeze behind those honest eyes.

What the fuck was Jungkook doing?

The courtroom had gone completely silent.

Taehyung's chest started to do something painful. His tongue got bitter.

"It's about time you stop lying to him, little brother," Yoongi said out loud.

Little brother.

The words landed like something dropped from a great height. "Taeyung, baby –” Jungkook didn’t get to complete it, though.

Taehyung stepped back.
