Chapter 4

The alpha woke before the sun came up.

Jungkook placed his feet on the wooden boards and sat there for a slow count of breaths, letting his body wake up properly. He did it every day to regulate his body.

Today was a hunt day. The pack needed to build up their stores before winter really picked up, and that was everything he had to do today.

The more meat they could gather for everyone, the better off they'd all be when the cold months hit hardest. Now, as the pack alpha, it was his duty to make sure every single member of the pack stayed well fed and never went to bed with an empty stomach.

He moved towards the living room. The hearth had gone out sometime during the night, leaving the air chilly.

He bent down and set a small fire, just enough to warm a pot and take the edge off the cold. Then he went to check the stores: rice in the clay jar, leftover turkey from the night before, an apple that was starting to soften, and a pear.

He would reheat the meal and cut up the fruit. That was what he always did, and there was a kind of comfort in the routine by now. Living by himself had made his routine pretty simple and peaceful.

He boiled water and dropped a few dried herbs into the kettle while it heated.

Nothing fancy; just a simple wash for the throat and something to ease the muscle aches.

Taehyung's legs had been doing better these past few days; the worst of the bruises were fading into a pale yellow color. It was a good sign.

Still, Jungkook figured the tea might help. Mornings were always the worst. Every old bruise and injury hurts a little more after waking up.

He reheated the turkey until it was warm all the way through, tested the rice with his fingers to make sure it wasn't too dry, and sliced the fruit into even pieces.

He arranged the plate without thinking much about how it looked. Fruit to one side, rice in the center, meat placed neatly to stay cool and ready to eat.

He set the cup of herbal tea on the tray last, watching the steam curl up and then fade as the liquid cooled. He left the tray on the table and straightened the chair beside it for when Taheyung wakes up, of course.

Everything he did was always for Taheyung nowadays.

He tied his coat around his shoulders and checked for the hunting tools he'd need later. Knife, leather straps, and the small satchel for salt and preserves. He thought about the forest routes they'd take today, which paths would be slick with frost, and which clearings would be safe enough to hunt in at dawn.

As the pack Alpha, everything was his concern. It was a good headache, though. He loved ruling.

He made a mental list of everything that needed to be done.

They weren't going to the Liora forest, which belonged to their pack. No, today they were heading to the Kaelen forest, a good distance away from Moon Village.

It didn't belong to any pack in particular, but it was open to all the neighboring packs for hunting because Kaelen was known for having an abundance of wildlife. It was a good opportunity to hunt and bring back enough food to keep the village fed through the lean months.

He paused at Taheyung’s bedroom door and looked toward the bed where Taehyung was still sleeping. His mate looked soft in the early light, peaceful even, with his hair falling across his face and his mouth slightly open.

There was even a little bit of drool on the pillow, which made something warm flicker in Jungkook's chest before he pushed it away.

He didn't stand there for long, though. There were other things to do, and there was a certain kind of distance he tried to respect between them.

He'd left the tray where it would be easy to find when Taehyung woke up. That felt like enough for now.

Outside, there was a chill in the air that hadn't been there before. Jungkook pulled his coat closer as he stepped away from the cabin. He moved out into the morning, shutting the door behind him.

On the walk to the gathering spot, he passed a few huts that were still dark. A dog was asleep on someone's porch, curled up tight against the cold.

Someone had tied a bundle of kindling outside their door, ready for the day's fire. The village was just starting to wake up. He reached the meeting place where the other alphas and hunters were already gathering. Men and women arrived with packs slung over their shoulders, their breath  were visible in the cold air like little clouds.

They greeted each other with short nods.

There was a quick exchange of information about which route they should take and which traps had failed the week before. Jungkook listened carefully, marking the routes he would personally check and the areas where they'd need to warn the keepers if they found signs of rogue trails through pack territory.

"Everything is ready, alpha," Myul said, stepping up beside him. "We have all the weapons loaded and the carts ready to carry the meat back."

Jungkook nodded his approval, then took one last look at the satchel hanging from his belt.

His thoughts drifted back briefly to his pretty mate. The way he loved cooking for his pup.

That was the kind of small, personal work he did between the larger, more public tasks: making sure there was food on the table and making sure the body had something warm to settle the stomach.

It was practical. It was necessary. It was what he could control when so much else felt uncertain.

He stepped forward with the pack and followed them into the trees. The forest got quieter the deeper they went, the branches blocking out more and more of the light.

He kept his eyes ahead. The hunt would take hours. They'd track whatever they could find, bring the meat back, then salt and store it before winter came and made everything harder.

He probably should have told Taehyung where he was going, but he didn't.

He'd let the omega sleep because it felt wrong to wake him up just to explain his whereabouts, especially when Taehyung already seemed to resent every small thing Jungkook did.

Jungkook kept his focus on the task at hand, on the way the forest changed underfoot, and on the occasional sounds that might mean game was nearby.

Even while he walked, he kept thinking about the cabin. The cup of tea. The plate he'd left on the table. They were small things, but they kept coming back to him anyway.

He didn't regret doing it. Once he'd made up his mind, that was usually the end of it. There wasn't much point wasting time wondering if he'd done the right thing.

That was how he kept the pack supplied and safe. That was how he kept people alive. He did it now, leaving the house with one small thing already set right for whoever was waiting inside it.

—

Taehyung woke up late. He felt so cozy that he wanted to cuddle up in blankets forever. Waking up wasn’t something that he wanted to do today.

He stretched his legs carefully, waiting for the familiar ache, but it never really came. The first few days walking had been painful. Now the bruises had turned yellow, and there was only a little soreness left. After washing his face at the basin by the window, he pulled on the same wool shirt he'd worn the day before.

He ran his fingers through his hair to smooth it back, not bothering with anything more than that. He was grateful, in a strange way, that Jungkook had gone back to his old house and brought his things here: the thread, the crochet needles, the small pieces of his life that had somehow survived everything.

Those small objects gave him something to do while he healed, something to focus on that wasn't just the four walls closing in. It was the one gesture that had made this place feel a little less like someone else's home and a little more like somewhere he could breathe.

When he stepped into the kitchen, the sight waiting on the table was no surprise at all. It was the same as the last seven mornings: a neat plate of food, a cup of herbal tea with steam that was already starting to fade into the cool air.

He stood there for a moment, just looking at it. The arrangement was careful, like someone had spent time thinking about it.

It softened something in him for a breath, that quiet attention to detail that he hadn't asked for but received anyway. Then the warmth twisted and turned sharp.

Who did Jungkook think he was?

The thought rose up fast, hot and bitter in his chest. These gestures; the food, the tea, the fire that was always lit before dawn. They felt too much like someone trying to win back something that had never belonged to them in the first place.

Taehyung hated the part of himself that still responded to it, that still felt something warm and grateful when he saw rice and fruit arranged on a clean plate. He hated that it could twist something inside his chest and make him feel things he didn't want to feel.

He sat down anyway, because hunger was ordinary and real, and spite alone wasn't enough to fill an empty stomach.

He ate a little, slow bites that he barely tasted. Between each one, the anger came back in a quieter form, settling into his bones like cold water.

Jungkook's face flashed through his mind wearing the same expression he'd had the night he pulled Taehyung from the forest and claimed him. That word still burned in his mouth every time he thought it. Claimed.

Six years. Six years of running and hiding from every scent that even hinted at an alpha. Six years spent building up the illusion of safety, brick by careful brick. And it had all ended like this anyway, with him trapped under another name, another roof, another man's sense of duty and responsibility.

He finished the tea and pushed the cup away with more force than necessary. The small kindness only made the resentment dig deeper.

Taehyung had thought this before, and he thought it again now: in the end, alphas could never really be good. They only pretended long enough for people like him to forget what they really were underneath.

He gathered up the empty dishes and set them in the basin.

The day stretched out ahead of him, another endless loop of hours to fill with thread and sketches and silence that pressed against his ears.

He sat by the window for a long while after that, just watching the way the light shifted through the leaves outside. The morning felt heavy and slow. Eventually, he decided he needed to do something with his hands or he'd go crazy just sitting there.

His sweaters had started to wear thin around the cuffs, the threads loose and soft from too much use.

He could have asked Jungkook for new ones, but even the thought of it made his chest tighten with resistance. He didn't want to owe Jungkook for everything, didn't want to add one more thing to the invisible list of debts that seemed to be piling up between them.

Besides, his mother had taught him to make his own sweaters when he was small, before the wars and the wandering and everything that hurt in between. He could still hear her somewhere in the back of his head, counting stitches in that quiet way she had. He reached for it now and let his hands follow.

He dug into the old canvas bag Jungkook had brought from his house a few days back, the one that still smelled like lavender and cedar, like home used to. Inside were small balls of wool, some half-used and knotted up with old thread, and a couple needles tucked into the corner.

He caught himself smiling a little, despite everything. The things had made it through better than he had.

He set everything out on the low table by the window and curled his legs underneath him. The wooden floor was cold against his ankles, but the sunlight pouring through the glass made up for it, warming his skin and making the room feel almost peaceful.

He began to untangle the yarn, rolling it into smoother, neater circles, and then he looped the first few stitches onto his needle. The familiar rhythm came back to him easily: in, pull, over, through his fingers moving almost before his mind could catch up with what they were doing.

He started humming under his breath without really meaning to, just a tune that had no real shape to it, something to fill up the quiet space around him.

The sweater already had some shape to it at the shoulders, a patch of soft gray wool that he'd started weeks ago, back before everything went wrong and his whole life fell apart.

He traced the edge of it with his thumb, feeling the texture, and then began another row. He watched the loops grow one after the other, slow, like they were building something solid out of nothing.

Sometimes he paused to glance outside, where a few pack members passed by in the yard. He could see them laughing about something he couldn't hear, their voices carrying faintly through the glass.

He wondered if any of them noticed him sitting here in the window. He wondered if they still whispered the things Jungkook had mentioned the night before about the rumors, the assumptions, and the ugly words people used when they thought no one important was listening.

The thought made him knit faster, his stitches getting tighter as if he could somehow weave all that noise right out of his own head.

After a while, though, the tension in his shoulders started to slip away. It was replaced by focus, the sort that made the rest of the world fade into the background.

Stray strands of wool clung to his sleeves and caught in his hair. One piece got tangled near his ear, and he brushed it away with a small, frustrated huff that was almost a laugh.

The sweater was really beginning to take shape now. It was a little uneven at the edges, far from perfect, but it was his. He'd made it with his own hands, and that felt like something he could hold onto.

He leaned back after an hour or two had passed, stretching his shoulders and rolling his neck to work out the stiffness.

His stomach growled, reminding him that he hadn't eaten much that morning, but he didn't want to stop yet. He wanted to finish at least this part, to have something solid and complete to show for the day.

When he finally set the needles down in his lap, the beginnings of a warm, thick collar sat there waiting. He held it up to the light and inspected it carefully, looking at the uneven stitches and the places where his tension had shifted.

He smiled again.

Maybe later, he thought, he'd ask Jungkook if there was more wool stored somewhere in the house.

The sweater sat half-finished in his lap, and his fingers were still loosely holding onto the yarn even as his eyelids began to droop.

He didn't realize when his breathing started to slow down or when his hand finally slipped away from the knitting needle and went limp in his lap. He was right there on the edge of sleep; but then a sharp knock broke through the quiet like a stone thrown through glass.

Knock.

Then the knocks were urgent and hurried, like whoever was standing outside didn't want to wait another second.

His heart gave a hard, uncomfortable stutter in his chest.

No one ever knocked here. Jungkook certainly never did. He'd just walk right in, barefoot, and fill up the entire space before Taehyung could even blink or prepare himself. But this... this was someone else.

He sat up way too fast, and the half-finished sweater slid right off his lap and onto the floor. He stared at the door like it might give him answers.

Who could it possibly be?

For a second, real fear clawed its way up the back of his throat.

What if it was someone from the pack? What if they'd somehow found out the truth and they'd realized he wasn't marked, that his scent didn't carry Jungkook's claim the way it should have by now, the way everyone assumed it did?

"...Who's there?" No reply. Just silence.

He swallowed hard. He glanced once toward the window, half expecting to see someone, but there was nothing.

"Who's there?" he tried again, forcing his voice to come out a little louder this time.

Still nothing. No answer at all.

He bit down on the inside of his cheek until he tasted blood. Fuck his anxiety.

Maybe it was Jungkook, back early and teasing him for some reason.

Maybe it was someone else entirely with bad intentions. He took a breath and forced himself to move toward the door.

The house felt way too big all of a sudden, and every corner seemed to echo his own heartbeat back at him.

When he finally reached the door and unlatched it, his hands were trembling so badly he almost couldn't grip the handle properly.

"Irene?" His voice shocked completely at her name.

There she was, standing right there on the porch like she'd appeared out of nowhere.

She looked exactly the same as usual, yet completely out of place standing against the backdrop of this house, this life that wasn't supposed to include her.

"What are you doing here?" he whispered, his shock barely contained. The words came out rushed and confused, but before he could say anything else, she lifted one hand and pressed a finger to her lips, shushing him urgently.

"Not here," she said quickly.

She stepped past him before he could even react, moving into the house like she had every right to be there. "Close the door, Tae."

He did it automatically, before his mind caught up. The latch clicked shut behind them.

The tension sitting in his chest didn't fade at all; if anything, it got heavier. Irene never should have been here. Omegas weren't supposed to come near an alpha's home, especially not alone, especially not unannounced like this. If anyone saw her...

Taehyung turned around, and words started tumbling out of his mouth before he could stop them. "Are you crazy? If someone sees you here…"

"I made sure no one did," she cut him off, her breath still coming a little uneven. "I had to see you, Tae."

That was all it took. Everything he'd been holding in gave way, and before he even realized what he was doing, Taehyung stepped forward and wrapped his arms tightly around her.

The familiar scent reached him straight away. Lavender. It felt like coming home.

"I thought..." His voice caught halfway through the words. He buried his face against her shoulder and tried again. "I thought I'd never see you again."

Irene held him just as tightly, her arms closing around him until there wasn't any space left between them. One hand came up to the back of his head while the other stayed between his shoulder blades.

"Shh," she whispered. "I know." Her hand rubbed slowly over his back.

"I know. I missed you too. So much."

He pulled back just enough to look at her properly, and he knew his eyes were probably red now. "Does anyone know you're here?"

"No," she said quickly, shaking her head. "And they can't find out, Tae. If they do, it'll be bad for both of us. Really bad."

He frowned, confusion threading its way through the relief that was still making his chest feel tight. "Then why would you risk coming?"

"Because I needed to see with my own eyes," she interrupted, and her gaze was scanning his face now, his arms, looking for the bruises from the branches that hadn't completely faded yet.

"They're saying things, Tae. People are saying you're not okay here. That he's hurting you."

Taehyung froze completely. His breath caught in his throat. Because that was exactly what they'd tried to make people see, wasn't it?

That things weren't okay between them, even when they were okayish. That was the whole point of the act.

"They're wrong," he said after a long pause.

"I'm fine. Really, I am."

Irene searched his expression like she didn't believe him at all; she wanted to reach out and shake the real truth out of him until he admitted whatever she thought he was hiding. But she didn't do that. She just sighed, and her shoulders relaxed.

"I just..." she said softly, looking down at her hands. "I had to make sure for myself. I couldn't just sit there and wonder."

He almost smiled at that, because it was so perfectly Irene. Bold where he wasn't, fearless in all the places where he always hesitated and second-guessed himself.

Still, he said what he needed to: "You really shouldn't have come here."

"Too late for that now," she said, and she crossed her arms over her chest with that familiar, stubborn tilt of her chin that he knew so well. "Now tell me the truth, Taehyung. Are you really fine here? Don't lie to me."

Irene kept glancing toward the door as if she were expecting to hear footsteps outside at any moment, but Taehyung could see her hands shaking just a little bit. He wanted to ask her how far she'd come and if anyone had followed her, but before he could get the words out, she spoke first.

"I shouldn't let anyone see both of us together," she said, her voice hushed even when the voices won't go outside the house.

"He told me not to."

Taehyung blinked at her, confused. "He? Who's he?"

She hesitated for just a second, searching his face like she was trying to figure out how he'd react. Then she answered simply, "The pack alpha."

Taehyung's lips parted in surprise. "What do you mean?"

"He sent me," Irene said.

"He came to me early this morning. Said you've been alone all day while he's gone with the pack on the hunt. He didn't want you to feel...I don’t know."

She paused, choosing her words. "Trapped, I guess. So he told me to come sit with you for a few hours. Said it might make the day feel less quiet and lonely."

Taehyung just stared at her. For a second, his throat went completely dry, and he couldn't have spoken even if he'd wanted to.

Jungkook, the same man who'd dragged him here against his will, who moved through this house like it was some kind of cage meant to keep him safe and controlled, had actually sent her? Had he thought about him being alone and done something about it?

"That doesn't sound like him," he murmured under his breath, half to himself, trying to make sense of it.

Irene tilted her head slightly, watching him. "Maybe he thought I could help make you more... I don't know, petite? Docile?"

He almost laughed at that, but the sound never actually made it out of his throat. It was mean. Instead, something like confusion crossed his features, a small flicker of disbelief that wouldn't leave his chest no matter how hard he tried to push it away.

Before he could say anything in response, Irene's eyes suddenly narrowed. She took a small step closer to him, scanning him more carefully now, and then her gaze froze on one specific spot.

"Wait," she whispered, and her voice had changed completely.

"Tae... your neck."

Taehyung went completely stiff, his hand instinctively flying up to cover the side of his throat. The skin there was bare. There was no faint scar, no scent mark, nothing that belonged to anyone else.

"W-what about it?" he asked, even though he already knew exactly what she'd seen. Or rather, what she hadn't seen.

Her eyes went wide, and he watched the realization hit her like a physical blow. "He didn't mark you."

"Irene–"

"Taehyung," she cut him off, and her voice was so shocked. She was going to make a scene out of it.

"He didn't mark you? At all?"

He shook his head quickly, his eyes darting nervously toward the closed door like someone might burst through it at any second. "No. Please-please don't say it out loud. If anyone hears us—"

"But everyone thinks—"

"I know." His voice cracked on those two words.

"I know what they think. They all said it, didn't they? That he marked me, that he knotted me so rough that I was bleeding badly–" He stopped himself abruptly, his breathing unsteady and harsh.

"They think I bled. They think he claimed me completely."

Irene's face went pale, all the color draining out of it at once. "Gods," she whispered, one hand coming up to cover her mouth.

"They were saying it like it was some kind of victory. Some proof that he'd really made you his. I didn't believe them, but... Tae, how did you let this happen? How did it get this far?"

"It wasn't like that," he said quickly, the words rushing out. "We just- we had to pretend. It was the only way to keep me safe. If the pack knew the truth, they'd banish me. Or him. Or maybe both of us. We didn't have a choice."

Irene's eyebrows pulled together, her expression caught somewhere between shock and concern.

"So you've been lying to the entire pack this whole time."

He nodded, feeling helpless and exposed. "Please, Irene. You can't tell anyone. You're the only person who knows the truth now. The only one."

She looked at him for a long moment, and he watched the shock slowly give way to something softer and more protective. She reached out and gripped his hand tightly between both of hers.

"I'm your best friend, remember? I would never tell. Not a single word. Not ever."

Relief flashed through him so suddenly and intensely that it actually hurt. He squeezed her hand back, and a small sound left him. "Thank you. Really. Thank you."

She gave him a faint smile. "You're lucky I love you too much to throw you under the wolves."

That pulled something that was almost a real laugh out of him.

Then her expression shifted and became more serious again. "How's living with him been? Really?" The question just hung there in the air between them.

Taehyung hesitated, trying to find the right words. "I don't know," he said finally, and it was the most honest answer he could give.

"It's... strange. He's quiet most of the time. Doesn't talk much. But he does things without asking first. He fixes things around the house, cooks meals, and always leaves food out for me. Like he's trying to make up for something I never even asked him to make up for."

Irene nodded slowly, listening carefully to every word. He looked away from her, his fingers finding a loose thread on his sleeve and tracing it over and over.

"You know I never wanted any of this. I never wanted to be tied to an alpha, to belong to someone like that. That's exactly why I ran away in the first place. I wanted to be my own person and to live my life without being owned by anyone."

"I know," Irene murmured, and her voice was soft with old memories.

"I remember the night you came here... You were shaking so badly, but you said you'd rather die alone in the woods than live your whole life under someone's control. You were so small back then, oh moon goddess!"

"Yeah," he whispered, his throat tight. "I meant every word of it."

A quiet settled over them again. Then Irene shifted her position, turning to face him more directly. She took his hand again, and when she spoke, her voice was gentler but somehow more serious.

"Tae... does he hurt you?"

“What?” He asked. He just told her that he didn’t mate him.

“Did he rape you?”

His head snapped up so fast it almost hurt. "What?"

"Does he hit you? Force you to do things you don't want to do? Did he force sex on you?"

"What? No," Taehyung stammered, his eyes going wide with genuine confusion. "No, he doesn't–he's never done it."

"Look at me," she said firmly, and it wasn't a request.

He did, and she could see the truth written all over his face: confusion, a flicker of defensiveness, and something else underneath that he couldn't quite name.

"He doesn't hurt me," he repeated, and his voice was quieter now.

"Not once. He grabbed me pretty hard that first night, dragged me out of the forest, and it hurt my leg. But he really didn't mean to cause that much damage. He was actually kind of... saving me, in a way."

Irene studied him for a long moment, searching for any crack in his words, any sign that he was hiding something darker.

"Then maybe that's exactly the problem."

He frowned at her, genuinely confused. "What do you mean?"

She sighed, and her thumb brushed against his knuckles in a gesture that was meant to be comforting. "Tae, you've always been too soft-hearted. You see a little bit of kindness, and it starts to feel like safety. You start to forget what's really underneath."

He opened his mouth to argue with her, to tell her she was wrong, but she didn't let him get a word out.

"Did you grow a soft spot for him?" she asked, and her eyes were steady on his, unflinching.

"Did you forget what he really is?"

Taehyung's breath caught in his chest. The words hit him harder than he expected them to.

"He's an alpha, Tae. A killer. Everyone knows what he's done to get his place in this pack and keep it."

Taehyung's lips parted, but no sound came out. The words just echoed inside his head, stirring up something he couldn't quite name.

He knew Jungkook had killed the previous pack alpha. Everyone knew that. But what could have been the reason behind it? What had driven him to do something that brutal?

And for the first time in days, maybe longer, he couldn't tell if the defensive feeling rising in his chest was meant to protect Jungkook... or himself.

Irene sat cross-legged near the window while Taehyung sorted through the loose skeins of yarn, winding them back into neat, manageable balls.

"So they still think he's the same old monster," Irene was saying, keeping her voice low like the walls might be listening.

"You know what the villagers say about him. Half of them think he keeps bodies buried somewhere under this house."

Taehyung rolled his eyes but couldn't help the faint smile that tugged at his mouth. "They think anyone with claws sharper than theirs is automatically a monster."

She laughed at that, leaning back on her palms and stretching her legs out.

"Maybe that's true. But you can't really blame them for being scared, Tae. He has actually killed people, you know. The fights for dominance, the border raids, all of that.”

"That's just pack life," Taehyung interrupted, and he didn't even realize he'd done it until the words were already out.

"That's survival, Irene. All of them do that. Every alpha in every pack."

Her eyebrows lifted high, and there was something knowing in her expression. "Oh? Since when do you defend alphas?"

"I'm not defending anyone." He bit the words off too quickly and defensively. "I'm just saying it's not as black and white as people try to make it sound."

"Mm-hm." She tilted her head to the side, clearly unconvinced. "You've been spending too much time under the same roof with him; that's all this is."

"I haven't–" he started, then stopped himself completely. He stared down at the ball of wool in his hands, twisting the thread around his fingers until it started to hurt and left red marks on his skin.

"I just don't think he's exactly what everyone says he is. That's all. I'm still here, aren't I? Still alive and breathing."

Irene gave a small, knowing hum in the back of her throat. "That's exactly how people end up getting bitten, Tae. They think they're different. They think they're special."

He didn't have an answer to that, so he just stayed quiet. They sat there together in the silence for a while, and it was starting to go a little dark now.

Irene started talking about Mingyu then: how he'd gone on the big hunt that morning with all the other alphas, how the village stores were running dangerously low, how much she hated sleeping alone in their bed at night. It was normal talk, safe talk.

Where they couldn’t argue.

Taehyung listened, nodding along and smiling at the right moments. He even laughed a little when she told him the story about the baker's son who'd accidentally dyed his hair blue while trying to fix some problem with the mill's water dye system.

For a little while there, sitting in the pack house with his best friend beside him, it almost felt like the old times. Like everything that had gone wrong hadn't happened yet.

But when the sun finally began to dip below the horizon and the chill of evening started coming in, Irene glanced toward the door with obvious reluctance.

"I should probably go. Mingyu will be back soon, and if anyone sees me leaving here…"

Taehyung nodded quickly. "Yeah. I know. Please be careful, okay?"

She stood up and immediately pulled him into another tight hug, her hair brushing against his cheek and smelling like home.

"You too. And Tae? Remember what I said."

"What, that my alpha's secretly a killer?" he tried to tease, but his voice came out weaker than he wanted it to.

She smiled at him, but her eyes stayed completely serious. "That you don't owe anyone your heart just because they're treating you kindly. Kindness doesn't erase what someone is underneath it all."

He swallowed hard and nodded once.

He walked her to the door and watched as she slipped outside quickly, pulling her hood up over her head to hide her face. The silence that filled the house after she left felt so much heavier than it had before.

The space seemed too large again. Taehyung stood there for a moment just staring at the place where she'd been, then forced himself to move.

He gathered up the scattered balls of wool from where they'd rolled across the floor and folded his half-made sweater as carefully as he could manage. He stacked the needles neatly on the table, brushed off the couch cushions, and tried to make the room look lived-in and busy enough that it wouldn't feel quite so empty.

He'd just started carrying the basket of supplies back toward his room when he heard it.

The sound of heavy boots on the threshold made him freeze in place, his hands tightening on the basket handle.

Jungkook came in the hall.

His chest was bare as usual, and his skin was streaked with mud and thin scratches from whatever he must have done tonight. His dark hair was pulled back and still damp from the cold air outside, and there was a large hunting bag slung over one muscular arm.

"I'm home, pup," he said; his voice sounded all tired.

Taehyung didn't even turn to face him properly. "I have eyes," he muttered, keeping his tone flat and unimpressed.
