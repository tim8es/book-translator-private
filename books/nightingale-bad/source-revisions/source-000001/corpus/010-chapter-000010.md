# Chapter 9

"Good morning, Kurapika. How are you feeling?" Kaiser asks.

Kurapika is sitting in his winter coat with his limbs arranged to pantomime relaxation.

"I'm feeling fine. Yourself?" Kurapika replies.

"I cannot complain."

Kaiser makes a mark on his clipboard.

"How are your sessions with Chrollo?" Kaiser asks.

Kurapika knew this question would be coming and he knows how he needs to answer. He crosses his
ankles and laces his fingers together. He answers slowly. Deliberately.

"They’re going alright. Chrollo is nice but…"

He allows the "but" to linger for a few beats. Kaiser doesn't give any nonverbal prompts to continue like a

normal person would for a conversation. Kurapika continues anyway.

"… But I think he's a little limited in his self-taut education."

The words have a slimy texture in Kurapika’s mouth. Usually, Kurapika doesn't feel this way when he's

criticizing people. The problem is that it's a lie. It's a lie Chrollo knows he's telling because it should help
him seem like a better match for Tserracnich, but it still feels wrong.

"I don't think he's incapable," Kurapika adds to maintain his sweeter persona. "It's just that he hasn't been
exposed to a broader range of topics…"


Kaiser stares at Kurapika impassively.

"… Do you find it frustrating? To talk to someone of lower education?" Kaiser asks after a long beat of

silence.


Kurapika is stunned by the loaded question. Kaiser has never asked something so leading before. It's far
from  the  "non-directive"  therapy  style  Chrollo  mentioned.  In  fact,  Kurapika  thinks   it's  wholly

unprofessional.

And it's not far off from a complaint Kurapika thinks Tserracnich would make.

Kurapika’s hands clench inside the sleeves if his jacket.

"… Sometimes," Kurapika hears himself answer quietly, feeling distant.


Kaiser makes another mark on his clipboard. Not a single muscle on his face so much as twitches.

"You told him I'm too stupid for you?"


"I said it more kindly than that."


"Good."

Kurapika taps his fingers against his forearm. "Boss," he thinks. Regardless of whether or not Kurapika's

words to the therapist get him closer to Tserracnich, Chrollo would want the administration to
underestimate his intelligence.

Even so,  it's hard for Kurapika to focus on what Chrollo’s getting out of this when Kaiser's strange
question sticks in his mind. It felt almost too specific. Kaiser couldn't know that Kurapika knows that
Tserracnich is an option. Perhaps Kaiser knows that Tserracnich is getting frustrated with his omega and

is looking for a new candidate in advance. But  if that is the case, being careless with the omegas'

therapy just to look for a new toy for Tserracnich indicates a troubling level of corruption. It wouldn't be
the first bad sign about the program, but it could be the worst…

"What's got your wheels spinning?" Chrollo asks.

Kurapika's head snaps up as he's pulled from his thoughts. Kurapika hadn't even realized that he's still on
his feet and wearing his coat. Meanwhile, Chrollo’s seated comfortably in his usual spot on the couch.

Kurapika sighs and shrugs off his coat, dropping it over the back of the chair. He combs his fingers
through his hair as he tries to decide how honest he wants to be with Chrollo.

"… I wonder if Tserracnich talks about his compulsion towards exploitation in therapy or if that all gets
wrapped up into typical alpha dominance issues…" Kurapika grumbles, not really answering Chrollo’s

question.

"Therapy didn't go well," Chrollo infers.

Kurapika joins Chrollo on the couch, resting his elbows on his knees and letting his head hang between
his shoulders.


Chrollo tilts his head.


"Did Kaiser try to invalidate your complaints about me?" He asks.


Kurapika shakes his head no.

"He was too eager to… accept them."

Chrollo steeples his fingers. "… That is odd."


"I don't know what to make of it, but I don't like it." Kurapika says.

Chrollo’s fingers knit together and he looks straight ahead at nothing.


"I couldn't imagine what Tserracnich talks about in his therapy sessions." Chrollo says, going back to
Kurapika’s earlier musings. "When  I overhear him talking to other inmates he's an egotistical gender-

essentialist, but he probably doesn't speak like that to the people who control his access to an omega."
Chrollo speculates.

"Maybe. Or he's completely honest and the people running the program think that forcing him to spend
time a good omega who he can't assault and dispose of will fix some of that." Kurapika counters.


"So, you think he might be actually doing therapy right by being himself?"


Unlike us.

"I didn't say that," Kurapika dismisses quickly. "But if he's such an egotist, wouldn't changing himself for
others be beneath him? And being a dominating freak just so happens to be working out for him again?"

"Maybe…" Chrollo drawls. "Well, you're the one who's been studying him for years."

Kurapika leans back into the couch with a deflated sigh. He lets go of the urge to argue and tries to see
Chrollo’s perspective a little more. Since Chrollo’s in the same prison and therapy group as Tserracnich,
he probably does have a better sense for what the alpha is like.

"… I truly don't know. The only time I ever saw the man personally was in the courtroom. During the trial I
thought he was very… direct." Kurapika says, much more subdued now.

From what Kurapika could see, Tserracnich was upfront about the things he did and didn't do that strictly

corresponded to the evidence. Of course, Tserracnich would have been coached by his lawyers in
advance, just like Kurapika was before he went to the stand.

Chrollo hums idly. "As I said before, it is difficult for me to parse what a man like Tserracnich could be

thinking. I can admit to you that I don't have the proper feelings when it comes to taking a life but-"

"Tserracnich is different." Kurapika finishes, perhaps too hastily. Chrollo lets him without comment.

"And you don't talk about how you feel about killing in your therapy sessions." Kurapika surmises.

"I don't talk about anything that matters, same as you." Chrollo says softly. "Similarly, I haven't spoken
about Tserracnich in many years."

Kurapika looks directly at Chrollo, trying in vain once again to read his serene face.


"Not even with your people?" He questions.

Chrollo shakes his head. "Not with them. With them, I have to be… I have to be strong."


Kurapika knows what Chrollo really means - that he has to be a leader - but it seems he still won't tell

Kurapika that.

"… Her name was Sarasa."


Kurapika straightens up as Chrollo begins to share something Kurapika never thought he would.


"She was one of my friends at the church-run orphanage I grew up in." Chrollo shares.


Kurapika listens quietly. Against his wishes, he begins to feel closer to Chrollo with every word. That

feeling manifests as a growing ache in his heart.


"Sarasa was sweet and outgoing. She was an honest and punctual sort of girl. The day she went missing
without explanation wasn't like her at all. It terrified me the very moment I noticed she wasn't where she

was supposed to be. When I found her, or rather, what was left of her, she was in a sack."

Chrollo is staring at a blank wall as he recounts the details, his voice low and closed off from any

emotion despite saying how he felt that day.


"I don't know that it was Tserracnich personally - it probably wasn't. But I believe the culprits were in the
exploitation ring he's the head of now."


Mentally, Kurapika connects Chrollo's story to the facts. Tserracnich's in his late forties now, so he would

have been in his twenties when Chrollo was a preteen. It's possible that Tserracnich could have been
been personally involved in what happened to her but no evidence was ever recovered.

The nature of the crime is strikingly similar to Kurapika’s experience as well. There are details that

Chrollo couldn't have possibly known in order to manufacture the story for Kurapika's sympathy.

When Chrollo’s eyes finally meet his, Kurapika stares into the black, vacuous holes. Kurapika’s always
thought there was an emptiness to Chrollo’s gaze but now it's so clear to him. Chrollo fills himself up
with things - his books, his gang, his chosen identity as a thief - but nothing is enough. For the first time,
Kurapika reaches for Chrollo’s hand first. Kurapika doesn't think he can't hold it in anymore.

"… I used to be a preschool teacher…" Kurapika confesses.

In the past, Kurapika’s been teased for having such a damn stereotypical omega career. Reflexively, he
braces himself but the mockery doesn't come.

Kurapika takes a shaky breath and continues.

"… I make better money at a shady bar now then I did when I was taking care of people's children, if you

can believe that."

"I absolutely can believe that." Chrollo says.

Kurapika smiles dryly."But that wasn't why I quit."

He curls into himself as though trying to shield himself from the memory though he knows he can't. Even

back when Kurapika and Chrollo were playing with those stupid icebreaker cards he could hardly stand
the mention of being a teacher.


"One of my students  - the sweetest  little boy  - his name was Parrio." Kurapika closes his eyes,

remembering the child's face. "He was so shy but he loved bugs a lot. I always had to keep an extra eye

on him to make sure he didn't wander off during recess."

Kurapika’s brows furrow as his imagination takes him down the spiral of the painful memories.

"And one day he went missing. Everyone was so worried - I was so worried. Every waking moment his
parents were searching for him. It took months for us to find…"


Feeling a wave of nausea, Kurapika covers his mouth. In his mind, he's following the same paths as he

did that day. Stepping off the  trail in the woods between neighborhoods with deadfall crunching
underfoot, losing his footing as he climbed over a rotting, fallen tree. Seeing the corner of a plastic bag

that was most likely litter but he had to check. He couldn't overlook any detail.


"I found him by accident. It wasn't police work or detectives, it was me. If  I didn't wander off in the

direction I did, his parents might've never had anything to bury."

It's just like how Chrollo found Sarasa. Kurapika tries to stop himself from imagining how a little boy with

black hair and wide eyes would have handled seeing the things Kurapika saw, but he can't. Chrollo is right
here as an example. Kurapika tips his head back, struggling to breath. He feels feverish and clammy.

Chrollo squeezes his hand, grounding Kurapika enough for him to continue.

"I found Parrio in parts. And the things that had been done to his h-head…"

Kurapika pitches forward, dry heaving. He can't describe what he saw. Parrio's head was recognizable at
best but there were obvious signs of heavy abuse. Kurapika couldn't tell if the abuse happened before or

after Parrio's death. Maybe it was both. According to the autopsy, his body parts had been dunked in
bleach before being tossed away so none of the evidence could be linked to specific individuals.
Fortunately, they were able to gather other types of evidence to add the the growing case file detectives

had already been building against Tserracnich's group. But in the end, Tserracnich himself was never
charged for what happened to Parrio. They could only get him for lesser charges. It was better than

nothing, but still not enough.

"I know Tserracnich is responsible. I can't stomach the fact that a monster like him is still living in this

world. I don't care if he can't hurt anyone else anymore - I want him dead." Kurapika whispers viciously,
his voice harsh and bitter with hate. "And I want to be the one who does it."

Chrollo’s fingers interlock with Kurapika’s and he shifts closer until their shoulders are pressed together.

"Did you like being a teacher?" Chrollo asks.

The simple, unexpected question almost causes Kurapika to collapse. For a few aching heartbeats he

can't breath. The worst part is he doesn't know why it hurts him so much. Kurapika nods, blinking away

the tears stinging his eyes before they can fall. Still, his voice trembles.

"I loved it. I loved working with kids."

Even now, he's certain he could identify the developing handwriting of every child that was in his class.

And he won't ever do it again.


Kurapika shuts his eyes, wanting to hide them. He knows without checking they're rimmed red. Despite
how hard he's worked at keeping himself from crying over every little thing, he can't help it. Grief, anger,
even happiness… Kurapika wishes he wasn't so emotional. He doesn't cry like he used to, but his eyes
still get red.

Chrollo lets go of Kurapika’s hands. Cool fingers startle Kurapika as they lightly touch the thin skin
around his eyes. He's tense as Chrollo’s thumbs caress the dark blotches under his eyes and brush along

the tips of his lashes.


"I believe that revenge is going to be the best form of therapy." Chrollo whispers.


Kurapika laughs humorously, some of the tension leaking out and gathering in his eyelashes.

"I shouldn't listen to your mental-health advice." Kurapika’s voice is shamefully wet.

Chrollo’s thumb swipes across his lashes line again, drying his eyes.


"But you will because it's what you want to hear. Let's kill that son of a bitch."

Aside from the few times they've spoken, Babimyna is the sort of man who has been very good at

minding his own business. But since Chrollo's started to scent Kurapika more heavily - enough for it to
linger for days at a time - Babimyna's been watching Kurapika more openly. Kurapika pretends not to

notice the way the guard's stare has changed but he is a little worried he's pretending to be too dense. It
has gotten rather… obvious.


As soon as Kurapika's alone in the visitation room for his next appointment he lets out a sign of relief.

Kurapika is still pretty sure that Babimyna is a good man, but that's part of the problem. The other part of

the problem is that Babimyna works for the prison system. Although Chrollo insists that Babimyna is
attracted to Kurapika, it would never work out between them. Not unless Babimyna would be willing to

actually do the right thing and put Kurapika in a room alone with Tserracnich. That would never happen
so Babimyna will never be on Kurapika's side.

Kurapika sits at the table to wait for Chrollo like usual. He can tell when Chrollo arrives but this time he's
stopped at the door. Kurapika's ears perk as he hears low voices briefly conversing. He holds his breath,
but it's still too muffled for Kurapika to make out anything.

There are cameras in the hall so whatever Chrollo and Babimyna are saying can't be that damning. Then
again, camera footage only exists when it's convenient for the people in charge. Kurapika swallows
thickly and counts how long the exchange lasts - five long, tense beats. Then the door opens and Chrollo

steps inside, carrying a roll of stained paper in his hands.

Kurapika frowns, glancing from the paper to Chrollo's face.

"What were you talking about?" Kurapika asks.

"This," Chrollo gestures to the paper. "He wanted to inspect it, but I think that was an excuse. I think he
just wanted to show me that he's in charge."

Kurapika takes that opinion with a grain of salt and turns his attention back to the paper. Chrollo's never

brought anything like that before. Kurapika had no idea it was allowed. Babimyna could be right to be
suspicious.


Chrollo approaches the table and unrolls the paper. He places it in front of Kurapika and smooths it out
with his hands. The paper is thick and won't lay completely flat.


"My fellow inmates and I did an art group therapy session. I made this for you." Chrollo says. "I also

brought up the idea of an ice cream social and the other inmates liked it."


Kurapika takes in his deadpan expression for a moment before taking a closer look at what's on the
paper.


It's an abstract blend of bright paints interwoven with strips of magazine that have been cut and pasted
onto the surface. Kurapika can't make out any hidden symbols of patterns… He squints and lifts the
paper up to the light to see if he can make out whatever message Chrollo is trying to convey to him that

way.


"There's no secret message." Chrollo says abruptly.


Kurapika looks over at Chrollo with confusion.


"What?"

"Well, I suppose that's not true, because all art has meaning. But this isn't about our deal or anything like
that. I just wanted to make something… for you." Chrollo explains.


Kurapika stares for a few moments in disbelief but Chrollo never cracks a smile. He stays completely
serious.

"… Oh," Kurapika lowers the page and looks at the colors and shapes again.

Instantly, he's back in his classroom, seeing the vibrant imagination pouring out of the children despite

how few supplies they had to express themselves with. Against Kurapika's will, he sees that same
liveliness in Chrollo. In this moment, he's no longer a deadpan, alpha convict confined to a give-and-take

relationship with a desperate omega as a way to get a glimpse of life outside the prison.

Suddenly, he sees that Chrollo is right. This paper is so loaded with meaning it's hard to look away.
Kurapika swallows thickly and runs his fingers over the rough, dried texture of the cheap, water-based
paint and the glue that's seeped out of the edges of shapes of the shards of magazine. He's being given
a glimpse of Chrollo’s vast internal world.

"I… thank you. I…"

Kurapika hesitates. Can he even say he likes it? Something about that doesn't feel right.

"You're radiating with passion." Chrollo says.

It's the sort of thing Melody would say.

He reaches down to take Kurapika’s hand and guides it to a particular section of the piece. The texture is
rough from the way the water has soaked the paper. It's a violent burst of color that bleeds all over the

rest of the piece.

"Just like this."

Suddenly nothing about the art looks messy or flawed.

"It's beautiful," Kurpika thinks.

"I think so too."


Kurapika chokes, realizing he's spoken his thoughts out loud. Chrollo curls over Kurapika, his chest

pressing to the line of his shoulders. It's warm like a winter coat and a cup of coffee on a cold, Yorknew
morning. Just like he's stepped out into a frigid sunrise, Kurapika takes a deep, satisfying breath. He
feels strangely at peace. Kurapika's sure the way he feels is fleeting, but it's as Melody said. If Kurapika

doesn't appreciate the things in life that are temporary, then there isn't anything to appreciate at all.


Kurapika turns just enough for Chrollo to understand that he should loosen his hold. He stays close,
looking down at Kurapika like a cat watching a bird. Slowly, Kurapika lifts his hands and beckons Chrollo

to come closer. Chrollo bows his head and stays still, patiently letting Kurapika unlock his muzzle and
ease it away from his face.


This time, Kurapika has nothing for Chrollo to eat or drink. He places his hands on Chrollo's cheeks,

feeling the smooth planes of his pale face. Again, the red marks left by the muzzle catch Kurapika's

attention. Rather than shoving down the empathy he feels at the sight of them, Kurapika soothingly rubs
the irritated skin with his fingers.

Chrollo leans into the touch, pressing his nose to Kurapika's palm. Kurapika watches as Chrollo raises his
cuffed hands to fit a fingernail beneath the adhesive scent patch on Kurapika's wrist. He starts peeling it
off slowly like he's expecting Kurapika to stop him. Distantly, Kurapika thinks that he probably should.


Chrollo tosses the scent patch to the side where his muzzle rests. Chrollo presses his lips to Kurapika's

skin. His breath hitches at the faint touch and his pulse pounds. Just behind Chrollo's gentle lips are his
alpha fangs - barely a centimeter from the unmarked gland in Kurapika's wrist. Kurapika still doesn't think

Chrollo would but he could. Kurapika closes his eyes.
