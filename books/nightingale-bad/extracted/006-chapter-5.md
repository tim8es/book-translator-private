# Chapter 5

"Next time, bring me a copy of Paradise Lost. I'll be able to make a message my people will understand with
that." Chrollo said.


"But you still won't give me any names?" Kurapika pressed one more time without fearing that Chrollo would

threaten him again.


Chrollo shook his head. "You don't need any right now."





Despite being annoyed that Chrollo still hasn't given him any details, Kurapika did reveal that he's able to
investigate people's pasts. Chrollo must think that Kurapika would use any details to find out more than
Chrollo wants him to about his accomplices.

Chrollo is right, of course, and Kurapika can't find fault in the level of loyalty Chrollo has for his people.

There's a saying that there's no honor among thieves, but it seems Chrollo would disagree with that idea.
Kurapika just hopes Chrollo’s people feel as strongly as he does so they can make this deal pay off.

Kurapika sighs through his nose, idly flipping through the fresh pages of his new copy of Paradise Lost.

With how protective Chrollo got when Kurapika brought up his secret allies, he may as well have

screamed in Kurapika’s face how important they are to him. That and having a strange code Chrollo is

certain they'll get means there must be a lot of history between them.


Kurapika is sitting up in bed, skimming the text by the light of his bedside lamp. He doesn't expect he'll
find anything useful in a naked copy of the book since Chrollo needs to get his hands on it first. Mostly

Kurapika is just looking for a distraction. His whole body is aching from how tense he was for the few
hours he managed to sleep - three or four maybe… Yorknew City sleeps even less than Kurapika. Flashes

of light from passing cars move just beyond his plastic blinds, casting shadows that move through his

room.

It's the nightmares keeping him up. Again. He doesn't have anything to take the edge off them except the
distraction he's chosen for the night.

Kurapika doesn't think he's above the desire for taking things like a shot of whiskey or a small sedative

for the pain of the past. It's the opposite. He's very, very certain he's exactly the sort of person who would
struggle to stop after just one shot or just one pill. He thinks he'd get addicted to the absence of pain. So

Kurapika stays away from all of it.

He's already broadly familiar with Paradise Lost on a thematic level. It's easy for him to imagine why a

person who's been banished from society would be into something like this.

Kurapika yawns and traces the text with his fingers.


"What is dark within me, illumine."




Kurapika walks into the psychologist's office - cradling a coffee in one hand and the book in the other. It's

a requirement in the program that Kurapika meets with a licensed therapist once a month - not just a
counselor or the front desk. So, he's here again, just before his next appointment with Chrollo. Instead of

hiding in the corner until he's dragged into his appointment like he wants to, Kurapika pushes himself to
say good morning to Eliza with a small smile. Eliza leans over her desk to whisper to Kurapika, cupping

her hand over her mouth.

"How was the private room?" She asks. "Anything exciting happen?"

"It was nice. We just, ah, got a bit closer on the couch and held hands. We're taking it slow." Kurapika

whispers back, keeping his head low and demure.


He squeezes the book as he remembers how Chrollo took his hands and almost broke his thumb.

"Aw, so cute." Eliza fawns over him. "I just knew something happened. Chrollo was in for his therapy
appointment a few days ago and he was barely saying a word to me.  I knew he had to be hiding

something!"


Kurapika bites the inside of his cheek.


"I guess he can be shy too," Kurapika says.


But he's pretty sure Chrollo was just thrown by their last meeting. Maybe he's still pissed that Kurapika
decided to use his accomplices as additional leverage.

When it's time for his appointment, Kurapika goes into the therapist's office. Much like the visitation

rooms, there are two cushioned chairs positioned across from each other. But in this room, there's a
framed picture of a serene, misty mountain.


The therapist is already seated the chair on the far side of the room. The therapist is mature man with

neat, black hair and glasses. He gestures for Kurapika to have a seat in the other chair. There's a mild
attempt at making it appear more cozy with a light blue blanket folded over of the back of it. Kurapika

sits, his posture straight enough so his back doesn't touch the blanket. He sits with one leg crossed over

the other, the book in his lap.

"Good morning, Kurapika. My name is Kaiser," The man's voice is low and he speaks without inflection.
"This meeting is just to check in with you. How have your appointments been going?"

"They’re going well," Kurapika is tempted to leave it at that but if he's going to make Eliza and Squala

think he's falling for his alpha convict, he might as well do the same here. "Chrollo asked me for another
book. He mentioned doing a lot of self-study in the humanities."

Kurapika pats the cover of the book and Kaiser nods.

"Yes, he seems well-read." Kaiser replies neutrally.

Kurapika smiles a little and silence falls over them. The therapist is clearly waiting for Kurapika to fill it in

without being leading. Kurapika crosses his ankles so he won't start tapping his foot under this man's

watchful eyes. Kaiser looks at Kurapika without even a hint of judgment, but Kurapika doesn't trust it. In
the end, this therapist is working with the Justice Department. Kurapika has to play this carefully.

"He, ah. Chrollo said he's most likely going to finish the last book I lent him soon. I'm looking forward to

getting to talk to him about it." Kurapika says.

Kaiser nods again, not even glancing at the clipboard in his lap.

"That's good. I'm glad you have things you're looking forward to. Do you usually look forward to seeing

Chrollo?"

"Yes. With my work schedule  it can be difficult to get out, but I'm always glad when  I come here."
Kurapika replies without hesitation.

It's partially true. Kurapika’s always found mustering the energy to seek people out more arduous than

being around them naturally, so there shouldn't be anything suspicious about that.


"That's nice. You mentioned on your private room request form that you feel safe with Chrollo. Can you
tell me more about that?"

Kurapika can see a few ways he's being invited to respond. Either he can focus on himself and how he is

being made to feel safe, or he can talk about Chrollo and how he believes Chrollo to be a safe person.

So of course he chooses to talk about Chrollo.


"Chrollo is never aggressive -"


Except for last time.

"- and he always seems more interested in my thoughts than my status as an omega."

That much is true.

"And if he was able to get approval from the program for his good behavior while serving his time…"

Kurapika flatters the system, pretending to believe in their judgment as well as his own.


"Then I think he's safe. Plus, Babimyna always reassures me that he's close by in case I need him."

"Babimyna?"

Kurapika falters.

"… My… The guard who usually escorts me."

Surely it's not strange for him to know the man's name if he sees him regularly. But it's so difficult for

Kurapika to get a read on what Kasiser thinks about anything he's said so far. It's like talking to a stock
photo of a professional man rather than a person.

"Ah…" Kaiser nods and adjusts his glasses.


Don't read too much into it. It doesn't mean anything. Act natural.

"Yes, I understand. Those are all fine reasons." Kaiser says.

Another beat of silence passes and Kurapika swallows. The therapist's words remind him of something

Melody said... About how often she notices him talking about how he rationalizes things rather than how
he feels about them. Maybe it was a mistake to talk about his sense of safety this way. Maybe he should
have hummed and hawed over his instincts and how soothing Chrollo's pheromones are to fit in better
with his sensitive profile.

But Kurapika just can't get a read on him. He has no idea if Kaiser finds his behavior to be incongruous or
if he cares at all.

Quickly, Kurapika decides that since there's no way for him to figure that out, he should move onto his

next request. If he has to talk to this therapist, then he needs to try getting something out of him rather
than waste time.


"I was thinking I'd like to to take the muzzle off Chrollo. Just to give him some coffee." Kurapika says.


It should follow the theme of safety for this session well enough.

"I think that's a nice idea." Kaiser says without inflection. Kurapika is starting to find it hard to believe this

man thinks anything is nice.

"If you're still sure you want to take it off of him after this appointment, we can give you a key for next

time. There's a vending machine in the hall by the guards' station if you want to bring him something to
eat too." Kaiser finishes.

Kurapika leaves the office, letting his sigh of relief out slowly. He kept  it short and only said good,

reassuring things. He's a perfect, little therapy-omega. That therapist may have seemed like the most
bland person on the planet, but he’s agreed to give Kurapika what he wants. Everything is going as
smoothly as it could be.

Shaking off that unsettling meeting, Kurapira gives Babimyna a shy smile and they go down the hall to

the private room. Babimyna lingers in the doorway while Kurapika sets the book and the coffee on the
table. Kurapika pretends not to notice that Babimyna is taking longer than usual to shut the door.

When Chrollo arrives they barely exchange pleasantries before he grabs the book and opens Kurapika’s

coffee mug to get to work.

Kurapika sits across from Chrollo at the table, watching him with his arms crossed. Chrollo flips through
the pages, searching for something in the text. When he finds whatever it is, he dips his index finger into

the coffee to smear a few droplets into the paper, creating his message. As he watches Chrollo work,
Kurapika finds himself slowly relaxing. Chrollo’s focus makes the silence comfortable, unlike the silences
that Kurapika shared with Kaiser

Whatever code Chrollo is crafting takes the majority of their time together but that suits Kurapika fine.

He's sick of talking today already.

When he finishes marking the book, Chrollo blows the final drops of coffee dry before shutting it.

"What's got you worked up today?" Chrollo asks without looking at him.

Kurapika’s crossed arms tighten.

"The therapist Kaiser."

"He is an odd one. I believe he's going for a non-directive therapy of sorts." Chrollo says.

"He's uncanny."

Chrollo shrugs.

"I think so too. But it doesn't really matter if we like him or he likes us. What matters is that we get what
we need."

Kurapika takes a deep breath, his chest expanding within the cage of his arms.

"I know," Kurapika glances at the door, wondering if he's just being paranoid. "… and I think Babimyna's

keeping an eye on me."


"Who?"


Kurapika doesn't bother to hide his expressions from Chrollo. His lip curls and he rolls his eyes - is he

really the only person who thinks knowing his escort is important?


"The guard who's always with me."

Chrollo’s brows raise, then he chuckles.


"I'm not  really surprised that he's paying attention to you." Chrollo says, his tone dripping with
amusement.

Kurapika scowls. He's half-convinced he's overreacting too but that doesn’t mean he wants Chrollo to

mock him.


"I meant more attention than usual. Today he stood in the doorway watching me when he usually-"

Chrollo lifts a hand to cut him off. "No, I got what you meant. I still meant what I said. I'm not surprised

he's paying extra attention to you. He's an underpaid nobody and you're a beautiful omega who bothered
to learn his name."

Kurapika’s frown remains firmly in place even as heat slowly spreads across his face.


"He gave me his name," Kurapika argues.

"And you remembered it. I trusted your judgment on Eliza and Squala now you do the same."

Kurapika closes his eyes and takes a few more deep breathes. Alphas reading too much into basic
kindness is nothing new to Kurapika so there really isn't anything wrong with Chrollo’s assumption… It
just feels frustrating coming from him.

Kurapika opens his eyes and holds out his hand for the marked book.

"Fine. Time's almost up, so where am I taking this?"





For one more night, Kurapika clutches Paradise Lost to help him push through his fear. He's white-
knuckling the book in one and hand keeps the other hidden in his pocket, fingers threaded through the

rings of brass knuckles.

All of his senses are alight as he walks down the alley behind he dark building Chrollo told him to go to.
Yorknew City is never silent but this is as close as it gets. Only the distant sound of cards passing
through the main roads and the scuffling of strays accompany Kurapika’s light footsteps. He takes a
strange, winding path through back roads that almost feels like a dream until he finally finds the building
Chrollo told him to go to.


13th Street Printing Company.

The front of the building has a faded sign but it's the sort of place Kurapika would normally avoid. The

type of building that children would say is haunted. The type of business that couldn't possibly be staying
afloat by actually doing what the sign says.


He goes around to the emergency exit in the back and stops just outside the dented door. Under the
flickering streetlamp he can just barely make out scratches on the doorframe that are just above his eye-

level. It looks like an asterisk or some sort of radial burst with twelve points and a crude circle drawn
through the center of the lines. Kurapika doesn't know what it means exactly, just that it's yet another

code that Chrollo told him to look for. Kurapika glances up and down the alley, making sure no one is

lurking nearby.


He takes a steadying breath and knocks on the door.

… Nothing.


Kurapika bites his lip, feeling frustration building. It's hardly a surprise no one is answering and he can't
tell if anyone is inside.


If someone is inside, he must have their attention. He only has one more idea. If Chrollo's so sure about
this book being the key, then Kurapika will use it. He opens it to the first page and begins to recite a few
lines.

He doesn't even get to finish the first verse of the poem when the door opens. A tall, alpha man with a

crooked nose and long hair in a sloppy a top knot is standing in the doorway.


"Who are you?" The man demands.


Kurapika stands tall, not showing fear even as his heart pounds. He holds the book out to the man.


"I'm here with a message from Chrollo," Kurapika says rather than giving his name.

The man raises a brow and looks out into the alley over the top of Kurapika’s head. Then he snatches the
book and fans  it open. His eyes flicker over the stained pages as the lingering scent of Chrollo’s

pheromones waft from it.

"… Get in here."

Having left his better judgment with Melody, Kurapika follows the man inside.

The man shuts the door while Kurapika takes in the room. It's a small, floor-level office space with no
windows. There's a door that likely leads to the storefront but that still doesn't leave Kurapika many

choices for escape. He really has lost his mind.

There are two other people seated in the office. A small person with shaggy hair that covers most of their
face is seated at the office desk. Leaning against the desk is a young woman with pink hair and sharp
eyes. Kurapika can't discern a secondary gender from either of them since they don't seem as keen on

displaying it like the alpha who answered the door.

The man reaches past Kurapika's shoulder to hand the book to the small person on the desk. The woman
turns on the desk lamp then leans over to look at the pages with them.

"This is his work alright," The small person says in a quiet voice.

The woman nods in agreement then fixes Kurapika with a hard, skeptical stare. The light behind her

casts cutting shadows across her face.


"How are you getting in contact with him?" She questions.


"Are you aware that the Yorknew Prison's participating in the Omega Therapy project?" Kurapika asks.


The woman nods.

"The what?" The alpha behind him balks.

Kurapika continues speaking, mostly to the woman in front of him.

"I'm one of the volunteers and Chrollo’s assigned therapy omega. We made a deal."

The woman's eyes flicker up to exchange a look with the alpha behind Kurapika. The small person at the

desk seems more interested by what Chrollo put in the book than Kurapika's story. They've turned away
completely to pour over the message under the yellow lamplight.


"What kind of deal?" The woman asks.


"I'll be his messenger and he helps me get something from the prison."


"Something from the prison?" The man presses incredulously.

Kurapika's jaw tenses. He looks over his shoulder at the man blocking the door.

"That's as much of your concern as the contents of Chrollo’s messages is mine."

In a flash, the alpha's face changes from skeptical to downright hostile. Kurapika's fist tightens in his

pocket as his adrenaline spikes.

"Cool it," the woman snaps at the alpha. "Whatever it is, it's Chrollo’s call."

The man's hackles lower slowly, like the pressure on a spring being released in a way that's controlled
rather than letting  it snap. Kurapika is relieved that these people want to help Chrollo and trust his

judgment - Chrollo could have just been a fall-guy. As hair-raising as this situation is, it seems like his
own trust in Chrollo wasn't misplaced either.

Suddenly, the person at the desk flings their arm up in the air. Pinched between their forefinger and

thumb is a little, folded piece of paper.

"Our reply," They say.

Kurapika glances between the woman and man to gauge their reactions before taking a few steps

forward to take the slip of paper.

"I'll take it to Chrollo when I see him in a week." He promises.

Kurapika doesn't believe in miracles, but he does believe that he was very lucky to be able to return to his
apartment before morning.
