Jason’s fifteenth birthday is bittersweet for Tim. It’s been an amazing three years of laughter, growth, and love for Tim since he joined the Wayne family. However, on this day, Tim feels

doused with a bucket of cold water when he remembers Jason’s death at the hands of the Joker this coming year.

As an outside observer at the time, Tim never really knew the inner workings of what caused Jason to go off on his own in Ethiopia, and it was always too sore a subject to ask anyone. Jason liked to be cruel with his reminders, but he never really went into detail about the cause.

This causes Tim to have heightened tension the weeks following Jason’s birthday, waiting for the shoe to drop and hoping it’s something he can prevent.

It finally happens one seemingly innocuous patrol. Parakeet is sipping his last coffee of the night (darn Alfred for setting a limit) when Robin is shouting through the comms in anger at what sounds like a rapist. Valid. Parakeet thinks, looking forward to the justice that is about to be served.

Unfortunately, justice seems to be served accidentally when there’s a yelp and thud. Batman is bellowing down the comms, “ROBIN!”

Parakeet jerks up in worry. “Report!” he cries, “What’s wrong?” He can’t gain a visual of where they are.

“Parakeet, call paramedics to the scene. A suspect is unresponsive and no pulse is detected. We’ll be discussing this back at the cave Robin,” Batman states gravely. He sounds more serious than usual, which is saying a lot, and Parakeet gets a sick feeling in his stomach.

“I didn’t kill him!” Robin yells, “He tripped and fell!”

There’s a sigh, and Batman says, “We’ll discuss this later. For now, focus on helping the victim.”

When the two get back to the Batcave, there is a heavy tension between them. “Parakeet, thank you for your help today. I need to have a private discussion with Robin about what occurred on patrol. Please give us privacy,” B requests monotonously.

Parakeet and Robin trade wide-eyed looks and Parakeet tries to give Robin a reassuring smile, but he looks away, obviously nervous.

When Tim gets back up to the manor, he can hear muffled yelling echo from behind the grandfather clock. He debates if he should just go back in time now and try to prevent this whole altercation from happening, but something stops him.

Instinctually, Tim somehow suspects that this might have been the starting point leading towards Jason’s death. He ponders on whether to see how this all plays out and prevent the death with more information or if he should stop this right now and hope that Ethiopia or the Joker won’t happen anymore.

He can’t put anything to chance. He needs to be completely sure on how to proceed. It was hard losing his Robin. It’ll be even harder losing his brother if he can’t save him. Tim knows that Jason will be revived and he’ll stalk his grave every day for that possibility, but he can’t shake the vision of rage in Jason’s pit-green eyes when looking upon Tim. He can’t handle that hate anymore, especially when he’s had Jason’s love.

Tim decides to head to his room, waiting for the sound of Bruce and Jason going to bed as well. He eventually hears Jason’s angry footsteps stomp into his room and the door slam. Tim slides out of bed and patters across the hallway, softly knocking.

“Go the fuck away!” Jason yells.

“Jason it’s me,” Tim replies in case Jason thinks he’s Bruce. Jason just grunts in response (very Batman- like, though he’d probably hate the comparison right now).

Tim opens the door and finds Jason laying face down in his bed curled under his blankets. Only his head of hair sticks out, ruffled from the windy Gotham air.

“What’s going on, Jay?” Tim asks quietly, climbing onto the bed and flopping onto the blanket burrito.

A sniffle sounds in the silent room, and Jason chokes out, “Stupid rapist tripped and fell when I was fighting him, but Batman didn’t see what happened and thinks I pushed him or something. I didn’t! I’m not a killer!” he insists defensively.

“I know you’re not, Jay,” Tim replies confidently. At least not yet. Tim is determined to keep it that way in this lifetime.

Tim’s trust seems to make Jason boneless with relief, full on sobs now heaving from his shaking body. Tim quickly snuggles under the covers with him and holds him comfortingly.

“Can’t you go back in time and fix this?” Jason wails, “I don’t want my dad to hate me.”

“I will,” Tim promises, “just give me time.”

The next morning, Tim tries to defend Jason to Bruce, but Bruce seems reluctant to believe him as Tim wasn’t there. Tim burns in anger at the thought that Bruce wouldn’t trust his own son, but then takes a breath to think about it rationally. Bruce tends to separate Bruce Wayne and Batman, so he might not have realised the impact his disappointment is having on Jason.

Before Tim can explain, however, Alfred herds him out the door for school. Looking back, Tim remembers that Bruce’s dark circles looked extra dark that morning.

The following days show not much improvement despite Tim’s best efforts at conflict resolution. He’s reminded strongly of Bruce’s fights with Dick and how they just wouldn’t talk to one another. He is tempted to get them to make up through his previous methods, but it wouldn’t work with Jason knowing his identity.

Damian also seems to sense something is wrong. The usually independent six-year-old turns uncharacteristically clingy, wanting everyone to pay him attention and be in the same room together. Dick tries to help, but his work and patrol keep him too busy to visit very often, and phone calls don’t seem to have the same effect.

It’s been a week of this weird tension when Tim finds Jason packing a bag.

“Where are you going?” he asks, startling his older brother.

“Away from here,” Jason grumbles, “If Bruce doesn’t want me anymore, I’m going to find a parent that does. Turns out my mom was actually my stepmom and I have a biological mom working in an aid camp in Ethiopia.”

Tim’s heart drops.

“Maybe you should try to talk to Bruce before you go alone,” Tim tries to convince him desperately, “He’ll probably be able to help arrange a meeting with her. It’ll be dangerous to go by yourself.”

Jason snorts. “That’s so hypocritical. You literally wanted to free an assassin child on your own,” he sneers.

“Yeah, and you went with me because you thought it would be too dangerous for me alone. Plus I literally have a time travel power. Maybe this is me telling you that you shouldn’t go because something bad happens in Ethiopia,” Tim protests.

But Jason is too angry to be rational. “Whatever. If you won’t fix this, then I’ll fix it my way,” he grunts, rolling his eyes. He’s out the window before Tim can say anything else.

A few days later, Tim wakes up to Bruce’s familiar devastation. Apparently, Jason had gone to meet his biological mom, Sheila, even though Bruce had told him to stay put while Batman went after the Joker. She had sold him out to the Joker.

Tim closes his eyes when he hears. He’s sick of parents letting their kids down. Poor Jason just wanted someone to love him unconditionally like a parent should. Bruce does, but it’s hard for him to show it.

Tim sucks on a throat lozenge in preparation for his haunted house scream as he makes note of the timeline, coordinates of the fight with Felipe Garzonas, and Sheila Haywood’s information. He needs to make sure there is solid evidence of Jason’s innocence and Sheila’s guilt.
