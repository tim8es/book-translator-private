Tim’s had a lot of fun saving lives over the years. It’s a lot easier when he has the ability to control Z.

The most notable, currently, is the time he saved Superman’s life. Imagine that. Regular old (okay not that regular) Tim being able to save the Man of Steel.

A large reason why Lex Luthor’s plan succeeded so well was because he made Batman doubt Superman, and he used Superman’s mom as a bargaining chip. His machinations led to Doomsday being able to kill Superman.

Tim had multiple pathways he could have taken to stop this from happening, and he also had the resources to try all of them.

First, he tried helping defeat Doomsday before he could kill Superman. He was able to do so, with knowledge about a large store of Kryptonite Lex Luthor had. However, though he was able to rid Luthor of his Kryptonite supply, Tim was unable to get him to truly face any consequences. He just went on to his next evil supervillain plot the next day. Additionally, this led to irreparable damage to Batman and Superman’s trust in one another. For people he knew to become best friends, this was not something Tim could live with.

On the second try, he prevented Martha Kent’s kidnapping by kidnapping her first and taking her to Wayne Manor. She and Alfred got along splendidly. Sorry Lex, only Tim’s allowed to kidnap people. He also put a lot more focus on preserving Batman and Superman’s friendship, helping them maintain their trust in one another. He made them do trust falls and everything. This way, they were able to work together to defeat Doomsday, but Luthor got away with the part he played.

Finally, Tim eventually realized to completely achieve victory, he needed to go straight to the source. Tim focused all his efforts into completely ruining Lex Luthor. He got a fake internship at LexCorp under the name Alvin Draper and investigated every. little. infraction Lex had made, including his tax evasion and unpaid parking tickets.

He anonymously sent what he found to one Clark Kent at the Daily Planet, and he asked Bruce Wayne to make sure that Lex isn’t able to pay off any courts. All corrupt corporate lawyers were suddenly disbarred when their corruption was exposed, so Lex was only stuck with ethical lawyers and judges. After a month-long trial (the shitload of felonies he committed took weeks to go through and the evidence was overwhelming) Lex Luthor was convicted and lost all credibility (and probably money with the amount of fines he was given).

As LexCorp’s stocks tanked, an anonymous buyer ended up taking ownership of the corporation to root out its corruption and compete with Wayne Enterprises for Most Ethical Company (seemed like a good way to piss Lex off).

If Tim can be CEO of WE at seventeen, he can probably run LexCorp a lot better than the previous owner. For example, all Kryptonite was turned over to the Justice League, and a deal was made with them if any more shows up to make sure it doesn’t get into the wrong hands.

As the new owner, Tim gets access to the various projects they are working on. One project of particular interest, CADMUS, revealed the illegal genetic experimentation with cloning technology. As much as Tim is glad CADMUS brought Kon into this world, he made sure to completely destroy all of what Luthor worked towards as the once powerful man sits in a cell.

One clone of Superman is enough.

This time around, Tim also made sure to help develop close trust and friendship between Superman and Batman, so it seems Superman picked up Batman’s penchant for taking in blue-eyed, black-haired boys. Conner Kent’s newfound adoptive parents, Clark Kent and Lois Lane, make sure the boy gets all the support he needs.

Without the support of Lex Luthor, with Tim’s preparation, and with the Justice League’s new access to tons of Kryptonite, Doomsday is no match for the Bats. He is actually defeated quite easily.

All in all, this goes to show how Tim has been able to find the best case scenario in different possible tragedies, and save lives while doing it. Tim wouldn’t call himself the Chosen One or anything like that, but he feels like his power is a bit OP. Villains have no way of winning unless they also possess time travel powers themselves, and usually he lets Flash handle them.

Here we are again. Tim sighs, looking over the chaotic battlefield. To think this was the incident that has led him to this moment (again).

He eyes the Omega Ray that gave him his power. Something tells him that it isn’t the original purpose of the ray. Why would Darkseid want to make his enemies more powerful?

He wants to see what would happen if he lets the ray hit Bruce. Will Bruce be sent back in time and develop the meta-power as well?

Tim watches as the ray bursts towards a distracted Batman, none of the other League members able to step in and help.

Tim doesn’t know what to expect. Will Bruce dodge out of the way because he had gone back in time and knows that it’s coming? Tim almost wishes that is what happened because when the light fades, it reveals the still form of Batman.

He stumbles toward the body, hoping that he’s just unconscious or something, but an instinct deep in his gut tells him that it’s not the case. Tim falls to his knees by Batman’s collapsed body, feeling for a pulse. When he can’t feel anything and there are no signs of breathing, Tim screams because the only other option is to throw up.

He stumbles when he lands, still feeling queasy. No matter his power, seeing his dad’s dead body is sickening. Tim needs to lock in.

He sucks in a deep calming breath and focuses on what’s around him. He had screamed too loud and was transported right before the beginning of the battle.

He doesn’t want to take any chances. He needs to make sure that B is completely out of the line of fire. He also doesn’t want to try different variations like he normally would do. No way in hell would he be able to take seeing B die again.

The only way to be completely sure is either if he can convince B not to join the battle (literally impossible) or to make sure to transport B out of the way. Tim still hasn’t used his power while touching another living being, but he doesn’t know how else to prevent the beam from hitting anyone without going through multiple different pathways. If he keeps B out of the way, someone else could be hit instead.

Parakeet sticks to Batman like glue in the ensuing battle. When Tim had turned fifteen, Jason had asked to pass the Robin mantle over to him because he wanted to go out as a new vigilante called Phoenix. Tim had refused, loving how it felt to be Parakeet. He had told them to save the mantle for Damian if he wants it when he’s older. One thing that changed after that proposition, however, was that Batman had let Parakeet go out in the field for emergencies.

Tim had missed being able to patrol from time to time. Sure, he still preferred his role inside the Batcave, but being able to stretch out his atrophied fighting skills was amazing. The Bats, bar Jason, were very surprised at how easily Tim picked up his training, but he was able to use martial arts classes as an excuse. Damian, always admiring Tim, asked him to train him as well so Tim started teaching him a few safe ways to defend himself. They all agreed not to actually train Damian as a vigilante until he is much older and brings it up himself.

As Darkseid’s attack constitutes as an emergency, it’s all hands on deck right now. Although Tim had never been Robin in this timeline, Parakeet and Batman still fight fluidly together. They move in sync in a way that reminds Tim so much of his time as Robin. The nostalgia should probably not be kicking in in the middle of an invasion, but Tim can’t help it. Fighting alongside his family is exhilarating.

When Tim notices the ray powering up, he grabs Batman in a bear hug and cries out.

Time-traveling with a person is not that much different than it usually is. Sure Tim collapses from the unexpected weight of Batman in his arms, but it’s almost like holding a giant teddy bear. One that flails a whole lot.

Batman grunts in surprise and shock as he finds himself experiencing deja vu from within his son’s arms and at a different angle.

“What happened?” he growls.

Tim gulps. “Um.. I think we got hit by that big ass ray gun?” he says hesitantly, trying one last ditch effort to cover up his power.

“No. This happened before it hit.” Batman responds stubbornly.

Tim sighs. “I’ll tell you later, B. Right now we need to kick some alien ass.” he says, trying to distract his attention for now.

Batman gives him a long searching look before grunting in agreement and moving into the fray, being extra cautious to avoid the Omega ray now.

The rest of the battle is a blur, and the Justice League (and allies) come out panting with exhaustion, but victorious. Tim is now officially in new territory having passed the moment he got sent back in time.

Tim is so not ready for the discussion that he knows is going to happen, but he subjects himself to his fate and follows the rest of the Bats back to the Batcave.

After completing their reports, Bruce (now with the cowl off) veers on Tim.

“Now,” he announces, “we are going to talk about something that occurred on the battlefield that Tim promised to explain.”

Tim shrinks in on himself, but he knows that tone. It’s one that brooks no argument. After 7 years of hiding his power from Bruce and Dick, Tim has no idea why he still does so. Maybe he’s worried Bruce will go all Frankenstein and start treating him like a science experiment.

Okay that’s bullshit. Tim can always scream and go back in time if he ends up like that, but he knows Bruce would never do something like that even if he’s curious.

What he finds so hard to admit to himself is that he’s worried they won’t believe him. Jason and Damian were so young when they found out. Damian, the curious child, had gone through his notebook he used for patrols a couple years ago, so Jason and Tim had to explain and swear him to secrecy. Ever eager for approval, Damian has been very tight-lipped, and thus has been let in on their shenanigans.

Because Jason and Damian were so young, they were more open to the possibility (and Jason was willing to believe anything for shelter, food, and safety). Tim is scared Dick, Bruce, and Alfred would think he’s crazy or reject him. Though he knows it’s unlikely they will hate him for being meta because they all have meta friends, Tim also knows all about the no metas in Gotham rule and is worried they’ll try and kick him out.

Nevertheless, he is here now, and he has to have faith that everything will work out. At least he has Jason for moral support.

“Can we have a family meeting with everyone? I don’t want to explain it more than once,” Tim asks.

Dick and Bruce exchange glances and nod while Jason looks questioningly at him, concerned.

After they have all changed into civilian clothes and have gathered with Alfred and Damian in a sitting room (the same one where Tim gave his presentation for adoption in), Tim nervously reveals his secret.

“Um..you know the giant ray that Darkseid had in the battle? Originally I was hit by the ray and sent back in time. I ended up getting a meta time travel power that I have been using to help fix the timeline. Like for example, Jason died in my original timeline, and I ended up taking the Robin mantle, because Batman in mourning was getting too violent..” Tim starts explaining. Some of this information is new to Jason and Damian as well, so they’re all listening raptly.

“In this timeline, I prevented it, so I was never Robin, but I don’t mind because I enjoy being Parakeet more! This is also why I knew about Damian. In my timeline, Talia drops him off when he’s ten and he’s brainwashed by the League of Assassins,” Tim continued.

“What’s the League of Assassins? And who’s Talia?” Damian interjects.

“Don’t worry about it Damian. They’re an evil cult, but I won’t let them get you,” Tim reassures.

“You’ve been helping us all behind the scenes?” Bruce speaks up, sounding stunned.

“Yup, he’s been babysitting all of our asses on patrol. Haven’t you wondered why it got so much easier?” Jason retorts.

“YOU KNEW??” Dick exclaims, sounding betrayed.

“I knew too,” Damian announces, not wanting to be left out.

“WHAT?! Tim, why am I the only one of the siblings that didn’t know?” Dick questions, turning to Tim teary-eyed and pouting.

Tim decides to be completely honest with his family from now on. He trusts them completely. “I was worried you, B, and Alfred wouldn’t believe me because you’re a lot older than Jason and Damian were when they found out. Children are easier to convince and are more open to things,” Tim explains.

“Of course, we would have listened to you and tried to understand, Master Tim,” Alfred replies.

Bruce, the one who is usually the most paranoid, also replies, “Although I will need additional proof, the fact that I trust you and witnessed it first-hand is enough for me to

believe you, sweetheart.”

Tim tears up, grateful for the support. “Wait, B witnessed it firsthand? What do you mean?” Jason asks curiously.

“I used my power to bring B back in time with me because that Omega ray would have killed him,” Tim says mournfully.

“Ah, so you can bring people back. I volunteer as tribute to try it out next time,” Jason says excitedly.

“No, I want to!” Damian argues.

“Wait, you didn’t know you could before you did it?” Dick asks.

TIm shifts uncomfortably, “I hadn’t tried before because I was nervous there would be ill effects, but I was desperate this time because, you know, B would have died,” he explains.

Dick nods in understanding and then continues, “By the way, I also believe you! Think of all the potential this could have for chaos!”

Bruce sighs, looking like he already dreads what is to come. “Dick please do not use your brother for mischief-making,” he warns.

“But what if Tim wants to?” Dick asks, giving him puppy-dog eyes.

This makes Bruce turn back to Tim. “We need to establish some ground rules about you using your power,” he pleads, looking scared that Tim would use them to take over the world (which he totally could if he wanted to), “I’m worried about how safe it is if you use it. Are there any drawbacks?”

Tim relaxes when he realizes that the rules are out of concern and safety, not control. “Not that I know of other than a sore throat sometimes because screaming is what activates it,” Tim replies.

Dick continues the interrogation, “Have you used it to help me too?” he asks eagerly.

“He’s the one that got you two dumbasses to talk when you were fighting. He’s the reason you’re adopted Dickhead,” Jason butts in.

Dick and Bruce freeze, while Alfred just looks amused. “YOU’RE THE FORTUNE TELLER??” Dick yells.

Tim smirks, “Wasn’t it obvious. Jeez, I thought you two were detectives. Why else would they be so involved in Bruce Wayne and Batman’s lives? I literally just said I could go back in time.”

“Wait, wait, wait,” Dick flails his arms, still looking flabbergasted. Bruce is in a similar state. “I need you to show me this in action. Alfred and I are the only ones here who haven’t seen you do it!” Dick insists.

“Ok.. how about you show me a card and I’ll go back in time and guess it,” Tim suggests.

With everyone in his family convinced about his power, Dick mischievously announces that they are now going to have bonding nights after Saturday dinners where they are going to use Tim’s power to have a little fun. Now that they are all in on it, the Waynes are going to have a blast messing with everyone.
