# 7. Dillon

June quickly turned into July as we all settled in, being a family of four. And as the days passed, and the more time I spent with Cade, the closer we became. We did everything together. Adults teased us that we were attached at the hip, and I didn’t mind that fucking one bit.

While we grew as close as any brothers could be, I kept having weird tingling sensations whenever we touched or were near. I couldn’t explain why. He made me feel funny in a good way that no other friend had. Or was it a good way? I didn’t think I should’ve felt a weird anything for my brother.

*Stepbrother.*

It was kind of cool that I instantly called him my brother in such a short time, showing how close we grew. He never picked on me for not talking enough, and he didn’t bother me if I needed alone time. Like he understood me so well, we sort of synced.

He also pulled me out of myself, so I talked and got out more. Just like I quickly learned when he had one of his sad moments, though, he tried to hide them between dimpled smiles and jokes. And I loved when he talked a lot, though he slowed down as if to stay on more my level. We shared this weird balance between us.

But there were moments when he’d glance at me, trying to sneak a peek, and didn’t want me to notice. Or he’d brush his arm on mine, which seemed like he did it on purpose. Sometimes I’d catch him blushing. Shit, I did that all the time, too. It started to make me think he might like me more than a brother. Stupid, because why would he? Well, it seemed that way. I could’ve just seen what I wanted to. But what did I know?

I guess if I was being honest, I might have had a little crush on my brother. *Stepbrother*. It was the only thing I could think of. I’d never had a crush on a girl or a boy before, so I wasn’t sure. All I understood was that I needed to be around him all the time, and I liked touching him, looking at him, and making him smile. And I wanted to protect him and take care of him.

If I was crushing on him, then I could never let him find out. He’d freak. Mom would freak. His Dad would definitely freak. Shit. In the short time I had known him, he seemed like kind of an asshole and judgy. I would get in so much trouble.

But what did it all mean? Was I gay? All that I understood was Cade… felt right.

The day was sweltering without a cloud in the sky as we both sat on the fence, feeding the horses carrots, and enjoying just being near Cade, as always, when Mom called us. When we turned our heads, we saw Mom standing there next to a cream-colored horse with an almost white mane. It was what you would call blond in a person, and it was beautiful.

Cade and I jumped off the fence and headed toward Mom and the horse. I wanted to run, but we would’ve scared it.

“Whoa, Mom. Did you get a new horse?”

“Yes, and with Jared’s permission, I got him for Cade, so he has his own horse.”

What did she mean by ‘Jared’s permission?’ Why would she need his permission to spend her own money on Cade? Then again, it was a big gift.

Cade stopped in his tracks, wide-eyed and jaw-dropped. “What?”

Mom laughed. “Close that jaw. Flies love the mouth.”

He snapped it shut and walked slowly toward the pretty horse.

“It’s… for me?”

“Yes. We wanted you to have one too so you and Dillon can ride together and not have to share Artemis anymore. She’s all yours if you want her. She’s an American quarter horse. Now, you can name her if you want, but since the other two mares are named after Greek goddesses, I thought you would like to do the same, but you don’t have to.”

He nodded, petting her nose. “No, that’s cool. She looks like a goddess.”

Mom laughed again. “She does. How about Athena?”

“Yes, that’s perfect.”

“Your dad wanted to be here, but as you know, he’s still on the road.”

Jared sure was gone a fucking lot. I got it. He had to work, but still. This was a big moment.

“That’s cool. I… I can’t believe you got me a horse. This is the best gift ever. Thank you, Evelyn.”

Cade reached for my mom and gave her a big hug, and she hugged him back.

“You’re welcome, sweetie. But I expect you to take good care of her as Dillon takes care of Artemis.”

“Definitely.”

“I… know I can’t replace your mom, but I want you to always feel at home here, and know that I love you, too.”

“Y-you do?”

“Yes, sweetheart.”

They hugged tightly again, and I couldn’t stop smiling. Cade deserved a mom who loved him.

“Now, she’s been broken in, but she’ll still need to get used to you as a rider. So take your time showing her she’s yours. But she’s very sweet and gentle.”

Cade finally looked at me with a smile on his face, but his eyes were red-rimmed and wet. He was definitely surprised and happy. It was a big gift. Then again, maybe Mom’s love was the bigger gift. That he was this blissfully happy did something to my heart, filling it with something warm and comforting.

After that day, we spent the better part of a week riding our horses together. Cade couldn’t get enough of Athena. And if we weren’t close before, we were now, riding our horses anywhere we were allowed to. Most of the time, we took them and Tilly to ponds nearby to fish.

It was the best summer of my life.

Cade and I sat in the kitchen, eating cereal for breakfast on Thursday morning, while Mom was at the shop and Jared was back home from driving to Connecticut. When he walked into the kitchen, he clapped me on the back and walked over to the counter to make some coffee.

“Morning, boys.”

“Morning,” we both said.

After he had his coffee made, he sat at the table with us. “Listen, Dillon. Why don’t you and I toss the football around today? Let’s see what you got. I mean, it’s good to be ready for high school football, right?”

“Yessir,” I said, glancing at Cade, who had on his sad face. I sighed, wondering if I should find a way to get out of this or include him. “Maybe Cade wants to toss the ball with us, too.”

Jared looked at his son as if he didn’t know he even hurt his son’s feelings. “Cade doesn’t like football, do you? It’s not like you’re going to try out, anyway. We need to get Dillon ready for the fall.”

“It’s fine,” Cade said, but his face told a different story.

I was hit with guilt, not wanting Cade to be hurt. It also made me fucking angry that Jared left him out for no reason. Maybe Cade didn’t want to toss the ball, but that didn’t mean his father couldn’t have asked him to join us.

He looked at me and gave me one of his fake bright smiles again, which only made me feel worse. He didn’t have to pretend he wasn’t hurt.

“It’s okay, Dil. You should practice. It’s important,” he said.

“Are you sure? We can do something else today.”

Jared coughed and eyed me. No, I wasn’t getting out of this. “Cade’s right. You need to practice.”

“Yessir.”

That afternoon, when I finished my chores, Jared and I headed to the backyard, and Cade stayed inside to play video games. I hated that he was being left out.

“Are you sure we shouldn’t ask Cade to play too?” I tried again.

“You heard him. He’s fine. Now go further back. I’m going to toss the ball to you. Let’s see how well you catch and throw it back.”

I ran backward until I was far enough away, and where he wanted me. Jared reached back behind him over his head and threw the football in a perfect spiral right to me. I caught it effortlessly.

“Nice catch! Now throw it back. Try to toss it back smoothly. Fingers on the laces.”

I’d thrown a football a lot while in P.E. class in middle school, so I had some idea of what I was doing.

I stepped back on one foot and leaned back, tossing the ball overhead straight to Jared, who caught it.

“Well, Dillon. It seems you’re a natural. Great job and a great arm.”

“Thank you, sir.”

He needed to be telling Cade these things, praising him, and telling him he was proud of his son.

We tossed the ball back and forth for an hour. Sometimes, he’d run in a different direction to get me to focus quickly and throw it accurately. By the end, my arm ached, and I wanted to sit.

“I’m tired,” I said.

Just when I thought he was going to argue, he smiled and walked over to me, wrapping his arm around my shoulder. “You’re a natural, Dillon. You’re going to be great at football. I wouldn’t be surprised if they made you the quarterback. You have quick reactions and high accuracy in your throwing.”

“Thank you, sir.”

“Let’s practice three days a week for the rest of the summer while I’m in town. I’m sure they’ll make you the starting quarterback on the junior varsity team. I just know when you reach the varsity team, you’ll be the best.”

“Yessir.”

I didn’t care that much about football, but he was excited about it. That I participated with Jared would also make Mom happy, I guess. I didn’t hate football either, but that meant I had to hang around a lot of guys all the time. If I was the quarterback, I’d also be the team’s captain. Shit. I wasn’t exactly a leader. But I sucked it up if it made Jared happy.

When I walked into my room to grab clothes before my shower, I expected to find Cade there playing on my computer, but my room was empty. I went next door to Cade’s room and knocked on his door. “Cade?”

He didn’t answer, so I tested the doorknob to find his door was unlocked and walked into his room to see him lying on his bed with his back to me facing the window to the backyard, and I could tell he was awake because he was on his phone. Why didn’t he answer?

“Cade?”

“Go away.”

I shoved back the sting and hurt of his words, ignored the uncertainty about giving him space, and walked around his bed to face him, but he rolled over, hiding his face.

What was happening here? Was it about his dad? The guilt tore at me again. I shouldn’t have practiced with his dad without him.

When I walked over to face him again, he rolled over again.

*Dammit.*

Before he avoided me again, I climbed into his bed, grabbed him by his shoulders, and forced him to face me. The tears on his face gutted me. No wonder he was hiding. I’d never seen him cry before.

*Fucking Jared.*

“There, now you see what a fucking baby I am.”

Was that what he thought?

He tried to pull away, but I wouldn’t let him.

I wasn’t sure what possessed me, but I wiped away the tears from his face, which only made him cry more while his mouth quivered.

“You’re not a baby. You’re allowed to be hurt and upset. I’m so sorry, Cade. I shouldn’t have practiced with your dad. And he shouldn’t have left you out.”

“Whatever. It’s fine.”

“It’s not fucking fine.”

He tried to roll over again, but I wouldn’t let him. I had this need to hold him and reassure him. To let him know we weren’t only brothers, but best friends. That I cared about how he felt, and that he hurt. So, I pulled him to me with surprisingly little resistance. I wrapped my arms around him and his head fell on my shoulder. He didn’t sob, but his hot tears soaked through my shirt. Soon his arms loosely twined around me, too.

“It’s not fine,” he finally agreed.

“You know, I still haven’t shown you my favorite spot to be alone because… well, I wanted to keep the place to myself, but I wouldn’t mind sharing it. Do you want me to show you?”

He sat up and wiped his eyes and nose with his hands. “Okay. Are you sure?”

“Definitely.”

Cade followed me out of the house and towards the barn. It wasn’t any big deal, but I liked it. When we stepped inside, the two barn cats wanted pets while the rest of the animals grazed in the fields. But we were here for Cade, so we said our quick hellos to them and climbed the ladder at the back of the barn, and straight up to the hayloft.

Mom kept a few bales of hay up here, but none were in the way. As soon as I reached the top, I opened up the doors to where they delivered the hay. Most of it dropped below, but we stored extra in the loft during winter.

“See, the back of the barn faces the field and woods, with no one else around. No one can bother us up here. It’s not a big deal, but I love it. Sometimes I sit up here, lean against a bale of hay, and draw, or play on my phone. Sometimes I just stare out there. If I’m lucky, I’ll see a golden eagle or a hawk flying around, or a fox running through the field. If I come out early enough, there are deer roaming around.”

I sat down and stretched my legs towards the edge, but not dangling them. If I fell, Mom wouldn’t be happy. Cade sat next to me and leaned against my shoulder. My stomach fluttered as it always did when we touched, making my body fill with this weird electricity. I had gotten used to the feeling, like it was the most normal reaction to him.

“I like it,” he said.

We sat in silence for a while when suddenly, to my surprise, Cade turned, lay down on his side, and rested his head on my lap, staring out at the fields as the sun slowly sank over the horizon. It was a move to seek comfort, and he wanted it from me. My entire body froze, not sure what to do or what to make of it. But I liked it, too.

“I understand you like to be alone, but I don’t. I’ve always been too alone. Mom left us—left me—and I never fucking heard from her again, and I have no idea why. Did she not love me? Dad just fucking ignores me, or he’s traveling all the time. My grandma shoves me in front of video games or to play outside. Then Dad plays football with you. I mean, I didn’t want to play, anyway, but—”

“He could’ve asked.”

“Yeah, exactly. Sometimes Dad does stuff with me when he’s in a good mood, like taking me to see a baseball game, or we go hunting or fishing. But it doesn’t happen a lot. Until we moved here, I’ve been kind of… lonely, I guess.”

My hand hovered over his light brown curls that turned golden from the setting sun with this need to touch them. It wasn’t only to run my hand through them, but to comfort Cade the same way my mom always comforted me when I was upset. I wanted to do the same for him. So I swallowed my nerves and did it.

Cade didn’t even flinch as I touched a lock of curl, which was surprisingly soft. Like how I imagined clouds would feel when I was younger and didn’t know they were made of water. I fixed his hair at the wedding, but I had stuff in my hands to tame them. This was different. He had nothing in his hair.

“I won’t play with him anymore,” I promised.

“No, don’t do that. It’s okay. I’m… not upset with you or jealous. Besides, Dad’s right. You need to practice.”

Soon my fingers combed through his hair, and he tightened his body closer to mine. How did it all feel so right, yet also so wrong? But I didn’t care. I liked having Cade on me. Needing me. I wanted to protect him against anything that hurt him.

“If you’re sure.”

“I am. It’s okay. Really.”

We sat in silence again for a while as I comforted him with my fingers through his hair. I could’ve stayed like this forever. It was so peaceful and the sky was pretty with hints of pink and orange. Everything felt right, especially with Cade feeling better and talking. This was how we should always be.

Cade felt like home to me.

“Dil?”

“Yeah?”

“Thanks for… not making me feel so alone. Thanks for being my friend.”

My eyes watered, hurting for him, but I swallowed it. “We aren’t just friends, Cade. We are best friends.”

“Yeah, we are.”

“Boys! Where are you? It’s time for dinner!” Mom called.

“Well, I guess we better head in.”

When we stood, Cade pulled me into a hug and hugged him back.

“Thanks for this, Dil.”

“Any time. I will always be here when you need me.”
