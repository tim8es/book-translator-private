# 23. Cade

I hadn’t seen or heard from Dillon since the end of February, and now it was well into our senior year. Dad moved us into some crappy apartment, and whenever he was out of town, he dumped me with my useless grandmother. Always watching like a prison warden. I could barely remember what freedom tasted like.

Since I hadn’t heard from the Copelands, and there were no issues with my ‘gayness,’ Dad started relaxing more and more, especially when he learned my new friend was a girl. Granted, he wasn’t pleased Raven wasn’t some fucking cheerleader, but at least she had tits, right? He’d also let me get a job bagging groceries which paid pretty well, so I could save a decent amount of money.

I hadn’t dared leave again to find Dillon. Dad made it clear if I left the house like I did last time, I’d be shipped off and Dillon would be arrested.

My only reprieve from soul-crushing loneliness was my cross-country running and Raven. I was back on the varsity team, but for my new school. When the school learned that I’d won three times, they gobbled me up. Not that I gave a fuck about winning anymore. Before, it was about pleasing my Dad. Now I didn’t give a shit about him, or pleasing him. Honestly, there wasn’t much I cared about at all, anymore.

I couldn’t even remember the last time I smiled. Depression and anger were my new best friends. The longer I was without Dillon, the more bitter I grew, as the doubts and insecurities festered like an untreated infection. Why was it taking so long? Did he forget about me? Was I not enough? Was he just another person to abandon me like everyone else in my fucking life?

I wanted to hold on to hope that he’d eventually come for me, but it’d been almost eight months now.

It was Saturday afternoon, and I followed Raven inside the tattoo parlor. Raven’s mom was a tattoo artist there, which was why her daughter was covered in them, along with all her piercings. Her mom practiced on her a lot, but Raven looked good, and she appreciated her mom’s work.

Normally, I’d need permission to get a tattoo or piercing since I wasn’t quite eighteen yet, but Raven’s mom was looking the other way because I was her daughter’s friend.

I’d been saving my money for this all summer long, putting in long hours at work to keep me distracted from Dillon and my crushing loneliness. I kept to myself and didn’t make any friends except for Raven, but I was polite to the management, so they tolerated me. I was also a hard worker.

While her mom wrapped up a client, I sat on a chair next to Raven, bounced my leg, and pulled out the piece of paper I tore from one of my notebooks. It was a doodle Dillon drew, and the one I wanted on my skin.

It was a continuous contour line of two boys kissing. It was supposed to be him and me. Underneath, I planned to have ‘Home’ tattooed. It was the only way I could think of to have Dillon as a permanent part of my life.

When I felt the emotions crash into me, I switched it to anger. Anger I could handle. Anger kept me going and kept me falling too deeply into despair. All I had to do was focus on Dad and grandma.

“That’s going to look so fucking good, Cade.”

“Yeah.”

“I don’t mean to pry… Well, I do. I’m a prier, but have you thought about getting some therapy? It might help.”

I scoffed. “Yeah, like dad would take me to get some mental help. He doesn’t give a fuck as long as I’m not *‘gay.’*”

“Bummer.”

“Besides, I like being pissed. It’s better than the alternative.”

“What about a school counselor?”

“No.”

She shut up about it, thank god. I didn’t want to talk about it with anyone.

Raven’s mom came up to us just then. She had even more tattoos than her daughter. She had ink all over her arms and legs, and straight up to her neck. The only blank canvas of flesh that I could see was her face.

“Hi, Cade. I’m Vera. So, you’re here to get your first tattoo, huh?”

“Yeah, and I have extra money to get some piercings too.”

“Great, let me see what you’ve got.”

I handed her the drawing and explained what I wanted exactly, then she led me to the back and had me lay down in a tattoo chair while Raven sat next to me to watch.

“Do you want the ink first or the piercings?”

“Ink.”

Once she cleaned my chest with rubbing alcohol and got her tattoo machine ready, she dipped it in black ink and began the line drawing over my heart. At first, it only stung a little, but since the lines were pretty thick, she had to work back over the tender area, and soon it began to hurt more, but I sucked it up and used the physical pain to take away my emotional pain.

It was only four inches, and the word was short, so it didn’t take too long. Vera sealed it with plastic wrap, then she pulled out her piercing kit and went through the different-sized earrings. I was having both ears pierced, along with a small, thin hoop through my nose.

Dad would never see the tattoo because he’d flip about the two boys kissing, but I didn’t give a fuck what he thought about the piercings.

The captive bead earrings were fourteen gauge and taking a plug out of my ear hurt like fuck, but again, I sucked it up. It only took a few minutes before they were done. My nose was a little trickier since she had to punch through cartilage.

Then it was done. My ears, nose, and left pec were on fire, but I couldn’t bring myself to care. Eventually, I’d come back for more tattoos, but it was a start. Vera gave me a list of instructions on caring for my new piercings and tattoo, then Raven and I left to hang out. She was the only one I was willing to put up with, and only because she wouldn’t leave me the fuck alone. She wasn’t so bad, though.

We stepped outside and Raven reached up and shuffled my curls around, getting my hair just right. I’d grown it out, and it was past my ears now.

“There. Now you look like a badass. I’d suggest dying your hair black, but I love your light brown color because it has streaks of gold when the sun hits it just right. It’s pretty.”

“Thanks… for, ah, coming and talking your mom into tattooing me.”

“Of course. It looks so fucking good, too. Your boyfriend is going to love it when you see each other again.”

“Yeah.”

As if I was ever going to see him again. The day I got out of his mom’s car, I had a feeling it would be the last I ever saw of him, which only fueled and festered my growing depression.

I walked into the apartment later to find Dad at home after Raven dropped me off. I still wasn’t allowed to drive, but a lot of my money had been put aside for a cheap car, so when I turned eighteen, I’d buy one for myself and fuck what everyone else wanted for me.

“Where have you been?” he asked.

“With Raven.”

He stepped out of the kitchen and took a long look at me. “What the fuck is that shit in your nose and ears?”

“They’re called piercings.”

“You look like a fag. I thought you were past this shit, Cade.”

“You know, even rock stars have them, and they aren’t gay! Fuck!”

I tossed my hands in the air, growled at nothing, and stormed past him to my room. I tried to slam the door, but he was close behind me and opened it.

“I’m coming to your race on Saturday. Sound good?”

Was he fucking high? One minute he was pissed, the next minute he tried to be my best bud. That ship had sailed years ago. He lost his chance with me.

“Whatever.”

“Good, and I’m sure you’re going to win. I’ll take you out to dinner if you want afterward.”

“You know I don’t like big meals after a race.”

“Oh, right. Okay. How about some ice cream?”

“No. I’ll be tired.”

“You know, I’m trying here, Cade. Why don’t you cut me some slack? I know you’re a hormonal teen, but really.”

*Fucking christ*.

“Do you mind? I have homework.”

He looked like he was about to say something, but he turned and shut the door behind him.

I blasted my music, stood in front of the mirror, and lifted my shirt to look at my tat.

“Where are you, Dillon?”

\* \* \*

I shouldn’t have bolted out of the start. You’re supposed to go steady. Not too fast. Not too slow. But I was filled with too much anger, resentment, frustration, and depression. I wanted to fucking hurt something, but all I had was myself. So I ran and ran hard. I wanted to feel the burn in my muscles. To have my lungs demand what the fuck I was doing to them. To make my heart feel like it was about to explode in my chest.

My first mistake was going to the football game last night. Dillon’s team played against mine. Loudoun County won, but that wasn’t the worst of it. Dillon didn’t even look for me. I thought he’d try to find me in the stands. That I’d wave to him and remind him I was still waiting.

*Wait for me,* he said. *Don’t give up on us*, he said. What a fucking joke.

I even had a little hope that after the game, he’d come and try to find me, but he piled on the school bus as soon as the game ended with the rest of his team and left as if I was nothing to him.

He fucking forgot about me. He moved on and didn’t love me anymore.

*Fuck my dad. Fuck my grandma. Fuck Dillon. Fuck Evelyn. Fuck everyone.*

That was my mantra as I ran, hugging the tight corner to pull into the lead. Though there were over fifty students running and a large crowd, the only thing I heard was my own footsteps pounding in the grass and my heartbeat drumming in my ears.

*Fuck my dad. Fuck my grandma. Fuck Dillon. Fuck Evelyn. Fuck everyone.*

As my heart and mind filled with hate and anger, I picked up speed. My body was numb. I’d hit my zone as I pulled away from everyone, leaving them in my hate-fueled fucking dust.

When I saw the finish line ahead, I ignored the burn in my muscles and pumped my legs to pull out the last burst of energy I had to break my record, which I knew I did before I even crossed the finish line.

16:9.3 minutes. A district record.

Whatever. As if it mattered anymore.

My legs were rubber and I could barely catch a breath, wheezing as I walked off the pain, feeling like I was about to collapse and vomit at the same time. The cheers were loud and people were congratulating me and patting my back, but I ignored them. It wasn’t until Dad showed up, trying to hug me, that I shoved him away because I didn’t have enough breath to tell him to get the fuck away from me.

But he was undeterred, patting my back with a massive smile I wanted to punch off.

After the race, Dad drove us back home. Back to the life I hated. Back to nothingness other than resentment and regret.

“God, Cade, you did amazingly out there. I can’t believe you broke your record. Cross-country is now my favorite sport.”

I scoffed.

“I’m so proud of you, son.”

“Yeah, only because I’m what you want me to be.”

“That’s not true.”

“Spare me.”

“Cade, I’m trying to be supportive.”

I laughed and shook my head. “You had your chance.”

“Please don’t tell me you’re still angry about Dillon. I thought you’d moved on.”

“I have. That doesn’t mean I still don’t fucking hate you. In fact, I hate all of you. I don’t want or need your goddamn support.”

“Yeah, well, you’re going to want it and need it if you plan to attend college.”

“So like you. You don’t get your way, so you threaten me. You always have conditions and the last fucking word.”

Dad was quiet for a while, then he reached out and touched my shoulder. I flinched, and he pulled away.

“Please, let’s try to get along,” he finally said when we pulled into the parking space.

All those abandonment issues were gone. Why have them when everyone you cared about was gone, anyway? Besides, it wouldn’t matter if anyone left me if I didn’t care about them at all.

“No.” I jumped out of the car, ignoring the pain in my legs, and ran up the few steps to our apartment, unlocked the door with my keys, and went into the kitchen to grab two ice packs for my knees, then headed into my room and slammed the door.

I stood there looking at my walls and ripped down every single fucking poster Dad put up and tore them to shreds. Then I opened my drawer full of girly magazines and tore them to shreds too. My eyes blurred with tears as the rage consumed me, ignoring the paper cuts.

Next, I pulled out all my notebooks Dillon ever doodled in, and I was about to throw them away, but I couldn’t. Though I resented him, it was still my last thread to him. That tiny, thin, last thread of hope. Instead, I put them in a small storage box and threw in his two T-shirts I had for good measure. Then I put the box on top of my shelf in my closet, along with my heart and soul. I wouldn’t need them anymore.

\* \* \*

It was just a month before my eighteenth birthday. I was almost an adult as I watched Dillon win the state championships back in December. He gave up on us. His life was perfect with a perfect mom, perfect friends, a perfect team, and perfect support. He moved on with his life to be successful, while I fucking failed at it.

But I went on to win the state championship for cross-country, earning me a scholarship to the University of Virginia. The only reason I tried so hard with running and my grades was to get the fuck out of here. It wasn’t a full ride to UVA, but it was enough.

So, the day I turned eighteen, I gave up on Dillon just like he gave up on me.
