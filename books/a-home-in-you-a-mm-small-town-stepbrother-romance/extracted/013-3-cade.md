# 3. Cade

It was Dillon’s fourteenth birthday in mid-March. I knew he was only two months younger than me, and not by much, but he seemed older. Maybe because he was more serious. Oh, and that he was much taller than me with a deeper voice.

Dad and I moved in three weeks ago, and it had been fun so far. The only problem was school. Dad didn’t want to take me out of my old school since I had only two and a half months left. So he or Evelyn had to drive me to school each morning, which took forever. I also hated being away from Dillon. But next year, we’d go to the same high school.

I loved living at the farmhouse. It was so much better than the apartment or going to grandma’s, and Evelyn was incredibly nice. The house was cool too and really old. There was something about the old wood floors creaking, and the place smelling like a fireplace, making it weirdly comforting. The home was pretty too, and comfortable. Evelyn came from money, or that’s what Dad said.

She moved me into the guest room and told me I could fix it up how I wanted. Dad put my old furniture in storage since what she had was nicer. The headboard, dresser, and nightstand were all antiques. I even had my own fireplace. How cool was that? Even better was the view of the barn, lush farmland, and forests. So much better than apartments, roads, and the view of a gas station.

Dillon and I got along amazingly well, and though he was quiet most of the time, he didn’t seem to mind that I talked a lot. And I think I did pretty good, trying to make him more like a future brother than a crush.

All four of us went out to dinner at a barbecue restaurant for Dillon’s birthday, which was his favorite. Then we headed home to have cake and open presents.

His mom handed him a wrapped present. Dillon smiled and opened the box to find two video games for his computer. “These are the ones you asked for, right?”

“Sweet! Horizon Zero Dawn and Injustice 2! That’s exactly what I wanted. Thanks, Mom!”

“You’re welcome, honey.”

My dad slid Dillon a gift too, but I picked it out. “This is from Cade and me. It's not much, but we hope you like it.”

“I’m sure I will,” he said, tearing through the paper to find a box of seventy-two Prismacolor Pencils. The people at the art store said they were the best. “Yes! I’ve always wanted these. And not much of a gift? These aren’t cheap. Thank you so much.”

Something in my chest grew warm, knowing Dillon loved the gift I picked out. That I learned enough about him to know what he’d like.

Later that night, Dad pulled Dillon and me aside to talk privately, man-to-man as he said, while Evelyn cleaned the kitchen. He pulled out a little black box from his pocket and opened it. Inside sat a diamond ring. Shit, did that mean what I thought it did? Was he really going to ask Evelyn to marry him on Dillon’s birthday? It didn’t seem like the best timing, but what did I know?

“I wanted to talk to you boys about something important, especially you, Dillon. Since this is your mom and you’re the man of the house, I wanted to ask your permission to propose to your mom. I want to marry her and for all of us to be a family. I would like to know what you think, too, Cade.”

That Dad asked my opinion was rare. I felt kind of honored that he’d care what I thought. But I let Dillon answer first. It was his mom, after all.

“Yessir, that’s fine with me. As long as Mom is happy.”

I glanced at him, but he didn’t seem fine at all. His brows were all furrowed, and he wasn’t smiling. I’d gotten to know Dillon enough to understand when he didn’t look too happy, though he tried to hide it.

“Thank you, Dillon. Cade?”

“I… uh, it’s great. And I really like it here, and I really like Dillon and Evelyn. I’m happy for you, Dad.”

Dillon looked at me and had a small smile. I’d hoped what I said would make him feel better about it. Though, I wasn’t too excited about having my crush also be my stepbrother soon. Or was it not too happy having a stepbrother I was crushing on? Either way, it was bad.

\* \* \*

Dad and Evelyn decided to have the wedding in June, and hold it at the house in the backyard.

Our parents were busy getting ready, so Dillon and I were left to get dressed before the ceremony. We stood in the bathroom we shared, combing our hair. Well, I tried to, but I struggled to tame my curls, so I gave up most of the time.

Dillon used some gel and combed back his long bangs from his face, opening it up like a flower or something. My breath caught seeing his whole beautiful face, but I hid my reaction. Or tried to. I wouldn’t ever dare let him find out how he made me feel all fucking fluttery inside. He was especially cute in his new dark gray suit and the lavender tie that made his eyes bluer. I loved how they always changed color depending on his mood or what he wore.

As he put some shiny, oily stuff in his hair that smoothed it out and finished combing it, I struggled to put on the stupid tie. I never wore one before, and Dad never showed me how.

“I can help,” Dillon said after seeing me struggle. “Mom taught me.”

I breathed out my relief. “Thanks.”

He took the lavender tie from me, lifted my shirt collar, and wrapped it around my neck. Dillon stood so close that I could smell him. He smelled of toothpaste, deodorant, and the shower gel we shared. His fair skin was smooth and only had a few freckles on his nose. When his fingers touched the skin on my neck and throat, my body broke out into goosebumps. That’s what his touch always fucking did to me. I liked it. A lot. Like, we shared this crazy electrical energy.

I acted so fucking stupid around him all the time, too. Sometimes I couldn’t stop talking, but then sometimes, when Dillon stood close like this, or he looked a certain way, I’d shut up. Like to have a quiet moment, so I could take him in. How did someone so in their shell have so much power? I wasn’t sure that was the right word. Strength?

I didn’t even pay attention to his instructions because I had been too focused on the touch of his hands, his smell, and his smooth skin on his face without one zit. He was a few inches taller than me, so I always had to stare up when we stood close to each other.

When he finished with my tie, he surprised me by pouring some of his shiny hair stuff in his hands and running his fingers through my messy curls, prying apart the tangles and smoothing them out, so they didn’t look so frizzy. God, I wish he’d touch me all the time. I’d die a happy man.

He took care of me so easily as if I was the little brother, and not him. Or maybe even like a boyfriend. Yes, that was a better visual. Sometimes he even made me breakfast or lunch whenever Evelyn had to work and Dad wasn’t around. And he never once got annoyed or complained about it.

While we were still in school, I struggled with math, and he helped me through some problems from my homework. He’d sit next to me, and I’d be distracted by how close he sat, making it hard to focus on what he was teaching me, but I did my best.

“You have really nice hair,” he said. Then he stuck out his tongue, concentrating hard on the top of my head.

My heart beat a little faster at the compliment, unable to take my eyes off his pink tongue. I always hated my hair, but that he liked it made me appreciate it more.

My stomach bottomed out when Dillon caught me staring at him. I hope he didn’t think anything of it.

“What?” he asked.

Yep, he noticed.

I shrugged and smiled, playing it cool. “Ready for our parents to be married?”

One thing he did all the time was stare at my dimples whenever I smiled. I liked to imagine he had a crush on me, too, and that he liked my dimples, so I smiled a lot for him. But soon, we’d be brothers, and I needed to shut all that shit down. Plus, he was probably straight. How I felt about him had to stay only a dream. I could only pretend.

“I guess so,” he said, still standing close and staring at me.

“Yeah… But we get to be brothers now.”

He smiled and nodded.

A sudden knock sounded on the bathroom door, making my heart stop. Dillon stepped back and opened it to my dad, all cleaned up and wearing a suit and tie that matched ours. “You boys about ready?”

“Yessir,” Dillon said.

“Good. It’s almost time. I’m going to stand by the altar, and you boys will take a seat in the front row, okay?”

“Yessir,” we both said.

The backyard was really pretty. Evelyn decorated it herself with twinkling lights in the big trees that would stand out when it got darker, flowers were everywhere, and she set up an altar covered in some white fabric and more flowers. The day wasn’t so hot for mid-June, but it was humid as hell, making Dillon and me sweaty already. His face was beaded with sweat and his hair grew wet. That was Virginia for you.

We sat next to each other in the front row with our knees pressed up against each other despite the heat outside. I pretended I didn’t pay attention to it, but all I could feel was Dillon’s electricity, and I refused to move. My body may have been still, but my heart was ready to fucking leap out of my chest. Dillon didn’t move either, but maybe he didn’t notice. I noticed it like a neon sign.

Soon the music started, thank god. I needed a distraction from my crush. We all turned our heads to see Evelyn walking down the grassy aisle toward my Dad, who waited for her. He smiled, clasping his hands in front of him.

Evelyn was an angel, and she was so pretty, too, wearing a lacy dress, but it wasn’t long like other wedding dresses. It fell to her knees. She held a pretty white and lavender bouquet and wore her black hair up with curls falling down. I guess she did that on purpose.

Thirty minutes and a gallon of sweat later, my dad and my new mom were married. I now officially had a brother that I had a major crush on. Fuck it. It wasn’t like I could control how I felt, but I really tried. I’d never had a crush like this before, and it was so fucking hard to ignore him. I mean, I found boys cute before, but not like Dillon. He was gorgeous, and he was really amazing too. He was fun and really sweet, even if he was more serious than me.

The wedding was pretty boring except for Dillon, watching him whenever I could when it didn’t seem weird. I liked Evelyn, too. With Dad always gone, and grandma helping out but not caring that much, it left me feeling too alone most of the time. I hoped things would be different now.

I tried not to think about that. Happy thoughts were always better, and Dillon and Evelyn made me happy.
