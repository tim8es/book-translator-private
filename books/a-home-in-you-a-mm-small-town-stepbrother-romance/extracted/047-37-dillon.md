# 37. Dillon

I pulled my truck up in front of the small, old farmhouse filled with nerves, but Cade probably had it worse than me. I glanced at him fidgeting and staring out the window, saying nothing on the way.

“We don’t have to do this, baby.”

Cade looked over at me and gave me one of his fake smiles. No matter how far we’d come, he always tried to hide his inner anxiety behind his cute dimples. At least we had our loft where he would often open up. It was our safe space, not that we needed safety from each other. Just safety from the world that wanted to see us fail.

“No. I… need to. This is more for me than them.”

“I understand.”

We piled out of the car and I took his hand as we made our way to the front door. Cade’s dad and grandmother knew we were coming. Honestly, it surprised me they allowed me to come. Maybe there was some hope after all. Or perhaps they wanted to tear me a new one for *‘corrupting’* Cade. Whatever. I was ready for them. They didn’t fucking scare me.

After Cade knocked on the door, his grandmother opened it, scanning us both for what seemed like forever before she stepped aside to let us in. His fingers tightened around mine and his hand grew clammy. Fuck, I wish I could’ve protected him from all of this, but he was so brave to face them and to tell them exactly how he felt. It was about damned time.

She led us into the living room where Jared waited for us, drinking some amber liquid, and when he saw us, his eyes focused straight to our joined hands, but he made no comment, and he didn’t show any cards on his face. It didn’t matter, though. I knew he hated me, and that he believed Cade wasn’t gay.

We sat on a loveseat and Cade sat so close to me that with one more shift, he’d end up on my lap. His hand trembled in mine, so I sandwiched it between my other hand and rubbed soothing circles.

“So, you’re here,” his dad said. “What do you want? To tell me to fuck off, as you have for the past two years? I’ve put up with it, hoping you’d finally come around to realize I was doing what’s best for you.”

Cade stiffened but didn’t argue back. “No, I’m done being passive-aggressive. I came here to convince you I’m gay, have always been gay, and there’s nothing you can do about it, no matter how much you wish it to not be true. Both of you. I’ve known I was gay at a really young age. Long before I was even interested in boys. A child doesn’t choose to be gay. A child is still trying to figure out the world, not who his boyfriend will be. A child is confused and tries to come to grips that he’s different from other boys. You both never talked about homosexuality or anything, so I had no idea how much you’d be in denial over this. Or how much hatred you have towards people like Dillon and me. It’s a shame, really.”

“You slept with your brother, for fuck’s sake,” his dad snapped.

“My stepbrother,” I corrected. “We aren’t related, and we’re no longer stepbrothers.”

Before his dad could tear me a new one, Cade spoke up. “Yes, it’s… unusual for stepbrothers to have a relationship. Yes, it’s generally frowned upon. Regardless, it happened, but I don’t regret a second of it. We grew instantly close together and did everything together. We formed a bond that nothing could break. Not even you, despite your best efforts. And this was before we even made our feelings known to each other. Honestly, this was inevitable. Dillon and I… No, you’ll never understand. I don’t think you really understand love at all. I don’t know why Mom left us, but I wonder now how much that had to do with you. I’d always blamed myself, and I think you blamed me, too, but I was too young to be at fault.”

His dad shifted uncomfortably in his chair and sipped his drink, while his grandmother refused to look at Cade, saying nothing. Fucking useless.

“I admitted I neglected you. Yes, I was angry, and maybe it’s partly my fault for you falling for… for you liking that boy—”

“His name is Dillon. My *boyfriend*.”

His grandmother scoffed.

“Whatever. I neglected you, opening you up to this sort of behavior. I tried to remedy that and protect you. I did my best, Cade.”

Cade didn’t even get angry as I would’ve. Like I currently was. I was a live wire ready to burn the place if his dad made one wrong move.

Cade sighed and shook his head. “Your neglect only opened my eyes to your behavior. But I knew you wouldn’t get it. Your mind refuses to believe that your son was born gay. I mean, how *dare* I come from your manly *loins* and turn gay, right? And you didn’t take care of me. You controlled every aspect of my life and dangled rewards over my head like a good puppy who didn’t piss on the floor. That’s the extent of your love. Look… I get it. I’m sure you did your best, but your best is terrible, which I’m sure had a lot to do with Grandma.”

His grandmother’s face turned red, and she fisted her hands on her lap. “Now hold on, Cade. Don’t you blame me for this. I took care of you.”

“You neglected me as much as Dad did. I’m sure you raised him the same. So, his behavior isn’t entirely his fault.” Cade looked back at his dad, filled with sadness. “That doesn’t mean you didn’t hurt me. You understood yanking me away from Dillon would do that, and you didn’t care about my happiness at all. You hit me twice for it, and hid me away, forcing me to look at nude pictures of women. In the end, you made your own choices.”

His dad was angry, but he didn’t rise to the growing tension, which surprised me. But there was a stewing going on that threatened to boil over. “And what would you have me do, Cade? Accept you and that… boy? To tell the world I love my gay son and join some queer community or group?”

My heart stopped, worried for my boyfriend when he threw back his head and laughed, then he sighed again. “God forbid, you’d accept your gay son and be tolerant. I wouldn’t dare *dream* of it, Dad. I’m only here to tell you like it is. You either accept it or you don’t. I just want you to realize that I’ve said my peace and that I don’t care what happens between us from here on out. You can no longer control me.”

When Cade stood, I followed. He put his arm around me and looked at his dad and grandmother once more. “You both have a choice. Accept me, and perhaps we can work things out to where I see you more, and maybe we can try to be family again. Or don’t. The only way we can be a family again is for you to accept me as I am and that I love Dillon… and work your asses off to earn my trust. The choice is yours. If you don’t want me in your life as I am, I really don’t care, either. I won’t miss either of you. You’ve done too much damage.”

We walked out without looking back when Jared called out to Cade. “Son… wait.”

“What are you doing, Jared? Let them go,” his grandmother said, making me want to tell her fucking off, but this was Cade’s show; not mine.

“No.”

*Thank fuck*. Will his dad finally see reason? Was it the threat of never seeing his son again the final blow that woke him up?

“Jared!”

“Shut up, Mama. Cade, please…”

Cade turned around and held me tighter. “What.”

“Admittedly, I’ve thought a lot about you since… I watched you run your last race. I’m really struggling with all of this, Cade. I didn’t grow up with this gay stuff and was always taught it was evil and wrong. But my son isn’t… evil. I was scared for you. For me. How people would look at us. Hate you. Hate me. That you’d burn in hell or something. I didn’t know what to do. I can’t say things will be easy or that… I won’t have issues. Just don’t leave me like she left. Let me try.”

Cade turned his head into my chest and cried. I could only tell because his body shuddered. I held him tight as he reached for his father. Jared took Cade’s hand and held on. It was all Cade ever wanted. To be accepted and loved as he was. His dad didn’t make promises, but that he wanted to try to accept Cade’s sexuality was promising.

“Okay, Dad,” Cade said in my chest. “I’ll give you a second chance, but you have to be nice to Dillon, too. He’s in my life for good.”

Jared looked at me, unable to hide his scowl. He would forever believe I was at fault for my relationship with his son, and honestly, he wasn’t wrong. I made the first move. But I did it all out of love.

“I’ll try. Can… Can I see you at Christmas?”

“I’m not sure. It may be too soon. We’ll see.”

I’m glad Cade made no commitments. His dad had more to think about, and he had to work hard to win Cade’s trust back. I hoped Cade wouldn’t make it easy on the man. It would sure as hell take me a long time to trust Jared again, let alone forgive him.

\* \* \*

We’d gotten a little big for the old cast iron tub, but we managed. Cade had his back to me, sitting up between my legs as I lathered his hair. He had his head leaning back as I dug my fingers in, massaging his scalp. I loved doing anything to bring him any sort of happiness, and he deserved it. I was the lucky one, filled with all I could want with a loving and supportive mother. Cade always got the short end of the stick in life. Despite it all, he persevered and survived. He succeeded in attending a good college and winning his races. He said I was the strong one, but he was wrong.

“Have I told you how proud I am of you?”

“About twenty times in the car on the way back from grandma’s place.”

“Well, this will be twenty-one times because I am. You amaze me. You’re so strong and brave.”

He craned his head to look back at me with a small smile on his face. “I… don’t feel like I am.”

“You are. Never doubt it.”

Once his hair was rinsed, he leaned against my chest and rested his wet head on my shoulder. I wrapped my arms around him and kissed his temple. How could one love someone so much? How was it possible? Looking at him with his eyes closed, so content and relaxed, made my heart beat a little faster and my eyes well with emotion. Life didn’t allow me to love anyone else because it made Cade only for me, and I was just fine with that.

“I love you. You’re my home. Forever.”

Cade’s smile was broad with deep-set dimples. “I love you. You’re my home. Forever.”

I lifted his hand and played with his fingers, not caring that the water was growing cold. “You’re going to marry me one day. We’re going to have kids, dogs, cats, horses, and maybe even some chickens. We can raise the kids on the farm, and then save our money to take them to Europe or something to expand their minds. To show them the big world out there beyond the happy little bubble we’d create.”

“Sounds perfect.”

“I’ll run the store with Mom and maybe open a gallery. You’ll take care of injured kids in athletics. We’ll grow old together while sitting on the porch drinking lemonade and bitching about the younger generation, and them driving by on the country road too fast, calling them *‘young whippersnappers.’*”

He barked out a laugh, making me smile. “That’s oddly specific, Dil.”

“I’ve got it all planned.”

“I don’t doubt it. It doesn’t matter as long as we’re together.”

“Always.”
