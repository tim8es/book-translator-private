“I know,” he said. “Have you thought about talking to him? I can talk to Mom about anything.”

“Please don’t ever leave, Dil. Is that unfair of me to ask?”

“We’ve already promised each other, and I intend to keep it. You and I will always be home to each other.” He lifted my chin to look at him again. His blue eyes turned gray with intensity and seriousness. “I will do everything I can to keep us together, Cade. There are people out there, though, who would love to hate us and try to separate us. We just have to stay strong, okay?”

“Hey, if anything, your dad brought us together. That’s one good thing about him, right?”

I laughed and nodded on his shoulder, feeling way too needy. I hated it, but I loved having Dillon there for me when I got like this.

It was the first week of school, and Dillon and I ran laps around the track to keep up with our endurance. We should’ve run more during the summer, but we’d been… preoccupied.

“Ugh, I don’t know how… you do this for a… sport. You’re… killing me,” Dillon panted as we kept a steady pace.

“Talking won’t help you.”

“Ugh.”

As we completed three laps, a few girls sat in the bleachers to watch us. Two of them waved as we passed, probably for Dillon. Sure, I’d had a couple of girls interested in me, and I needed to find a way to get out of it, but most girls liked Dillon. He was one of those tall, dark, and handsome types. While he didn’t grow as much this summer, he still stood at six-foot-one. He also grew muscular from football and regular workouts. I’d reached five feet nine, and if I got lucky, maybe I’d reach six feet, though I doubted it. I was muscular too, but it was all lean from running.

“Someone likes you,” I said.

He glanced over, just noticing them, wiping the sweat off of his face with a towel. “I doubt it. You’re hot, and I’m sure they want you.”

One thing I learned about Dillon since we’d met; he was never into anyone at all. He never showed any signs of being attracted to a guy or a girl and had always focused on me. While I trusted him, it didn’t cure my growing jealousy as time went on and more and more girls hit on him. I worried there would be a day when he found a strong connection with someone else and left me. Part of me wouldn't blame him. It would be so much easier to date someone else who wasn’t a stepbrother or a guy. Our sexuality and being brothers seriously complicated things. But the other part of me would die without him. I’d be fucking crushed.

We took a breather and some water, walking it off.

“Stop it,” he said.

“What?”

“You think I don’t know everything about you? Don’t even think about me leaving you for someone else or that those girls are even interested in me, or that I’m into them. You’re the only one for me.”

It was something I needed to hear, and too often now, as Dillon grew in popularity. I had a lot of friends too, but I was also the one with insecurity issues, while Dillon just screamed confidence.

Ugh, and this year was going to suck because Dillon and I didn’t have any classes together. At least we had lunch, I guess.

Dillon was running late for lunch when I sat down and opened up my paper bag filled with snacks, juice, and a sandwich. Suddenly, someone plopped their ass where Dillon belonged.

I let out an easy breath, seeing it was Darren. There had been a couple of boys who picked on me a little bit last year, but this week, they’d stepped up their game. They kept asking me about all the hot girls in school, and I had a feeling they were testing me about being gay. Not that they knew anything, but I think they were assuming. I hated playing it off like I even thought about girls at all.

“I didn't get to see you guys too much this summer,” he said. “I went to a month-long football camp Mom and Dad sent me to. Probably to get me out of their hair, but it was a good experience, anyway. Wish Dillon would’ve been there.”

I playfully elbowed him in the shoulder. “Dillon’s gifted. You’re the one who needs the work.”

Darren scoffed, then laughed. “Asshole.”

He shoved a school hamburger in his mouth and ate almost half of it in one bite. “Someone forgot to feed you?”

“What? I’m a growing boy,” he said with his mouth full.

I scanned him up and down and smiled. “I don’t think you need to grow anymore. What are you, like seven feet now? You’re bigger than most adults.”

“Six-five. But I hope not too big,” he said with furrowed brows.

“Uh, why? What does that even mean?”

He leaned toward my ear and whispered. “There’s a new kid here at school that I’m crushing on. He’s in my biology and Spanish classes.”

My eyes bugged out, and I choked on my sandwich. “*He?*”

“Shhh.”

“Uh, okay. I didn’t know you liked dudes. When I said you were closeted that day, I was kidding.”

“I dunno. Maybe I’m bi or something. He’s just really pretty. I don’t think he likes me too much, though.”

“Why, because you're a scary Yeti?”

“Yeah, I guess I am. But I can be a teddy bear when I want.”

I scoffed. “Sure you can. A teddy bear with thorns.”

He elbowed me hard in the ribs and jutted his chin towards the skinny guy with wavy, dark red hair falling to his jawline, holding a school lunch tray, looking for a place to sit. He was clearly not straight, wearing dangled pearl earrings, a matching pearl choker around his throat, and some lacy shirt. I didn’t get it. He and Darren? It didn’t get more opposite than those two.

“Yo, Christian! Come sit with us,” Darren called out.

Christian stared at him wide-eyed and scampered off like a scared little fawn.

“See, you’re scary.”

Darren huffed and pouted, making me laugh.

Dillon suddenly plopped down next to me, and I felt instantly at home again when we touched knees.

“What are we talking about?”

“Apparently our Darren likes boys now,” I whispered.

Dillon leaned over to face Darren on the other side of me, completely unfazed. “Who?”

“That new boy, Christian.”

“Oh, the cute redhead? Yeah, I’ve got him in my algebra class.”

My body stiffened. *Cute redhead?* Hell no. My face must have had jealousy written all over it.

“Cade, come on. You know I meant nothing by that.”

“And you better not, because he’s mine,” Darren said.

“You both fucking know how I feel about Cade. Anyway, be gentle on Christian, Darren. He’s like a third your size.”

Darren huffed again. “I can be gentle.”

Dillon and I both laughed. “Sure you can.”

It was the first football game of the season for the junior varsity team, and it was a home game, so Dad and Evelyn came to watch, but I didn’t sit next to them and instead sat with a couple of our friends. If Dad showed up for Dillon, I hoped he showed up for me. I had a big track meet next week. Then again, the races could be long, and hard to watch the entire thing since we ran five or eight kilometers, and I guess it wasn’t nearly as exciting as football. At least Dillon always supported me and cheered me on.

Our school, Loudoun County Captains, was up against the Broad Run Spartans tonight. They were a good team, but not as good as ours. Dillon and Darren should kill it.

Dillon took the snap and quickly scanned the field to find one of his wide receivers open and ready. He stepped back and tossed it down the field for thirty yards. The wide receiver caught it with little effort and took off down the field like a bullet. He was one of the team’s fastest. He was tackled twenty yards shy of the goal.

As they worked their way closer and closer to the goal, Darren did an amazing job as the middle linebacker, protecting my boyfriend from harm.

It didn’t take long for them to make a touchdown and the extra point. Dillon jogged back to the sidelines and removed his helmet, shaking out his sweaty hair. Fuck, he looked hot. He turned and scanned the crowd, and when our eyes met, he smirked and waved. My heart fluttered as I waved back. Two brothers could wave and smile during a game, right? There was nothing to it, right?

I didn’t like football all that much, but watching Dillon play was a thing of beauty. He was smooth and precise. When there was no one to throw to or hand off the ball, he’d run it. My heart would stop each time they tackled him, but I breathed again when he stood up, unharmed.

By the end of the game, our school won. Everyone screamed and cheered, and I laughed as Darren lifted Dillon off of his feet in celebration. As soon as he stood on his feet again, three cheerleaders rushed him, jumping around him. One girl threw her arms around him, and he laughed, hugging her back. My heart literally stopped. Fuck, it was the same girl who’d been watching us run every other morning. I *knew* she liked him.

My previous jealousy morphed into something new. Something that had been growing inside me the closer Dillon and I got. Fear. Fear he’d leave me or that he’d fall for someone else. No matter how much he told me he loved me or would never leave me, I couldn’t help but doubt it.

I climbed over my friends to run out of there. I couldn’t stare at the girls fawning over him. Then I saw Dad and Evelyn meet Dillon on the sideline. Dad gave Dillon a big hug and patted him on the back. My eyes watered as the growing fear festered. I didn’t want to be afraid. I wanted to be confident like Dillon, but I couldn’t control it.

He looked up at the bleachers, scanning the crowd. Then they landed on me, and he frowned. I turned and left, trying desperately not to cry. He was going to leave me. I knew it. What would I do? How could I still be his brother if we weren't together? I’d fucking die.

I didn’t know where to go. Evelyn and Dad were our ride home, but I just had to leave somewhere away from others. I didn’t want them to see how upset I got, and Dad would get pissed.

“Cade!”

*Go away, Dillon*.

“Cade! Where are you going?”

I walked faster, then I ran. If I was good at anything, it was running.

Who knew how Dillon caught up with me? Maybe I let him. I was being stupid like that, anyway.

He grabbed my arm and yanked me to stop. “Hey, what’s going on? Why are you running from me?”

I yanked my arm away. “Not here.”

His eyes looked hurt, and that killed me. Why did I have to be this way? Why was I always so fucking stupid?

I gave him one of my smiles to distract him, but I didn’t fool him for a second. “Never mind. It’s fine. I’m just… being an idiot.”

“That’s not going to work on me, Cade. We’re going to talk about it in the loft as soon as we get home.”

“No, I’m… fine.”

“You are far from fine. We’re going to talk as we always do. Don’t shut me out now.”

I nodded, and we headed to Evelyn’s car, where our parents waited for us. Dillon removed his football gear and tossed it in the trunk.

“Is everything alright?” Evelyn asked, looking back and forth between Dillon and me.

I just nodded and got in the car.

Dad didn’t even acknowledge me when he told Dillon how proud he was of him. At least Evelyn picked up on something wrong. Dad was fucking useless.

When we arrived home, I rushed to the barn. “Gonna pet Athena for a while,” I said as an excuse.

When I stepped into the barn, I kept the light off, but I could still see enough from the single light on the tall pole next to the barn outside. My eyes watered as I had time to think in the car. I felt like an idiot. I overreacted, and if I kept doing that, it was going to annoy Dillon. He’d get tired of trying to deal with my fucking insecurities.

He wasn’t far behind when he came in and closed the barn door behind him. “Up. Now.”

My gut tightened. He was upset with me. Angry. I’d pushed him enough, and he was tired of me. I took a shuddered breath and climbed the ladder to the top.

We kept the loft doors closed, and Dillon pulled out one of our lanterns and turned it on. His hair was all over the place from being sweaty and wearing a helmet, but he looked really cute like that. I glanced down and played with some straw.

“Talk to me, Cade. Please. You looked so… upset, and it nearly killed me. But I can’t help you if you don’t tell me what’s going on in that beautiful head of yours.”

“It’s… god, I saw that girl hug you, and you… looked happy and hugged her back, and then Dad treats you like you’re his real son and I’m not. And sometimes I think you’d like to be with someone else more because being with your brother is probably really annoying because we have to hide everything. But I also got really scared. Not jealous. Just afraid that you’d leave me. I know I’m being stupid, but I can’t help it. And…”

God, I hated when I word vomited, but if anyone could sort out all that, it was Dillon.

“Your parents did this shit to you, didn’t they? I understand your fears.” He cupped my face in his big hands to make me look at him. “I only hugged her back because I was still high from the win. That’s all. There is no one else for me but you. God, I also get afraid that you’ll stop loving me. I have those insecurities, too. I also have these fears sometimes.”

“Yes. But I will tell you—every day if I have to—that I love you and will never leave you. You’re afraid, and I totally get why. So, if I have to prove to you all the time that I’m always going to be here for you, I will.”

“I’m going to start now, Cade.” He looked deep into my eyes with his usual serious intensity. “I love you. You’re my home. Forever. Now say it back to me.”

“I love you, Dillon. You’re my home. Forever.”

“Good. I will tell you this every fucking day.”

I nodded, and he pulled me into him. We crushed our bodies together, holding on as if to seal our promise.
