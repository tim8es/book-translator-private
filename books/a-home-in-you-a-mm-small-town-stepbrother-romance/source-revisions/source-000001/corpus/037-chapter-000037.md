# 27. Dillon

Starting college had been busy as hell, from getting situated in the dorms, finding my classes, attending said classes, practicing every day for football, and studying.

Because I was a freshman, I couldn’t be the starting quarterback, but the second string, though the coach felt I was good enough to play a couple of games to see how I did. My high school record spoke for itself, but football was also different playing at the college level. The University of Virginia Cavaliers had horrible colors of orange and blue, but I’d wear them with pride, anyway.

I wish Darren could have lived with me in the dorm, but we didn’t have a choice since the school assigned us roommates. We didn’t find out until too late, we could’ve put in a request. But at least we took Personal Finance class together. We figured the class would be a good and practical course to take. I decided on History of Art to be my major, with a minor in Fine Arts. It was important to understand art, not just create it, since I planned to take over Mom’s shop one day and perhaps expand on it. Maybe open an art studio close by, or even a gallery. It was safe, like everything else I did in my life. Except for loving Cade, and I’d risk it all for him.

My busy schedule also left me little time to find Cade, but I did, with only a quick search through the student directory to find him. That it was so easy, and he never tried to look me up, along with him not finding me when he turned eighteen, held me back from approaching him right away.

As soon as I found his name, my heart soared. For the first time, a sense of hope filled me. Finally, we’d have our time again. To be together again. But as the past couple of weeks went by, I knew it wouldn’t be that simple. Not that I assumed it would be.

I just prayed he hadn’t moved on. That he didn’t have a boyfriend now. I’d fucking die if he did. And typical me, I had absolutely no interest in seeing anyone else other than Cade, and I wasn’t sure I ever would. At least I wasn’t wallowing anymore. The antidepressants also helped me get through day-to-day life, along with Darren keeping me on the straight and beaten path.

“I’m going to get you back, Cade. I swear it,” I mumbled to myself as I made my way to training.

Darren was already suited up in the locker room when I arrived. The big orange and blue UVA on his shoulder was like a neon light. I never asked what Christian thought about it.

“About time you showed up.”

“Class ran late.”

I quickly stripped down and got my gear on. Darren waited for me as I got ready, and we both jogged onto the field. I looked around at the stadium, still in awe at the sight. It was massive compared to high school, and high school football was a big deal in this country.

Darren whacked me in the chest with the back of his hand. “I can’t fucking wait until we play a game with the stands full of people cheering us on! Goddamn ego-boosting.”

I chuckled at his excitement. I’d never seen him happier since he left home. “Yeah, it’s not so bad.”

“Not so bad? Dude, it’s the best!”

*It’ll be even better once I get Cade back into my life*.

I should be grateful he stuck with the plan to attend UVA. As mom used to tell me when I was younger and did something stupid, everything was fixable except death.

While Darren went off to work on his hitting and tackling drills, I practiced with the other quarterbacks and receivers with throwing drills. After two hours in the heat, because the cooler weather hadn’t started, we all panted and dripped sweat.

After a short speech from the head coach, we all hit the showers.

The locker room reeked of sweat, and it grew humid from all the showers. It was loud as everyone talked and laughed. Then one of our team members looked over at Darren and glanced between his legs.

“Hey, Wilson, I heard you like boys. Keep that monster away from us, perv.”

Darren’s face turned red and stormed over toward our new bully. I couldn’t remember his name. Jesse, maybe.

“Dare… Easy,” I said.

“You some homophobe, Jesse?” Darren pointed between his legs. “Because this cock hates homophobes. Or maybe you *want* this monster? Is that why you’re such a little bitch right now? Hate to break it to you, but my monster has no interest in your flat ass.”

That made some of the team chuckle, while Jesse turned red with anger, clearly not liking the tables turned on him. He was bullying a bully after all.

The irony of ironies. I loved Darren, but he had been a dick to Cade and me about our sexuality the first time he saw us together. I suspected it was because he had been closeted, and Cade called him out on it, which got him punched. Now he got a dose of what it felt like. Regardless, he made up for his behavior in spades, so I had his back.

Jesse tried to lunge at Darren, but his friends pulled him back. Back in high school, Darren had been one of the biggest boys, and no one messed with him, but in college, all the guys who played football were big.

I stepped in front of Darren, but I was over this hiding shit, too. While Darren was open, I wasn’t, which would end starting now. “Guess what, boys? Your second-string quarterback has a boyfriend, too.” So that wasn’t a total lie, but I also didn’t want to go into how I wasn’t really gay, but demisexual. They wouldn’t understand or care.

“Great, two perverts. I refuse—”

“What do you refuse, Jesse?” asked Head Coach Evans.

Jesse looked suddenly terrified, but stood his ground and pointed at Darren and me. “Them. They’re fags, Coach. No one said there would be fags on the team. I refuse to shower around them.”

God, I hated that term. Cade’s father talked like that, making me want to fucking punch something… or someone.

Darren folded his arms and scoffed. “As I said, no one wants your flat ass.” Then he elbowed me. “Why do these guys always think it’s all about them? That we want them just because we like dudes?”

I smirked and shrugged.

“That’s enough! Jesse, you’re benched for the first two games.”

“Hey!”

“Listen up! We will not have bigots or racists of any kind on this team. Do we understand each other?”

“Yes, Coach,” most of us said.

“We’re a team. A team that has to trust each other and work together in order to win. I don’t care how good you are on this team or what you contribute. If you cannot tolerate someone who’s different or work together with anyone else on this team, it will affect the rest of us. Do we want to win? Or do we want to lose? But by all means, feel free to leave if you have no tolerance. You will only hamper this team if you stay. Is. That. Understood.”

“Yes, Coach!”

“Jesse. A word.”

Jesse slunk off to follow the coach. Thank fuck for those who were accepting. Thank fuck for Coach Evans.

I clapped Darren on the shoulder. “You okay, Dare?”

He blew out a laugh. “Yeah, so weird being on the receiving end.”

“I got your back.”

I walked off to shower before he grabbed me by the arm. He furrowed his brows and frowned. “You… came out for me.”

I shrugged. “Sort of. It’s not easy coming out as a demisexual, since I’m not really gay. Neither are you, really.”

“Yeah, I liked a girl once, but I just saw Christian, and I wanted him.”

“I’m here for you, and I’m done hiding who I am.”

After our showers, I said bye to Darren and headed off toward the track and field arena. I didn’t bother telling him where I was going because he’d want to come. Darren had been my ultimate supporter in finding Cade. He never once told me to let him go and move on. But Darren stood out like a sore thumb, and I didn’t want to reveal myself to Cade yet.

While I loved Cade more than anything, he always struggled with insecurity, which hadn’t been his fault, but his fucking family’s doing. After being torn from me, who knew what shit his dad and grandma put into his head. And because I couldn’t reach him all this time, it wouldn’t have surprised me to find that Cade resented me. I needed to prepare for it, anyway. It would also explain why he never reached out to me when he turned eighteen.

I put on a baseball hat and some sunglasses and made my way to the bleachers. The cross-country team was supposed to be training, so I hoped he was still running. Surely he was, since the school recruited him because of his talent.

Sitting behind some people to hide, I scanned the stretching runners, getting ready for practice. They wore their orange and blue uniforms, so they blended together, but I could pick out Cade in crowded Times Square. There he was, stretching with some other guys on his team. He had his back facing me with ‘Beckman’ on his shirt.

My heart stopped, and I held my breath, almost willing him to turn around and see me. God, the pain of missing him hit me like a Mac truck. It had been a year and a half since I’d seen him. Not since that day Mom and I took him back home to his grandma’s. I covered my mouth to hide my quivering lips. At least the sunglasses hid my watering eyes.

*My Cade. My home.*

He was so close, yet so out of reach.

He was my Cade yet he looked different. His locks grew longer, and almost to his jawline, which he tucked behind his ears. He had tattoos on his arms too. Not tons, but a few. Did he have others? Shit, he looked hot. He’d always been beautiful and sexy, now he was doubly so, and looked more like a man now. Hell, he was a man.

I didn’t quite feel whole yet, despite the short distance between us.

*Soon, baby. Soon we’ll reunite.*

Cade finished stretching, and he walked over to the track with the other boys to get ready.

A girl in front of me covered in tattoos and black hair called out his name, and I quickly ducked my head when he looked over to wave at her.

Fuck, I picked the worst spot.

I would’ve been jealous of the girl had I not been certain Cade was gay. Unless his dad… Nah. No way his dad converted him. You couldn’t un-gay someone, as far as I knew.

Once he started running, I moved to sit somewhere else as I watched him take off. He looked so strong and fast. At least he looked good. Healthy. Better than the last time I’d seen him.

Cade ran around the track several times for the next twenty minutes before he and his team stopped to walk it off. With hands on his hips, he paced back and forth until breathing grew easier, which was my cue to leave. I didn’t want to risk him seeing me yet. I needed the right moment to approach him, hoping I knew it when I saw it.

One thing for sure, I’d do my best to be at most of his practices and races. I could at least support him from afar.

That night in my dorm room, as my roommate Aaron read a book in bed, I pulled out an old photo of Cade from my phone and sketched his face. It had been a long time since I drew him. I hadn’t been able to without crushing my heart. But after seeing him today, the urge came back. Being so close gave me the first glimmer of hope that we’d have a chance to go back to what we once were. Maybe not exactly, but close.

As I sketched his face from when he was sixteen, I sharpened his jawline, added some scruff, then I drew in his soft curls, but made them longer, which strangely made his hair less curly. It was wavier now. His brows were slightly more pronounced and his nose was sharper… just a little. I recalled seeing a glint of metal in his ears, so I drew in some small hoops.

I held the sketchbook further from my face as I scanned his face. He looked closer to the Cade today, but his features looked younger in my picture. And he was missing his dimples. The ones I swore I’d keep on his face and fucking failed at.

“Whatcha drawing? Can I see?”

My roommate, Aaron, was blond and brown-eyed of average height and weight. He was kind of quiet, which I liked, and a pretty nice guy.

I showed him the image of a more mature Cade. “Jeez, that’s amazing. Who is he? Someone you know?”

I nodded, unsure what to tell him. Should I explain he had been my stepbrother? My ex-boyfriend? Current boyfriend? Boyfriend and stepbrother? It was still confusing, and I didn’t want to completely out us until I talked to Cade first.

“He’s… it’s complicated,” I said, and figured most of the truth would work. I just didn’t mention the brothers part. “He was my boyfriend for three years until his dad found out and prevented us from dating. I… promised him I’d find him, and if I couldn’t, when he turned eighteen, he was supposed to find me, but he never did. It’s been a year and a half, and I came here on a prayer that he stuck with our plan to attend UVA. He’s definitely here, but I haven’t approached him yet. I think… he’s mad at me for not finding him sooner.”

I held my breath to see if my nice roommate turned into some homophobe. You never knew until you came out. And I soon realized that I would have to come out for the rest of my life with every person who would become more than an acquaintance.

Aaron’s eyes grew wide with a stupid grin on his face. That was good, right? “Dude, that’s like… a romance movie. You ask your lover to wait for you, separated for years, until you find him again. Fucking epic.”

I let out a breath and laughed. “Yeah, it’s pretty fucking tragic, man. No joke. It’s been… stressful.”

“I can imagine. So, are you like gay, or bisexual? Sorry, it's not my business.”

“Nah, it’s cool.” Should I tell him? Why the hell not? I’d told him this much. “I’m demisexual.” He looked confused, which I expected. It wasn’t a well-known sexual orientation to non-LGBTQ+ people. “It just means I need… an emotional attachment to someone to feel sexual attraction to them. That’s the gist, anyway. Although I’m not one hundred percent because I’ve had no one like Cade in my life before, and I’ve never been sexually attracted to anyone else either. I mean, I find people attractive, but nothing says, ‘wow, I’d love to bang them.’”

“Huh. Interesting. That’s pretty cool, actually. So, no one-night stands and shit?”

I shook my head. “Not interested in it, even if Cade wasn’t my soulmate.”

“Dude, you’re going to have to keep me up-to-date with your story. I hope everything works out for you.”

“Thanks, man.”

Aaron went back to reading, and I did some touch-ups on my drawing before I rolled over and stared at his face, tracing my fingers gently over the lines, so I didn’t smudge them.

“I’m coming, Cade,” I whispered. “Just give me a bit more time.”
