# 21. Cade

I sat at the kitchen table at my grandma’s house, where Dad and I moved into picking at my fingernail. It took me forever to stop crying, but now I felt empty. Like someone scooped out all my organs and left a shell of me while the two talked to me. Talk to me? What a joke. More like talking *at* me. I had no say in the fucking matter. No say over my life or who I loved. Apparently, I couldn’t even choose my own biology because being gay was an ‘evil lifestyle.’ What the fuck ever.

Grandma took a sip of her beer straight from the bottle as she stared at me. “How could you let that boy pervert you? Hmm?”

I didn’t bother telling them Dillon wasn’t a pervert and that he didn’t corrupt me. They wouldn’t listen, anyway. It was like beyond their ignorant brains to even comprehend that I was gay and had always been so.

“And fucking Evelyn knew the entire time,” Dad said to her. “She fucking hid that shit from me. She’s no better than Dillon.”

“I warned you. I told you she was no good for you.”

“That you did, mama. I’ll listen next time, if there is a next time.”

My rage suddenly overflowed. “What a joke. Evelyn was the best thing that ever happened to you! She’s kind, sweet, and loving. She’s been more of a parent to me than you ever were!” It was better to be angry than crying or empty. Anger would keep me going until I got to see Dillon again.

“You ungrateful little—”

“I got this, Mama.”

“Listen up, Cade. You’re going to start a new school. Next fall, you’ll resume your running. I expect you to continue to get good grades, and date if you must, but it will only be with girls. You will not call that pervert. You will not go see him. If I find out you did, I will sue her, get a restraining order, and have Dillon arrested for raping you.”

“He didn’t rape me! Fuck, you’re sick!”

“Says you. You’re just confused, son.”

I was living in a literal hell*.*

“And when you start college, I better not find him attending. If so, I will pull you out of there and force you to go to a new school. If you refuse, I will stop paying for your tuition. And we need to talk about this gay thing. I don’t know how you let him corrupt you, but you put it out of your mind right now. No more boys. No more looking, kissing, or doing other perverted things with other boys. If all else fails, it’s off to military school for you. Is that understood?”

“Whatever.”

“I’m doing this for your own good because I love you, son.”

I looked at him and my grandma, letting the anger fester. “Lies! You never loved me. Neither of you. I see everything so clearly now. You’ve always blamed me for her leaving.”

I stood up and stormed out of the kitchen and into my new bedroom, ignoring them calling me back, and slamming the door behind me.

After turning out my lights, I fell into my new and unfamiliar bed that was so different compared to my old one. It was hard and lumpy. If Dillon didn’t find a way to find me or bring me back, I’d die. I couldn’t imagine a life without him, the farm, Evelyn, Athena, Tilly. No more secret kisses behind the barn or camping out at our favorite spot. No more having school lunch together.

Soon, I fell asleep from emotional exhaustion.

\* \* \*

By Tuesday, Dad already had me in a new school. Heritage High School. It was a strange place, with strange school colors, and I didn’t know fucking anyone. School would be over in three months, but Dad wouldn’t even let me finish up in my old school. I’d hoped Dillon and I could’ve met up at lunch, but nope. Dad had everything figured out.

At least it was close enough that I could walk and not take the bus. But not even that was a silver lining. And Dad wouldn’t let me drive because he feared I’d go see Dillon. He wasn’t wrong.

When I walked into English class, the stupid teacher made me stand in front of everyone while she introduced me and made a scene about how everyone needed to be nice to the new kid, and blah blah fucking blah.

I sat down at the only available desk and pulled out my notebook. When I opened it, my breath caught. I’d been studying some vocabulary last week and Dillon thought it’d be cute to draw silly faces. He was always drawing on my homework and other notebooks like he was trying to remind me of his presence even if he wasn’t around. Then there was one with a heart that had our initials inside it. My finger traced the heart as my eyes threatened to leak again.

I took a shuddered breath. *Not here*. God, if I cried in front of the students, they’d eat me alive.

“Hey, those are cool doodles. Did you draw them?”

I glanced at the emo girl with dyed black hair, black makeup, black clothes, with tattoos and piercings on her nose, lip, and eyebrow.

“No.”

I slammed my notebook shut. The pictures were for me only.

She shrugged and went back to paying attention to class.

When lunch came, I tried not to think about the painful ache in my stomach and heart. I picked a place in the far corner of the cafeteria that didn't have any kids and sat down, pulling out my sack lunch. Inside was some crappy prepared lunch thing of cheese, crackers, and salami. I shove it back in the bag and pulled out my notebook again.

I sat cross-legged on the chair and rested my forehead on the table as I looked through my notebook on my lap, touching all the doodles. My eyes watered again when I saw he wrote, *‘I love you. You’re my home. Forever.’* When did he write that one? I didn’t remember. Maybe he wrote it as a surprise for me to find.

I choked back a sob, but it was so fucking hard. I was still so raw from missing him, making love to him, and all that had happened. Was it really only two days ago? Was this what grieving felt like?

Someone put their books on the table and sat down across from me, but I ignored them. There was no way in hell I wanted to make new friends. Just get me through the rest of the year, and hopefully, things would settle down, and I could go back to my old school, and back to Dillon.

“You’re that new kid.”

“Yeah, no shit.”

Apparently, my bad attitude didn’t deter her.

“It’s unusual for someone to start school just a few months before it’s over.”

I still wouldn’t look up, tracing his words over and over with my finger. “Life is fucking unusual.”

“Isn’t that the truth?”

I finally looked up to see my tattooed English class classmate and scowled. “Just… leave me alone.”

“No can do. You got some serious bad vibes going on. You need a friend, Cade Beckman.”

“I don’t need fucking anyone.”

“Of course you do.”

“I’m sorry, but is there a reason you keep contradicting me? I know myself better than you.”

“You’re Cade, district cross country champion three years running. Pun intended. Attended Loudoun County High School.”

“Knowing *of* me isn’t the same as knowing me.”

“True.”

God, why couldn’t she just leave me alone? I scanned the cafeteria, but it was packed, and no other empty tables.

“I’m Raven.”

I sighed. “Of course you are. Goes perfectly with your emo vibe.”

She reached out a hand and touched mine resting on the table. “I know when someone is hurting. My mom has a lot of mental breaks. She’s had a rough life, but she’s a good soul. You *need* a friend.”

I gave in to my fate and humored her. I guess it was better than wallowing alone, so I pulled out my notebook and showed her the doodles Dillon did. One thing I also decided not to do anymore was hide my sexuality. Dad already knew, despite being in fucking denial, so it couldn’t get any worse.

“My… boyfriend drew these.”

She took my notebook and looked through all the images with a small smile on her face. “They’re really good.” Then she put her hand on her heart. “Oh. My. God. How cute is that? And I don’t even *do* cute. *‘I love you. You’re my home. Forever.’*”

Raven handed me my book back. “Yeah, he’s pretty amazing.” Interesting that she didn’t even comment on my sexuality at all. I liked that. As if Dillon and I were just two normal boys and there was nothing wrong with being boyfriends.

“Are you going to tell me why you’re here?”

“Fine. Dad found out I was gay. He blamed my boyfriend for it and took me out of my old school. He refuses to believe it.”

“Oh, lovely. You have a homophobic father.”

“Yeah. We’ve been together for three years and were so careful, but Dad found out anyway.”

“Wow, that’s a long time. No wonder you’re hurting.”

“I really don’t want to talk about this anymore.”

I shoved my notebook back into my bag and tossed my lunch in the trash. “See ya around, Raven.”

“Bye, Cade.”

\* \* \*

I pulled on my winter jacket and shoved my hat and scarf on. The same hat and scarf that Dillon put on me when we first met. He gave them to me because he was sentimental like that. At least Dad didn’t know. If he had, he’d probably burn them, like he probably burned those drawings Dillon made me. God, that one he drew of him and me from a selfie he took was so beautiful. If I had that, maybe I could get through each day without so much pain, but Dad ruined those, too.

When I stepped outside into the biting cold, I made my way back to my grandma’s small farm just over a mile away. Once I was away from people, I let my guard down and let the pain wash through me. My eyes watered, but I didn’t allow the tears to drop.

“Hey, Mike, that’s the new kid I was talking about.”

“He looks like a baby, and he’s about to cry.”

*Fucking bullies.*

No one messed with me when Dillon and Darren were around. Now, I had no one. Shit, this was why I had to get my emotions under control. Anger. I needed the anger. Anger at my dad, my grandmother, my situation, and my bullies.

I turned to face the two boys. “Got a problem?”

“Aren’t you that runner from the other high school? Are you going to run from us, little runner?”

I sighed. “And? Is there a fucking sign on my head that says, ‘bully me?’”

The two boys laughed. “Now, how did you know?”

The taller of the two was strong and boring to look at. But I was also so fucking over being afraid, and I didn’t even wait for him to hurt me. I threw a punch straight to his nose, jumped him, forcing him to land hard on his back, catching him off guard, and I beat the shit out of his face.

All my pain, loneliness, ache, and hatred festered in that one glorious moment as I made that boy fucking bleed and cry. The other boy yanked me off, and I took a swing at him too, but he ducked and raised his hands.

“Do you want some too? I’ll make you bleed and cry, just like your baby friend there!”

“Hey, it’s all good.”

“I’m fucking over you bullies. You tell your pals to leave me the fuck alone! Fucking losers.”

The other boy picked the bleeding bully off the ground and when they left, I turned to head to my grandma’s place, not bothering to glance back.

My hand throbbed, but I used it to keep me focused on my rage, which was so much better than my ache for Dillon.

\* \* \*

At dinner, I shoveled my food around my plate with my fork, resting my head on my hand. Two whole days without him, and it seemed like a year. We’d never been apart this long. Even when we couldn’t be who we wanted to be together, we always managed to touch, or he would doodle for me. Shit, even I was smart enough to sense the growing depression. I was completely empty and alone. Half of me was missing.

“Hey, what happened to your hand?” Dad asked.

I looked at my hand moving the food around, which was bruised and crusted with blood. I barely noticed. “It’s nothing.”

“You need to eat,” Grandma said.

“I’m not hungry.”

It was like my stomach was as depressed as me.

“I have a surprise for you,” Dad said, as if I gave a fuck about his surprises. He was just fucking sucking up to me, happy now that Dillon was out of my life and assuming I wasn’t gay after all. “It’s in your room.”

I put down my fork and sighed. “Can I be excused?”

“Aren’t you going to ask what it is? You used to love surprises.”

“I’m tired.”

I left the table before they gave their permission and went into my room. When I turned on the light, my stomach flipped upside down at the sight. What the ever fucking love was this new hell I was in? Was this his fucking surprise? Dad covered my walls with posters of half-naked girls with big tits and plump lips. Girls I had no interest in. Girls I wasn’t sexually attracted to. Did my dad seriously believe this would ‘convert’ me?

I fisted my hands as my eyes watered. I hated him. I hated him so much. Fuck, no wonder Mom left. All my life, I wanted my dad to love and respect me unconditionally. But no, everything was always on his terms. He would love me as long as I behaved exactly how he wanted me. But I would never be good enough. I wasn’t tall enough, big enough, manly enough. I was so desperate for him to see me that I became blind to the man he really was.

“What do you think?” he said behind me. “Looks pretty good, right? Just like a teen boy’s room. I had posters of girls all over my walls with my favorite sports team, rock bands. That’s how it should be.”

I rushed to one of them, and was about to rip it off my wall and shred it to pieces, but fuck it. If this shit got my dad off my back and left me alone, then I’d suck it up.

“Whatever.”

I plopped on my bed and hit the power button on my radio, but of course, Dad couldn’t take the hint. He came in and sat on my bed. “I know you’re still young, but you’re going to be a man next year, right?”

I ignored him, but apparently, my dad sucked at taking hints.

He pulled out a rolled-up magazine from behind his back and handed it to me. “This will really get you into those girls, as you should. They have no clothes on.” He gave me a wink that made me want to hurl. “Don’t show anyone, okay? It’ll be our little secret.”

What the actual fuck? Was he insane?

I put it on my nightstand. No way was I going to look through it. When dad reached for it, probably to show me his favorite girl, I said, “I’m tired. I had a long day.”

He patted my leg and stood. “Well, I’ll leave you to it, then.”

“Yep.”

When Dad closed the door behind him, I reached under my bed and pulled out a T-shirt. He hadn’t realized the day he caught us that I had put on Dillon’s shirt when I got dressed. I curled up on my side in bed and inhaled his earthy scent from the horses with some body wash, sweat, and laundry detergent.

As I held a piece of Dillon, I allowed myself to cry as I fell asleep.
