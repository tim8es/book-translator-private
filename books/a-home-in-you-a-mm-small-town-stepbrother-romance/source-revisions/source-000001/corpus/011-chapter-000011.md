## *Leesburg, Virginia - February 2017*

Fuck, I really needed to get away and get out. I pulled out a knit hat from my coat pocket and shoved it on my head as I stepped outside into the wintry afternoon. The sky was gray, and the temperature was freezing. The news said to expect more snow overnight, though we just had some a few days ago. I wished it hadn’t been Friday. If it were during the week, we’d get school off. I shoved my hands into my pockets with my pencil case and sketch pad tucked under my arm as I plowed through five inches of hardened snow crunching under my boots.

Tilly, my Blue Heeler who never left my side, walked next to me as I headed towards the red barn. It was one of my favorite places to be when I needed some alone time, which was pretty much always. Not that the house was crowded. It was only Mom and me living here. But the barn had been my special place since I was really little. A place where I could get lost in the animals or drawing. Sometimes I’d hang out in the hay loft and look out over the fields and woods. In the distance, the Blue Ridge Mountains loomed, looking like big, storming waves over the ocean.

When I walked inside, the horses greeted me with snorts and stomping of their hooves. While it was still cold in the barn, at least the wind wasn’t biting. Plus, we had a heater to keep the animals warm enough. The barn wasn’t freezing, but it wasn’t exactly warm either.

I petted the orange tomcat with a chunk of ear missing. He looked mean, but was as sweet as could be. “What’s up, Hawk?”

The tomcat rubbed his face against my gloved hand while Snickerdoodle, the calico, wove her body in and out of my legs, looking for pets.

After giving attention to the barn cats, I headed towards the first horse in the stall. We only had two, but Artemis was all mine. Mom bought her for me three years ago when she was just a foal.

She brought her nose out of her stall, reaching for me. I kissed her velvety nose and rested my forehead on her snout, inhaling her horse smell. When I first met the little baby horse, I kissed her nose. Now she wanted it kissed every time I came to visit.

I pulled out some sliced apples from my pocket, feeding them to her, though I’d already given her breakfast that morning. Artemis grew to be a big thoroughbred mare at sixteen hands and had a chestnut color to her. Her pelt was thick from her winter coat, and she wore the blue plaid blanket I picked out just for her. I loved her dribble of white on her nose like someone poured milk on it. But I especially loved riding her.

“Hey, Arty. How’s my pretty girl? When the snow melts, we can go for a ride again, and get you some exercise,” I said to her as she munched and crunched on her apple slice. I took another deep breath from her, loving the smell of horses. Some people hated it, but I loved it. That, along with Mom’s love of boxwood surrounding the farmhouse with its unique, earthy scent. The two mingling smells would always remind me of the farm and home.

Aphrodite, Mom’s black Arabian, must have sensed treats because she snickered and snorted at me, so I headed to her stall to give her some apples, too, but no kisses for her. She was a little more stand-offish than my Artemis, who was always calm and sweet. Aphrodite was a queen and my mom treated her like one.

Once the horses got their treats, I made a nest of hay in the corner of the barn, away from the wind, pulled out my sketch pad, and dug out a pencil from the pouch with my kneaded eraser. With my knees raised, I took off my gloves and started to sketch Arty’s velvety nose while Tilly curled up by my side, resting her head on my lap. I wasn’t the best at art, but I was still learning, and I found it fun to do, anyway. Mom said if I kept practicing, I’d get better.

It was hard to ignore the cold as I drew, shivering, and I kept wiping my running nose with the back of my hand, but I didn’t want to go back into the house. I didn’t want to meet that man and his son. So, I sat in the barn to hide and suffer in the cold along with the animals. Mom would find me anyway. She always did, but I really needed to be alone for a while. Soon enough, I’d have to pretend I liked those people when I didn’t. I’d have to smile and be polite to two people who were worming their way into our lives.

Why did Mom have to fucking date, anyway? We’d been doing just fine on our own since Dad died eight years ago. I barely remembered him since I had only been six years old at the time. Some brain thing, Mom said. We had pictures of him, but I was more familiar with the images of him than any real connection. I had no lingering feelings when I looked at his pictures. He became like an old memory, and the harder I tried to think about it, the less I remembered. I had flashes of playing ball in the backyard; him swinging me in the air, but not much else.

The barn door opened, letting in the cold air. I looked up at Mom walking in.

“I thought you’d be in here,” she said.

And there she was, finding me like always. Not that I tried that hard to hide. Plus, she knew all my secret places. Were they even a secret if she knew about them? Still, I wish she gave me more time.

Mom sometimes called us twins because I looked just like her. Her long black hair was shoved up into a knit cap and instead of a coat, she wore her thick down vest with a flannel shirt, jeans, and boots.

“Please come inside, honey. Jared and Cade are going to be here any minute, and it’s way too cold outside for you to be in here long, even with the heater running.”

Mom met a man a couple of months ago that came into her store to buy a birthday present for his mother. She showed him around, and they got to talking until he asked her out to dinner, and they’ve been together ever since. Last week, she only just mentioned about me meeting him and his son.

“I don’t want to,” I said, continuing to sketch. “Why do you need some lame boyfriend, anyway?”

Instead of getting mad, Mom walked over and squatted in front of me. “Dil, we’ve already talked about this. I wasn’t looking, but I found someone I care about, and he cares for me. I wouldn’t be introducing you to him and his son if it wasn’t serious.”

I looked up at her gray-blue eyes, which were also like mine. “But we’re fine with only you and me.” Mom and I were really close, and we did a lot together when she wasn’t working in her antique shop. Even then, I helped out in the store when she needed it, so we spent a lot of time together. We didn’t need anyone else.

She ran the back of her fingers against my cheek and gave me a sad smile. “I love it with just you and me, kiddo, but… I get lonely sometimes, too. It’s been eight years. When you grow into an adult, you’ll understand. Will you please give them a chance? Cade is your age. You could become great friends if you let him in and not hide away every time things get difficult.”

“What if I don’t like them?”

“That’s why we’re meeting. To test each other out and get to know one another. I’m meeting Cade for the first time, too. I’m sure he’s as nervous as you are. But I want you to know I would never choose Jared over you. You’re my son.”

“I’m not nervous,” I huffed, though I was a little. At least Mom would choose me over him.

“You’re always going to be my beautiful, serious young man. Nothing’s going to change that. I promise you.”

Was that what I was afraid of? That Mom would like Jared and Cade better than me? Maybe.

“What if… what if they don’t like me?” I didn’t have many friends because I was so quiet and introverted. Middle school was lame anyway, and I couldn’t wait for it to be over.

“Of course, they’ll like you. Everyone who meets you likes you when you don’t shut people out. You’re polite, kind, helpful, and smart. You love taking care of animals, and you go out of your way to help people in the shop. But if they don’t like you, then it’s over between Jared and me. I won’t accept anything less.”

Mom stood and reached her hand out to me. “Come on. Time to go in. It’s too cold and they’ll be here any minute. Let’s not be rude, Dillon. That isn’t like you.” She put on her Mom voice, making it hard to disobey her.

I grabbed her hand, and she helped me stand. I brushed the hay and dirt off of my butt and followed her back to the old stone house, which had been in our family for generations. Mom said it was built in the early 1800s, and I loved it here. It was surrounded by farmland, woods, animals, ponds, and rivers… everything an exploring kid like me could want. Even the mountains weren’t too far away.

We stepped into the mudroom and stomped the snow off our boots, then peeled off all our layers, hanging everything on hooks, and putting our hats and gloves in a basket.

The house was filled with warmth from the wood stoves and fireplaces throughout the house, even though there was a gas heater. Even my bedroom had a wood-burning stove. It also smelled like Mom’s roasted chicken. My stomach growled, reminding me I didn’t grab a snack this afternoon.

As soon as we stepped out of the mudroom, someone banged the knocker on the front door.

“Okay, they’re here,” she said, fixing my hair and trying to tuck the length behind my ears.

I brushed her hands away. “Ugh! Stop, Mom… It’s fine!” I hated when she babied me.

“You need it cut. Anyway, I trust you to be nice, Dil. To both of them. I understand you’re introverted, but try to talk a little with Cade, okay?”

I said nothing as she went to answer the door.

A man stepped in smiling and gave Mom a hug and a kiss on her cheek. He was a few inches taller than her, with brown eyes and regular brown hair. I didn’t know what she liked about him, but what did I know? I was only fourteen. Well, next month I would be.

The man held out his hand for me, and I shook it. “Hey, you must be Dillon. It’s nice to finally meet you. Your mom told me a lot about you. I’m Jared Beckman. What a big man you are, and don’t you look just like your mom.”

“Hello, sir.”

“Dillon, this is my son, Cade,” the man said, waving his hand behind him.

I hadn’t even noticed the other boy, but how was that possible? He stood out with light brown hair thick with curls and eyes a soft brown like his dad, but he didn’t look like him. He had a splash of freckles on his face and wore a really big smile with two big dimples, one on either side of his cheeks. Was it weird that his smile made you want to be his best friend forever? He was shorter than me, but I was tall for my age. Or that’s what Mom said, saying I had my dad’s height.

Cade was… pretty. I’d never thought I’d ever call another boy pretty, but I had no other word to describe him. It was the dimples and the thick eyelashes, and that unruly, curly hair. He looked good in his dark green sweater, too.

“Hey,” I said.

He gave me a brief wave and kept smiling. “Hey, Dillon.”

I could already tell he was the outgoing type. Shit, and here I was, the quiet type. We probably wouldn’t get along at all. Fucking great.

“This is Tilly,” I told him, determined to make an effort for Mom.

Cade squatted in front of my dog and let her sniff his hand. Then he gave her a pet on her head.

“She’s really pretty. What kind of dog is she?”

“Thanks. She’s a Blue Heeler from Australia.”

At least my dog liked him, but she liked everyone.

“Dil, would you and Cade mind setting the table, please, while I hang up their coats and get Jared a drink and dinner ready?”

“Yes, ma’am.” I led Cade to the cabinet in the dining room, where Mom kept all the fancy dining stuff. We usually ate together in the kitchen and I rarely set the table, so I knew she wanted the good stuff set on the dining table to impress her fucking new boyfriend. Whatever.

“I like your house. It’s really cool. I bet you’ve got secret places here. The old houses usually do. Is there an attic? Dad and I only live in an apartment. He’s on the road all the time, so he said he doesn’t want to bother with a house and a yard. Though, I spend most of my time with my grandma. She’s okay and kinda crabby sometimes, I guess. She lives on a farm, too, but her house is small and not as nice as yours. At least she has a horse and lets me ride it sometimes. I probably spend more time with her than with Dad.”

“Uh, okay. Cool?”

I wasn’t used to people talking to me so much, let alone verbal vomit all over me. And while I had a couple of friends in school, and we talked, it wasn’t like Cade, which was a lot. I missed half of what he’d just said.

“Cool? Yeah, I guess the horse is,” he said, shrugging. “You don’t talk much.”

“And maybe you talk too much.”

Admittedly, while his rambling was a lot for me, I found it strangely endearing, too. He reminded me of an excited, playful squirrel or something.

He laughed and shrugged again. “Yeah, Grandma says that, too. She says I wear her out, then puts me in front of the TV to play video games. I don’t mind, since I love to play, anyway. She recently bought me an Xbox for Christmas. I like to play Call of Duty.”

I said nothing as I opened a drawer and handed him the tablecloth and placemats, both in some flowery blue and white pattern. Mom loved blue and white stuff. We spread the tablecloth across the old and worn table. Mom said it was an antique, but to me, it just looked old. After we set the placemats down, I grabbed a stack of four dishes and told him to get the silverware out of the drawer.

“So, what do you like to do? I like to fish sometimes. Oh, and the horses, as I said. Reading is fun. I like fantasy stuff. You know, with swords and magic. Video games… Oh, I said that already. Uh… I like running and I listen to music. I’m taking guitar classes this year, which I really like. I guess that’s it.”

He looked at me as if waiting for me to do something. “Don’t you like to do things?”

“Oh, ah, I like fishing, too, and horses.”

“Cool. Cool. So, are you dating anyone? It seems like all the boys in my school are dating already.”

My face burned, but I shook my head. I hadn’t been interested in anyone at all. But I noticed a lot of kids in my school dating too.

He shrugged and gave me another big smile, and my eyes pinged straight to his dimples. It was like they completely changed his face when they showed up, like his smile and dimples made his face brighter somehow. Prettier.

“Nice. What else?”

I shrugged. “I dunno. Uh, I like to explore the woods, swim, camp, and stuff. And, uh, art.”

“Cool. We should go camping sometime.”

“Uh, huh.”

I guess Cade seemed to like me, so I guess that was a bonus. And I didn’t really hate him either.

We walked into the parlor room, as Mom called it, filled with big cushioned furniture, old ornate rugs, and lots of decorations everywhere, especially the blue and white kind. Cade and I found Mom and Jared holding each other and talking. Ugh. At least they weren’t fucking kissing.

“Mom, the table’s ready.”

She blushed and looked at Jared under her eyelashes. Jesus. I’d never seen her look like that. Like she was shy or something, and I’d definitely never seen her blush. I tried to keep from rolling my eyes.

“Thanks, honey. Are we ready to eat?”

“I’m famished,” Jared said, rubbing his stomach.

We all sat at the table while Mom plated our food of roasted chicken, mashed potatoes with brown gravy, and sautéed green beans with onion. Then she poured two glasses of red wine for her and Jared, while Cade and I had milk.

“This looks delicious, Ev.”

“Thank you.”

I ate quietly as Mom and Jared chatted about his job as a truck driver and all the places he gets to travel to. They also talked about Mom’s antique shop in historic downtown Leesburg. When they moved on to kid stuff and school, I tuned them out. It was all boring adult stuff.

When I looked up from my food, I caught Cade staring at me from across the table. “What?” I mouthed. He just shrugged, smiled, and kept eating.

“What are your favorite hobbies, Cade?” Mom asked.

“Well, sometimes when I’m with grandma, I get to ride her horse. That’s really fun.”

“Oh, we love riding around here, don’t we, Dillon?”

I just nodded.

“Maybe when you come back, you and Dillon could go for a ride on Artemis. That’s Dillon’s horse.”

“I’d love that, Ms. Copeland.”

“Please, call me Evelyn.”

“How about you, Dillon,” Jared said. “Have you and Cade talked about your interests yet? Do you two have other things in common?”

“He likes to camp and fish, too, Dad.”

“Excellent. We should camp out when the weather warms up. There’s a great place to camp and fish near Harpers Ferry at the border of West Virginia. The Shenandoah River has great fishing. I can teach you how to find nymphs, which are dragonfly larvae, by the way. They’re great for bait.”

Mom had taken me there a couple of times already, and I knew how to catch bait, but I didn’t tell him that. Mom liked the outdoors as much as I did. “Yes, thank you, sir.”

I wondered why he didn’t tell me to call him by his first name as Mom did with Cade.

“You’ve got a fine young man, Ev. Very polite.”

My mom smiled at me and winked. I wanted to roll my eyes, but I was old enough to know it was rude, and I’d get in trouble for it.

After dinner and dessert, Jared took Mom’s hand and led her to our parlor room, while Cade and I cleared the table and cleaned the kitchen. Doing dishes was part of my normal chores, but I liked that Cade helped without being asked, making it go by faster. He didn’t need to, which was cool.

When we finished, we headed towards the parlor to find Jared with his arms wrapped around my mom, kissing her.

*Oh, for fuck’s sake*.

They didn’t notice us, and I felt like I was intruding, but I couldn’t pull myself away, either. I didn’t like this man touching my mom. And I hated that she was dating someone.

“Ev, I realize we’ve only known each other for several months, but this feels right. We’ve got great boys the same age and I want to tell you I love you. Yes, already.”

“I feel the same, Jared. These past several months have been wonderful. Perhaps we should take it to the next level. What about moving in here and seeing how we all get along?”

*No, no, no.*

I did *not* want them moving in. This was my house, and I didn’t want to share. It was too soon. Shouldn’t we have gotten to know each other more?

I glanced at Cade, who smiled and shrugged, then he grabbed my hand to pull me away from our parents, with Tilly close behind. I had never held a boy’s hand before. Was it weird? Cade didn’t seem to care, but he did hold it first. Instead of pulling away, I curled my fingers between his, almost like it was the most natural thing to do.

“Do you got any video games? The last thing I want to see is my dad making out.” He shuddered for effect.

I blew out a laugh. “Yeah, I don’t want to see them making out either.”

He didn’t let go of my hand until we reached my room, as if he knew exactly where to go, then he finally let go.

“What games do you have?”

“Uh, nothing too recent. I don’t play all that much. I’ve got Skyrim, Dragon Age Inquisition, and a couple of other games. My mom gave me a gaming computer this past Christmas, so I usually only played on the PC.”

“Oh, fuck yeah!” Cade scrolled through my games on my computer after I booted it up and handed him a controller. None of the games I had were for two players, but he could play. I wasn’t in the mood, anyway.

“Holy shit, your Skyrim game is so modded. You’re going to have to show me how to do that. Can I play?”

I nodded and sat in my chair, then he squeezed in next to me while Tilly lay on my bed as she did every night. We were both skinny enough to fit, and the chair was big. But I wasn’t used to sharing my space. I’d better get used to it since they planned to live here.

“Man, I can’t believe your mom said we could move in. I guess the next step is they get married. That would make us brothers,” he said, as he made his Dark Elf character. “I don’t have any brothers or sisters.”

“Yeah, me neither.”

“It’s weird, right? But kinda cool, too.” He looked over at me with a small smile. “I mean the brother part. And we’re almost the same age. Having a mom will be weird, though.”

I nodded. “Yeah, if they even get married.” Maybe they wouldn’t work out. Then it could just be my mom and me again. Well, until she found someone else to date, then I’d have to do this shit all over again.

“My mom left when I was little. She didn’t want us, I guess.”

I looked at him, and while he focused on his character build, there was some sadness there, and I didn’t like it. I barely knew Cade, but already I’d rather see him smile and be happy. His brightness drew me into him. You didn’t notice how bright he was until he frowned.

“My dad died,” I blurted. Fuck. That was the opposite of happiness.

He glanced at me with furrowed brows. “Sorry, man.”

As I watched Cade play, I grew more relaxed around him. He was kind of funny, with lots of commentary and swearing. I even laughed a few times when his character was killed. He made it fun, and he was so fucking loud.

“Hey, boys,” Mom said, knocking on my open door.

“Yeah, Mom?”

“It looks like the snow hit early tonight. It’s snowing too hard, already dropping a few inches on top of what we already have. Cade can share your room tonight, if you don’t mind. Or, Cade, you can share the guestroom with your dad.”

“I can sleep in here on the floor. That’s cool.”

“Great, I’ll grab extra blankets and pillows for you. And more wood for the wood-burning stove in here. It’s going to get really cold tonight. Is that fine with you, Dil?”

I just nodded, turning back to watch Cade play Skyrim.

When Mom didn’t leave my room, I looked up at her. “Dil… do you want to talk? I know you heard me mentioning that Jared and Cade could move in for a while.”

“No. I’m fine, Mom.” And I really didn’t want to fucking talk about it, not that I had a say in the matter. She’d probably make me talk later after Cade and Jared left, anyway. But it wouldn’t change anything. I was going to be stuck here with a new man moving into my house with his annoying, yet cute son.

She left, then came back a few minutes later, carrying a bundle of blankets and pillows, dropping them on my bed. “I’ll let you two sort it out.”

Before walking out, she kissed me on top of my head as she did every night, but to my surprise, she kissed Cade on the head, too.

“Night, boys. Don’t stay up too late.”

“Thanks, Evelyn.”

He looked over at me and smiled. “Can you show me the horses tomorrow, since we’re staying?”

“Yeah, sure.”

Cade quickly grew on me, so I felt pretty good we’d get along. But I wasn’t so sure about them moving in. I was used to having my own space and being alone. But Cade was pretty cool, I guess. If Jared and Mom got married, having a brother might not be so bad, I hoped.
