# 25. Dillon

Every bone in my body felt like I got hit by a car as I got sacked and piled on. The opposing team’s defense was too fucking good. That was twice I got hit before I could toss the ball away, and my body was feeling it.

Then something snapped in my chest. It felt like a rib. A flash of light behind my eyes blinded me as the pain radiated across my body. Once the pile of bodies well over two hundred pounds each got the fuck off of me, I laid on my back, struggling to breathe as I tried to calm the pain.

Darren and my coach rushed to me and hovered over my line of sight with concern on their faces.

“Dude, where were you hurt?” Darren asked.

“Dillon, are you alright?”

They tried to move me, but I cried out. “Just… give me a second. It’s my rib. I… don’t think it’s broken, but it fucking hurts.”

After five minutes, Darren and my coach helped me up and walked me off the field while the game was on hold. The audience applauded because, apparently, I lived. Whatever. It was just a stupid injury. It happened in this sport.

They got me inside the locker room where the physiotherapist inspected my ribs and said it was just a fracture, and tightly wrapped up my chest.

“That’s it, Dillon. I’ll send Stu in to replace you.”

Stu was the second-string quarterback, and hell fucking no. This was the state championship, and I wasn’t about to sit on the sidelines for it. I’d come way too fucking far and suffered too much for this.

“Absolutely not. I earned this. I worked my ass off for this. It only hurts a little, and it’s the last game of the season. The last game of my high school career. Then I can rest.”

My coach looked at the physiotherapist. “What do you think?”

The man shrugged. “It’s a fracture, but he’s fine for now. If he takes another hard blow, who knows.”

Darren gripped my shoulder. “I’ll do better at protecting you. If my boy here says he’s good; then he’s good. Let him play, coach. We only have one more quarter.”

Coach nodded and gave the green light for me to keep playing.

Darren and I jogged back to the field to finish the game and win. We were ahead by only one touchdown, so it was tight, and I had to keep up our lead.

“You sure you’re okay?” he asked.

“Yes.”

I took the snap and trusted Darren and my team to protect me as I stepped back, scanned the field, and threw the ball long to my wide receiver, holding my breath. He caught it effortlessly and made the touchdown. My ribs screamed at me, but they held on. By the end of it, I was going to hurt, but I’d have the rest of the school year to recover.

The crowd went wild with the touchdown and again with the extra point. Now we slowly pulled ahead, giving us extra breathing room.

I sat down on the sidelines and chugged some electrolytes as the defense worked their magic to keep the other team from scoring. But I got lost in thoughts of Cade. Was he watching? Was he here? I doubted his dad took him to see us play since Heritage wasn’t playing. Plus, I was playing, and Jared hated me. The feeling was fucking mutual.

I really needed Cade here, and the only person keeping me going and trying to win this damned championship was Darren.

At the end of the final quarter, we’d won by seven points. It would’ve been sweeter had Cade been here to celebrate with me. Everyone wanted to party afterward, but I just wanted to go home, so I bowed out despite everyone’s disappointment. Plus, I needed to pack my body in ice. The only one I really cared about on the team was Darren anyway, since most of them gave me so much grief after Cade was taken from me. There was no love lost.

\* \* \*

I drove through the center of town in my old pickup after I closed Mom’s shop for the day. Once a week, I’d go home in a different direction and stop by places I thought a teen boy would work at like convenience stores, grocery stores, or coffee shops. But I couldn’t find him. I was in Leesburg, but he could’ve been anywhere around Loudoun County, which was too fucking broad.

All I knew was where he went to school, but if anyone caught me snooping over there, I’d be in big trouble.

As time went on, I grew numb, even more so as January came and went. Cade was now eighteen, and I hadn’t heard from him as we promised each other. That didn’t bode well. He probably got upset when I didn’t come to find him that day I played at his school. I wish I could’ve told him I didn’t have a choice. That I was desperate for him. That I hadn’t stopped loving him.

After a year, and plenty of opportunities, I hadn’t been interested in anyone else at all, only confirming my strong emotional bond with him. And I swore I would find him. Even if he hated me now, I’d find him and make him love me again. But him not showing up still fucking stung.

By the following Monday, I was in a mood. I was so fucking tired of trying to hold myself together. How could I when half of me was missing? I was tired of the fake smiles, pretending I cared about my grades or the school spirit.

Darren and his cute twink redheaded boyfriend sat down next to me during lunch. I couldn’t believe Darren hadn’t broken the poor guy yet by his sheer size alone.

Darren nudged my shoulder. “We’re going to a party this weekend. Jacob’s parents are out of town.”

Jacob was our wide receiver who helped us win the championship. Though we played well together, we weren’t exactly best buds either. Only Darren, and now Christian. “I’m not going to any parties.”

“You gotta. Come on, you’re the football star and state champion. People expect you and me to be there.”

“Do you really believe I give a fuck about these people? The same assholes who turned their backs on me after Cade left? No, thank you.”

Christian reached out and placed his hand on mine, ignoring the jealous huff from Darren. “You should go to show them that they have no effect on you. That you’re stronger than they are. If you hide, they win. This comes from personal experience. Besides, they’ve left you alone now, right?”

“Yeah, only because of Dare.”

“Come on, Dil. I don’t want to go without you.”

“Why? I suck at parties anyway. I’m no fun.”

“You better get used to them because there will be a lot of parties in college we’ll be expected to attend.”

I shook my head, tired of this conversation. I had no interest in parties at all. “My priority will be to find Cade.”

“And you’ll have a better chance at finding him through big parties.”

I stared at the ceiling and groaned. He was probably right. “Fine.”

“Yes!”

That Friday night, Darren, Christian, and I walked into the house blaring with loud music and drunken teens, most of them seniors.

“Wahoo! Dillon and Darren are here! Let’s get this party started!” someone yelled over the noise.

I rolled my eyes until someone shoved a red plastic cup full of something pungent. I took a sip of the sickening sweet alcoholic beverage.

“You two drink. I’ll drive,” Christian said. “I’m not much of a drinker, anyway.”

Darren kissed his boyfriend on the cheek. “You’re the best, babe.”

“You better believe it.”

I took another sip of my drink and winced. “Isn’t there some beer around here? This is disgusting.”

We all headed to the kitchen where they had a keg and poured ourselves each a beer, then wandered through the crowd. I guess most people were cool to me now. *Yeah, now that we won the championship*. How quickly they forgot about being such dicks. I hadn’t.

I really didn’t want to be here, especially without Cade. Where was he? What was he doing? Would he really not find a way to reach me? Did he forget about me? We had plans, and though I failed, he was to come to me when he turned eighteen in case I couldn’t reach him.

To sour my mood further, next week would be our one-year anniversary of when we lost our virginities. When we first made love. I rubbed my aching heart and chugged my beer, then poured another.

I lost sight of Darren and Christian, but I didn’t really give a shit.

After my third beer, I developed a pleasant buzz. People tried to talk to me, but I just wandered until I found a spot on a couch that had enough room for me and sat down. Again, people tried to talk to me, but I waved them off, uncaring if I was being rude.

Someone else handed me that nasty punch when I finished my drink, but my taste buds grew numb, so it didn’t taste so bad anymore. After that, someone handed me a shot that was bitter. I rested my head against the back of the sofa, enjoying my buzz as my mind blanked out at the spinning ceiling, listening to the thumping music. Weird. I’d never been drunk before, so I wasn’t sure what to expect.

“Hey, Darc, there’s our brother fucker. Told you he’d show up.”

“That you did.”

I raised my head to David and Darcy. “What the fuck you on about, *Davey*,” I said.

He narrowed his eyes. “It’s David.”

“Sure thing, *Davey*. Wait… what’d you just call me?”

He and Darcy snorted a laugh at my delayed reaction. “I can say it slower for you. Brother. Fucker.”

All my anger, depression, loneliness, frustration, and drunkenness culminated at that moment. I launched myself off of the sofa despite my screaming ribs, climbed the coffee table, and jumped David. We crashed on the floor as I threw punches at his fucking punchable face. Years I’d been dealing with this cocksucker. Years!

Every punch I aimed at him was in retaliation to every nasty word he’d ever said to Cade and me, and despite the pain in my hand and ribs, it felt fucking good.

“Bleed for me,* Davey.*”

We grappled on the floor, and he got in a few punches himself. “I’ve been looking forward to kicking your ass, Copeland.”

“Hurt him, David! Kick his ass!”

*Fucking Darcy*.

Kids were chanting, Darcy was yelling, and I let out all my pent-up rage. David’s fist slammed into my nose and I heard a crunch as things threatened to go dark, but I shook it off, rolled him over, straddled him, and threw punch after punch. My anger was blind to the pain in my face and hand and chest. To David’s bloody face. I no longer had Cade. He was gone, and I was forever broken, so I took it all out on David. He fucking deserved it, too.

Soon, he stopped moving, but I didn’t give a shit. Hands grabbed at me, trying to pull me off, but I wasn’t finished, driving by sheer rage. There was some girl crying in the background, but not even that pulled me away.

“I fucking hate you!” I yelled.

Strong hands yanked me off, and I tried to fight him too before I realized it was Darren. “I think David’s had enough, Dil.”

“So have I.” I shoved off my friend and stormed out of the party to walk home, uncaring of how far away I was.

As the adrenaline wore off, my grief increased, along with the pain in my body. Hot tears dripped down my cold face. I wiped them away, covering my hand in blood. I hadn’t even noticed I was bleeding before.

God, I hated crying, grieving, aching, feeling empty, alone, and missing Cade. Normal people probably would’ve moved on by now, but there was nothing normal about Cade and me. We were made to be together. No one else would ever do. I’d be forever broken without him. Incomplete.

A car suddenly pulled up alongside me and stopped. Darren got out and helped me in as I cried, laying in the backseat as he and Christian took me home.

\* \* \*

I sat and bounced my leg, looking around the office as my therapist made notes. It was comfortable and probably done that way intentionally. He sure had a lot of diplomas and achievements on his wall.

Mom insisted I see one after coming home a bloody, drunken mess after the party. Those hadn’t been the only things wrong with me. I’d been crying and grieving again. Something was wrong with me. Cade didn’t want me, and it hurt, but fuck, why couldn’t I move on? He sure as hell did.

Dr. Matthews looked over his glasses like some condescending asshole. I wasn’t sure I liked him all that much, but it was only my first session. After the first thirty minutes, I told him everything after he explained this was a safe space. I told him about my love for Cade, my stepbrother, how long we were together, what happened when he was taken away, and our promises.

“While it’s not unheard of for step-siblings to have relationships, it’s also not very common, especially with two boys. It’s clear you both established a bond early on and went from there to expand on that bond sexually.”

*No fucking shit, Sherlock*. I didn’t need college or a fancy degree to tell me that.

“Now, I don’t know Cade, but from your point of view, you feel you’re bound to him like a soulmate. This view keeps you from moving forward. Have you tried dating anyone else?”

I shook my head. “No, I think I’m demisexual. I’m not interested in anyone, and never have been, but being introverted, I never really established any other bonds except for Cade. So, it’s hard to say.”

“What about your friend Darren?”

“We have a good friendship, but it started off rocky. Plus, he has a boyfriend.”

“Losing Cade has clearly been hard on you, affecting your behavior which has become rash and impulsive, like getting drunk and starting fights. While Cade is not dead, you still grieve, especially when such strong emotions are involved, and when you believe you’ll never see him again.”

I sighed and rolled my eyes. How much did this asshole get paid for stating the obvious? “I know this already, Doctor.”

“You’re a smart boy, Dillon. Have you tried to make new friends? Surround yourself with positive people? Establish new bonds? If you’re demisexual as you believe you are, perhaps that is what you need.”

“I don’t want to. I only want Cade.”

“Dillon, it’s been over a year. He hasn’t reached out to—”

“What if he can’t? What if his dad is threatening him to keep him from leaving?”

The doctor made more notes. “That’s a possibility. But you should try to be realistic about this. Again, it’s been over a year. I think you should consider moving on. If he comes back to you, great. But if he doesn’t, you’ll be better prepared for it. You’re going to have to face the possibility he won’t return, or that you’ll ever see him again.”

My eyes watered, and I shook my head. “No. We made a promise. I’m not giving up on him.”

“This is bordering on obsessive behavior, Dillon, and it's concerning. It could lead you down a dark road if you never find each other.”

“I don’t care.”

“I can’t make you do anything you don’t want to. I can only offer suggestions. But you are suffering from some depression, so I’m going to put you on a low dose of antidepressants, and see how you do on them and if we need to increase the dosage or not. Even if you won’t move forward, I think you should still see me at least once a week to talk about your feelings about this, along with any other issues you’re having at home and school.”

I nodded and took the prescription he handed me, and then left.

No way in hell was I giving up on Cade. Everyone fucking gave up on Cade, and I refused to be that person, even if it looked bleak. If he was in college, I would find him, then convince him to come back to me. That I still loved him.

I had no other choice.
