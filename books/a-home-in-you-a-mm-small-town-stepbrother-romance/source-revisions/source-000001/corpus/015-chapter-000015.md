# 5. Dillon

After the wedding, Mom and Jared left for the Bahamas for a week, leaving us behind with my grandparents. I didn’t know them all that well since they left for Florida about five years ago, but they seemed nice enough. They were better than Cade’s grandma, anyway.

Cade and I hung out in my bed with Tilly sleeping at my side while Cade and I took turns playing Angry Birds on my phone. Mom said she’d get Cade a new phone soon, so I didn’t have to share mine anymore, but I didn’t mind. I liked sharing with Cade, which was surprising because I thought I’d hate him using my stuff after being an only child my entire life.

He had very little stuff when he moved in. I guess his dad didn’t buy him much, though his grandma bought him an Xbox to ‘get him out of her hair.’ His words. While cool, it seemed like a bitch-ass reason to get someone a present.

Angry Birds was an old game, but it was still a lot of fun. The squawking, pig noises, and funny music always drew me into the game. I loved it and could play for hours if Mom let me. But she wasn’t here, and my grandparents left us to our own devices.

When we got bored with that, I put on some YouTube videos. Cade moved closer to me as we lay there on the bed, sharing a pillow. I was too aware of his closeness to pay much attention to the gaming video we were watching. But if we wanted to watch on my phone, we had to get closer.

I noticed every movement he made, a brush of his curls on my face, a leg moving against mine. Again, I had that weird fluttering in my stomach I didn’t understand. Sometimes his being so near made it hard to breathe. It was so strange, but I didn’t hate it either. I imagined my hand running through his curls, or trailing my fingers along his arm.

I really needed to get out of my head about this. I wasn’t so sure I needed to have these thoughts about him.

“Hey, do you want to go bike riding tomorrow? There’s a cool trail that leads to a pretty big creek outside of Waterford,” I asked. “We can grab some sodas and candy at the country store there, too. Mr. Morris is really nice and sometimes gives me free bubblegum or a piece of candy.”

“Sounds fun,” he said, yawning. “Maybe the day after, we can go fishing? I haven't been in a while.”

“Yeah. I’ll take you to that pond nearby.”

“Does your grandad or grandma know how to cook fish? If we get some bass, we could eat them.”

“I don’t know. I’ll ask.”

“I need to go running, too. The idea of doing cross-country in school was a kind of last-minute idea. I love to run, but… I don’t know.”

“You want them to be proud of you.”

He glanced up at me and nodded. “Yeah.”

“I’m sorry your dad and grandma said those things.”

“It’s fine. I’m used to it.”

I huffed, feeling the burn of anger deep inside my body. That wasn’t something anyone should get used to. Fuck them for making Cade feel this way.

“We’ll go running together, okay? And instead of going to town, we’ll do that first because it’s important. We’ll go to town the next day.”

“Thanks, Dil. You’re the best *bro* I ever had.” He winked at me, making me roll my eyes.

“Dude, I’m the only *bro* you ever had, smart-ass.”

He laughed and leaned his head on my shoulder, and his hair tickled my chin as we watched another video. I pressed my nose into his hair without him noticing and quietly sniffed like a fucking weirdo. Who sniffed their damned stepbrother? It still smelled like our shampoo. I wasn’t sure what to expect other than a need to breathe him in.

Yup, I was definitely weird.

We stumbled across a viral video called ‘In a Heartbeat,’ an animated short story about a boy who was crushing on another boy. Again, my heart beat a little faster, and I held my breath as we both watched. The video felt… relatable somehow.

Cade said nothing about it as he moved just a little closer to me. It almost felt wrong somehow with Cade so close to me like this, but I liked it, too. What did I know? He was the first brother I ever had. Were all brothers this close? I had a feeling not.

I quickly switched the video to something funny, and after watching for a while, I glanced down at him to see he’d fallen asleep. His head rested on my shoulder and his body was curled and tucked into me. It felt right somehow. Like it was perfectly normal and natural for him to be right where he was.

I turned off my phone and eased out of bed, careful not to wake him. Then I put my phone on its charger, went to the bathroom to brush my teeth, and put on my pajamas. I climbed back into bed and found Cade had rolled over away from me.

*Should I wake him up to send him back to his room?*

It was a big bed, and it wouldn't have been the first time we shared it, so I shrugged and slid under my blankets, covered us both up, and switched off the lamp.

I woke up the next morning when the sun streamed in through my curtains. I always woke up early because I had to feed the animals as part of my chores. Mom hired a man to help clean out the stalls and move the animals to different fields, but she said by next year, I’d take over.

I blinked back the sleep from my eyes and rolled over to find Cade still sleeping and facing me.

Tucking my hands under my head, I watched him. His dimples were gone, only showing up when he smiled. But this was the first time I had noticed how full his lips were. Then again, I never really looked at his mouth before, except for that one time when we played in the snow. His mouth was parted open as he breathed, and his eyes fluttered behind closed lids and thick lashes. I really liked the splash of freckles on his cheeks and nose.

Staring at him reminded me again of how fucking beautiful he was. Like looking at a realistic painting, and you stared at it for a long time, wanting to see all the details the artist created.

But Cade was also funny, smart, and outgoing. I was pretty smart and got good grades, but I had little else to offer regarding personality, feeling I was pretty boring.

Cade’s eyes suddenly blinked open, and I quickly scrambled out of bed, stretching and yawning, pretending I hadn’t been staring at him like a creep. My heart leaped out of my chest and my stomach flipped around at almost getting caught.

“We need to get up to feed the animals, then we can head out and go for a run.”

“Did I fall asleep in here?” he asked with furrowed brows, looking around my room. Was he annoyed? Shit, maybe I should’ve sent him back to his room.

“Yeah, sorry. I guess I should’ve woken you.”

He gave me a sleepy smile. “Nah, it’s fine. I enjoy sleeping next to my ‘little’ brother.”

I rolled my eyes and tossed a pillow at him, which he caught and tossed back at me.

“Only by like a month, asshole.”

He snorted a laugh, then yawned. “Try a month and a half… *baby brother.”*

“I hate you.”

“You love me,” he said, giving me an air kiss.

“That’s it!”

Soon we rolled around on the bed, trying to pin each other down, laughing, grunting, and swearing. While he was smaller than me, he was surprisingly strong, especially in his legs. He kept squeezing the life out of me when he hooked his legs around my torso.

Cade smelled of sleep and sweat, but I strangely didn’t hate it. I probably smelled the same since we didn’t shower last night after the party.

“Say it! I’m the king,” he said, squeezing me tighter.

“Never! You already admitted… I was fucking king last night,” I gasped.

“You’ve been dethroned, asshole. This is a coup.” Cade made an evil laugh, making me giggle.

I fought to pry his legs apart, but it was no use. I guess I could’ve tried harder, but I wanted to give in to him, too. “Fine. You’re now King Cade.”

He let go, and I rubbed my ribs. “That’s more like it. It has a nice ring to it, doesn’t it?”

I got out of bed and pointed at him. “It won’t last.”

“Ha! Good luck with that!”

My grandmother came up the stairs and into my room. “Is there a herd of elephants up here or two boys? I can hardly tell. I’ve been trying to call you down. Breakfast is ready, and you need to feed those animals, Dillon. Hurry up now.”

“Yes, ma’am,” I said.

When she left, we both fought and raced to see who got the bathroom first. I won, of course.

\* \* \*

After breakfast and chores, we headed outside and stretched our legs. Cade had been researching how to cross-country train online while I fed the animals this morning.

“First, we probably need to figure out how far we can run before we start running through the countryside or in the woods. The point is to challenge the runner with obstacles.”

“Cool, so we stick to the road for now?”

“Yep.”

Cade sat on the ground, spread his legs, and reached for his toes while I did some lunges.

“I just want you to know that I hate running,” I said. “I’m only doing this because I like you.”

He snorted a laugh. “Well, I appreciate your suffering for me. You’re going to have to run too if you want to play football.”

“Yeah, don’t remind me. I’m so not a sports guy.”

Shit. I hated when he frowned. Every time he did, I wanted to go to those who always made him feel bad and punch them in the fucking face or something. In reality, I wish I could tell his dad and grandma to pay more attention to Cade and see how they hurt him.

“Then why did you agree to the football stuff? You didn’t have to.”

“It’s not that big of a deal.” I had to make him laugh or something. “Hey, think of it this way. Soon, I’ll be so strong, I’ll permanently dethrone you.”

There were those cute indentations. “Ha! I’d like to see you try.”

Cade stood up and brushed the grass and dirt from his butt. “Ready?”

“Yep. You take the lead and I’ll follow.”

We started at a slow jog, so we didn’t strain ourselves or get tired too fast. I knew that much about running.

The countryside was quiet except for the smack of our shoes on the road, the birds, and the wind in the trees. At least there we had a breeze. But the only thing I really focused on was Cade’s strong legs. I hadn’t noticed before, but I felt them when we were roughhousing. He also didn’t sound like a horse clopping like I did. Cade was as light as air on his feet.

His arms under his tank top weren’t thick, but they had wiry muscles. I wondered why I never paid attention to those things or even looked at a guy that way before. But all I could do was watch Cade run. He was rhythmic, and it helped me keep pace with him, and ignore the burning growing in my legs and chest.

As we got lost in our run, I remembered the video last night and how it affected me. When I imagined the two boys, I saw Cade and me. What did that mean? I shook the thoughts out and focused on running.

He was going to be amazing at cross-country running. I could tell. When he won his races, maybe then his dad and grandma would be proud of him. And I’d do my best to run with him and help him stay motivated. Someone had to have his back.
