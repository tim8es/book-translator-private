# 29. Dillon

The University of Virginia didn’t have the best football team, but fuck, winning my first game in college had been an amazing experience, and I still rode the high from it. Too bad it was at the expense of the starting quarterback, who was out for the rest of the season, if not for good. But who would've thought I’d ever love this sport?

The coach hadn’t planned to put Darren in the game yet, but Darren convinced Coach Evans that he was the only one who could protect me. And protect me he did. I didn’t get sacked once.

It was early evening as I rested my muscles in bed and iced my shoulder. Later tonight would be party time. Several fraternities and sororities decided to throw parties in celebration of our win, as if college students needed an excuse to party. I didn’t intend to go because it didn’t seem right. I needed Cade with me. Soon. I’d approach him soon.

I had the dorm room to myself since my roommate had a date, so I used the time to work on some drawings when my phone rang. I saw it was Mom, so I answered it.

“Hey, Mom.”

“Hey, back. How’s college life? I’m still coming to grips that you’re an adult in college. Where did my little boy go?”

I rolled my eyes but smiled. “We gotta grow up sometime.”

“Yeah, don’t remind me. How did your first game go?”

“I hate our quarterback getting injured, but I don’t hate that I was the one who got to help us win the game.”

“You did?! Wow! You’ve always been good at football. It’s the one thing I don’t hate Jared for. I realize he pushed you into it.”

Just the mere mention of Jared’s name sent me up in flames. Then they were extinguished with thoughts of Cade.

“Have you found Cade yet?” she asked as if reading my mind.

“Yeah…”

“But… you haven’t reached out to him.”

“No. I’m kind of afraid to. As you know, he never reached out to me when he turned eighteen. What if he feels I abandoned him? And I wouldn’t blame him. I mean, everyone else did. He probably hates me.”

“Oh, honey. I doubt he hates you. It’s possible he’s not happy with you, but you won’t know for sure until you talk to him.”

“I’m there every other day, watching him practice. I’m going to go to his races too. His first competition starts in October.”

“I am sure he would love to know you’re there, cheering him on.”

No, I didn’t think so. God, I hated that we were still separated. First, because of Jared. Now, because of my insecurities and fear.

“Dil, reach out to him. Tell him you still love him and tried so hard to find him. If you never approach him, you’re going to regret it for the rest of your life.”

Fuck. She wasn’t wrong.

“Okay. I will soon.”

“Good. Tell me how it goes when you do.”

“I will. Love you, Mom.”

“Love you too, honey.”

\* \* \*

Darren, Christian, Aaron, and I walked to Fraternity row to party. Being the starting quarterback now, everyone expected me to make an appearance. I didn’t want to fucking party, but when did I ever? The biggest thing that pushed me was maybe I’d get lucky and stumble into Cade. A few drinks in me could make me less of a coward and finally talk to him.

*Liquid courage, here I come*.

Christian rode piggyback on Darren as we headed toward the first fraternity house. I had no idea which one. Greek wasn’t my thing. While there was a party going on at the house, I couldn’t tell where the party began and where it ended with people in the street, yards, and inside. It was crazy, and I’d seen nothing like it.

We walked inside the first house blasting with music, colored lights, and all-around craziness. In the kitchen, they served jello shots, so we each had a couple, then grabbed a cup of beer from the keg. I never liked beer all that much, but this particular one tasted pretty good and smooth. And it went down entirely too easy.

We talked to some people about the game who congratulated Darren and me for an amazing game with lots of handshakes, back-patting, and more drinks shoved in our hands. I struggled to escape their attention when all I wanted to do was explore room to room and house to house looking for Cade, assuming he’d even be partying. But I was sure a lot had changed since we saw each other last. At least he did in appearance. Fuck, and how he looked like a man now.

I left my friends to talk with the people while I scoped out the house, looking for any sign of Cade. The only places I didn’t look were the bedrooms because I didn’t want to know. To find him with someone else would fucking annihilate me.

When I cleared the house, I headed back to my friends and dragged them away to the next house, which was more or less the same as the last one other than the buzzing and dizziness grew more in my head.

“Maybe he’s not here,” I pouted.

“Dude, *everyone’s* here,” Aaron said.

“Yeah, but Cade’s never been the party type.”

Darren patted me on the back. “I love you like a brother, Dil, but you haven’t seen Cade in a long ass time. You don’t know that. People change, and he lived with his piece of shit dad for too long. I’m simply warning you, he might not be the same boy you used to know.”

He was right. Who knew what his dad did to him or turned him into? And that was something I’d have to come to terms with. If we reunited, we’d have to learn each other all over again, if he’d even let me.

By the third house, I quickly gave up hope, and I just wanted to go home. I was getting too buzzed and tired of talking to people. While I got used to hanging around a bunch of dudes from football, being in a crowd like this for too long got overwhelming and stressful. I was still an introvert and needed my quiet, alone time.

I was also getting moody from not finding Cade. Did I really expect him to be at these parties? Not really, but I’d hoped. Even that diminished flicker of hope was disappointing.

“Guys, I wanna go.”

Aaron shoved another jello shot in my hand. “Not yet.”

I gulped the overly sweet watermelon and vodka shot out of reflex and shuddered.

“You’re not giving up now, are you?” Darren asked.

“At least for tonight. I know where to find him, but approaching him in front of his teammates might not be the best idea, especially if he wants to throw a punch at me.”

“Then let him throw a punch at you, and afterward, you can beg for his forgiveness,” Christian suggested. “If he lets off steam, he might be more willing to listen. Then you two can make up and fuck.”

I really didn’t want to get punched. Been there; done that. The fucking sounded pretty good, though.

“Just one more house, and if he’s not there, we’ll go. Okay?” Darren asked.

“Fine.”

“I overheard that the next house has got real drinks. Like you can get a mixed drink or something.”

Darren and Christian grabbed each of my arms and dragged me toward the next house. My brain spun and spun, but I pushed onward. One more house. If he wasn’t there, then I’d suck it up and talk to him on the track.

The music pounded in my ears straight through my body, making it hard to hear anything else or talk. That didn’t stop people from getting in my personal space and shoving their faces in my ear.

“Yo! It’s Copeland, and the man of the hour!” yelled a guy I didn’t know over the music, shaking my hand. “Nice play last night!”

“Thanks!”

“With games like that, maybe UVA will make it to the big leagues with the rest of the universities.”

“Uh, yeah, sure. I’ll try.”

While I enjoyed playing; I had no intention of going pro or making a big name for myself. I wanted to go back to the farm and live a quiet life surrounded by the outdoors with the boy I loved.

We headed to where they mixed the drinks. I had no idea what I wanted since I never drank them, but Aaron ordered a whiskey and coke for me. Whatever. I took the glass and a tentative sip. The strong liquor burned my throat and straight down to my stomach. Then I left my friends in search of Cade.

Once I stepped into the large sitting room, I sensed it. Was it weird to have that much of a connection to Cade, even after all this time, that I instantly knew he was there in that room? Only more proof of our fated and strong connection.

The room was crowded and dark, but I moved through the throng of people with the air thick with weed, beer, and too much cologne and perfume.

And there he sat.

My heart literally stopped at the sight of him. He wore a short-sleeved navy blue button-up that he had fully unbuttoned, exposing strong abs and tattoos. And did he have a nose ring? Hot. He smoked a blunt while taking sips of some alcoholic drink.

But Cade didn’t look like himself. Like he had lingering anger, simmering just below the surface, before he blew. You could sense his burning from here, despite him laughing. I always knew when he hid behind smiles and laughs. And he hid behind them now. I had to tread carefully. My stomach fluttered with fear and the only thing keeping me going forward was my heavy buzz, and being slightly dizzy from all the weed filling the room.

I swallowed my pride and forced out what little courage I had left, and walked toward him. He was talking to some black guy and that girl I always saw at his training. She better not be a fucking girlfriend.

Then I stopped in my tracks when he suddenly stopped laughing and looked directly at me. My heart flipped on itself and my hands grew clammy. Instead of surprise or happiness, his face showed nothing. Did he already know I was here at school? So he didn’t look for me after all. That confirmed it. It also confirmed that he probably resented me. I had hoped it wasn’t the case, but I kind of expected it. Was I prepared for this? Fuck no. Would it stop me? Hell no.

I took a few more steps inside, but he narrowed his eyes and stood. I thought he’d head my way, but instead, he approached a young man shorter than him with wavy, chin-length dark hair. Cade wrapped an arm around him and pressed a kiss to his head, all the while never leaving my eyes. He understood exactly what he was doing and tried to upset me. It fucking worked.

Rage simmered inside me because I tried to find him. I’d been miserable without him, too. And that dude did *not *belong to Cade. I did. And Cade belonged to me. No one else. Ever. And when the other guy touched Cade’s arm, I nearly stormed over there to rip his hand off.

Cade steered the other guy away from us to another room.

*Hell fucking no*.

I downed my drink and chased after them. We’d probably have a blowout between us, but so be it, as long as I got my Cade back. As Mom said, the only thing that couldn’t be fixed was death.

Cade and I still breathed and lived.
