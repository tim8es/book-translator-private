# 4. Dillon

Cade was so full of life. Like fucking sunshine or something. We hadn’t known each other long. Only a few months, but it seemed like forever. He sucked me into him, and I really wanted his outgoing vibe. We were so different, but we worked somehow. God, and I couldn’t stop staring at his stupid fucking dimples. Every time he smiled, my eyes laser beamed straight to his cheeks. Sometimes he’d come across as sad, but I hadn’t had the nerve to ask about it yet. Then he’d light right up again, hiding that sadness I wanted to take away.

Why did I even care? Why did looking at his dimples give me a weird feeling in my stomach? And why was there this need to protect him? And from what? I couldn’t figure it out, not even when our knees touched during the wedding service. I refused to move my leg away from him. He just made me happy. God, I’d never tell him that, though. How fucking embarrassing.

Did I like him? Like more than a brother? Was that what was going on with me? I’d never really liked anyone before, so I didn’t have much to go on. All that I knew was that I wanted to always be close to him and make him smile.

Once the wedding service ended, I was starving since we hadn’t had lunch. Cade and I rushed to grab plates full of food Mom had catered. We picked a spot in the grass away from others and stuffed our faces with roast beef, macaroni salad, and cinnamon apples, ignoring the mustard greens with onion and bacon.

It grew dark outside, but all the strings of lights through the trees lit up the entire backyard filled with boxwood, hydrangeas in purples and pinks, and some other plants I couldn’t name, showing off Mom’s gardening skills. Scents of boxwood and earth filled the air, and the darkened areas of the garden and woods were sprinkled with fireflies. Sounds of laughter, music, and eating made the night strangely comforting, especially sitting next to Cade.

“Do you think they’ll have any kids together? Like maybe we’d end up with a little sister or something,” Cade said. “That’d be fucking cool.”

That wasn’t possible. “Nah, Mom can’t have anymore. She said having me was rough on her body. They tied her… you know. What do you call them?”

“Eh… tubes? I think so, anyway. That’s what I learned in health class.”

“Yeah.”

“So, only you and me, huh?”

“Yep.”

“I always wanted a brother or sister, and now I officially got a brother today.” Cade gave me a playful shove with his shoulder, though we were already touching. We were always touching somehow. I always found a way, but I wasn’t sure what it meant.

“Honestly, I was happy with only Mom and me. We’re really close. But… I like having a brother now, too.” I looked at him underneath my fallen bangs, hoping I didn’t hurt his feelings. I just wanted him to know I was happy to have a brother. That it wasn’t so bad. Though I still wasn’t sure about his dad. He wasn’t very interesting and didn’t pay us much attention, but he seemed to like Mom, I guess, and she was really happy.

“Aw, I feel so loved,” he said, laughing and shoving me again. I pushed him back and soon our plates were put down and we were roughhousing in the grass, getting grass stains on our new suits. I finally pinned him down by sitting on him and started tickling him. It was the first time I’d thought about his tight body under me, feeling those growing muscles trying to fight me off. Why did it feel suddenly more than just roughhousing?

“Off, asshole!” he yelled and giggled at the same time.

“Say Dillon is king. Say it.” I wasn’t sure where this side of me came from since I’d never roughhoused in my life, but Cade brought it out of me. I just wanted to make him laugh and smile.

*Stupid dimples.*

“Dillon is king. He’s king!” he gasped through his laughs, trying to shove me off him.

“Don’t you forget it.”

He got up and wiped the grass from his pants. “Who the fuck are you, and what have you done with Dillon?”

I gave him a playful punch to his shoulder, and he punched me back. Soon we were rolling around in the grass again.

“That’s enough, you two,” said Cade’s dad, pulling us apart. “You two better not be fighting. This is a special day for Evelyn.” He looked back and forth at us and realized we’d been laughing.

“We were just messing around, Dad.”

“Good, because I expect you two to get along.”

“Yessir,” we both said.

He nodded and looked back at me. “Instead of roughhousing, why don’t you put that strength in a sport, Dillon? Do you have any interest in playing football? I bet you’d be good at it.”

I shook my head. “I hadn’t really thought about it, and I didn’t play at all in middle school except for what they taught us in P.E.”

“You should try it. It couldn’t hurt. Though I can’t get Cade to try out for the life of me.”

I didn’t like his tone. It was like his dad was angry that Cade wouldn’t play. Why did it matter if Cade played football or not? If it helped Cade and made Mom happy, I’d try. “Yessir, that sounds fun.” Fuck, it didn’t sound fun at all. Though I loved the outdoors, I had no interest in sports because that meant I had to interact with a lot of kids. Plus, in high school, they were serious about their football.

Jared beamed at me and grasped my shoulder. “Good for you. You should always try your best. How about when your mom and I get back from our honeymoon, we toss around the ball? See how well you do.”

“I’d like that, sir.”

He smiled and left us. I glanced at Cade, who had that sad face again. Dammit. Did I do something wrong? I probably shouldn’t have agreed. Shit. I was only trying to help, but I think I made things worse. “Sorry. I just thought… well, that he wouldn’t pressure you if I agreed to it.”

He hid behind his smile again. “Nah, it’s fine. I never liked football, anyway.”

“Are you sure?”

“Yep, totally.”

I watched him for a second to see if he’d get his sad face again, but he didn’t.

We finally finished eating and removed our ties and stained jackets. It was too humid out to wear any longer, even as the night cooled off.

Cade and I were the only kids at the wedding. Then again, the ceremony was super small. My aunt and uncle showed up, but neither was married. They both lived in California, and I barely knew them.

My grandparents came up from Florida. They retired down there, leaving the house we lived in with Mom, who was the only one out of three kids who wanted the farm to give me a happy place to be raised in. I hadn’t seen my dad’s parents in forever and I’d hoped they’d be here. But after Dad died, I saw them less and less. I wondered if they cared about me at all. I never asked Mom because I didn’t really want to know the truth. Though, they sent me a card with twenty-five dollars every birthday and Christmas.

Some of Mom’s staff from her antique shop also came.

Cade didn’t have much of a family, and his dad didn’t have any friends. I guess that happened when you were on the road all the time, but his grandma showed at least.

Speaking of which, she headed in our direction with a beer in hand, wearing a khaki skirt, some sneakers, and a yellow polo shirt. It didn’t feel very wedding-like, but it hadn’t been exactly formal either. Her hair was cut around her jawline, looking like a blond helmet, and she was kind of overweight.

“Are you boys behaving?”

“Yes, grandma,” Cade said.

Today was the first time I’d met her, and I didn’t fucking like her at all. She seemed mean, but Cade never really complained about her, so what did I know? Maybe she was just having a bad day. She probably didn’t want her son to marry my mom.

“Well, you look like a fine boy, Dillon. Bigger and stronger than our Cade here.”

I quickly glanced at Cade to see him trying not to frown, and I got the sudden urge to protect him. Why did his dad and grandma have to say those things about him? Why did they have to make him feel bad? I didn’t normally stand up to adults because Mom always taught me to respect them and be polite, but his grandma was mean. There was nothing wrong with Cade. He was perfect. Who cared if he wasn’t as tall or didn’t play football?

“Cade is still growing, ma’am.” So, I didn’t say what I wanted to, like tell her to fuck off and leave him alone, but I hoped I said enough to show him I had his back.

She looked at him up and down. “Yes, I suppose. He won’t be able to play football, though. His dad wasn’t big enough to play either.”

“I like to run, grandma, and I’m thinking about joining the cross-country team this year,” Cade said with this hope in his voice. I thought that was an amazing idea. Cross-country was seriously hard. I definitely couldn’t do it.

“Yes, running,” she said, but didn’t seem all that interested. “Well, I’ll leave you, boys, to it. Stay out of trouble.”

“Yes, ma’am,” we both said.

After Mom danced a few times with Jared, she found Cade and me eating cake on the steps on the side of the house that led to the sunroom. I didn’t like to think of my mom as looking pretty, because… Well, she was my mom. But she did. Her dress was lacy and in cream color. She put her black hair up in this weird bun thing with trails of curls falling. I’d never seen her so fancy before.

“There you are,” she said to me. “I’ve been looking for you to dance with me.”

“Oh, god, do I have to, Mom? People are going to stare.”

“They’ll stare because of how handsome you are.”

I rolled my eyes, and she laughed.

“Pretty please? For your old Mom?”

“Fine,” I groaned.

She put her arm around my shoulders and turned back to Cade. “And don’t assume you’re getting out of a dance with me either, young man.”

But Cade didn’t seem as bothered about it as I did. “You got it, Evelyn. We’ll show everyone how it’s done.”

I couldn’t help but snort a laugh, and Mom chuckled as we headed to the center of the garden. That was one great thing about Mom. She was nice to everyone. I knew she’d be good to Cade.

The dance was slow, and I didn’t recognize the song. I felt so weird, and I hated being the center of attention, like ants were crawling all over me or something.

“Thank you for humiliating yourself for your mother,” she teased as we swayed to the music.

I couldn’t help but smile. “Yeah.”

“Are you happy, Dil? I understand this has been a big adjustment.”

“Yeah, it’s fine, Mom.” I didn’t really like Jared, but Cade was cool.

“Do you like Jared, okay?”

*Not one bit*.

I shrugged. “He’s fine, I guess. You’re the one married to him, not me.”

She laughed and shook her head. “How are you getting along with Cade? You two seem to follow each other everywhere. And don’t think I missed all that wrestling earlier, and both of you ruining your suits.”

I smiled then. “He’s pretty cool. It’s kind of fun having a brother.”

“I can’t tell you how happy that makes me, Dil. I hope you two continue to grow close. I wasn’t as close to my siblings, and I wish it had been different.”

“I’m sure we’ll get along.”

“I’m going to miss you while Jared and I are gone.”

“I’ll miss you, too. Will you bring us back a present?”

“You got it.”

When the song was over, she pulled me into a tight hug and kissed my cheek. “I love you, Dil. You’re my everything. Now it’s time to embarrass Cade.”
