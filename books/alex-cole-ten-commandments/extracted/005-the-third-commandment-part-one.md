# The Third Commandment | Part One

Alex

---

The men brought me on board a large yacht, offered me a bottle of water, blankets, and something to eat. The food was left untouched. The water was downed in one go and by midday, a helicopter arrived and we boarded.

The Dubai skyline came into view not long after, then came the rows of rugged mountains and vast brown barren lands, before the enormous orange-gold dunes of what I came to know was the Sharqiya Sands of Oman. Finally, sitting in the middle of the desert, the glass box I called home for a month.

I could see the vastness of the compound from above. There was the large spectacular pool. The tennis court. A garden. The helipad. Luxury cars lined outside an open garage. A guest house separate from the main building. A few armed men paroled the grounds, speaking into their radios. It was too beautiful of a home to be in the middle of nowhere, but I was too numb to be in awe of any of it then.

We descended on the helipad, then I was escorted through the compound into the sleek, minimalist, ultra-modern home, curated and designed with Arabic accents. Almost every space was covered in beige, brown, and white, surrounded by glass walls. London seemed so far away as we walked through the hallways, up the stairs. The interior decor was unlike anything I had ever experienced. It was so foreign, so new, much like everything else that had happened since my arrival in Dubai.

"In here, Signore," the guard gestured into the master bedroom. I walked into the space and in an instant, it was clear the room was designed for Raf. It stood out from the rest of the home with its grey and black tones. "Let me know if you need anything. I'll be downstairs."

I nodded, turning to him. "Where are we?"

"Oman, Signore."

"Oman? Where is that?"

"We're still in the UAE, Signore."

Another nod. "What's your name?"

"Matteo."

"Thank you, Matteo."

He offered me a small bow before leaving me to the deafening silence.

It was so quiet, that for the first time in hours, I could hear my own breathing and heartbeat clearly. I walked across the large space and pulled apart the curtains, revealing a glass wall, beyond it, nothing but sand. Nothing but desert.

Stepping under the shower was liberating. It felt good to rid myself of the stench of blood and vomit, but then I couldn't bring myself to get out of the bathroom. I sat on the marble shower floor and remained there for God knows how long, rehashing my memories. I could still hear the sound of bullets, swords, flesh breaking, blood spewing. I could still smell it, that awful metallic stench of blood that made me nauseous. There had been so much of it. Too much of it.

The sky was dark when I finally left the bathroom and crawled under the heavy duvet naked, too weak to move even a single limb. Each time I closed my eyes, piles of dead bodies appeared. My mind was playing this wicked game of over exaggerating just how many people Raf had killed in a day.

Maybe it wasn't.

Maybe I was the one in denial about how many they actually were.

It was one of the many things that scared me about being alone. Being without him. I was afraid my mind would crack and deceive me, and I wouldn't be able to tell imagination from reality.

I needed to refamiliarize myself with the man I knew, not the who laughed about cracking a man's head open. The one who made the silliest jokes. The one who would say anything to receive a fragment of praise from me. The one who made my life so simple, so easy, the soft, that I had forgotten how to take care of my own self. I had forgotten how to worry. It was a strange thing to know that man, and then to witness him in his purest form and be crippled by the fear because of it.

I can wait two days, I thought. He'd be with me in two days.

But two days came and went, and there was no Raf.

"Why isn't he here?" I asked Matteo on the fourth day, the ache of his absence slowly making me crack.

"I can't say for sure, Signore."

"Get him on the phone."

"We can't, Signore."

"Why the bloody hell not!"

"It's not part of the code black protocol. We are currently under code black. That means we have to wait to be contacted first. No matter what."

My heart stopped for a moment. "Is something wrong with him. Is he... okay? Is he alive?"

"If he was dead, we'd be receiving a code white. He's not dead, Signore." Matteo voice was compassionate and calm enough to make me feel guilty about how harshly I had spoken to him. I exhaled, shivering, grateful that at least he could confirm that. "May I get you something to drink, Signore?"

"Yes, please. Bring me the whole bottle of the whatever is the strongest." I turned away from him and walked back to the lounger by the pool and sank into it, my gaze finding the far horizon once again as the sun set.

More days slithered by, and they were pretty much the same. Wake. Lie in bed and stare at the ceiling for hours. Around midday, alcohol until I was numb. Sometimes I showered. Sometimes I didn't. The same with food. Crawl into bed before midnight and fight sleep so I don't see the dead bodies in heaps.

Wash. Rinse. Repeat.

"Good afternoon, Signore," Matteo greeted, breaking me away from my half nap, half-drunk state. It was the end of my first week in the desert. The sun was high in the sky and I had already found my place under it by the pool.

"Not hungry, Matteo."

"Call for you, Signore," he said.

I sprang up, the glass cup nearly slipping from my grasp. "Raf."

He stepped forward and handed over a satellite phone to me. "Signore Sforzani, Signore." With a slight bow he turned away, and I stared at the phone in my hand for a moment before bringing to my ear.

"Dante?"

"Alex," his calm voice came through the phone.

I exhaled, my body shuddering. "Where is Raf?"

"Relax, Alex. He's fine."

"Where is he?"

"I can't tell you that."

"Why the fuck not?"

"Because telling you could jeopardize him."

"What's going on? What happened with the Yakuza?"

"Alex, I just wanted you to know that he's fine, so you don't worry yourself into a heart attack. This line will only be secure for a few minutes."

I sank back into the lounger, placing a hand over my forehead, leaving the silence between us for too long, my racing heart slowly finding its rhythm again.

Dante was familiar, he was the most familiar thing I had encountered in a week and I found myself, clinging onto the silence of our phone call.

"Alex."

"Yeah."

"Are you alright?"

"No," I answered after an exhale. "I'm... very drunk."

He giggled. "Of course you are. You've earned a few bottles."

Silence. Then I felt tears prickle my eyes.

"Dante." I hesitated for a moment. "I'm... scared."

"About Raf?"

"The guns. Since that night I... I look at them and it turns me into a nervous wreck. I think... I think it is fear."

It was quiet again for a time before Dante spoke. "I used to be scared of the dark as a kid."

"Find that hard to believe."

Another giggle. "It's like a lifetime ago now. Can I tell you how I got over it?"

"If it'll help me stop feeling so stupid and small." I drank from my bottle.

"One evening, after dinner, my father took me on a walk through the vineyard. It was a nice stroll. I was excited to spend time with him. We walked for about an hour, until I was exhausted and then he suggested we go sit by a bench under a shed. The shed was there, but there was no bench. Only the five feet pit he shoved me into."

My brows furrowed. "What?"

Dante laughed. "Dear father had very interesting ways of helping me get over phobias."

"I don't get it. How did that help?"

"He shoved me in the pit and he said, 'I am not raising a sissy boy. Get over it.' I remained in there for four days trying to claw my way out."

I sat up. "Jesus Christ, Dante. That sounds nothing like the man Raf described to me."

"Of course not. Raf was never scared of the dark. He was the thing in the dark. Father's golden boy."

The silence returned, and even in my drunken state, I became very aware of my body.

"I... I don't know what to say."

"My point is, I did make it out of that hole. And I was never scared of the dark again. His means were unconventional, but the lesson was frank."

"What... is the lesson?"

"Sometimes, the only way through is through. There are no shortcuts, Alex."

"Right..."

"Enough of the drinking, okay?"

I took a gulp from my bottle. "How old were you?"

"Six."

"Fuck."

"You're being dramatic."

"Must be the alcohol."

"I have to go."

"Dante."

"Yes?"

"Is he really alright?"

"Yes," he assured. "I promise he is. He's just..."

"Just what?"

"Give him time. Be patient with him."

It got easier after my call with Dante. I still saw the bodies each time I closed my eyes, I could still smell the blood, and when an armed guard crossed my path, the sight of their gun still made me shatter, but I was no longer crippled by it. No longer nauseous. I could push through. I could chew and swallow, and have it actually sit in my stomach without acid tormenting my throat for hours after.

The men kept their distance, barely speaking to me, save for Matteo, who emerged when he was needed and disappeared when he was not, but I did try to get to know their names. Their distance helped with the jitters I had suddenly developed around guns. That is until sometime around midday when my anger at Raf reached its peak and with it, the jitteriness soared back to the surface.

Be patient with him, Dante had said. And I suppose by the end of the third week, I had found my rhythm with patience, but I was still angry at him.

During the day, it was easy to keep that anger alive. I was determined to make sure it remained burning in my veins. I blamed him for all of it, replaying the look in his eyes when he pushed me out of the chopper. Raf had been so deceiving, so manipulative with his intentions, how easy it was for him to let go despite my tears, my pleas, how pathetic and small I had made myself for him. Maybe that was what I hated the most about it, how he had seen me, like something weak and feeble he had to put away.

I burnt with that anger during the day, and then at night, when I crawled into bed, and there was no sun to help flame my anger, the chill of loneliness hit me in the chest with a heavy pang and I'd run to his closet and find one of his hoodies, slip it on and fight back tears.

I missed him.

I missed so much.

I needed Raf to touch me. Hold me. I needed to feel his rings on my skin. I need him to undo it all with his kisses and his scratchy stubbles and his miserable jokes. It was a cruel thing for him to show me the worst of his darkness and then push me off a chopper and leave me to deal with the demons alone. At night, I simply needed him. The anger melted away and I became a weaker, even more pathetic shadow of myself.

I tried stroking myself in the shower for the first time sometime in the third week. I wanted to, in some way, take back my power, remind myself that before him, before Dante, before their sick twisted world, I wasn't just some helpless pretty boy that pinned over older men and waited around for them like a dog. My body was still my own, and so was my mind. I was my own person. I wasn't dependent on him for strength and validation.

It made me miserable when I couldn't get it up but instead ended up on the shower ground again, worrying about him. If he was safe. If he was well. Maybe he had abandoned me here because the danger was severe with the Yakuza. Maybe it was very bad. The last time we had gone weeks without seeing each other, he barely ate. I worried about that too.

Soon the worry turned into resentment. Resentment at myself for not being stronger that night. I had insisted on coming to Dubai, I had acted like I could withstand it all and proved to him the exact opposite. The resentment was the beginning of my determination.

I was drinking less by the fourth week and was now taking daily swims. It helped with anger. The sun was sweetly scorching, I had come to enjoy it quite a bit in those weeks. A guard came around the pool for a routine security check, and my body responded the way it usually did at the sight of his gun. My breath became labored, I become woozy, my heart race spiked.

I climbed out of the pool and stood there watching him for a time before speaking.

"Enzo," I called.

He turned and approached. "Signore."

"Do you mind?" I pointed at his gun. "I'd like a closer look."

"Signore... we're not allowed to—"

"I know what you're not allowed to do. It would be brief and you're here to watch me, right?" I managed a smile to put him at ease.

He considered for a moment before unholstering his gun and placing it in my palm. The anxiety made me blind for a second. My knees almost gave out on me as the nausea returned violently. I swallowed and swallowed again, my body shivering.

"Signore... are you alright?"

"Yes." I pointed the gun out into the horizon, my hand shaking, refusing to stay firm, warring with my mind.

When I placed a finger on the trigger, all the breath in my lungs vanished, then the blindness returned momentarily and with it, the ringing noise in my ear. The world was tilting underneath my feet, and I thought at any moment I would pass out.

"Show me what to do next," I managed to speak. "I'd like to make a shot."

"Signore—"

"Just do as you're fucking told, Enzo." I was impatient. It was as though at any moment, all my courage and determination would dissipate, and I'd tilt with the world.

"The safety is on. Here." He pointed, then showed me, helping my shaking hand steady until I undid the safety.

"Now what?"

He stared at me for a second, let out a nervous exhale and began speaking again. "Press down. Take your aim and—"

I pressed down on the trigger, blinking away when the bullet went off. The vibration in my palm damn near broke me. The ringing noise was back as I wobbled back, it seemed even the world was shaky. I could hear the men swarm the pool in heavy stomps, but it sounded like a distant echo for a time before their voices became clear to me.

"Signore! Are you alright, Signore!" Matteo's voice found it's way through the ringing.

I stared down at the gun in my hand for a time before lifting my gaze up and peering across the nine men who stood armed around me. First, I found their disgruntled faces, then I found their guns all unholstered and ready to assault. They all quietly stared at me, waiting for a response, and I was noticing that the guns did not make my stomach turn.

"It's alright, Matteo." I smiled at him, then at Enzo who now appeared startled and afraid. "Thank you." I handed him back his gun and tapped his shoulder. "Thank you," I repeated.

That was the first night I slept without the dead bodies in my dreams.

I woke with the rising sun. A quick shower and fresh clothes and I descended to the kitchen.

"Morning," I chirped to the three men walking around in the kitchen sharing soft laughter and conversation. Their gazes drifted to one another in surprise before responding to my greeting.

"Signore, you're up early," said Matteo.

"It's a beautiful day," I said, taking a seat by the kitchen island.

"It sure is."

"We'll go run a parameter check," Antonio said, stepping out of the kitchen with the third guard.

"Have the men push back the sand in the garage," Matteo instructed and he nodded, passing through the door.

"Are you guys always fighting the sand?" I asked.

"Sometimes, Signore. Would you like something to drink?"

"No. Some water please. And breakfast."

"Breakfast?" He made a face with the question.

"Yes, Matteo, you know, the thing people eat in the morning after eight hours of sleep?"

We shared a chuckle. "Yes yes, sorry, Signore. It's just... never mind." He turned to the fridge, never finishing the sentence. I knew what he meant, there was no need to push for an explanation. "I made the men pancakes. Was going to make some for myself."

"I'll have what you're having." I reached for the grapes sitting in the bowl. "Can I have these?"

"Yes, Signore,"---he offered me water---"They're freshly washed."

"So, tell me, Matteo. How does a man like you end up becoming part of the Italian mafia."

He grinned, cracking the eggs. "Blood, Signore. My father and his father before him. I never really thought of doing anything else."

"Why?"

"Italian fathers like having things their own way. But it never really occurred to me that I could be anything else. My family has always sworn loyalty to the Sforzani's. But I'm taking my own path with Signore Hale. Though I will continue to keep my family in Italy."

"Why Hale?"

"He's a good man. And he's fair. I'm happier under him. No one is left behind under Signore Hale. My sons will serve under the Hale bloodline."

I crushed the grape between my teeth and smiled. "Already planning generations ahead."

"A father must. I intend to have five sons. We have three so far. I'll give the stubborn ones the Sforzani bloodline, and keep the best for the Hale bloodline." He winked at me and we shared a laugh.

"How old are they?"

"Ah..." He dug his hand into his pocket and brought out his phone, turning the screen to show me lockscreen with his entire family on it. "This is Clemente. He's thirteen, the oldest. This is Michael, one of the stubborn ones," he laughed. "He's nine. This is Basilio, he's six and this is my beautiful wife Fiorella, the brightest beauty in all of Sicily. She's pregnant again. I hope for a girl this time before we try again for a boy."

"You married quite young."

"Fiorella was eighteen and I was nineteen. We met on our wedding day. My parents handpicked her by themselves. The best fifteen years of my life."

"Does she know... about you?"

Another firm laugh. "How else will a marriage last? I cannot lie to my wife. It would be like lying to myself you know." He stirred the milk into the eggs.

"She must be worried sick about you. We've been here for sometime."

"Yes. My poor angel. But I'm sure they've informed her of the code black. She knows I'm okay even though she doesn't know where I am. Would you like sugar in your pancakes?"

"Please."

He reached for it and a guard made an entrance. "A message, Matteo." He handed over a tablet and left.

"Well, Signore. It seems, I'll be seeing my beautiful flower sooner than expected."

"You will?"

"Signore Hale has sent word to bring you back to London. We leave in the morning."

With a slow inhale, my body weakened.

Four weeks and with one sentence it was over.

I left Oman with only the clothes on my body. Dawn was breaking when the helicopter arrived. Matteo handed me a new passport before we boarded. We traveled back to Dubai and once again, boarded a private plane.

"Signore Hale for you." Matteo approached with a satellite phone, a couple hours into the flight.

I stared back at him for a time before reaching for the phone slowly, bringing it to my ear as he walked away.

"Alex..." Raf's voice came through. Calm, gentle, a little scruffy.

My heart stopped, my breathing slowed, goosebumps scattered across my body. I was forced to squeeze my toes in my shoes.

"Puppy... I can hear you breathing," he spoke again and I covered my mouth, trying not to breathe. "Al—"

I hung up, shoving the phone to the side. The sound of his voice brought me back to that godforsaken chopper, watching him push me off. I was beginning to feel small again.

Throughout the flight, I convinced myself that the conversation we needed to have was better off in person, and I'd be ready for it by the time we landed. Once I saw him, the words would flow, everything would be easier. There'd be no tightness in my chest at the sound of voice, there'd be no jitters. Raf had this strange ability of making me feel larger than life, and I was certain. I just needed to lay my eyes on him. The sight of him would be enough. It had to be.

We arrived in London late in the afternoon, and I was welcomed by a slow drizzle of rain. It made me smile as we stepped onto the wet tarmac. I hadn't thought much about rain in the desert. For a month, everything had been sand and sun. London, by comparison, was beautifully miserable.

The sky was one uninterrupted sheet of grey, the air damp and cold enough that I felt it immediately against my face. I stood there for a second longer than necessary, letting the drizzle settle over my hair and shoulders before joining Matteo and the two other guards. Two Rolls Royce were waiting nearby, black and polished despite the rain. I climbed into the back, sank against the leather and watched Heathrow disappear through a window already speckled with water.

There wasn't much to see at first. Motorways. Concrete. Airport hotels. Warehouses. Gantries hanging over several lanes of traffic. We joined the M4 heading east, and for a while London remained somewhere ahead of us rather than around us. By the time we came off the motorway and followed the A4 through Hammersmith, the city had begun closing in. Buildings crowded closer to the road. Buses appeared. Cyclists slipped between lanes. People waited beneath awnings and at crossings with umbrellas angled against the drizzle.

I watched all of it.

And God, I was beyond grateful to be home.

We continued along Cromwell Road at the slow, impatient crawl that seemed uniquely London. Brake lights glowed red against wet asphalt. Water sprayed from beneath tyres. Tall white and cream buildings rose on either side of us, their windows catching what little remained of the afternoon light.

The car slowed again as we approached Hyde Park Corner. Through the rain-streaked glass, Hyde Park appeared dark green beneath the grey sky, its trees wet and heavy, pedestrians moving along the pavement beneath umbrellas. Then we swept around Hyde Park Corner and turned onto Park Lane.

Black cabs. Red buses. Expensive cars trapped in exactly the same traffic as everyone else. Old stone buildings disappearing behind trees. The dull glow of headlights reflected endlessly on the wet road. We didn't stay on Park Lane for long. The driver turned right onto South Street, and almost immediately the city changed. The noise seemed to fall away. Another turn took us onto South Audley Street, where the buildings became grander and the pavements cleaner. The frantic movement of Knightsbridge replaced by something quieter and more deliberate. Mayfair.

Even in the rain, it looked expensive.

Georgian façades. Black railings. Warm lamps beginning to come on behind windows despite the fact that it wasn't quite evening yet. Men in coats disappearing through discreet doorways. Chauffeured cars idling beside the kerb.

I tensed when turned into Mount Street, knowing now for certain where they were taking me now. The same place Raf took me for first date in the city.

The street was narrower here, lined with red brick and stone, the rain turning the pavement almost black beneath us. Shop windows glowed against the gloom. Umbrellas drifted past like dark little islands. Moments later, the car slowed and pulled towards the kerb. I looked through the window and there it was. Scott's.

For several seconds, I didn't move, only managing to swallow, that twisting feeling coming back.

The door to the restaurant opened. Dressed in a hues of black and grey from his turtleneck, to his trousers and long coat Raf emerged.

He stepped onto the pavement with his hands in his pocket, his gaze already softened, and mine softened the same. Relief washed through me, and I let out a breath, my body relaxing a fraction. I realized just how tense I had been for four weeks.

He was unharmed. He was safe. He had been all this time. I was grateful, and for more than a few seconds, joyous. That changed when he continued toward the car and my chest tightened again. I shook my head and he stopped walking, the little smile on his lips disappearing as his face found a new expression that pierced deep into me.

"Drive," I said, keeping my eyes on him.

The driver turned, lowering himself to catch the eyes of Raf.

Raf blinked. Hesitated a moment and then nodded. We held each other's gaze until the car turned the corner, then I felt my cheek wet.

"Where to, sir?"

"Home."

The city was a blur after that. My gaze pinned on the drops of rain cascading the window. I felt empty. Maybe a little small. It helped to pay attention to nothing. I had thought I'd be ready. I had thought it'd be easy. The fear returned and I panicked.

I retrieved the spare key from its hideout when I arrived home and made my way in, the soft scent of Mara's perfume welcomed me and then quickly following was her hasty footsteps running from her bedroom.

"Alex!"

"Hey, M." I hurried to her, squeezed her in my arms, and shut my eyes tight. "It's so good to hold you. I missed you so much. So so much."

She turned into her room and I turned into mine, tearing open the box. It was a brand-new iPhone. Laptop. EarPods. Smartwatch. All of it. And then a small note that read: "I'm sorry, puppy."
