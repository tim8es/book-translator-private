# Style Guide

Use this file to record evidence-based stylistic observations and recurring voice decisions for the book. Describe the source; do not invent traits that are not supported by the text.

## Narration

- Point of view: First-person, alternating principally between Raf and Alex; chapter/section viewpoint markers are explicit.
- Narrative distance: Close and immediate; the narration stays inside the viewpoint character's perceptions, reactions, memories, and judgments.
- Register: Contemporary, informal literary prose with romance/thriller/fanfiction conventions; shifts into blunt or crude language when the source does.
- Typical sentence length and movement: Alternates flowing descriptive sentences with short emphatic sentences and isolated fragments.
- Fragmentation / syntactic complexity: Frequent intentional fragments, one-line emphasis, and abrupt sentence breaks; preserve their literary function.
- Emotional distance or immediacy: High immediacy, often emotionally intense and bodily/sensory.
- Figurative language density: Moderate; romantic description and dark metaphor appear alongside plain action.
- Humor / irony / sarcasm: Character-driven, often dry, teasing, or dark; do not neutralize.
- Internal thought / free indirect discourse: Frequent direct internal reaction within first-person narration; preserve shifts between observation and thought.
- Meaningful punctuation habits: Dashes, isolated lines, scene separators, and short fragments are often used for pacing.

## Prose tendencies

Record only tendencies supported by the source.

- Terse or expansive: Mixed; descriptive passages can be expansive, dialogue/action can become terse.
- Plain or lexically rich: Mostly accessible contemporary vocabulary with occasional heightened romantic or violent description.
- Fast or meditative: Alternates reflective intimacy with fast suspense/action.
- Restrained or expressive: Expressive, especially in attraction, fear, jealousy, anger, and violence.
- Conversational or formal: Predominantly conversational, with situationally formal/security language.
- Repetition-heavy or synonym-rich: Meaningful repetition is used for emphasis; do not automatically vary it.
- Fragmentary or flowing: Deliberately mixed; preserve fragments and abrupt standalone sentences.
- Concrete or abstract: Strong concrete bodily and environmental detail, with recurring abstract reflection on love, danger, salvation, morality, and control.
- Understated or emphatic: Frequently emphatic.

## Character voices

### Character: Raf

- Formality: Informal, often terse.
- Vocabulary / education markers: Direct contemporary speech; no invented refinement.
- Age-coded register, if textually supported: Adult contemporary.
- Slang / profanity: Preserve when present.
- Verbal habits: Short commands, dry responses, controlled threat, teasing intimacy.
- Humor: Dry/dark and teasing.
- Typical sentence length: Often short in dialogue.
- Hesitation / confidence: Generally confident and controlling; preserve moments when that breaks.
- Forms of address: Preserve source-specific pet names/forms of address consistently.
- Notes: Narration is darker and more self-condemning than Alex's; retain moral fatalism without embellishment.

## Recurring stylistic decisions

| Source pattern / context | Target-language approach | Reason / evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spoken direct speech in dialogue | In Russian, introduce each spoken turn with an em dash `—`; do not enclose direct speech in quotation marks. Use standard Russian dialogue punctuation around author tags. | Explicit user requirement for this translation. |
| Intentional sentence fragments / isolated lines | Preserve as fragments/standalone lines when natural Russian permits. | Source uses fragmentation for rhythm and emphasis. |
| Profanity, sexual language, violence, gore | Translate at equivalent intensity; do not sanitize or intensify. | Required by source fidelity and the literary contract. |
| Chapter/POV markers | Preserve and translate headings/markers consistently. | Structural and viewpoint information. |
| Scene separators such as `---`, `-`, or isolated dash lines | Preserve a visible separator rather than silently deleting it. | Source uses them as meaningful pacing/section markers. |
| Italian dialogue followed by an explicit English gloss in source | Preserve the Italian utterance, then give the Russian gloss immediately after it, retaining the source’s speaker/action attribution. | Source deliberately presents both the Italian and its meaning. |
