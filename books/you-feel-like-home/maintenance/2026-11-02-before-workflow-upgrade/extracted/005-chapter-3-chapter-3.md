Caleb loves horses.

There’s something about the way he swings himself on the saddle that makes the rest of the world fall away. All the expectations, the cameras and the endless conversations about diplomacy, successions and appearances seem to just disappear.

There’s only his beautiful horse, the wind against his face, the rhythmic thunder of the hooves and the familiar weight of the polo mallet in his hand.

Out here, Caleb isn’t the fourth child of the Whitmore Family. Or the royal prince, or the family’s favourite disappointment who always turns into a perpetual subject of newspaper speculation.

He is simply Caleb. And he is very, very good at this.

He leans forward, as his horse accelerates, black mane whipping violently as the ball shoots across the grass ahead of him. Caleb follows and swings fast.

The crack of the mallet against the ball echoes across the field, and it flies through the goalposts.

The crowd erupts.

Caleb straightens in the saddle with a grin, lifting his mallet triumphantly as the cheers roll across the ground. From somewhere on the opposite side of the field comes a sharp whistle.

Caleb turns his head to find Eleanor grinning at him.
“Show-off.” She yells as he laughs.

“You’re only saying that because you haven't scored yet!”

Eleanor responds with an offended gesture before turning her horse back toward the play. Their younger sister, Cecily is considerably less restrained.

“Go, Caleb!” She cheers from a distance as Caleb takes another score.

This is what he loves about these days. For a few hours they are simply just siblings, not heirs or royals competing for the crown as the political pieces placed by their father.

The annual Polo and Equestrian Tournament has been a royal tradition for decades. Officially it's a sporting event, in practice, it's something much larger.

Half of London’s elite seem to gather on the sprawling grounds wherever the royal family hosts it. Foreign royals occupy the private viewing boxes. Ministers, diplomats, industrialists and aristocrats fill the grandstands alongside celebrities and influential figures who have managed to secure invitations.

The event is photographed from practically every angle, where every outfit is analysed, and every interaction becomes a gossip, and every member of the Whitmore family is expected to perform their part.

And Caleb is so good at that, he smiles for the camera, waves when expected, and charms people he has no intention of remembering.

Andrew, naturally, has declined to participate. He considers these tournaments beneath his time. According to him, spending an afternoon chasing a ball around a field is hardly an appropriate use of the future king’s schedule.

Caleb personally thinks Andrew could use a little less scheduling and considerably more sunlight.

Fortunately, his older brother isn’t even in London. He’s been sent abroad on a diplomatic assignment several days earlier, which means Caleb can enjoy at least one royal event without having Andrew’s cold stare follow him around.

His horse suddenly shifts beneath him, Caleb adjusts to it easily and then he feels something coming toward him fast.

Across the field, Matilda is coming straight toward him, and he recognizes the look on her face immediately. The smirk on her face is all too familiar, cause the woman is ready to best the entire crowd with one move.

She accelerates and Caleb quickly does the same.

For several seconds they ride shoulder to shoulder, each trying to reach the ball first, and then Caleb swings, and misses.

Fuck.
Matilda sweeps in, her mallet connects cleanly, sending the ball sailing through the goal.

The crowd cheers again, as Caleb stares in disbelief.

“That was mine!”

Matilda turns in the saddle laughing. “You were too slow.”

“You literally stole it from under my horse.”

“That sounds suspiciously like an excuse.” Matilda says, as she casually fixes her glove, still smirking, she raises the mallet in victory and cheers with the crowd.

“Try to keep up, little one.”

Caleb pouts as Matilda laughs and rides away. Caleb shakes his head, unable to stop the smile from returning. There are very few people in the world he would willingly lose to.

Matilda is one of them.

The match continues, until the whistle finally sounds for the break.

Caleb guides his horse toward the stables, breathing hard but grinning as he dismounts. The stable area is considerably quieter than the field. Servants and grooms move between horses, offering water and cooling them down while the players prepare for the next round.

Caleb loosens his gloves and runs a hand through his damp hair, as Eleanor appears beside him almost immediately.
“You’re on fire today.”

“Thank you.”

She rolls her eyes. “That wasn't a compliment.”

“And yet, I took it as one.” He winks at her. She laughs and gives him a solid thump between the shoulders, causing him to nearly stumble.

Just then, Paul appears before Caleb, holding out a bottle of water.

Paul has been assigned to him years ago, and has developed the unfortunate distinction of being the man responsible for preventing Caleb from doing anything catastrophically stupid.

It’s a losing battle everyday.

Caleb takes a long sip of the drink, before noticing the expression on his assistant’s face.

“Don’t look at me like that.”

Paul folds his arms, a crease forming across his forehead. “I haven’t said anything.”

“You don’t have to. I know that face.”

“And yet, Your Highness continues to give me reasons to make it.”

Caleb smiles, as Paul lets out a small sigh.

“Your Highness, I would like to remind you that last time you decided to perform one of your little stunts on horseback, I spent three hours explaining to your father why you thought jumping over a hedge was a perfectly reasonable idea.”

“It was a very impressive hedge.”

“It was six feet tall.”

“And I cleared it with flying colors. See Paul, you worry over nothing.”

“You nearly broke your neck.” Paul explains in a deadpanned expression.

“Well I didn’t”

Paul sighs again. “That is precisely the problem with you.”

“Relax, I’ve been riding since I could walk.” Caleb says as he takes another sip from the bottle.

“That is not what worries me.” Caleb glances at him as Paul takes the bottle back from Caleb, and brings up a towel to wipe it gently down his face. “You enjoy doing things that make me question my career choices, and age me faster.”

Caleb laughs. “That’s a sweet confession Paul.”

“Please…just behave.”

“I am going to win this tournament and steal Matilda’s victory right back.” Caleb says as he wears his helmet again, and grabs his mallet.

“I would prefer you remain alive.”

“Have some faith in me”

“It’s my blood pressure I’m worried about." he murmurs, causing Caleb to laugh again as he turns back to his horse. .

“Alright, wish me luck”

“Don’t die” Paul deadpans again.

Caleb swings back into the saddle again, as the second half of the game is about to begin. He guides his horse back toward the field, the noise grows louder as they approach, applause and music playing in the background.

He can already see the royal enclosure above the field. His father sits in the centre, surrounded by security, and a collection of important figures from across Europe. Caleb barely glances toward them.

Until something catches his attention.

A man is seated beside the King. Caleb slows down.

He can't see him clearly. The stadium is enormous, and the royal enclosure is situated high above the field.

Suddenly, Caleb can’t see anywhere else. There is a man sitting next to his father, in a black suit, and dark sunglasses. Everyone around him is talking, but the man isn’t.

He’s sitting there composed, perfectly motionless, as if the noise of the entire event exists somewhere far beyond him.

Something strange moves through Caleb’s chest, a feeling he can’t immediately place.

Then the man turns his head slightly as his father says something to him, Caleb can’t see it clearly but a feeling of familiarity washes over him. The angle of his face, the sharp jawline even though his eyes are covered by sunglasses, it’s still so vaguely familiar.

It can’t be.

Caleb’s heart stumbles.

Four months. It’s been months since Paris, since the explosions, and the gunshots, and since a stranger in black appeared from nowhere and dragged him away from death.

Caleb had stopped thinking about it, at least he had gotten better.

And God he still tries not to remember.

For the first few weeks, it was difficult, the black baseball cap had remained hidden beneath his pillow, he had told himself it was because it had been the only thing he had to hold onto during those terrifying minutes in the stairwell.

Eventually he had moved it to the back of his drawer.

He didn't think about the stranger every day anymore. At least that is what he keeps telling himself. But sometimes, late at night, Caleb still remembers.

The woody, smoky scent of his jacket, and the warmth of his hand around Caleb’s wrist. As though Caleb is something worth protecting, as though, for those few minutes, nothing else in the world mattered.

Caleb swallows, as the man nods slightly to whatever his father is saying to him. For one fleeting second, the sunlight catches the side of his face and Caleb’s pulse jumps.

It can’t be him, it absolutely cannot be the stranger from Paris sitting beside his father.

“Caleb!”

Cecily’s voice snaps through his thoughts. He turns, she is pointing toward the field impatiently.

“Are you planning to join us or shall we send someone to collect you?”

Caleb blinks.

Oh, right, the game.

Caleb looks down at his horse, and then back toward the royal enclosure. The man is still there.

He forces himself to look away as he nudges his horse and starts heading into the field.

His heart is beating too loud in his ear and he can't shake the feeling that somehow, impossibly, he just looked directly at the ghost that had been haunting him for four months.

Which is utterly ridiculous and Caleb convinces himself that his mind is simply just playing tricks on him.

The whistle sounds and Caleb rides on. But for the first time, he isn’t thinking about the game. The remainder of the match, he keeps missing shots, he would normally have made without thinking.

Matilda steals the ball from him twice. Eleanor scores while Caleb is looking entirely in the wrong direction. Even Cecily shouts at him from the other side to pay attention.

He barely hears her, or anyone. His eyes keep wandering back to the enclosure, even though from this distance he can barely see anything, still his heart isn’t in the right place anymore.

The match finally ends, and Caleb barely notices.

The crowd erupts around him, but the first thing Caleb does is turn his horse back around toward the stands. His gaze immediately finds the place where the stranger had been sitting.

Empty.

Caleb’s stomach drops, he stares at the vacant seats, before looking everywhere else. And then there, at the stairs, there’s a figure disappearing through the upper passageway.

Black suit, dark hair.

There are two royal guards behind the man, walking several steps away from him. Caleb’s heart begins pounding and he doesn’t think.

He simply drops the reins, dismounts and starts toward the stands.

“Caleb!”

Someone calls his name, he ignores them. He pushes through the crowd, eyes fixed on the staircase, but by the time he reaches it, there is no one there.

He goes up the staircase and through the door, but finds himself in an empty corridor. Caleb stands there, breathing harder than he should have been. He doesn't know exactly what he was expecting to find.

How ridiculous and bizarre it would be to find a man in a baseball cap standing at the end of the corridor.

Hey, you look like the guy who saved my life in Paris, are you him?

He is definitely losing his mind because there is no one here.

He looks back at the royal enclosure, but the empty chair stares back at him.

Four months of trying not to think about the stranger, and convincing himself that whatever happened in Paris was nothing more than an extraordinary night that he will eventually forget.

And now the memories have appeared again, as if they never really disappeared at all.

Caleb goes back to the enclosure, and stares at the empty seat. Then very quietly he removes his helmet and runs his hand through his hair as if that would shake the thoughts out of his head.

“What the hell is wrong with me”

Back in his room, Caleb is alone in his balcony, one hand resting against the stone railing as he stares out at the London’s skyline while the sun has almost set.

The city stretches endlessly beneath him, a sea of lights glittering against the dark. Usually, he loves this view, from this height everything looks smaller, but tonight, it does nothing for him.

His mind is still back at the grounds where the crowd was loud and beside his father sat a distant figure dressed entirely in black.

Caleb exhales slowly, watching the smoke curl from between his fingers.

He rarely smokes, but tonight he feels like taking one, just to quiet the thoughts running through his head.

Paul knows that, which is precisely why the soft knock at the door followed by a long, unimpressed sigh doesn’t faze him.

“Your Highness.”

He smiles faintly, “Paul.”

A moment later footsteps approach across the marble floor. Caleb continues staring ahead, taking another drag from the cigarette.

He barely has time to enjoy it before Paul reaches over, plucks it straight from his fingers and crushes it against the stone ledge. Caleb stares at him.

“Did you really just do that?”

“Yes.”

“You realize I’m technically your employer.”

“And you realize cigarettes are disgusting."

Caleb rolls his eyes and turns back toward the city.

“I am not in the mood for another lecture.”

“Good. Because I’ve given you the same one at least twenty times and you’ve ignored it every single time.”

A reluctant smile tugs at Caleb’s mouth. Paul stops next to him and folds his arm against the railing. For a few moments, neither of them speak, then Paul’s voice softens.

“You left lunch early.”

Caleb’s smile fades. “I was tired.”

“You disappeared before dessert.”

“Tragic, I know.” Caleb says, shrugging his shoulders.

Paul studies him from the corner of his eye. “Are you alright?”

Caleb doesn’t meet his eyes. He knows Paul can take one look at him and see something’s wrong. Paul is that one person in the palace who pretty much spends the most time with him, over the years they have developed a sort of friendship where they understand each other. Paul is probably the one person who has seen the cracks in Caleb’s very carefully built persona and never judged him for it.

“I’m fine.” Caleb says, quietly, but Paul doesn't stop.

“Have crowds become too much for you these days? You’ve been convincing everyone that you’re fine since the attack, but I’ve noticed the way you’ve started avoiding them”

Always so perceptive, damn you Paul.

The words settle between them, Caleb’s finger tighten slightly around the edge of the railing.

“Paul…”

“I know you don't want everyone fussing over you.” His voice remains gentle. “But you don’t have to pretend with me.”

Caleb swallows. “I wasn’t pretending.”

Paul gives him a look, that says he’s not fooling anyone and that causes Caleb to sigh.

“Alright. Perhaps a little.” Caleb turns around and leans back against the stone ledge, tilting his head up into the sky. “I just…I don’t know, I suppose I haven't quite shaken it.”

Paul nods slowly.

“It comes back to me in fragments, sometimes it feels like I am back there again surrounded by gun fire.”

“It must be a lot for you.”

Caleb gives a small shrug.

It’s true, that there are nights when a sudden sound outside his bedroom makes him sit upright before he’s even fully awake. Nights, when his mind drags him back to the museum, the gunshots, the screaming and the darkness of the stairwell.

And then there was the stranger, the only person in the chaos of that night who had managed to keep him grounded. Perhaps that was why Caleb couldn't seem to get the man out of his head. When he had been certain he was going to die, the stranger had somehow made him feel safe.

Caleb hates how his mind always returns to him.

He glances sideways at Paul.

“Can I ask you something?”

“Of course.”

Caleb hesitates, but then continues. “Do you think, it’s possible to meet someone only once ... .really only once…and somehow have that person stay with you?”

Paul doesn’t answer immediately. Caleb gives a quiet, embarrassed laugh.

“That sounded much less ridiculous in my head.”

“No.” Paul says. “I think I understand. ”

“You do?”

“My wife.” He says, smiling gently..

Something softens in Caleb’s expression. “Right…” Caleb chuckles. “You never told me how you two met.”

Paul laughs. “It’s a rather embarrassing story.” And Caleb leans against the railing silently implying that he’s listening.

Paul gives in. “Alright then, we met at a train station. We were on different trains. They stopped beside each other, and I happened to look out the window.”

“And?”

Paul lets out a sigh as he stares into the distance, a soft smile on his face, reliving probably one of his fondest memories.

“And she was sitting right across from me with a book.”

Caleb smiles. “That sounds romantic.”

“It wasn’t, I was staring, quite shamelessly, actually.”

“Ah, that makes sense. At least you're honest.” Caleb laughs.

“Her scarf slipped out the window, and I tried to rescue it.” Paul says with a sheepish grin. “I ran all the way down to the platform, picked it up and ran back to give it to her.”

Caleb’s eyes go wide. “Paul…you ran across an entire train platform for a woman’s scarf?”

“It does sound ridiculous, doesn’t it? I wasn’t thinking clearly. I handed her the scarf and she looked at me as though I’d lost my mind.”

“Very reasonable, you see a pretty lady and pretty much wants to run across the train station to court her.” Caleb laughs.

“Yeah well, then my train started moving and I wasn’t on it, so, like a fool I started running after it and she saw everything and probably thought I am a complete lunatic”

Caleb bursts into laughter. “Wait, so you missed your train because you were staring at a woman. Paul, you're hopeless.” Caleb is still laughing, his hand resting on Paul’s shoulder, while Paul just stands there with his cheeks red and a sheepish smile on his face.

“I spent the next year trying to find her, and then one day she showed up at a little diner, after that I decided I wasn't going to let her disappear again.”

Caleb watches him fondly, there’s something painfully beautiful about seeing that soft expression on Paul’s face. Something stirs in his heart and then he briefly realizes how he came so close to doing something as foolish as Paul today.

Running after some unknown stranger because he looked familiar. Whatever in the world he was trying to achieve by doing that?

“That’s got to be the most romantic thing I have ever heard.” Caleb tells him.

Paul shrugs.

"Love makes people do stupid things."

Caleb's smile linger and his mind betrays him immediately, before his thoughts wander off to the stranger, he looks away.

And then, Paul’s next question catches him off guard. “Have you ever been in love, Your Highness?”

Caleb’s head lifts. Four months ago, he would have laughed, and the answer would have been effortlessly, a no.

Now, however, the word refuses to come.

“I don’t know.”

Paul nods, as though that answer makes perfect sense.

“I suppose you don’t always know until it happens.”

“What happens?”

“You stop being able to control it. You don't plan for it, you decide you're going to fall in love with someone. One day, they are just simply there, and then suddenly they are everywhere.”

Clabe’s throat tightens.

“That sounds terribly inconvenient.”

Paul smiles. “It is.”

Caleb laughs softly. “And what happens when they are not there?”

Paul’s expression becomes thoughtful.

“You miss them.” The answer hits Caleb harder than it should have. He goes completely quiet and stares down at the ground.

How ridiculous it is that Paul is saying exactly the same things he feels right now for a stranger and calls it love. Because that man saved his life, and somehow, impossibly, Caleb misses him.

Paul reaches over and gives Caleb’s arm a gentle squeeze bringing him out of his thoughts.

“You’ll find your person eventually, whoever she is.” Paul says. Caleb glances up and raises his eyebrows.

Paul lets out a chuckle and quickly corrects himself. “Or he.”

A smile finally breaks through Caleb's expression.

"Better."

Paul chuckles. “Let’s keep this particular detail between us.”

“Oh agreed. I’d rather not have my father summon the entire Cabinet to discuss my love life.”

They share another laugh and for a little while, the world feels normal again.

Just then comes a soft knock.

Paul quickly straightens, when a royal guard steps into the room.

“Your Highness, His Majesty requests your presence in the Great Hall.”

Caleb’s smile disappears.

“Of course he does.” Caleb straightens his shirt, and runs a hand through his dishevelled hair to tame them down a bit. “I guess I am in for another lecture for skipping lunch.”

“Think you’re gonna need some good luck?”

“Yes, thank you Paul”

Although he wonders why his father is calling him to the main halls, usually it’s over dinner he gets an earful, but he doesn't think much of it and follows the guard outside.

Caleb immediately feels something is wrong.

The main hall is too quiet, usually, this particular room is always alive with movement. Staff crossing through adjoining corridors, guards standing at their posts, advisors coming in and going out, folders tucked beneath their arms.

Tonight, there is none of that.

Only the King, and Matilda wait for him.

Several royal guards are stationed around the room, but something catches Caleb’s eye. Near the entrance, two men stand apart from the others, dressed in attire distinctly different from the royal security uniform.

They stand perfectly straight, dark glasses concealing their eyes, their hands clasped neatly in front of them. Small Bluetooth earpieces sit discreetly in their ears, the crisp black suits making them look more like private security than royal guards.

Caleb has never seen them before.

He stops just inside the doorway as he passes them by.

Brilliant, his first thought is to ask whether someone had died.

The second is to turn around and leave, unfortunately Matilda sees him before he could do either.

“There you are.”

Her voice is gentle, as she smiles at him. Caleb smiles back as he walks toward them.

“You both look terribly serious. Should I be concerned, or have I finally managed to become the subject of a family meeting?”

His attempt at humour earns him a small smile from Matilda, but the King says nothing, his expression remains firm.

That’s when Caleb’s amusement fades.

Matilda gestures toward the chair opposite them. “Come and sit, Caleb.”

He does, though not without glancing between them.

“You look like you’re about to drop some terrible news on me.” Caleb says cautiously, looking straight at Matilda.

She gives him another sympathetic smile.

“Caleb,” Matilda starts. “We need to talk about the Paris attack”

The smile disappears from Caleb’s face, he leans back.

“What about it?”

“We’ve gone over the official report several times,” she says. “The security team recovered most of the sequence of events. They know where you were when the attack began, and they also know how you reached the exit.”

Caleb remains silent, Matilda watches him carefully.

“But there is one part, you very intentionally skipped.”

Caleb’s pulse stumbles.

“The man who got you out.”

Everything inside Caleb goes still. For one horrifying second, he wonders whether his face has betrayed him. And suddenly he’s forcing himself to breathe.

“I don’t know what you mean.” He keeps his voice steady, trying hard to not let Matilda see past his wall.

Matilda’s expression softens. “Caleb…”

“I was separated from the guards during the attack, someone helped me find an exit, it’s not a big deal.”

“Someone?”

Caleb shrugs, hand squeezing the handle of the chair. “Presumably a security personnel.”

“Caleb, there was another man.” Matilda says, leaning forward silently, tone gentle trying to get through him.

Caleb’s heart drops, as though something has physically fallen through his chest and landed somewhere beneath his stomach.

They knew, and they are just trying to coax it out of him now.

All this time, the one thing he had kept entirely to himself, the one strange, impossible memory he had been guarding as though it belonged solely to him.

But it’s not his secret anymore.

“How much do you know?”

The question slips out before he could stop it. Matilda exchanges a brief look with the King.

“Enough to know he wasn’t part of your official security detail. And we believe he may have saved your life.”

It all comes back to him again like always, the darkness, gunshots and the strong hand around his wrist. The stupid fucking hat that has spent four month hidden at the back of his drawer because he couldn’t bring himself to throw it away.

Caleb lets out a short breath. “I didn't think it was important,” he says quietly.

Matilda’s expression tells him she knows that isn’t entirely true.

“Perhaps not.” she leans back. “But the circumstances surrounding the attack have become considerably more complicated.”

Caleb raises an eyebrow. “How complicated?”

His father answers this time.

“The man who attempted to kill you belonged to one of the four powerful Sicilian mafia families.”

The room suddenly feels colder. Caleb blinks once, twice.

“You cannot possibly be serious?”

“Caleb…” Matilda speaks but is quickly cut off.

“You thought that was something I needed to hear casually over an evening conversation?”

“We just wanted to be sure of ourselves first.”

“No, I am just trying to understand how we have gone from someone who shot at me in Paris to Italian organized crime wanting me dead.”

Matilda’s expression remains composed.

“Which is precisely why we need to take this seriously.”

Caleb leans back into his chair, almost slumping and lets out a tired sigh. “This is wonderful.”

“There is more.”

Of course there is.

“You have two diplomatic assignments in Italy coming up.”

Caleb nods. “I know.”

“Your visit to Sicily is particularly important. Under normal circumstances, we would have relied on your existing security detail.” Caleb gives another nod urging her to continue.

“But now, we are adding someone.”

Caleb stares at her.

“Someone, you mean another body guard?”

“Someone who understands the people you’ll be dealing with, from the inside.”

Caleb is not dumb, he can put the pieces together and know exactly what Matilda is talking about.

“You want to put a mafia operative on my security detail?”

Caleb notices Matilda is looking nervous now, as she presses her folded hands together.

“Not quite.”

“And that sounds considerably worse.”

“The Conti family has agreed to assist us.”

Caleb stares at her. He has heard the name before, everyone has.

The Conti family is not merely a criminal organization whispered about in dark corners. They are old money, old power, old blood. Their influence reaches far beyond Sicily, and their reputation has survived generations of governments, rival families and shifting alliances.

Caleb rubs his forehead.

“I have enough guards.”

“This isn't about numbers, it’s about proximity.”

“Proximity?” Caleb questions. “What, you want someone following me around every second of the day?”

“The person won’t be your ordinary bodyguard.”

“That is hardly reassuring.”

“Caleb you need to understand….” but then Matilda is cut off as footsteps are heard from a distance.

Slow, measured. Coming from the corridor beyond the halls. The guards standing near the entrance, the same guards in suits, straighten up.

The King turns his attention as well, Matilda says nothing as she also stands up from her chair. Caleb glances up as the footsteps grow closer. And then enters the hall.

Caleb forgets how to breathe.

A man walks into the hall. Tall and impossibly composed.

Dressed perfectly in a tailored black suit, that seems to absorb light around him. His dark hair swept neatly away from his face.

No baseball cap. No leather jacket. No careless shadow obscuring him.

Nothing hidden or accidental, because the man in front of Caleb looks like he belongs here.

His expression is cold and unreadable, his shoulders straight, and his movements calm.

He stops right in front of the three members of the royal family, eyes on the King.

But Caleb doesn’t look anywhere else, because he knows that face. Not from the photographs, or newspapers.

From memory.

His heart begins pounding, and he doesn’t even care about the painful expression he’s wearing right now.

No.

It can’t be.

His mind rejects it before his heart ever has a chance.

Then the man’s eyes lift to the side, and meets Caleb.

There it is.

The same intense gaze, only now there is nowhere to hide from it. Caleb feels the blood drain from his face.

It’s him. The man who racked up his brain for months, whose voice has followed him into his dreams. This same man he’s been secretly, stupidly, hopelessly searching without even knowing his name, is now standing right in front of him.

Matilda’s voice comes from somewhere beside him.

“Caleb, I’d like you to meet Arian Conti.”

Caleb doesn’t hear anything after that, because Arian is already looking at him, and there’s a faintest flicker in those dark eyes.

Recognition.

Not surprise, or confusion.

He had known, of course he had known.

Caleb’s stomach twists unpleasantly, as he stares into the eyes of the stranger who has never been a stranger at all. And somehow, that hurts more than the assassination attempt ever had.

Arian inclines his head.

“Your Highness.”

The voice comes out smooth, and deep, and Caleb’s entire body goes cold.

For several minutes they just stare at each other.

And then…Caleb smiles.

It’s a smile he wears whenever something hurts too much to show.

“Well…that’s certainly one way to ruin a perfectly pleasant evening.”

Arian’s expression barely moves, but Caleb sees it, the small tightening of his jaw. And suddenly Caleb understands something else.

Arian isn’t pleased to see him either. That realization should make him feel better, but it doesn’t, not even a little.

Finally the King is the first to move from his chair, extending a hand toward an empty chair beside him.
“Arian, we are happy you could join us, please have a seat.”

Arian inclines his head, and then crosses the room with the same measured composure Caleb remembers from Paris. He takes the offered seat beside the King, settling into it as though he has every right to be there.

Perhaps he does.

Caleb hasn’t looked away from him once since he has walked in, not even when his father folds his hand and begins speaking.

Caleb notices how casually his father is addressing Arian, as if they had known each other for a long time, and it twists something ugly in Caleb’s stomach. His entire body is operating at a different speed from the rest of the room right now.

And Arian…
Arian looks perfectly and infuriatingly calm.

Caleb finally turns toward his father. “So this is your solution?”

The King frowns.

“Caleb…”

“You decided the best way to keep me safe was to assign another one of your guard dogs to follow me everywhere?”

“Caleb, watch your tone.”

But Caleb smiles, not particularly a pleasant smile. Matilda quickly intervenes before the King could.

“Arian isn’t a member of your security detail, he has an alliance with us.”

“What kind of alliance?”

“A transactional one.” Arian speaks suddenly, voice calm, composed and deep. Caleb’s eyes shift to him.

Then Arian leans back. “The Conti family has longstanding influence throughout Sicily and extensive connections with the other major families. I can provide the royal family with information and protection that your own security services simply cannot.”

Caleb gives a humourless laugh. “So you’re a glorified informant”

And something changes in Arian’s face, but Matilda intervenes, standing up to clear the tense air.

“No, he’s not. He’s the Godfather of the Conti Family.”

The words land heavily, and perhaps for the first time that evening, Caleb has absolutely nothing to say.

Godfather.
The word carries a weight that needs no explanation. He looks at Arian again, a thought occurring to him for the first time. How old is he? He certainly doesn’t look old enough to hold a position like that.

And yet, the title says otherwise.

The gravity of the knowledge slowly sinks in. To become Godfather at an age like his means Arian must have earned his place through bloodshed. He is powerful. Dangerous. And far more ruthless than Caleb had initially realized.

“You brought a mafia godfather into the palace.”

“We brought in someone capable of protecting you,” the King says.

Caleb laughs in disbelief. “From criminals, yes. And your solution to protect me from criminals is to assign me one?”

His eyes return to Arian and he deliberately lets the word hang between them. Arian doesn’t react.

The King leans forward.
“You don't seem to understand the seriousness of the situation.”

“No, I understand perfectly, you say it is a form of safety measure for me but it’s actually about protecting the Crown’s reputation.”

“Caleb…” Matilda starts.

“Isn’t it? Everything always comes back to the Crown.”

“Caleb that is enough out of you.”

Caleb ignores his father, he can't stop now. Not with Arian sitting there watching him, not when the truth is suddenly becoming unbearable.
“Tell me, Father,” he says quietly, “have you never considered what happens when you keep a wolf on a leash for too long?”

The room is silent, as Caleb smiles thinly.

“Eventually, it stops remembering who put the collar on it.”

“Caleb please…” Matilda’s voice comes out softly, almost brokenly.

Arian moves. The scrape of his chair against the floor is quiet, but it immediately draws everyone’s attention.

Caleb’s eyes snap toward him.

Arian rises to his feet, and for the first time since entering the room, something shifts in his expression.

It’s definitely not anger, but something colder, and controlled. His gaze remains fixed on Caleb.

“Allow me to speak, your Majesty.”

The King gives a small nod and Arian’s eyes are on Caleb again. Caleb suddenly understands why people fear this man. It isn’t because he has threatened people before, or belongs to a powerful mafia family.

It’s the controlled calm with which he stands and commands the entire room, completely comfortable even though he’s surrounded by the people who are supposed to outrank him.

“When I agreed to this arrangement,” Arian says, “I did not agree to become a servant of the Crown.”

Caleb’s expression hardens. Arian continues.

“The Conti family provides the Crown with access to territories, intelligence and alliances that would otherwise remain beyond its reach. And in return, the Crown provides us with political protection where our interests intersect. That is the arrangement.”

He looks toward the King.
“Neither side serves the other, we cooperate.” His eyes returns to Caleb, emphasizing the last word.

Arian steps away from the chair.

“I am not here because His Majesty commands me to be. I am here because I agreed to be.”

Something about that makes Caleb’s chest ache, because this isn’t what he wanted to hear.

He wanted to believe that Arian had been here for him, not because of some agreement, or the King, or because protecting the Prince was useful to the Conti family.

But because he had looked at Caleb that night and chosen him.

Caleb hates himself for ever thinking that.

Arian’s voice brings him back. “And while I am responsible for your protection, I will not be treated as though I wear the Crown’s collar.”

Caleb’s eyes narrow. Their eyes lock, neither of them refusing to look away.

“How noble.”

“I do not require your approval, Your Highness.”  And that does it. Caleb quickly stands up, pushing the chair several inches back.

“Don’t call me that.”

A slight smirk forms on Arian’s face. “Then stop behaving as though the title is beneath you.”

Caleb stares at him. The room seems to disappear around them. And then Caleb smiles back, sharp, beautiful, defensive.

“Don’t worry Godfather, I wouldn’t dream of making your job difficult.”

Arian’s eyes darken almost imperceptibly, the smirk still there.

“Good.”

Caleb’s heart stutters, because he says it in the same way he spoke to him that night, because despite  everything, his mind betrays him and takes him back to the man beneath the hat.

“That’s enough, Caleb.” The King speaks, voice lower, as if he’s almost tired of saying the same thing over and over again. He looks at him with the kind of expression that leaves very little room for argument.

“You will cooperate this time. I will not have you turning this into another spectacle because you dislike the arrangements made for your protection.”

Caleb opens his mouth, but the King raises his hand.

“This is your best option. You will follow the instructions given to you, and you will do so without causing another problem for this family. And if you refuse, Caleb, there will be consequences.”

Caleb knows better than to test that tone.

His father rarely threatens him directly, but when he does, it means the decision is already made.

The King turns toward the guards. “See that Mr. Conti is given everything he requires.”

Then without another word, he walks out of the room.

Matilda is the first to move after, she approaches Caleb slowly, her expression soft as she reaches for him and places a hand against his arm.

Caleb flinches, it’s barely noticeable, but Matilda notices it.

“Caleb….we had no choice….” she says quietly. “I had to make your protection the priority.”

Caleb stares at her, tiredly because he is absolutely done with everything at this point. The hurt is still sitting there, ugly and raw beneath everything else.

“Sometimes, you speak to me in that tone and I almost forget that, at the end of the day, all you care about is making Father happy.” His voice comes out deceptive, almost cruel.

Matilda’s expression falls.

But he doesn’t care, he is angry now, and sometimes angry people have a remarkable talent for knowing exactly where to put the knife.

“You're just like Andrew.”

The words land as Matilda’s lips part slightly.

For one brief second, Caleb wishes he could take the words back, but he doesn’t.

She lowers her gaze, taking the blow with the quiet dignity she seems to carry everywhere.

“I hope one day you understand how wrong you are.”

Her fingers give his arm one final, almost affectionate squeeze before she turns away.

She approaches Arian, who has been watching the exchange silently. “Mr. Conti, We are delighted to make your acquaintance. Our guards will escort you to your room.” She says politely.

Arian gives a small nod.

“Likewise, Your Highness.”

Matilda leaves before glancing in Caleb’s direction one last time, and suddenly there are only two people left in the room.

Caleb and Arian.

The silence changes immediately.

Caleb shoves both hands into his pocket, forcing his shoulders loose. He will not let this man see him rattled, not after everything.

“So,” he says casually looking him over. “What exactly are you?”

Arian doesn’t blink. “Your protection.”

“Very reassuring.” he gives a small humourless laugh. Something shifts in Arian, as a smirk appears at the corner of his mouth, and then he’s coming closer.

Caleb’s stomach does something deeply inconvenient, but he holds his ground. He refuses to step back, even when Arian stops barely a breath away.

Up close, he looks nothing like the man Caleb remembers from Paris. And yet, it’s unmistakably him.

Same eyes, same controlled presence.

The same man who had pulled him out of the chaos of the night and held his wrist while gunfire echoed through the stairwell.

Caleb swallows, when Arian stares down at him.

“You don’t have to like it,” Arian says quietly.

Caleb laughs softly in his face. “Oh don’t worry, I don't."

Neither move, as the silence stretches, and for one treacherous moment, Caleb forgets where they are. Arian is so close, he can smell that faint scent of warm sandalwood and smoke, just like back at the rooftop when his body had pressed so close to his, he felt the warmth for hours.

And then his eyes drop, only for a second to Arian’s mouth. He catches himself just as quickly and looks back up.

Too late.

Something in Arian’s expression changes, and Caleb’s heart stumbles.

He hates it

And he hates that this man is standing in front of him after four months of wondering, the mystery he had carried around like a secret has suddenly become a person.

And most of all, he hates how much he wants to know him.

And then, before Caleb could stop himself, speaks quietly. “You know, I spent a rather embarrassing amount of time wondering who you were.”

Arian says nothing. Caleb’s smile falters.

“I wondered if I’d ever see you again.”

There, it’s out, far too honest and for the briefest moment, something flickers across Arian’s face. So quickly that Caleb almost convinces himself he just imagined it.

He immediately covers his vulnerability with a crooked smile. “But apparently I was being far too sentimental.”

Arian’s jaw tightens.

“You’re simply my father’s very expensive attack dog.”

“I already told you, I don’t work for your father.”

“Hmm, that is a pity.” Caleb says, tilting his head. “I think you’d look rather good on a leash.”

Arian inhales, almost as if he’s trying to calm his nerves.

Caleb continues deliberately, enjoying the provocation now. “I could even have a little name tag made. Something tasteful.”

Then Arian smiles, but not kindly, something far more dangerous.

He tilts his head, closing the remaining distance between them by another fraction.

“Careful, Your Highness.” Caleb's pulse kicks in. “You seem to be forgetting something.”

“And what's that?”

“I may not work for the Crown.” Arian says, holding his gaze. “But I am the Crown's appointed enforcer.”

His voice drops lower.

“That distinction matters.” Arian smirks, as Caleb’s smile fades. “The King has entrusted your protection to me. That means that while you remain under my protection, you follow the security protocols I established. You go where I tell you to go. You stay where I tell you to stay.”

His eyes narrow slightly.

“And if I tell you to run, you run.”

Caleb stares at him, there is no arrogance in Arian’s voice, and that is the worst part, he isn’t trying to intimidate him, he is simply stating a fact.

Caleb doesn’t know when he has started to lose his breath, because he is trying to catch it now. “You don’t command me.”

“No.” Arian says as his gaze doesn’t waver, they have gotten so close that Caleb has to look up to meet his gaze. “Your father does. And because your father has placed your safety in my hands, you will obey me when it concerns your protection.”

And there it is, the loophole. The damn bastard is right and Caleb hates that he is right.

Not being able to handle it, Caleb looks away with an irritated huff and takes a step back.

“Obey you?” he scoffs. “I’d rather die.”

Arian watches him for a long time, then quietly. “I suppose we’ll see.”

Caleb looks back and Arian is already stepping away. He watches as Arian raises his one hand, signalling the two guards by the entrance who immediately approach him. They bow their heads and Arian turns toward them.

“We’ll discuss your schedule in the morning. Don’t be late.”

And with that he starts to walk away.

Once he’s gone, the room becomes unbearably quiet.

Caleb’s carefully constructed composure collapses, he sinks into the nearest chair staring at the empty doorway.

For four months, he'd wondered about a stranger.

A man he'd met for perhaps ten minutes.

A man whose hat he'd hidden in the deepest corner of his drawer because somehow possessing something that belonged to him had made Caleb feel less alone.

He imagined a hundred different possibilities. He never imagined this.

A mafia godfather.

His father's secret weapon.

His new protector.

Caleb leans back and closes his eyes, trying to make the tears go away but he is too overwhelmed at this point to stop.

“Fuck.” For the first time, there is no sarcasm in his voice.

Only heartbreak.
