## Chapter 5

Caleb suspects that The Royal Palace of Brussels is specifically designed to make ordinary people feel poor.

There is simply no other reasonable explanation for it.

Crystal chandeliers hang low from gilded ceilings painted with scenes of gods and angels, lights scattering across polished marble floors and catching on enough gold leaf to fund a small country. Towering arrangements of white roses occupy every available surface, while an orchestra plays somewhere beyond the adjoining ballroom.

Caleb has visited enough palaces and attended enough banquets to know exactly the kind of people who gather here. Presidents, ministers, diplomats and politicians, stand alongside aristocrats, whose family names stretch further back than some nations.

Waiters move effortlessly through it all, balancing trays of champagne flutes and elaborate hors d’oeuvres.

Everything is tasteful, yet everything feels suffocatingly performative.

Caleb smiles, because he’s exceptionally good at being performative. He plays that part very well.

His white suit has been tailored within an inch of its life, paired with a soft beige shirt left open at the collar, two fine chains catching occasionally beneath the light, despite the demands from his stylist to wear a tie, he felt like letting loose a little tonight . His hair had been styled before he left London this morning, though his curls have predictably begun to rebel before they even arrive.

He shakes hands, remembers names, congratulates the Belgian delegation on the newly formalised diplomatic partnership with the Crown. Listening attentively while an elderly minister explains an infrastructure initiative Caleb has already read forty-three pages about on the  flight.

And while he’s heavily engaged in socializing, his attention remains infuriatingly elsewhere.

Because Arian Conti has disappeared.

Caleb had seen him when they arrived. Arian stood beside the car as Caleb stepped out, dressed predictably in black, a perfectly cut suit over a white shirt, black tie sitting neatly at his throat. Tall, composed and offensively handsome beneath the entrance light.

Then as soon as Caleb entered the ballroom, Arian vanished. Now it’s been an hour and a half and Caleb hasn’t seen him since.

Which is exactly what Caleb had asked for.

You won’t see me, and you won’t know where I am.

Apparently, Arian had meant every word, Caleb has spent days insisting that if the man intends to follow him around Europe, he should at least possess the decency to make himself invisible.

Well.

Congratulations to him, perhaps the Sicilian underworld really does issue invisibility cloaks.

Caleb should be delighted, instead, he keeps catching himself searching the room more times than he cares to count.

Not consciously, just the occasional glance towards an entrance, or at the upper gallery in hopes to catch a glimpse of him. Knowing that Arian, wherever he is, is still watching from somewhere nearby, but Caleb didn't think he’d be this good at keeping himself hidden.

Caleb takes a sip of his champagne

It really shouldn't bother him, at all. He doesn’t give a damn where Arian is.

A tap lands against his shoulder. Caleb turns, and stops.

“Landon.”

Landon Reed smiles brightly at him.

“Your Highness.”

Of all the people Caleb expected to encounter tonight, Landon had not been among them.

He looks annoyingly good in a charcoal suit, with a pale grey shirt beneath, his dark hair styled neatly away from his face, and there is that smile. Warm, charming and painfully familiar.

Caleb knows this smile, once used to be exceptionally effective on him.

“It’s good to see you,” Landon says.

Caleb recovers quickly enough to return the smile. “I thought you were overseas with your father.”

“That was initially the plan, but work interfered, and father wanted me to attend tonight out of courtesy.” Landon smiles. “Although considering I get to see you tonight, I can’t say I’m particularly disappointed.”

Caleb opens his mouth but nothing particularly useful comes out.

Their last encounter didn't exactly end gracefully and he hadn’t seen Landon since the night he’d practically thrown himself at him while his thoughts had been consumed by another man entirely.

The same man who has since returned to Caleb’s life and became a spectacular pain in the arse.

His gaze drifts over Landon’s shoulder, but he stops himself.

A far worse thought occurs to him instead.

Has Arian investigated Landon?

He must have, Arian had admitted as much when they negotiated the bloody security arrangement, that anyone Caleb spent significant private time with would be screened.

Which means…

Nope, absolutely not, Caleb doesn’t want to know how Arian's background investigations are.

“You look good tonight,” Landon says.

And there it is, the slightly softer voice, and the lingering look, as though Landon has momentarily forgotten that there are other people in the room.

Once, it would have sent something pleasant curling through Caleb’s stomach. Tonight, a strange heaviness settles instead, accompanied by the sudden, unbearable awareness that somewhere in this room, Arian might be watching.

Caleb resists the urge to look, because why should he care? Arian certainly doesn’t.

I’m not interested in who you sleep with.

Excellent, because Caleb can speak to every man in the room, dance with every woman, take Landon upstairs and shag him against one of Belgium’s historically significant walls if he so desires.

What Arian thinks about any of it is completely irrelevant.

The thought makes Caleb inexplicably miserable.

“Thank you,” he says at last. “You look rather nice yourself.”

Landon’s familiar smile returns. “I was hoping I’d get the chance to speak to you tonight.”

Caleb's fingers tighten slightly around his champagne flute. "About anything in particular?"

Landon steps closer, Caleb has the instinctive urge to step back. But he stays in his spot.

“About last time.”

“Landon…”

“Just hear me out, okay.”

Caleb exhales but nods. Landon's expression softens

“I don't want things to be awkward between us. Whatever happened that night, we can leave it there. I know things between us have always been complicated.” he smiles faintly.

“But I don't want to stop seeing you altogether.” Landon continues. “Even if we are not doing that anymore. I liked being your friend before everything else became involved. I’d like to have that again.”

Despite that, friendship has never come particularly easy to them, Caleb smiles.

“I’d like that too.”

The happiness that immediately brightens Landon’s face makes guilt twist quietly in Caleb’s stomach.

“Good,” Landon lifts his glass. “Then perhaps our first act of renewed friendship should involve getting you another drink.”

Caleb laughs despite himself. “That sounds suspiciously like how we got into trouble the first time.”

“I’m willing to take the risk.”

“How very brave.” Caleb scoffs.

Landon’s hand settles against the small of Caleb’s back as he guides him towards the bar. Caleb barely stiffens but doesn’t react.

The touch has always been familiar, but Caleb is suddenly very aware of everything tonight.

He thinks that wherever Arian is currently, he doesn’t seem interested enough to intervene, which means Landon may have cleared his screening with flying colors.

Whatever, he shouldn’t even be thinking about Arian.

Caleb wishes his own brain would develop similar indifference as the mafia leader.

++++

One drink becomes two, before Landon casually mentions that several of their mutual friends intend to visit the private club after the banquet.

“It’ll be like old times.” Landon says. “Come with us.”

Caleb hesitates, normally he would, that is precisely the sort of invitation Caleb prefers to these dreadful evenings.

Except Arian doesn’t know. And under their agreement….

Christ.

When did his life reach the point where spontaneous drinking required notification to the Sicilian mafia?

“I can’t tonight.”

Landon’s disappointment is immediate, though he covers it quickly.

“I’m guessing some royal obligation?”

“Unfortunately, I still have half a speech to finish for my upcoming visit to Sicily, and if I arrive without one, Father may finally disown me.”

“I highly doubt the British public would allow that.” And then Landon sighs. “Maybe next time then, let’s just settle with the champagne for now.”

They speak for another few minutes before Caleb sets his glass aside.

“Excuse me. I need the restroom.”

“Of course”

Caleb walks away but he doesn’t go towards the restroom, instead he wanders instead. He is certainly not looking for Arian. Arian is doing exactly what Caleb had asked him to do. Remaining unseen, giving him space and allowing him to socialise without a six-foot Sicilian shadow looming behind him.

He should be pleased.

Instead, every minute he goes without seeing the man seem to wind something tighter beneath his ribs. Perhaps it is simply knowing Arian is there but not being able to tell where.

Yes, that sounds reasonable.

He doesn’t exactly feel comfortable knowing he is being watched but can not for the life of him tell where he’s being watched from. This is just general anxiety of the unknown. Beyond that he doesn’t care what Arian does, who he talks to, where he goes.

Caleb nearly laughs at the absurdity of his own thoughts.

He is halfway through one of the quieter adjoining galleries when he is stopped again.

A Belgian greets him, then his wife. Two young women Caleb vaguely remembers from another reception approach afterward, and he spends several minutes asking about their studies and listening to an enthusiastic account of someone’s recent engagement.

This part always comes naturally to him.

Caleb loves to socialise, he likes people, and more importantly he likes being made feel that they matter. So he always listens, and smiles, and remembers details as much as he could.

Eventually the crowd thins as he wanders farther from the main ballroom, the orchestra fading into distant murmur. He has just begun considering turning back when a voice calls after him.

“Prine Caleb.”

Caleb stops. The man approaching him looks to be somewhere in his fifties. His dark green velvet jacket would have been questionable enough without the collection of enormous gemstone rings occupying nearly every other finger.

Caleb has never seen him before.

The man behaves as though they are old friends. He reaches Caleb before an introduction could even be offered, seizes his hand enthusiastically and pulls him into an unexpected embrace.

Caleb immediately goes rigid.

The man’s palm lands against his back, too low, but not enough to create a scene over.

Caleb steps away immediately.

”I have been hoping to speak with you all evening,” the man says, his accent thick. Caleb summons the smile he’s been trained to produce since childhood.

“I am afraid you have me at a disadvantage.” He speaks gently.

“Of course. My apologies.”

The man introduces himself explaining that he is a longtime business associate of one of the Belgian ministers and owns several private establishments like clubs and bars throughout Brussels.

“I have heard so much about you.”

“I am sure,” Caleb replies pleasantly.

Something about the way the man’s eyes travel over him makes his skin crawl.

“I would be honoured to host you at one of my establishments the next time you’re in Brussels. Personally, of course, I could ensure you have a night you won’t forget.”

Caleb’s smile tightens.

“That’s very generous, although I don’t expect to be visiting again anytime soon.”

“Then perhaps tonight.”

“I’m afraid not.”

“There is one nearby.” The man insists.

“No, thank you.” Again, Caleb declines politely.

Although his palm is beginning to itch with the urge to smack it across the man’s face, doing so would inevitably result in yet another public scandal, one he is in absolutely no position to cause.

And then the man laughs as though Caleb has made a joke.

“You might reconsider. These events must become terribly boring for someone like you.”

Someone like you.

Caleb’s expression hardens and then his blood runs cold as the man steps closer.

“My place is much more suited to the sort of evening you prefer. At least, judging from what the newspapers say.”

And there it is.

Caleb has encountered his type before. Men who mistake photographs of him leaving pubs as invitations, men who see headlines and decide they know what he would permit. Men who assume that because Caleb laughs loudly, drinks occasionally, flirts openly and refuses to live like a porcelain ornament, they are somehow entitled to familiarity he has never offered them.

His smile disappears.

“I think you have mistaken tabloid speculation for an invitation.”

The man’s grin falters, only briefly.

Caleb straightens his jacket. “Now, if you’ll excuse me, I should find my sisters.”

He turns, just then a hand closes around his wrist.

Caleb stops.

For one strange second, everything inside him goes completely still.

Then comes anger.

“Let go of me.”

The man’s smile has become almost cosnpiratorial.

“Come now, Your Highness. There’s no need to be so serious. Think of it as business, you walk into my club with that pretty face, the photographers lose their minds, and tomorrow my establishment is on every front page of Europe.”

Caleb pulls his wrist free.

“Touch me again and publicity will be the least of your concerns.”

That should have ended it.

Apparently the man is either drunk, stupid or possessed of the remarkable confidence afforded to mediocre men who have gone too long without consequences.

Because the man is reaching out for him again, this time for the front of his jacket.

But the hand never makes contact.

Another hand appears from nowhere and catches the man’s wrist in midair. Hard.

Caleb hears the sharp intake of breath before he fully registers what has happened. Then he sees the black sleeve, and his gaze travels upwards.

Arian.

For the first time all evening, Caleb can breathe properly. The realisation is almost as unsettling as the situation itself.

Arian stands between them.

Caleb has seen him irritated before, amused and infuriatingly composed, but he has never seen him like this.

There is no anger on Arian’s face, perhaps that would have been less frightening. Because right now, his expression is almost entirely blank.

His grip tightens, as the man’s face twists when Arian turns his wrist, not enough to injure him, Caleb thinks, but enough that the man immediately bends under pressure.

“What…you’re…Mr. Conti, what are you doing here?”

So he knows who Arian is.

Arian says nothing at first, he simply moves, one step, then two until the man backs up enough for Arian’s body to occupy the space between Caleb and the man completely, broad shoulders cutting Caleb off from view as naturally as though he’s been built for precisely this purpose.

Caleb stares at his back, as something strange happens inside his chest.

Arian finally speaks.

“The prince declined your invitation.”

His voice is quiet.

The man’s eyes dart towards Caleb, Arian shifts slightly, the line of sight disappears.

“He declined when you approached him. He declined when you propositioned him. He declined when he walked away from you.” Another slight turn of the wrist, Caleb thinks Arian will actually break it if he puts any more pressure to it.

The man hisses, almost whimpers, which sends a spark of satisfaction inside Caleb.

Arian leans closer. “So I am struggling to understand which part confused you.”

“Please, Mr. Conti…I mean no disrespect” the man begs.

“No?”

“No Sir, of course not.”

And then Arian tilts his head, a hint of smirk appears on his lips.

“Good.” His voice somehow becomes softer. “Then there  should be no misunderstanding when I tell you this.”

The man is trembling under Arian’s hold.

“You will never put your hands on him again, not at any banquet, not at one of your clubs, not anywhere you happen to find fortunate enough to occupy the same room.”

The man’s face noticeably drains of all color.

Arian releases his wrist, for half a second Caleb thinks it’s over but then Arian calmly straightens the man’s cuffs where the grip has disturbed it. The gesture is so absurdly civil that Caleb almost forgets how dangerous the man standing in front of him actually is.

“If you ever forget that,” he says quietly. “You won’t have the opportunity to be reminded twice.”

There is no shouting, no dramatic threat, the silent way he controlled the entire situation is what’s terrifying.

This terrible, effortless stillness is what makes Arian dangerous.

The man certainly understands, as the next thing he does is scrambles away.

Caleb realizes something, as he stares at the broad line of Arian’s shoulder, while he placed himself between him and a man who had made his skin crawl.

He isn’t afraid of him.

Rather, he feels safe.

He waits until the man disappears behind the corridor, before he speaks.

“He knew you.”

Arian turns in that moment and their eyes lock. For a minute they hold each other's gaze, Arian’s eyes running down his face as if inspecting any signs of physical harm, there are none.

“He was afraid of you.” Caleb speaks again, trying to break the tense atmosphere.

Arian doesn’t answer, and the amusement disappears almost quickly as it comes because there is something wrong with his expression. The danger hasn’t left it.

If anything, now that the other man is gone, it seems to have redirected itself entirely towards Caleb.

“You weren’t supposed to leave the main hall.”

Caleb blinks. “What?”

“You were briefed to remain within the secured areas.”

For several seconds Caleb can only stare at him as disbelief arches up.

“Are you seriously angry with me?”

Arian steps closer, causing Caleb to take a slow step back.

“Do you value your life so little, or have you simply made a habit of wandering away from your security whenever the opportunity presents itself?”

Oh. So this is the moment where Caleb’s gratitude evaporates.

“I was trying to find the restroom.”

“There are restrooms inside the secured perimeter.”

Caleb stares at him. “Wonderful. Perhaps someone should have thought to mention that before dropping me inside a palace the size of a small country.”

“This isn’t funny.”

“I wasn’t joking.” Caleb counters back, with a frown now.

Neither is Arian, that becomes increasingly obvious the longer Caleb looks at him. There is something unnerving in his stillness, his jaw set hard, one hand curdled tightly at his side.

For one strange second, Caleb thinks it looks remarkably like fear. Not of anything, but fear for him, and the thought sends an unexpected warmth through his chest.

Arian had been watching. He had seen something wrong and crossed whatever distance had been between them before the man’s hand touched Caleb again.

Stood in front of him, protected him.

“If you want me to respect the conditions we agreed upon, then you need to cooperate with me.” Arian says.

“What exactly is that supposed to mean?”

“It means I kept my distance because you asked me to. You wanted discretion and privacy tonight. I gave you both.” Arian’s voice remains controlled. But Caleb can hear the strain beneath it now. “If you continue leaving secured areas without warning I will have to visibly remain beside you at all times, and I won't entertain a single complaint about it.”

“I had the situation handled perfectly well before you appeared” Caleb tries to protest. He’s seen Arian annoyed with him more times he can count but he’s never seen him genuinely angry, not like this.

When Arian doesn’t say anything, Caleb steps forward. “I didn’t ask you to intervene.”

“I am not doing this out of the goodness of my heart Caleb. I’m doing what I agreed to do, protecting you is my responsibility.”

Something sinks unpleasantly inside Caleb.

Because how stupid of him to think it was ever anything more than just a duty. For one embarrassing moment, standing behind Arian and watching that man tremble beneath his hand, Caleb had allowed himself to believe there was something personal in it.

Caleb should know better than most that duty could look similar to affection when viewed from the right angle.

His expression hardens.

“Then consider your responsibility fulfilled, just go back to wherever you’ve been hiding all evening and leave me alone.”

Caleb moves past him and barely makes it two steps, Arian’s arm shoots out. His palm lands flat against the wall beside Caleb blocking his path, causing him to almost bump into it.

Caleb stops abruptly, Arian is suddenly very close.

“What the hell are you doing?”

“You’re going back to the ballroom.”

“Move your arm.” Caleb says, voice hard, but Arian doesn’t budge.

Instead he steps closer. Caleb has seen enough men attempt intimidation throughout his life to recognize it, unfortunately for Arian, Caleb has never been particularly good at being intimidated.

“You’re going to turn around, walk back into the ballroom and stay beside Princess Matilda until I make sure that man has left the premises.”

“You are seriously acting insane right now, nothing happened.”

“I need to determine how he got close enough to you without being flagged.”

Caleb almost laughs. “That extraordinary little habit you have of forgetting that you do not own me.”

Something flickers across Arian’s face. “I don't need to own you to keep you alive.”

“And protecting me does not give you permission to control me.”

“It gives me permission to remove you from an environment I no longer consider secure.”

“Right, because some revolting businessman grabbed my wrist?”

“Because someone I didn’t clear got you alone in a corridor.”

The words land, shutting him up. Arian’s fist is still clenched beside him. He doesn’t know what to make of this. Arian says he’s performing his duty, but Caleb sees genuine worry reflecting in his eyes and this confusion does something dangerous to Caleb’s anger.

So he steps closer. He doesn’t stop until barely any space remains between them, close enough to see the darker ring around Arian’s irises, the tension running through his jaw.

One more inch and their noses would brush. Neither moves.

“You need to calm down.” Caleb says quietly. Arian’s eyes narrow.

“Do I?”

“Yes.” his voice drops further. “Because you protecting me does not mean I stop being a person. You don’t get to decide where I go, who I speak to and when I leave simply because something might happen.”

“And what if something does happen?”

“Then I listen. You tell me to run and I run right?”

Realization flickers in Arian’s eyes. Caleb may be bound to him by security protocols, but he will not blindly bend to his rules, nor will he allow anyone to control him.

For one suspended moment, the argument seems to disappear beneath something else, Arian’s shoulder slightly relaxing and Caleb sees it, the hint of acknowledgement and respect reflecting in his eyes.

Something in Caleb relaxes too, then he remembers he’s still angry with him.

“I’ve had quite enough excitement for one evening. Please ask Paul to bring the car, I’d like to return to London.”

He doesn’t wait for Arian to respond back, and turns to leave, walking straight into the ballroom, doing exactly as Arian had instructed.

He finds Matilda near one of the tables. She gives him a questioning look when he takes his place beside her, but he offers no explanation.

By the time Caleb glances back towards the corridor, Arian is gone.

Arian has just finished reviewing the last of the security reports, as midnight falls.

The place has gone quiet around him.

His temporary study sits in one of the private wings reserved for senior members of the royal household and their guests, far enough removed from the ceremonial rooms that the noise of the palace rarely reaches it.

Arian sits behind the desk with a glass of whiskey in one hand and entirely too much of Prince Caleb Whitmore’s life spread out before him.

Personal files, security reports and photographs.

Names of people Caleb sees regularly, places he frequents, establishments he has been known to disappear into unreasonable hours. Friends and former lovers, even members of the staff Caleb is close to. People who have had enough access to the prince over the years to warrant looking into.

Arian has looked into all of them.

His gaze returns to the photograph lying directly in front of him.

Landon Reed.

Arian takes another sip from his drink.

There is absolutely no reason for this, but something about Landon Reed ticks him off and the more he screens him, the more details about this man irritates him.

Arian reaches for the file again, despite having already read it twice.

Twenty-eight, son of a prominent senator. Cambridge graduate. His finances are clean, by political standards, at least. No meaningful criminal associations, no unexplained travel, and no connections to anyone remotely linked to the Paris attack.

Even his more questionable youthful decisions are disappointingly ordinary.

A few drunken photographs here, or one speeding offence there.

Arian closes the file.

He has investigated men with significantly less information and found enough dirt to destroy entire careers. Landon Reed on the other hand has apparently committed the unforgivable crime of being perfectly respectable.

The photographs beside the files adds to Arian’s annoyance. There are several of Reed and Caleb together.

Most are press photographs, collected over last year. The two leaving a club, are surrounded by security and cameras. Caleb laughing, as Landon holds his hand through the crowd. Another catches them embracing outside some private function. In one, Landon’s arm is draped comfortably around Caleb’s shoulders while Caleb appears to be saying something that has made them both laugh.

They look familiar with each other.

Arian’s jaw tightens.

Not particularly an irrelevant information, this is enough to know that Caleb is comfortable enough to be around someone who doesn’t pose any direct threats.

He moves to another photograph. This one is taken through the window of a car.

The quality is poor, blurred by rain and movement, but Caleb is unmistakable. He is slumped against Landon’s shoulder, eyes closed, curls fallen across his forehead. One of Landon’s hands is wrapped around Caleb's, their fingers loosely intertwined between them.

Caleb appears half asleep, possibly drunk.

There is nothing threatening about the photograph. If anything, Reed appears to be taking care of him.

Arian puts the photograph down. Then he reaches for another one.

This one he has already looked at several times. For security purposes, obviously.

It is nearly an year old and considerably more invasive than the others, taken somewhere the photographer clearly wasn’t supposed to be. A private terrace behind one of Ladon’s members’ clubs, according to the report. Caleb is backed partly against the stone wall, Landon close in front of him, one arm around his waist.

They are kissing.

Not the sort of kiss that could reasonably be dismissed as friendly affection either, because Caleb’s hand is curled into the front of Landon’s jacket.

Landon’s hand is at his waist.

Arian stares at it, then takes a slow drink of his whiskey.

The photograph had apparently caused a spectacular amount of trouble when it surfaced. The articles scattered across the desk make that abundantly clear.

Caleb had already been photographed with women over the years, enough that the British press had happily constructed several relationships on his behalf. The photograph with Landon had created an entirely different frenzy. Questions about the prince’s sexuality had circulated for months. Commentators had dissected old photographs, friendships and nights as though Caleb’s private life were a matter of national policy.

The palace never confirmed anything. Neither had Caleb.

Arian doesn’t particularly care which gender the prince prefers, he cares even less what the British public thinks about it. Who he takes to bed is his business, and Arian has already told him as much.

His gaze drops back to Landon’s hand around Caleb’s waist.

Apparently his own brain has chosen not to respect that position. He lets out a sigh before turning the photograph face down.

Last night had provided more than enough evidence that Landon doesn’t pose any threat to the prince.

Which is why when he had seen him approach Caleb he didn’t react nor move from his position. He had promised Caleb he would remain out of sight, not that he would stop watching him.

Reed had touched Caleb’s back, while guiding him toward the bar, had leaned close to his ear, and at one point, his hand had rested at Caleb’s waist considerably longer than Arian thought necessary.

And he had done absolutely nothing.

Because marching across a state banquet to remove another man’s hand from Caleb’s waist simply because Arian found himself inexplicably irritated by its presence would have been unreasonable.

Not to mention, there is also the inconvenient fact that Caleb would probably have murdered him. The thought almost makes him smile.

The prince had spent the better part of two weeks negotiating every possible inch of his independence. If Arian had interrupted a perfectly harmless conversation because someone Caleb had known for years happened to touch him, he would still be hearing about it when they reached Sicily.

His gaze drifts across the table one last time, scattered magazines, interviews, photoshoots of the Royal Family and a handful contain photographs from campaigns and charity events, even fashion, Caleb’s face appearing in most of them.

The British press seems obsessed with him. Makes sense because Caleb photographs extraordinarily well.

His gaze drops to one of the magazine covers. Caleb in a dark suit, curls pushed away from his forehead, looking directly into the camera with the kind of effortless confidence people spent entire careers learning to manufacture.

Over the past few weeks, Arian has seen stubbornness, intelligence, and his astonishing inability to stop talking.

But last night, Arian had seen Caleb turn serious. For the first time, he had seen the quiet authority in his eyes—the unmistakable promise that the prince would bend to no one’s will.

And despite himself, Arian had come away with a newfound respect for him. Perhaps even admiration.

Arian, on the other hand, had lost his temper. He rarely does, or allows anyone to see it when he does.

But the moment he had seen that man’s hand close around Caleb’s wrist, every bit of sense left his mind. He saw Caleb’s discomfort, the slight recoil, and the vanishing smile, and then he just saw red.

He could still remember the man’s wrist in his hand, how easily he could have broken it and how badly he had wanted to. But that wasn’t the problem, Arian would intervene again if Caleb found himself in a situation like that. The problem was everything that came afterward.

He got too angry, too transparent that Caleb had noticed.

You need to calm down.

Arian exhales slowly.

Very few people in his life have spoken to him that way and remained standing comfortably afterward. Caleb had stepped closer while doing it

He doesn’t know why, but Arian had calmed down after that, not because Caleb is a prince or demanded it, but because Caleb was right. He has heard the press call him reckless, spoiled, scandalous, a pretty prince with an unfortunate habit of creating headlines. Arian had believed in some of it.

He doesn’t anymore.

Cause he’s seen Caleb now, and there is a difference between recklessness and refusing to live afraid. Caleb has spent his entire life under scrutiny and somehow remains remarkably open beneath it. He speaks to people, remembers them, and lets strangers approach him without immediately assuming the worst of them.

At the banquet, Arian had watched him stop repeatedly for people who wanted nothing more than thirty seconds of his attention. He had given it to them, not because the cameras were pointed at him, but simply because he’s kind.

There is something about him that makes people want to move closer, how people laugh more easily, how the room sfotens around him, even Arian…

Arian’s fingers still against his glass.

He doesn’t let the thought finish, he doesn’t know why he even thought of that in the first place.

He needs air.

A walk through the palace grounds should clear his head, and perhaps by the time he returns he will have remembered that Prince Caleb Whitmore is nothing more than a security assignment attached to a political alliance. Nothing more.

When Arian leaves the study, the palace is almost entirely silent at this hour. Guards acknowledge him as he passes, but otherwise he encounters no one as he makes his way through the private wing towards the main staircase.

Downstairs, leading to the main hall, voices are heard from the corridor.

Arian slows.

One of them is Caleb’s.

Arian considers continuing towards the garden when he hears Caleb laugh.

Two figures down the corridor, stand near the tall windows. Caleb has his back partly towards him, several papers tucked beneath his arm.

The other man, blonde, elegantly dressed, almost the same height as Caleb is there with him. Familiar despite the fact that Arian has never met him in person.

Prince Andrew Whitmore.

Apparently he has returned recently from his diplomatic assignment.

At first, there is nothing remarkable about the scene, just two brothers talking, Caleb laughing, Andrew smiling, and then something shifts.

Andrew steps closer. Caleb retreats until his back presses against the wall. Every trace of his smile disappears, replaced by something Arian has never seen on his face before.

Fear.
