Caleb likes it when the palace is quiet during the night, without constant movement from the staff and advisers coming in and going. Perfect time to work on his speech, where he can sit in his study in silence and concentrate.

Moonlight spills through the tall windows in pale strips catching against the marble floor. He is just making his way over with several papers tucked beneath his arm, when he notices someone standing near the windows ahead.

“Andrew?”

His brother who has seemingly returned from his overseas assignment, turns and smiles at Caleb.

“When did you get back?”

Andrew smiles, as Caleb approaches him. “Three hours ago.”

“You might have warned us.”

“And ruin the surprise?”

“Considering I nearly walked straight past you, I wouldn’t call it particularly successful.”

Andrew laughs softly. There is something reassuringly familiar about seeing him after weeks away. Whatever else could be said about Andrew, his absence is always noticeable around the palace, as if the place re-arranges itself whenever the future King returns.

Caleb stops a comfortable distance away.

“I take it your visit was successful judging by the whole Europe raving about your upcoming coronation.”

Andrew keeps smiling. “It was exhausting, but yes, successful.”

“Hmm well…you missed the polo tournament, I won as always.”

“Not from what Matilda told me, you came second.”

“Only because she cheated.”

Andrew laughs again. For several seconds, everything feels normal, just two brothers having a light talk, but that usually changes very quickly, especially with Andrew.

Caleb sees his gaze change, and then Andrew takes a small step forward.

“I’ve also heard Father has been busy in my absence. How’s your new guard dog doing?”

Caleb’s smile fades slightly.

“So you knew it too? About this arrangement?”

Andrew laughs again. “About the alliance? Yes, father mentioned negotiations before I left.” Andrew’s jaw tightens. “About installing a Conti at your side.”

Something in his tone immediately irritates Caleb. “Nobody installed anyone.”

Andrew continues to take a few slow steps towards him again. “I leave for several weeks and return to discover Father and Matilda have handed my brother’s safety to a criminal.”

Caleb frowns. “A rather effective criminal.”

Andrew’s eyes flick towards him, the defence had escaped before Caleb could stop it.

“So you approve?”

Caleb rolls his eyes. “I didn’t say that.”

“You defended him.”

“I corrected you.”

Andrew gives a humorless smile. “I’ve handled some matters with the Sicilian mafia before, I know precisely what the Contis are, Caleb.”

“Then presumably you also know Father didn’t drag Arian from a back alley and hand him a palace pass.”

“Arian.” Andrew repeats. Caleb’s shoulders stiffen, something in the way his brother says the name bothers him.

"So you're on first-name terms already."

"Oh, for God's sake." Caleb steps around him but Andrew moves with him. Not enough to block his path but enough that Caleb gets the hint that he’s not yet allowed to leave.

"I had intended to accompany you to Sicily." Andrew continues. "Your first significant diplomatic assignment without Father." Andrew's expression softens. "I thought I might join you for part of it. Make sure you don't embarrass the family too terribly."

Caleb rolls his eyes. “Your faith in me remains touching.”

“I worry about you, because someone has to.”

The humor leaves Caleb’s face. Andrew steps closer again, this time Caleb takes a step back.

“You have a remarkable talent for putting yourself in situations you don’t understand, little one.” Andrew speaks in a low tone, he doesn't stop, he steps closer until Caleb’s back hits the wall, papers in his hand fall next to him, scattering on the floor.

Andrew reaches for him, his fingers close around Caleb’s wrist. Immediately, something inside Caleb goes cold.

“Andrew.”

“I leave for a few weeks,” his brother continues quietly. “And come back to find some Sicilian gangster scumbag has been given access to every part of your life.”

His grip on Caleb’s wrist tightens.

“Let go.”

“You don't know these people like I do.”

“Andrew, you're hurting me.” Caleb says a little loudly. Andrew’s fingers loosen a little but he doesn’t release him, instead he moves closer.

His heartbeat begins to climb, every instinct telling him to create distance while Andrew seems determined to remove it.

“I don’t like strangers being given that kind of access to you.”

“Arian isn’t…” but he stops before he finishes that sentence. Caleb hates himself for that instinctive defence and more than that, he hates the strange little satisfaction that appears on Andrew’s face when he notices it.

“Already defending him.”

“I’m not defending anyone.”

“No?” Andrew’s thumb presses against the inside of Caleb’s wrist. “You become attached far too easily. Which is why I am allowed to worry about who Father places near you.” The concern threaded through that low tone makes Caleb’s stomach turn. There is nothing normal about the way Andrew behaves around him, how quickly concern becomes control, how easily affection twists into something suffocating.

And whenever it comes to Caleb, Andrew seems to lose whatever restraint he possesses around everyone else.

“I simply don’t appreciate being replaced.”

Caleb is almost breathless, trying not to move, because he doesn’t know what else Andrew can do, how far he can go.

“What are you even talking about?” Caleb asks.

“I was supposed to be with you in Sicily.”

“You’re the Crown Prince. You have your own responsibilities.”

As if reasoning with this man is ever gonna do anything.

“And you’re mine. My responsibility, my little brother.”

Caleb swallows.

“I don’t need you to take care of me Andrew, we are not children anymore.”

“No,” Andrew’s grip tightens once more. “Apparently, you have Arian for that now.”

And then everything stills as footsteps are heard behind them. Andrew’s hand disappears from Caleb’s wrist, almost instantly.

That frightens Caleb more than anything else, because it means Andrew knows exactly how the scene looks. Caleb remains against the wall, as he watches Arian approach without hurry, every line of him composed as always.

For once, Caleb has absolutely nothing sarcastic to say about his timing.

Andrew turns, and Caleb can’t see the expression that takes over his brother's face but he can guess it’s not a good one.

His gaze moves first to Andrew, then at Caleb, and then downwards at his wrist. A faint redness was already  beginning to form around it. Arian’s expression doesn’t change.

Which Claeb has begun to understand. The less Arian’s face reveals, the more careful everyone should probably become.

“Your Highness.”

Andrew smiles. “Arian Conti, so we meet.”

Andrew takes a step forward and continues. “I’ve been hearing whispers about you over the months around the palace, pity I wasn’t around to welcome you on your first day here.”

As Andrew steps towards him, Arian stays in place. The difference in height becomes apparent immediately. Andrew is tall, but Arian is taller.

Caleb watches his brother register this fact with considerably less enthusiasm than Eleanor has.

“I’ve been anticipating this meeting ever since Father informed me of the alliance with your family.” Andrew says smoothly.

“Then I’m glad I could finally accommodate you.”

The words are perfectly polite, the tone is not. Andrew seems to have noticed, nevertheless he extends his arm out.

“A pleasure then.”

Arian looks at it, then takes it. Neither man release it immediately.

Jesus Christ.

Caleb’s eyebrows rise, as he thinks how ridiculous this entire situation actually is. Two extraordinarily powerful men standing in the middle of a royal corridor apparently attempting to determine superiority through a handshake.

Yet, there is absolutely nothing remotely humorous about their expressions.

Andrew eventually withdraws.

“I understand you’ve taken a particular interest in my brother’s security.”

Arian’s gaze flickers towards Caleb. “His security is part of our agreement.”

“Naturally.” Andrew says, as he moves closer. “Anyone invested in my little brother’s wellbeing is welcome within these walls.”

My little brother.

There is that tone again, like Andrew owns him.

Arian’s eyes return to Andrew, something cold enters them, Caleb sees it, so does Andrew, and a wider smirk forms on his brother’s face.

“My coronation is six months away. I’m sure you understand that once I become King, arrangements concerning this family will ultimately fall under my authority.”

Arian tilts his head, and then smirks. Andrew continues.

“I’ll expect a full account of what occurs in Sicily.” Andrew continues. “Where Caleb goes, who he meets, any concerns you develop, I prefer to know precisely what happens around him.”

“The security reports are submitted to Princess Matilda.”

Andrew’s expression tightens. “I’m aware.”

“Then we are in agreement.”

“I’m telling you that when I’m King, they’ll come to me.”

There is a pause before Arian speaks.

“No.”

Caleb’s attention snaps fully towards Arian, because nobody talks to Andrew like this.

Andrew’s smile vanishes, Arian remains perfectly relaxed.

“Our arrangement with the Crown is an alliance, Your Highness. Not employment, perhaps I’m not certain you understand that.”

Oh, fuck.

Caleb really should not be enjoying this.

“Information relevant to palace security will be provided through the channels agreed upon with His Majesty and Princess Matilda. Conti business remains Conti business.”

Andrew’s jaw tightens.

“Careful Mr. Conti, because when I’m King, the arrangements will require reconsideration.”

Arian steps slightly closer. “Intereference in my family’s affairs isn’t reconsideration," he says. “It’s a violation of the alliance.”

Andrew's eyes change to something menacing, but he stands his ground, even when Arian ends up in his space.

“And my family has never responded particularly well to those.” a final nail on the head, as Arian finishes his sentence, Caleb can practically hear Andrew’s ego being stabbed.

Understanding seems to settle on Andrew’s face, but then he slowly smiles.
“I suppose we’ll revisit this conversation when the Crown is mine.”

“If you wish.”

Something about those three words seems to irritate Andrew more than any threat, because Arian simply doesn’t seem to care. King or Crown Prince, Andrew Whitmore’s title means nothing beyond the political courtesy Arian chose to afford it.

Caleb has spent his entire life watching people rearrange themselves around his brother. Arian hasn’t moved an inch.

Andrew turns, his eyes land on Caleb, the coldness disappears beneath a smile.
“Well…” he approaches him. Caleb straightens. “Father certainly found you an interesting guard dog.” Caleb says nothing, but his expression shows he’d swing a fist at Andrew’s face if he could.

“Perhaps you should be careful whose leash you allow around your throat, little one.”  Andrew says. Caleb’s stomach twists, before he could respond, Andrew looks back at Arian.

“Do take good care of him then Mr. Conti.”

Something passes between them, a challenge, a warning, or perhaps both.

Then Arian gives him a smirk. “I intend to.”

Caleb flinches slightly when Andrew turns back to him and smiles softly. “Good night, little brother.”

Then he walks away.

For several seconds after Andrew disappeared around the corner, neither of them speaks. Caleb remains exactly where Andrew left him, his back pressed against the wall. He doesn’t realize it until he feels Arian looking at him. Something about the awareness finally makes him move.

Caleb pushes himself away from the wall and immediately busies his hands with straightening his clothes. His shirt has come partially untucked beneath his jacket, the collar sitting awkwardly against his neck. He fixes it quickly, smoothing his palms over the front as though there is something terribly important about looking put together again.

“I should go.”

He doesn’t wait for an answer. The papers he had been carrying before Andrew stopped him are scattered across the floor, Caleb quickly gathers them, not bothering to put them in order, tucking the uneven stack beneath his arm.

“Caleb.”

He has just started towards the door when Arian’s voice stops him.

There is something about hearing his name in Arian’s voice that seems to do that to him lately. Caleb doesn’t turn.

“I have to finish my speech, I’m already behind and father wants the final draft before breakfast.”

He’s surprised that he has managed to keep his voice remarkably normal, despite how he’s feeling right now. But when he starts to walk again, Arian moves.

Not aggressively, or with the abrupt authority Caleb has become accustomed to whenever security is involved. He simply comes around him, placing himself in Caleb’s path.

Caleb doesn’t look up, he can’t risk Arian seeing the unshed tears that have appeared in his eyes, or how much the encounter with his brother has affected him. But Arian sees it.

His eyes are wet, but he’s not crying.

No way in hell.

Caleb Whitmore does not cry in palace corridors because his older brother behaved like a controlling bastard. He has survived worst things.

He blinks rapidly and looks away.

“I really need to go.” he says, and fuck his voice breaks.
Arian doesn’t move. A tiny spark of humiliation catches fire in the pit of his stomach and then Caleb lets out a sigh and finally looks up.

“What are you going to tell me this time?” Caleb asks. “That I was reckless, that I shouldn’t have been standing in a corridor alone? Perhaps we should add another clause to your bloody agreement. Prince Caleb is no longer permitted to speak privately with members of his own family without armed supervision.”

The anger helps, the outburst had been long since coming, since yesterday and Caleb clings to it.

“Go on, then. Tell me what I did wrong.”

Aarian’s expression shifts. “I’m not blaming you for anything.”

Caleb lets out a laugh that sounds awful even to his ears.

“There’s a first.”

Arian absorbs the insult without reacting, which somehow makes Caleb feel worse.

“You must think I’m a complete fucking mess.” The words escape more quietly than he intends.

Yesterday, some revolting stranger had put his hands on him at a banquet. Tonight, his own brother had cornered him in a corridor and reduced him to this.

Caleb suddenly feels exhausted.

“I don’t.” Arian finally speaks. Caleb glances at him. Arian is watching him with an expression Caleb cannot read.

“I think you have an unfortunate habit of taking responsibility for things that aren’t yours to carry.”

The anger inside Caleb suddenly falters, his eyes flicker up at Arian, as Arian’s gaze lowers to his wrist. Caleb instinctively curls his fingers towards his palm, but Arian has already seen it. For a  moment, something dark passes through his face.

Then he reaches for him.

Caleb goes still. Arian’s hand pauses for a brief moment, before his fingers, gently wrap around Caleb’s wrist.

The contrast is so immediate that Caleb’s breath catches in his throat. Arian’s hand feels cool, gentle and careful. There is extraordinary strength in those fingers, Caleb has seen what they can do when wrapped around someone else’s wrist, but none of that is used against him.

Arian turns his hand over slowly, beneath the light, the redness around Caleb’s wrist is more pronounced than Caleb realized, faint impressions left where Andrew’s fingers had pressed too deeply into the skin.

Caleb watches in silence as Arian inspects it. There is something so strangely intimate about watching a man like Arian Conti handle him as though he might bruise beneath too much pressure.

“Has he done this before?”

Caleb’s attention snaps back.
“What?”

Arian lifts his eyes. “Andrew, has he touched you like this before?”

Caleb doesn’t know what to say, should he defend Andrew, make up an excuse? Andrew has always had no regard for his personal space, as they grew older it became even more evident, but he has never caused him any physical harm.

Arian’s thumb begins moving over his knuckles, and Caleb becomes very hyper aware of the movement. Caleb isn’t entirely certain Arian knows he’s doing it.

“Caleb.” Arian calls him again, bringing him out of his thoughts and Caleb quickly shakes his head.

“No…not exactly. Andrew is just….” he searches desperately for an explanation and discovers that every available one sounds ridiculous. “He’s difficult.”

When Arian doesn’t look convinced Caleb continues. “ He has a strange way of showing he cares.”

“By hurting you?”

Caleb realizes there is no judgement, or accusation in Arian’s tone, just a quiet question that Caleb finds considerably harder to answer.

“He’s the eldest, he spent his entire life being told everything will eventually become his responsibility. The Crown, the family, the country. Father expects him to look after everything, and Andrew….” Caleb exhales. “Sometimes he gets carried away with it.”

Caleb realises he’s rambling, and stops when he feels Arian hasn’t responded to anything he just said. He is simply listening.

Caleb doesn’t know what he wants anymore. His thoughts have tangled themselves into something that doesn't have the energy to unravel, which is why when Arian’s hand that isn’t holding his wrist settled quietly around his waist, his breath catches.

The touch is light, almost careful, drawing him in, no more than a little bit closer. It shouldn’t affect him nearly as much as it does. Caleb’s fingers curl around Arian’s arm on instinct, only to steady himself despite being in absolutely no danger of falling, and suddenly they have found themselves standing much closer than necessary.

Caleb feels Arian’s warmth in the narrow space between them, can smell the familiar trace of sandalwood and smoke he remembers with embarrassing clarity from Paris. Arian has always been imposing up close, too tall, too broad.

He should resent Arian’s closeness for the same reason he resents everyone else who mistakes his welcoming smile and polite manner for permission to touch him.

He doesn't.

There is no entitlement in the hand at his wrist, no demand in the fingers resting carefully beneath the marks Andrew left around his wrist. Caleb could move away right now and Arian would let him.

But he stays.

The realization comes quietly after that.

Arian feels safe.

When Caleb finally meets his eyes, he finds none of the usual irritation or the quiet arrogance that makes him want to argue on principle. Arian is simply watching him with an unfamiliar softness, as though there is something before him he hasn’t quite worked out yet.

It should not make Caleb’s heart beat harder.

“Caleb.”

Even his name sounds different when Arian says it like that.

Caleb’s fingers tighten around his arm. “What?”

“If he does that again…” Arian pauses, his thumb passing once more over Caleb’s knuckles. “If anyone does that again, you come to me.”

Caleb says nothing.

“You don’t explain it or justify it. You don’t convince yourself they meant well.” His voice drops, losing the last traces of the authority Caleb has become accustomed to hearing. “You come to me.”

And that, more than anything else, gets him.

There is no protocol hidden inside it. No “do as you’re told” or reminders that Caleb is a responsibility Arian agreed to take on. It doesn’t even sound like an order.

Just an offer.

Come to me.

“You shouldn’t have to carry all this alone, not anymore.” Arian says firmly and something in Caleb loosens.

Finally, a small smile finds him.

“Is this another one of your security clauses?”

For a moment, Arian merely looks at him, then he smiles.

“Non-negotiable.” Arian says.

Caleb laughs softly, and Arian’s smile lingers.

Somewhere during the conversation, the distance between them has become dangerously small. Caleb doesn’t remember either closing it, only that Arian’s hand remains warm against his waist.

And then, Arian’s attention drifts to his mouth.

It isn’t the first time Caleb has thought he’d caught him doing it. It is however the first time Arian lingers long enough, as Caleb’s own gaze drops with humiliating predictability. The few inches Arian has on him, has him tilting his head upwards. And there are perhaps two inches between their faces. Two stupid, politically disastrous inches.

There are scandals, and then there is whatever the hell this would become.

None of that makes Caleb step away.

For one suspended moment, Caleb wonders what would happen if they simply stopped pretending. That Caleb could simply throw caution to the wind and close the gap and just be done with it.

But the moment breaks before either of them can find out, as footsteps are heard approaching them through the adjoining door.

Arian's hand leaves his waist. Immediately Caleb notices the absence of warmth. He straightens his shirt, mostly because he needs something to do with his hands, and refuses to think about the sharp little stab of disappointment taking over.

Paul appears around the corner.

Paul's attention takes in the distance between them, Caleb's undoubtedly suspicious expression and Arian standing considerably closer than any security arrangement reasonably requires.

His face reveals absolutely nothing but Caleb knows Paul is a damn good observant, and has probably already read the room.

"Your Highness," he says pleasantly. "Your study is ready. I had the revised briefing documents brought over as well."

The study.

The speech.

"Oh, shit."

Paul closes his eyes briefly. Beside him, Arian's mouth twitches.

Caleb remembers the papers that had somehow slipped through his fingers again the moment Arian closed the gap between them and quickly moves to pick them up again. "I'm so far behind right now, Father will have my head if he discovers I've spent the last half hour accomplishing absolutely nothing."

"You have approximately 14 hours before you present it to the King, Your Highness," Paul says.

"That's remarkably comforting, thank you."

He gathers the pages with considerably less dignity than he would prefer. One escapes the pile, only for Arian to catch it before it reaches the floor. Their fingers brush when Caleb takes it from him.

Apparently the universe has decided subtlety is no longer necessary for them.

"Thank you," Caleb mutters.

"Anytime."

Caleb glances at him and immediately regrets it. There is something almost amused in Arian's expression, something that suggests he is considerably less unaffected by the last few minutes than Caleb would find convenient.

"Right. Speech. I should go."

Paul falls into step beside him. They have barely made it halfway down the corridor when Arian calls after them.

"Paul."

Caleb stops but doesn't turn.

"Get him some ice for his wrist."

Paul looks between them in curiosity but doesn’t question, instead, he simply nods. "Of course."

Caleb looks down at the faint marks Andrew left behind.

It is such a small thing. Arian simply noticed something Caleb has already almost forgotten, and made it certain that someone takes care of it.

His mouth curves before he can stop it. He can feel Arian’s gaze following him down the corridor, but he doesn’t dare turn around to confirm it. Only when he rounds the corner and disappears from view does Caleb finally release the breath he hadn’t realised he was holding, the smile still stubbornly fixed to his face.

What Caleb doesn’t see is that Arian remains exactly where he left him, watching the empty corridor for a moment longer than necessary, his own smile lingering long after the prince is gone.

Three days have passed. Three perfectly ordinary, uneventful days in which Caleb discovers that he has developed a problem.

The problem is approximately six foot something Sicilian, perpetually dressed like someone is about to either hand him a billion pound corporation or ask him to dispose of a body.

Caleb has always been aware of Arian, when a man has been assigned to keep you alive and possesses the irritating habit of appearing wherever you least expect him, it tends to happen. But since that night in the corridor, something has shifted, and Caleb would rather not investigate on it.

Arian’s presence has started to manifest in embarrassingly small ways. He enters a room and Caleb notices before anyone else, that low voice joins a conversation and his attention drifts towards it almost automatically, their eyes meet across a crowded hall and something warm and ridiculous unfurls beneath his ribs, before common sense has the chance to intervene.

At breakfast one morning, Caleb catches himself watching Arian over the rim of his teacup. Arian catches him doing it.

Caleb looks away and the humiliation after is immediate.

He does not look away from people. People look away from him. Caleb has spent the better part of his adult life being stared at by everyone, from heads of state to complete strangers outside nightclubs.

Yet apparently Arian Conti can reduce the Crown Prince of Britain to developing an intense fascination with Earl Grey simply by looking back at him.

Caleb decides it's a temporary neurological condition, which shall pass in a matter of days.

It doesn't.

Two days after, Caleb has a fitting in Mayfair. The boutique has been closed to the public afternoon, which Caleb considers excessive and his security team considers sensible.

Half the shop has consequently been occupied by royal protection officers, two increasingly nervous assistants and an elderly tailor who has known Caleb since he was sixteen and therefore fears absolutely nobody.

And then there is Arian.

He has positioned himself on a low leather sofa near the fitting area and has barely moved in forty minutes. He’s either seen sipping on his drink, or flipping through a newspaper. Caleb, unfortunately, has spent those same forty minutes repeatedly emerging from behind a curtain in varying states of expensive tailoring while being instructed to stand straighter by the old man.

“Shoulders back, Your Highness.”

“My shoulders are back.”

“They could be further back.”

“Any further and I shall dislocate something.”

The tailor ignores him with ease.

Caleb is smoothing a hand over the fourth suit of the afternoon when he catches Arian’s reflection in the mirror.

And he’s staring.

A glass of whiskey rests loosely in Arian’s hand, one ankle crossed over the opposite knee, his attention fixed on Caleb as if the man himself doesn’t realize he’s staring.

Or maybe he does, and he’s just enjoying seeing him in misery.

Caleb turns slightly as the tailor adjusts his sleeve. Arian’s gaze follows. When he turns the other way, those dark eyes follow again.

The tailor steps away to retrieve something, leaving Caleb with an opportunity he really should possess enough maturity not to take.

“You know,” he says, meeting Arian’s eyes through the mirror. “If you think I look that handsome, you could simply take a picture.”

Arian doesn’t answer.

For one dreadful second, Caleb wonders whether he hasn’t heard him, then Arian raises the whiskey to his mouth and takes a slow, measured sip without breaking eye contact.

He denied nothing, didn’t even smirk, not even an argument that Caleb’s accusation is ridiculous. Caleb’s stomach performs an entirely unnecessary turn. He looks away first as his tailor returns.

“Too tight around the collar,” he announces.

The tailor glances at him in confusion. “I haven’t touched the collar, sir.”

“Well something is.”

There is a faint sound behind him as Arian sets his glass down. Caleb refuses to look in the mirror again. He doesn’t need the confirmation that the bastard is smiling.

The following afternoon is worse.

Caleb is crossing the palace grounds with Cecily beside him, listening with moderate success as she delivers an increasingly passionate account of some university catastrophe involving a group assignment, and an incompetent classmate.

He really is listening, and manages admirably, in fact, right up until they reach the open training field and Caleb sees Arian.

Several members of the security team are scattered across the grass, some running drills, while others spar. Arian stands among them speaking to one of his men, and at some point since breakfast has apparently abandoned his usual suits.

This is very unfortunate for Caleb.

The dark undershirt fits close enough to leave very little to the imagination, stretched across the solid planes of his chest and the faint definition of his stomach beneath it. His forearms are crossed loosely over his chest, tanned skin catching slightly under the afternoon sun, and Caleb finds himself staring shamelessly with absolutely no intention of pretending otherwise.

As if that weren't bad enough, the heat has pushed Arian's dark hair away from his face, swept carelessly back from his forehead, and those goddamn sunglasses are sitting over his eyes. He looks unfairly good, less polished than usual, rougher somehow and whatever Cecily is saying beside Caleb begins to fade into meaningless background noise.

She is still talking, Caleb can see her mouth moving, but he hears absolutely nothing.

Unfortunately, Arian shifts, one arm flexing as he says something to the man beside him, and the fabric pulls tighter across his chest.

That does something horrible to Caleb’s brain, and it stops working entirely.

He’s shamelessly staring, no decorum whatsoever.

Because who the hell sculpted that?

“Ow.”

He turns to find Cecily lowering her finger she has just flicked against his forehead.

“Have you heard a single word I’ve said?”

“Every word.” he says, rubbing the sore spot on his forehead.

“Oh really, what did I say?”

Caleb opens his mouth with tremendous confidence and discovers absolutely nothing coming to his mind. Cecily's expression becomes smug.

“Something about the university,” he offers.

“I’ve been talking about the university for fifteen minutes.”

“And I’ve been very supportive.”

“You heard nothing. Seriously, where is your mind?” She asks innocently.

Caleb doesn’t respond, instead he loops her arm more firmly through his and begins steering her away before she can follow his previous line of sight. “Come along.”

“I was telling you…”

“You can tell me while we’re walking.”

She twists around, attempting to look behind them. “What were you staring at?”

“Nothing, keep walking.”

Had this been Eleanor with him instead, he would never have survived this encounter. She would have taken one look at his face, followed his attention straight to the training field and made his life unbearable. Cecily, mercifully, is still too clueless and preoccupied with her academic dilemmas to realize her brother had just dragged her halfway across the palace grounds because Arian Conti decided, today was the perfect day to walk around bare arms under the afternoon sun.

And if Caleb happens to catch Arian watching him before he turns away, that familiar hint of amusement already tugging at the corner of his mouth, well, Caleb chooses to ignore that entirely.

By the end of the third day, Caleb has reached the conclusion that Arian is everywhere.

Technically, this is exactly what his father wanted. Arian is supposed to be around him, overseeing Caleb’s protection naturally requires a certain amount of proximity. However, his mind appears to have missed that little detail entirely.

He sees Arian at breakfast and loses whatever he was saying. They pass one another in a corridor and Caleb becomes absurdly conscious of how little space there is between their shoulders. During security meetings, that low voice somewhere behind him is enough to send warmth creeping up the back of his neck.

Yesterday, during a security meeting, Arian had leaned over his shoulder to point out a change in one of the documents in front of him, one hand resting against the back of Caleb’s chair as he quietly explained an adjustment to their route. 
It had been a perfectly ordinary conversation about security, except Arian’s chest had been somewhere over Caleb’s shoulder, and his voice far too close to his ear, and Caleb had retained approximately three words of whatever the man had said.

And there was also the matter of Arian remaining there for several seconds longer than necessary, though that might have been wishful thinking on his part.

He is beginning to imagine many things. And it isn’t as though Caleb doesn’t know how to handle attraction, which is the most insulting part.

Caleb is very good at flirting.

He knows exactly when to smile, when to lean closer and when to lower his voice, he’s charmed men, women, diplomats, actors, politicians, and enough beautiful strangers to have provided the British tabloids with material for years.

Then Arian Conti looks at him and suddenly he forgets that words exist.

Worse, Arian notices everything.

Caleb has absolutely no idea what Arian makes of any of this. Perhaps he thinks Caleb is an idiot. Perhaps he is quietly reconsidering the entire alliance.

But Caleb is not entertaining any of these thoughts, because then he remembers the quiet way Arian had looked at him when he told him.

You come to me.

And he ends up remembering Arain’s hand around his wrist three nights ago, that has kept Caleb lying awake during the night, wondering what did all that mean?

He has taken an alarming number of cold showers since then too.

It’s not funny at all.

There is nothing remotely amusing about standing beneath freezing water at midnight because Arian Conti refuses to roll down his sleeves inside the palace grounds.

The truly infuriating part is that Arian seems completely unaffected by any of it. He remains exactly as he has always been: composed, and apparently immune to whatever humiliating internal collapse Caleb experiences every time the man enters his field of vision.

Fucking bastard.

Caleb has just decided that something must be done before the last remnant of his dignity is stripped away from him.

He glances across the corridor, and there he is. Arian standing beneath the archway, speaking quietly to one of the royal guards. There must be twenty people moving between them, staff and security crossing through the long gallery, yet Arian’s attention lifts straight to him.

Neither looks away.

And then Arian smiles, barely, but still that maddening little curve at the corner of his mouth, so slight that anyone else might have missed it entirely.

Something drops pleasantly and terribly low in his stomach. Caleb realises the bastard is very incredibly enjoying all the chaos that has taken over his brain.

His only other option is to get up and leave, but that would give Arian the satisfaction of knowing his presence bothers him, and Caleb would rather die. So he simply turns the page of his book and continues reading as though nothing has happened.

Arian doesn’t need to know that Caleb hasn’t registered a single word in the last five minutes.

++++

The threat isn’t inside the palace grounds, nor close enough to constitute an immediate breach. By the time the royal family is gathered inside one of the secured halls, Caleb has already been assured twice that the palace itself is under no danger.

Which is precisely why he cannot understand why Arian has gone outside.

“They are assessing the source of the threat,” Paul tells him again.

Caleb remains near the entrance, arms folded across his chest. “Yes, Paul. I understood you the first two times.”

“Then perhaps you’d like to sit down.”

“I wouldn’t.”

His family is gathered farther inside, surrounded by enough royal protection officers to defend a small country. Andrew is among them, which alone is reason enough for Caleb to remain exactly where he is. Besides, sitting would mean moving away from the doors, through which Arian could walk in at any time.

Not that he is specifically waiting for him, he simply wants an update.

An update Arian could have obtained perfectly well by sending someone else. He’s got the entire security team at his disposal, surely the Godfather does not require personally walking towards every potential threat that appears within a ten-mile radius.

Caleb refuses to examine the unpleasant twist in his stomach. Arian has survived considerably worse things than whatever is happening beyond those doors. Presumably, being the youngest godfather of a Sicilian mafia requires some basic skills in not getting killed.

His eyes remain locked outside,

When the doors finally open twenty something minutes later and Arian walks through, calm and perfectly unharmed, the relief that moves through Caleb is immediate.

There he is, not bleeding, or riddled with bullets, or whatever mafia-related bullshit Caleb’s imagination had constructed in his head.

Arian gives Caleb only a passing glance before going directly to the King. The report is brief: the source has been identified, the threat is contained and several people connected to it are already being dealt with by his men.

Caleb doesn’t want to know what the phrase dealt with exactly means.

When it seems like everything has calmed down, the king dismisses them. Caleb announces he’s going back to his study and leaves before anyone can suggest otherwise.

He has barely made it through the door when Arian follows him inside. Caleb doesn’t react, it has become quite recurring for Arian to come and go into his study whenever he needs to discuss matters on Caleb’s next public appearances. Such is the matter right now.

Arian closes the door behind him. “About your hospital visit tomorrow, I can increase security around the visit, change the route and reduce the number of people with access to you. Or, if you’d rather postpone until we are finished investigating tonight, we can arrange another date.”

Caleb is surprised by Arian giving him a choice, perhaps the conversation back at the banquet has really taken some effect, plus whatever came after that a few days ago.

“Do you think I need to postpone?”

“No.”

”Then I won't.” Caleb drops the papers in his hand onto the desk. “Do whatever you think is necessary with the security, I trust you.”

It’s subtle enough that someone else might have missed it, but Caleb has become rather unfortunately good at noticing Arian, which is why he doesn’t miss when Arian goes still for a second after his response.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That was clearly something.”

Arian’s expression settles back into its usual composure. “I’ll make the arrangements.”

Caleb lets it go, though something about having startled Arian pleases him more than it should. He begins sorting through the papers on his desk, only to remember the twenty minutes he’d just spent staring at a door and imagining increasingly unpleasant possibilities.

“You know, you didn't actually have to go running toward it yourself.”

Arian, who was just about to leave, stops and looks at him. “Towards what?”

“The threat.”

“That’s what I’m here for.”

“I know.”

“Then I’m not sure what you’re asking.”

Neither is Caleb, apparently. He only knows that when Arian had disappeared towards a possible gunfire, Caleb had stood by the entrance hating every second of it.

He exhales and sinks into his chair. “Never mind. Forget what I said.”

Arian nods as he’s about to turn around to leave again, Caleb calls him again. Arian already looks tired.

“Actually, when you told father your men were dealing with people responsible, what exactly does that mean?”

Arian sighs. “What do you mean?”

“Are we talking about stern questioning? Threats? Wait do you have like torture chambers, because I feel like that’s something I should know before spending several weeks in Sicily.”

“Why would you need to know that?”

Caleb shrugs. “Peace of mind?”

“I don't think knowing would give you a peace of mind."

Caleb considers it for a second, before slumping back into his chair again. “Fair point.”

Arian gives him a look that suggests this conversation is over, Caleb smiles and reaches for his abandoned speech.

++++

It is well past ten by the time they leave the hospital.

The streets have grown quieter with the hour, the route Arian chose taking them away from most of central London’s late night traffic. Shops have long since closed, pavements empty beneath rows of streetlamps, and the occasional car passes without consequence. Caleb barely notices.

It has been a long day, but a good one.

He spent most of it moving between the children’s wards, sitting beside beds and listening to stories that had very little to do with any of them being there. One little girl had spent twenty minutes explaining the social hierarchy of her classroom to him. Another boy had challenged him to a card game and accused him of cheating with utmost confidence when Caleb won.

He'd read the same picture book twice because three children had missed the first half, and by the end of the second reading he'd somehow accumulated an audience large enough that several nurses had stopped to listen too.

These are the royal duties Caleb likes.

There are no speeches written by someone else, or diplomatic conversations which go on for hours, no cameras demanding the correct angles of his face. He gets to sit on the edge of someone’s bed and make them laugh, he gets to listen, and imagine he can be useful simply by being there.

Caleb has donated millions to the hospital over the years, much of it directed towards pediatric care, research and treatment, and he knows those children are receiving some of the best care available to them. It doesn't get easier, seeing how illness in someone so young takes over, tiny bodies learning the language of treatments and procedures before they have had enough time to learn much else about the world.

He can’t fix that, sometimes all Caleb can give them is an afternoon in which they forget why they are in a hospital at all.

Perhaps that’s why he is still smiling when Arian looks over at him.

“You enjoyed yourself today.”

Caleb turns mildly surprised. Airan has spent most of the journey looking through something on his phone, and Caleb hadn’t realized he’d been paying attention.

“I did.” His smile returns. “I like talking to them. Children don’t particularly care whether you’re royal once they have decided you’re terrible at card games.”

“So it seemed.”

Caleb narrows his eyes. “I saw you smiling during my reading, by the way.” Arian looks up.

“Several times.” Caleb adds. “You seemed thoroughly entertained.”

A slow smirk appears. “I was simply considering how remarkably little difference there was between you and the children.”

Caleb’s smile disappears as Arian’s grows.

“You’re incredibly annoying.”

“So I've been told recently.” Caleb gives him a flat look and settles farther into the leather seat, refusing to provide the bastard with any more satisfaction.

The silence that follows is comfortable, which more than anything tells Caleb how much has changed between them. A few weeks ago, silence with Arian had felt like a challenge, something Caleb instinctively wanted to fill simply to see if he could provoke a reaction. Now there is nothing awkward about it.

Arian returns to his phone. Caleb lets his head fall against the seat and watches the passing lights blur beyond the window.

He's tired enough that his eyes eventually close.

He cannot have been drifting for more than a few minutes when the car stops so violently that his body jerks forward against the seat belt.

Ahead of them, red brake lights burn through the darkness, the two vehicles leading the convoy have stopped.

“What happened?” Arian asks. Whatever softness had been in his voice minutes ago is gone. He is already leaning forward, attention fixed on the road.

The driver touches his earpiece. “Lead vehicles stopped. There’s something blocking…”

A gunshot cracks through the night.

Caleb freezes.

A second gunshot follows, then a third, the sound is unmistakable. Arian moves before Caleb’s mind catches up.

His seat belt is off, his hand disappears beneath his coat and returns with a gun, his entire body changing with frightening speed from the man who had been teasing Caleb minutes earlier into something cold and alert.

Caleb catches his wrist.
“Wait, Arian…”

“Stay in the car.” There is nothing conversational about his voice now.

Caleb’s fingers tighten around him. “You’re going out there?”

“Lock the doors, do not open them to anyone except me, do you understand?”

Another shot sounds somewhere ahead. Arian’s gaze doesn’t leave his.

“Caleb, do you understand?”

Every instinct tells Caleb to argue, for once he doesn't.

“Yes.”

Arian is gone before Caleb can say anything else as the door slams shut behind him and Caleb waits. At first, he tells himself it will be a minute, but then several of them pass.

The driver locks the doors and draws his own weapon while other members of his security team close around the vehicle outside. Caleb becomes hyperaware of dark figures moving beyond the glass, distorted by the streetlamps and Caleb’s own reflection, but none of them are Arian.

Five more minutes pass, then eight and Caleb stops checking the time. Arian is still outside.

The city suddenly feels unnaturally quiet. There are no pedestrians here, no passing traffic, only the low hum of the engines and clipped voices from one of the men outside. Somewhere farther ahead people are shouting but Caleb cannot make out the words.

His heart is beating so hard against his chest, palms damp as he wipes one against his trousers and immediately hates himself for it.

Arian is fine.

And the thought doesn’t stop Caleb’s heart hammering harder every time a shadow moves beyond the windscreen. He tries to see between the vehicles ahead, but nothing.

*Where are you?*

Caleb contemplates if he should just fuck it and roll down the window to ask one of the security guards what's happening, but decides against it.

Another minute passes and Caleb leans forward.

*Come back. *

He doesn’t care about the convoy anymore, or what blocked the road or who fired first or what Arian has discovered. He just wants him back inside the fucking car.

Then Caleb’s door is wrenched open, he barely has time to react before a hand closes around his wrist.

It’s Arian.

Relief hits so hard, it almost makes him dizzy. Then Caleb sees his face.

“Out. Now.”

Arian reaches across him, releases the seat belt and pulls him from the car with enough urgency that Caleb nearly loses his footing.
“What happened?”

“Move.”

There is blood on Arian’s fingers. Caleb sees it only because they are wrapped around his wrist. Dark red has dried across his knuckles and disappeared beneath the edge of his cuff. There is more along the side of the gun in his other hand.

“Arian, you’re bleeding…”

“It’s not mine.” the answer comes quickly, it should make him feel better but it doesn't.

As Arian pulls him around the rear of the car, Caleb finally sees what had been hidden from him through the tinted windows.

A man is lying beside one of the vehicles ahead. One of Arian’s men is lying there, his body is unnaturally still, one arm stretched across the road, blood spreading slowly over the black tarmac beneath him. A few paces from the first body, another man is down, farther ahead.

Caleb’s steps falter. Arian’s grip tightens instantly.

“Don’t look, keep moving.”

Arian drags him away from the convoy and into the mouth of a narrow alley between two dark buildings.
“Arian, what the hell is happening?”

“Quiet.”

Caleb almost protests but there is the coldness in Arian’s voice that keeps his mouth shut.

They move quickly through the darkness, leaving the lights of the street behind them. Caleb has no idea where they are going, but Arian seems to. He takes one turn and then another without hesitation, leading him through narrow service lanes and deserted passages between buildings, always keeping himself slightly ahead.

All the arguments and sarcastic complaints die inside Caleb’s throat as he starts to understand the severity of the situation. Arian’s hand is locked around his, tighter than Caleb has ever felt it, and every second his thumb presses hard against Caleb’s skin as though reassuring himself that Caleb is still there.

It frightens Caleb more than the gunshots did.

By the time they reach the end of another long alley, both of them are breathing hard. A road stretches beyond it, empty and poorly lit, the buildings opposite are dark for the night.

Caleb nearly walks into Arian’s shoulder when he stops. For a moment, neither moves.

It’s an empty road, no security, no people and no car.

“Arian?”

He doesn’t answer, his gaze moving left and then right. Something changes in his face. It is small but Caleb sees it immediately. Arian’s expression doesn’t collapse, there is no visible panic or loss of control, if anything, he becomes unnaturally still.

But the color seems to drain from him as his eyes sweep across the empty road.

“This can’t be right.”

Caleb's pulse begins to climb again. "What can't be right?"

Arian touches his earpiece.

"Adrien."

Nothing.

"Adrien, respond. Where are you?"

Static crackles faintly through the silence.

"Adrien!"

The word comes sharper this time.

Still nothing. Arian swears under his breath and tries again, but Caleb is no longer watching the empty street, instead he's watching him.

For the first time since Paris, Caleb sees something in Arian that he has never seen before.

Uncertainty.

Arian doesn’t know what happens next and the realization turns Caleb's blood cold.

"Arian." His voice comes quieter now. He gives Arian’s hand a gentle squeeze. "What's going on?"

Arian lowers his hand from his ear. For several seconds, he says nothing.

Then he looks at Caleb and he sees fear reflecting in them, controlled, buried beneath years of discipline, but unmistakable now that Caleb knows where to look. Arian barely seems conscious of the blood drying across his hand.

His eyes are fixed entirely on Caleb. "There was supposed to be another car here."

"What?"

"Secondary extraction." Arian's voice is low. "Adrien was bringing it. He should have been here before we arrived."

The empty road suddenly seems much larger. Caleb looks past him into the darkness, only an empty street and the distant glow of the convoy they have left several blocks behind.

Arian's jaw tightens.

"The route was compromised, so is the extraction."

And Caleb understands something with dreadful certainty.

Arian has no plan.
