## Chapter 1: Prologue

Paris glitters differently at night.

Beyond the tall windows of the museum, the city is a constellation of gold, somehow the roads are always rain slicked during this time of the year, street lamps burning against the darkness.

Inside the light is softer.

Hundreds of warm bulbs hang from the museum’s impossibly high ceilings, golden light in soft warm hue spills across the polished marble floors reflecting in the glass protecting centuries old masterpieces. Murals stretched across the walls, portraits hang from gilded frames and sculptures placed beneath the pools of amber light while champagne moves through the room in crystal flutes.

Everything looks beautiful and expensive.

Arian stands where the light doesn’t quite reach.

His back rests against one of the enormous stone pillars bordering the main gallery, one ankle lazily crossed over the other. A black baseball cap sits low over his forehead, its shadow concealing most of his face. A dark leather jacket covers the rest of him, seeming deliberately out of place among the tailored tuxedos and designer suits.

He looks like he doesn’t belong, and that is precisely the point.

His gaze moves slowly across the room.

Security first, entrances, exits, balconies, windows blind spots, then the crowd.

Arian is trained to notice everything without appearing to notice anything at all.

For example the woman  in an emerald dress who keeps checking her phone.

A man in a crisp tailored suit stands too close to the eastern entrance.

Two members of the french security positioned near the private gallery. Three British guards near the prince.

A camera crew moving through the crowd capturing everything. Nothing out of the ordinary.

Then his attention returns to the center of the room.

To him.

Prince Caleb Whitmore.

The second son of the King.

And if the whispers surrounding the royal family were to go by, the most troublesome member of it.

Caleb is the reason half the people in the room even bothered to attend.

The auction is organized under his patronage, with the proceeds going toward several charitable causes. It has received an absurd amount of attention for an event that was on paper, nothing more than an evening of art and philanthropy.

But the royal family always understands the value of a spectacle.

And Caleb understands it particularly well.

There he stands, beneath the chandelier surrounded by cameras, diplomats, collectors and socialites, moving effortlessly from one conversation to the next.

He smiles when spoken to, laughs when something amuses him, shakes hands, accepts compliments and poses for photographs.

He’s elegant and untouchable. Born into the kind of beauty that seems almost unfair. He looks exactly like what the world expects a prince to look like.

Arian watches him for several seconds longer than necessary.

This is the first time he’s seen him in person. For months, Caleb existed only as a name on briefing documents, photographs, security reports and intelligence files.

Prince Caleb Whitmore, twenty-five years old, rightful heir according to the King’s wishes, despite the traditional line of succession being somewhat more complicated.

Educated, charismatic and occasionally reckless. Several reports mentioned minor scandals of rebellious nature, his reputation for disappearing from royal functions whenever he grew bored and an affinity for art.

He’s known for disregarding security protocols whenever he wants to, a handful of romantic entanglements that the British press finds endlessly entertaining. And a handful more that never reached the press.

Arian has read everything.

Apparently none of it prepared him for the actual man. Caleb moves again, and the crowd seems to move with him. Arian’s eyes narrow.

The king has already warned him, that Caleb is going to be a problem.

A soft burst of laughter reach him from across the gallery, Arian looks over.

Caleb has stopped in front of one of the paintings, a woman next to him asks something and Caleb turns toward the painting, expression changing.

“That one, its one of my favourites” his expression grows soft,  voice carries just enough for Arian to hear.

“I think people misunderstand it, they see the darkness and assume its about about grief.”

The crowd around him quiets instinctively.

The painting depicts a woman sitting alone on the floor, a torn piece of paper rests in her hands, stained in blood red ink, and a single tear traces the side of her face.

The woman next to Caleb smiles politely.

“Isn’t it grief though?” She asks.

Caleb gives a small smile and steps slightly closer. “I think it’s about wanting something you can’t have, or someone. It’s about loneliness, isn’t there something strangely beautiful about that kind of loneliness?”

Arian stares at him, maybe its the way Caleb speaks about art, or maybe its simply that the prince has somehow managed to make an entire room disappear around himself. Arian has spent months studying Caleb Whitmore, and he never expected the prince to be so….interesting.

And then Caleb is back to smiling widely again, more guarded, less open.

“Maybe that’s why I like it, but we all have our own interpretation of art i suppose” he says, smiling beautifully at the woman.

The crowd’s moving again with him to the next one, when Arian’s gaze shifts immediately.

A movement near the eastern corridor catches his attention. One of his men, in a black suit emerges from the door.

He stops beside Arian, without looking at him.

“Boss, your route is clear.”

Arian nods once. “The vehicle?”

“Secure. The area behind the museum has been swept twice. Your men are in position.”

“And the prince?”

“Three guards on him at all times. More outside.”

Arian glances toward Caleb, the man beside him lowers his voice.

“If anything happens, you’re the contingency.”

“I know.”

The man hands him a small black access card, Arian slips it inside his pocket.

“Stay close to the eastern exit.” He murmurs. “Don’t let anyone see you with me.”

The man nods and disappears into the crowd. Arian pushes himself away from the pillar and moves deeper into the gallery, keeping to the edges of the room.

The crowd thickens around Caleb as another group approaches him, Arian passes behind them.

For the first time he sees Caleb up close, just a quick glance was enough to notice the details.

A copper-brown vest fits neatly over a cream white shirt, the warm color almost glowing beneath the museum lights. His dark hair are soft and slightly disheveled, curls falling carelessly across his forehead.

There was something almost unfair about his face. A kind of softness, that seems almost contradictory beside the confidence with which he carries himself.

Then Caleb smiles and dimples appear. Arian looks away and continues toward the far end of the gallery.

There are fewer people here, the majority of the guest are flocking around the prince. Arian stops before an enormous painting that covers almost an entire section of the wall.

The painting is darker than others, a rainy street disappearing into fog, blurred neon lights reflect across the wet pavement, red green and purple bleeding together beneath the sky of black and charcoal gray.

There is no person in the painting, yet it feels occupied with something that can only be felt.

Loneliness, danger, peace.

There is something unsettling about the quiet, Arian likes it.

He doesn’t know how long he’s been standing there staring at the painting when suddenly he’s broken out of his reverie by the sound of soft footsteps approaching behind him.

Arian doesn’t move, even after the footsteps stop beside him, he already knows who it is.

“It’s a gorgeous one, isn’t it?”

Caleb.

Arian’s jaw tightens almost imperceptibly but he continues to look at the painting.

Caleb stands beside him, leaving enough distance to be polite. For a few seconds, neither speaks.

But then Caleb smiles.

“I couldn’t help notice you earlier.”

Arian’s gaze remains fixed ahead.

“You’ve been standing here for quite some time.”

Silence.

Caleb glances sideways at him. “You don’t talk much, do you?”

Arian finally looks toward him, only briefly and the prince is smiling, not the dazzling kind, he’s reserved for the cameras. A softer one, as if he genuinely wants to know the man standing beside him.

Before Arian catches himself again for staring too long, he looks away, back at the painting.

“I’m just looking.”

A flat, curt response, hoping it’ll make the prince go away. But unexpectedly it makes him smile wider.

“Right.”

Arian turns away first, and takes one step to leave when the words follow him.

“Wait.” The prince calls.

Arian stops, but doesn’t turn around. Behind him Caleb seems about to say something else when another voice interrupts.

“Your Highness.”

Arian glances over his shoulder, one of the royal officers runs up to him.

“Please stay with the group”

Caleb sighs, “I am literally standing right here Paul, don’t get so paranoid on me.”

Paul gives him an exhausted look of a man who has clearly heard this sentence several hundred times.

“I’m doing my job sir.”

“And I’m appreciating art.”

”You can appreciate it, where I can see you.”

Caleb rolls his eyes, Arian watches the little exchange for half a second longer and then finds his cue to leave.

He turns the left corner and almost reaches the eastern corridor when…

BOOM.

The sound tears through the walls of the museum. The floor beneath Arian’s boots tremble as his legs freeze for a millisecond. The glass shatters somewhere behind him, followed by a second explosion and screams erupt across the gallery.

Arian turns on his heels instantly as the lights flicker violently overhead, for one brief second, the entire museum goes black. Then the emergency lights come alive in a wash of red and Arian’s hand goes beneath his jacket, fingers closing around the weapon hidden there.

His eyes immediately finds the one person in the room who matters.

Caleb.

But the prince is nowhere in sight.

The first gunshot cracks through the gallery, then another and the museum erupts.

People scream and scatter in every direction, champagne glasses shattering against marble as people rush toward the exit frantically. Somewhere behind him, a woman is crying, security shout out orders over one another as more gunfire answers from the opposite end of the hall.

Arian barely hears anything, eyes sharp and laser focused, searching.

Caleb.

He finds the British security near the eastern doors, weapons drawn and formation broken as they return fire toward the far end of the gallery.

Arian’s jaw tightens, panic settling in hoping one of the guards got Caleb out already. They have protocols for this and have rehearsed this scenario many times.

He moves through the crowd fast, slipping between panicked guests, hand remaining beneath the jacket, wrapped around the grip of his gun as his eyes sweep across the room.

Just as he rounds a corner, he comes to a stop.

There, near the windows. The prince is alive and apparently has decided that the middle of an active gunfire is the perfect time to become a fucking hero.

Caleb is helping an elderly woman toward the nearest exit, one hand steadying her elbow while the other gestures sharply toward the doors.

Another wave of people surge past him, and Caleb helps direct them away from the gunfire and toward the exit. Arian stares at him for one incredulous second.

This idiot.

He’s standing in the open, making himself visible and an easy target.

Arian crosses the distance between them before he can think better of it, his hand close around Caleb’s wrist.

Caleb barely has time to turn before Arian pulls him sharply backward.

“What….”

Arian drags him behind the stone wall separating the gallery from the adjoining corridor. Caleb stumbles, as his back meets the wall, his eyes widen and for a second neither moves.

Arian’s hand remains on his wrist, while his other hand holds the weapon low against his thigh, he quickly looks over the prince, checking for injuries,

“Are you out of your mind”

Caleb’s startled voice cuts through his thoughts.

“What exactly do you think you’re….”

”Quiet.”

Arian tilts his head, letting the shadow of his cap shift just enough for Caleb to see his eyes.

“Come with me.” not a request and Caleb stares like he’s seen an alien. Perhaps he’s never been spoken to like this in his life.

“Excuse me?”

Arian doesn’t bother answering, he tightens his grip and pulls him toward the corridor.

“Hey, let go of me.”

“Keep walking”

“You cannot simply….”

“Keep your voice down”

”How dare you, I’m perfectly capable of…”

Another gunshot explodes somewhere behind them. Caleb flinches, and for a moment forgets that he’s being very forcefully dragged away by Arian.

Arian takes it as his cue and pushes them through a narrow door concealed behind a wall, a dark staircase appears and Arian brings Caleb to the front, shielding him with his body.

“Move.”

Caleb stares at him as though considering whether to argue again, but then another burst of gunfire echoes through the museum and his expression changes, he simply nods and climbs the stairs, Arian behind him.

They reach the rooftop doors, and Arian quickly shoves it open, and pulls Caleb through and slams the door shut behind them.

The cold air rushes over them instantly.

And then there’s silence. For a brief moment, there is nothing but the sound of their breathing. Caleb is standing directly in front of him, too close, back pressed into the door.

His chest rise and fall rapidly from the climb, his hair once carefully styled for the camera have now fallen loose across his forehead, his clothes slightly rumpled.

The moment breaks, when Caleb shoves him hard.

“Are you completely out of your mind?”

Arian lets himself be shoved, as the force moves him half a step back.

Caleb comes closer. “You dragged me away from my security, forced me through a crowd at gunpoint and brought me onto a rooftop in the middle of an assassination attempt.”

Arian says nothing, just lets the prince throw a fit.

Caleb’s eyes narrow. “Do you have any idea who I am?”

”Yes.”

“Then you should understand precisely how absurd this is.”

”You’re alive.”

“Should I thank you for stating the obvious?”

Arian sighs, already regretting this, yes the king had warned him that the prince will be difficult, but he didn’t mention he’ll be downright insufferable.

“You cannot simply abduct a member of the royal family, I can have you arrested and sentenced….”

Arian catches his shoulder when Caleb moves too close and swiftly presses him back against the rooftop door.

“Calm down, Your Highness.”

And Caleb goes quiet.

“Unless you’d prefer to give whoever is trying to kill you a clear shot.” And that shuts the prince up.

For approximately two seconds.

“That would be considerably easier if you stop pointing a gun at me.”

Feeling called out, Arian lowers the gun slightly.

“You’re welcome.”

Caleb raises an eyebrow. “For what?”

”Saving your life.”

Caleb stares, and then his eyes narrow.

“You caused this, didn’t you?”

Arian blinks, “What?”

“Tch, you appear out of nowhere, drag me away and now you’re standing here behaving like I ought to thank you.”

”I didn’t cause the attack.”

”You certainly have the look of a man who might.”

Arian inhales a breath trying to keep his temper under control and not lose it in front of the prince.

“Stay here.”

“I don’t take orders from strangers.”

”You do tonight.”

”I most certainly do not.”

Another gunshot sounds from below, this one is closer to the staircase.

Arian sees as Caleb freezes, the color draining slightly from his face. Arian steps closer immediately.

“Quiet.”

This time Caleb obeys.

Footsteps echo somewhere beneath them, slow and heavy, someone is coming up the staircase. Arian positions himself between Caleb and the door, one hand ready at his weapon.

When another gunshot fires, Caleb flinches behind him, as his fingers suddenly close around Arian’s arm. Arian can feel the prince is afraid, instinctively he holds onto the prince’s wrist and squeezes a little to keep him grounded.

“Stay behind me.”

Caleb nods, for once there is no argument. They wait, gun drawn out pointing at the door. Just then another shot is heard, and then silence.

They wait for five seconds, ten, fifteen, nothing.

His men have probably intercepted whoever made it this far. Eventually the footsteps disappear and Arian remains still for another few seconds, before finally releasing the breath he’s been holding.

His grip on Caleb’s wrist loosens, and then he lets go.

Caleb inhales sharply and leans back against the door, breathing heavily.

Arian glances at him, his hair is a mess now, cheeks flush from the cold and adrenaline, he looks shaken but is trying hard to keep a calm exterior. Then he lifts his eyes and looks at Arian, carefully, as though trying to figure him out.

“Who are you?”

Arian looks at him. “That’s not important.”

“It feels rather important at the moment.”

“Are you hurt?” Arian changes the subject. Caleb glances down at himself.

“No.” He looks back up. “You just saved my life.”

Arian takes a deep breath and then extends his hand out to Caleb.

“I need to get you out of here.”

Caleb stares at the offered hand and then at Arian.

“I don’t think I should trust you.”

Arian follows his gaze and understands, with a quiet sigh he slips the gun back beneath his jacket.

Then he steps closer. “Whether you trust me or not, I’m your best chance of leaving this building alive.”

“Is that meant to reassure me?”

The prince is so damn stubborn, Arian wants to punch a wall but he keeps calm. “It’s meant to be true.”

That earns him a laugh, light and soft. The prince has nearly been killed twice in the last five minutes and somehow he’s laughing.

“How exactly do you intend to save me while also threatening me in the same breath?”

“You can start by not standing here like a fool and expose yourself to the very eager assassin currently on the loose looking for you.”

Caleb’s smile is soft, completely inappropriate for the situation.

Arian has a sudden unwelcome thought that perhaps the photographers haven’t done him justice. He immediately dismisses it.

“You are one incredibly rude person.”

“Come.” He doesn’t wait, simply takes Caleb’s arm and this time the prince doesn’t resist. They cross the rooftop toward the opposite wall, and stop beside a narrow door.

“This leads to the rear staircase, the route is clear, your guards are waiting below, a car will take you back to the palace”

Caleb looks into the dark staircase and frowns. “You expect me to walk down there?”

“Yes.”

Caleb stares at the staircase again. “Right, so that’s not happening.”

”That is your only way out.”

Caleb sighs. “I have apparently been rescued by a man with absolutely no appreciation for how reassuring he is.”

Arian shoves the door open. “Go.”

Caleb doesn’t move, still contemplating, instead he looks at him. “You really aren’t going to tell me your name?”

“No.”

If Arian’s patience is beginning to fray, he doesn’t show.

“Not even after all this?” Caleb waves his hand between the two gesturing at him and Arian.

“No.” Arian says again.

Caleb takes another sigh

“You are terribly rude.”

“Your Highness, there is an armed assassin somewhere in this museum who would very much like to kill you.”

He gestures toward the staircase.

“Your choices are remarkably simple. You can walk down those stairs and leave safely, or you can return to the gallery and wait for the man trying to kill you to find you.”

“Seriously….”

”If you want to die that badly, I won’t stop you.” Arien says finally.

At this point Arian expects another argument, instead, Caleb slowly pulls his arm out of Arian’s grip.

Then he looks at him, really looks, as amusement fades from his face. Arian thinks perhaps he’s mistaken but a softer expression takes over on Caleb’s face.

“Do I at least get a name?” He asks once again.

Arian stays silent and Caleb sighs.

“Well it was worth a shot.”

Then he lifts his hand, and before Arian could understand what’s happening, the prince’s fingers catch the brim of his baseball cap.

Arian inhales softly. “Don’t…”

But it’s too late, Caleb lifts the cap from his head, and for the first time there is nothing between them.

Arian sees it happen as Caleb’s breath is caught, eyes wide slightly as they travel over Arian’s face, as though he expected something entirely different. A few strands of hair fall on Arian’s face from the wind and for a moment neither of them speak.

“Wow.” Caleb’s voice drop as Arian’s jaw tightens. “You really are my knight in shining armour aren’t you?”

Arian knows he shouldn’t stare too much, shouldn’t react the way his eyes follow the prince’s soft smile, should take the cap back that is still clutched in between Caleb’s fingers.

Instead he just remains there, watching. Caleb’s eyes are impossibly bright beneath the night sky, and far too close.

Arian reaches past him and shoves the door behind Caleb.

“Leave.”

His voice should have come out sharper, deeper, but he could only manage a soft breath, eyes still on Caleb’s face.

Caleb blinks, the wind sweeps his soft hair across his forehead. “So that’s it?”

“Yes.”

“After saving my life?”

“Go.” Arian realizes he’s almost on the verge of pleading at this point because he wants this to be over now.

Caleb smiles, and then, apparently survival has taught him absolutely nothing, he tilts his head.

“Not even a goodbye kiss?”

Arian goes completely still.

Of all the things the prince could have said.

His gaze betrays him, before he can stop it, down to Caleb’s mouth. Soft and slightly red from the cold. Close enough to make a very stupid thought cross his mind.

The prince notices, and something changes in his expression, the teasing softens as his gaze drops to Arian’s lips as well, and for one suspended second neither of them move.

The world below starts to feel far away, they are so close that Arian can feel Caleb’s breath, could hear his own. One movement, one mistake and the line between duty and something far more dangerous will disappear.

Arian inhales a sharp breath and steps back.

“Go.”

Caleb looks almost disappointed, and before he could say anything, Arian grabs his arm and pushes him out through the door, Caleb’s eyes widen.

“Wait…”

Arian closes the door shut and stands there for several seconds. He expects the prince to shout, to call him out but is only met with silence.

Then footsteps start descending the staircase, one flight then another.

Only when he’s certain Caleb has continued his path down the stairs, does Arian turn back toward the edge of the ledge.

He waits, three minutes, five minutes, six. Then one of the lights move.

Arian narrows his eyes as Caleb emerges from the rear entrance and 5 guards immediately surround him.

Safe at last.

Arian’s shoulders finally loosen, as he watches the prince being ushered toward the waiting vehicle.

Then Caleb stops.

Arian’s breath catch in his throat as the prince turns, and slowly almost instinctively looks upward, straight toward the rooftop, at him.

For several seconds they simply stare at one another. Caleb doesn’t smile, neither does Arian.

Arian looks away for a couple of seconds, and when he looks back again  Caleb is already inside the car.

As the vehicle safely pulls away from the street, Arian allows himself to breathe.

And then his expression hardens. The prince is safe, the job is done, now there is an assassin to find so Arian can put a bullet through his skull.

He storms toward the door, taking out his gun, eyes fixed on the entrance of the museum below.

Someone came dangerously close to kill Prince Caleb Whitmore tonight, and whoever did it, made a very serious mistake, they had threatened someone under his protection, and Arian intends to make the bastard regret it.

His fingers tightens around his gun as he turns toward the staircase, the softness of the rooftop disappearing behind the cold certainty settling back into his bones.

He takes two steps toward the stairs before something feels wrong.

Arian stops.

His hand goes automatically to the top of his head.

His eyes closed.

The prince took his hat with him.

Of course he had.

Arian exhales slowly through his nose.

Shit.

Somehow, he has a feeling that losing his hat tonight is going to be the least of his problems.
