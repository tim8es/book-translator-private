As soon as the car pulls away from Pietro’s mansion, Arian exhales a breath he didn’t realize he was holding.

The car has barely travelled ten minutes when Caleb leans forward and asks the driver to stop.

Arian lifts his eyes from the window. “What is it?”

“I’d like to walk.” Caleb doesn’t wait for his reaction, as he is already unfastening his seat belt.

“It’s late.”

Before Arian has a chance to remind him that concern for his safety is precisely why they are in a moving vehicle, with a driver and two men following behind in another car, Caleb has already opened the door.

As he steps out into the pavement, and glances up at the night sky, Arian lets out a sigh and closes his eyes briefly. A second later, he’s unfastening his own seat belt and following Caleb out.

The streets are quiet at this hour. Most of the shops have shuttered their doors, leaving only the occasional cafe open beneath the warm glow of its lamps. The old buildings stand close together along narrow cobblestone streets. Beyond the railings, the river moves lazily through the city along the footpath, reflections of streetlights scattering across its surface like ribbons of gold.

Caleb seems content to walk without a destination, as he moves at an unhurried pace, his shoulders loose beneath his coat, gaze wandering over the building and the water.

Arian finds himself matching his pace.

For a while they don't speak, it’s an unfamiliar sort of silence he finds that he doesn’t mind.

“So,” Caleb says eventually, looking ahead rather than at him. “Your fiance’s lovely.”

Arian turns his head, there is a soft smile on Caleb’s face but there is no accusation in his voice, which for some reason makes the remark more difficult to answer.

“She’s not my fiance.” Arian says, hoping just this simple statement leaves no room for doubt.

“Ah, I gathered as much.” Caleb nods thoughtfully. “Though she certainly knows how to put on a show. The bitterness slips through before he could disguise it.

Florence owns an estate I’ve been trying to acquire and she wants assurances that the Conti’s and Caruso’s stay committed to the deal together. Valentina and I agreed to give her those assurances.”

“How romantic,” he murmurs.

“You don't have to give me any explanations Arian, your business is your own.”

After that kiss with Valentina, Caleb had looked at him across the ballroom with an expression Arian cannot simply put out of his mind.

Arian sees the faintest shift in Caleb’s expression, the monetary uncertainty passing through his eyes before they settle back into that infuriatingly composed smile.

“I’ve known Valentina for almost 10 years, our families have done business together for a long time.”

“Which makes this marriage quite convenient.” Caleb says.

“Of course. Though I believe there’s some history there?”

Arian glances at him. “Casually, yes. There were no promises, and neither of us expected anything from it.”

He leaves the rest unsaid, and sees no reason to catalogue the details of a past that means very little to him, but he is not going to pretend it didn’t exist. Caleb seems to understand all the same.

“Well doesn’t she sound perfect? She is beautiful, clever and connected to your world.”

Arian’s lips tilt into a small smile, as he looks at him. There is something almost absurd about the care Caleb is taking to make his indifference convincing, the bitterness, and perhaps a hint of jealousy is something Arian finds difficult to ignore.

“She is not the one I want,” Arian says, quietly.

“Well, whoever has managed to capture your attention, I do hope they have a remarkable tolerance for the less charming aspects of your profession.”

Arian raises his eyebrows, as Caleb fixes his jacket and starts walking ahead.

“One can only imagine the sort of romantic inconveniences that come with being involved with a man like you.”

Arian watches him walk a few paces ahead, and is beginning to suspect the prince is not nearly as good at pretending as he thinks he is.

He should be offended by the suggestion that his affections might be an inconvenience, instead he finds himself positively entertained by the possibility that Caleb Whitmore might just be a little jealous. It’s a selfish thought, but Arian has never claimed to be a particularly selfless man.

And if the roles were reversed, he figures he would be considerably worse.

Arian watches the Prince walk ahead and follows, unable to quite suppress the smile that has started to play at his lips.

Caleb is beginning to realize that Arian’s horse, Nerone, is the only creature in the entire Conti estate with any sense. And that’s saying a lot, because he is one stubborn beast.

The horse has admittedly attempted to throw him into a hedge twice this afternoon, and has spent the first ten minutes of their ride behaving as though carrying a prince on his back constitutes a personal insult. But Nerone has eventually settled into a rhythm, and Caleb can't seem to stay mad at him for too long.

Horses are honest in a way people rarely are. They can be stubborn as hell, even downright vicious but they never pretend to be anything other than what they are. They are in Caleb’s experience, far easier to understand than most human beings.

Since childhood, Caleb has found peculiar peace in the way a horse feels beneath him, the simple trust required to move together, making the world smaller around him, perhaps he needed that freedom today.

Particularly after discovering that Miss Valentina Caruso possesses the audacity to arrive at Arian’s home whenever she wants, and demand a meeting. Worse is, how easily she is granted one.

Both Arian and Valentina disappeared behind the closed doors of Arian’s private study. One hour turned to several, and the bloody meeting continued.

It doesn’t bother him.

Arian can invite as many guests as he wants in his home, it makes no difference to him.

Although, he can't help wonder what could possibly constitute a meeting so private that the door to Arian's study remains shut, repeatedly, irritatingly. Valentina is apparently important enough to have him ignoring the rest of the world for hours at time. Apparently their little sham engagement requires extensive planning.

Caleb urges Nerone into a slower gait, as the sun descends towards the Sicilian hills, spreading amber light across the courtyard. He has been riding for half an hour, long enough to feel the worst of his irritation settle into something tolerable.

He figures, blaming Nerone for the state of his romantic affairs is hardly fair. The horse probably has enough problems of his own.

For him.

Again.

For a man who has just stared down an armed standoff without hesitation, Arian finds himself entirely unprepared for the sight of one furious prince.
