The newspaper lands in front of Caleb with enough force to make the silverware tremble. He glances down at it, the photograph splashed across the front page is unfortunately, a rather unflattering one.

It’s him in the picture, standing in the middle of the crowded museum hall, one hand extended toward the elderly woman while people hurry around him in panic. His copper vest is unmistakable beneath the flashing cameras, his expression far less composed than the newspapers have apparently decided to make it seem.

The headline is actually worse.

**CROWN PRINCE ACCUSED OF RECKLESS BEHAVIOR DURING PARIS ATTACK.**

Caleb sighs.

“Well,” he murmurs, reaching for his tea. “At least, they caught my good side.”

His father standing at the head of the table sighs, expression stern.

“Caleb.”

There are few words in the English language capable of sounding quite as much like a death sentence when spoken by the King.

Caleb takes a leisurely sip.

“Yes, Father?” He says innocently.

“You were specifically instructed to remain with your security.”

“I did.”

”You disappeared and left them in the middle of an active assassination attempt.”

“So I just relocated a bit, you are making it sound so dramatic.”

Across the table, Eleanor snorts quietly into her coffee mug. Matilda sitting across from him hides the smallest smile behind her hand.

Meanwhile Andrew remains emotionless, no expression on his face whatsoever like always.

The eldest Whitmore sibling is sitting next to his father with the sort of immaculate composure that seems almost unnatural. Tall and broad shouldered, with perfectly styled blonde hair and striking green eyes. Andrew possesses the sort of face that belongs on the cover of a magazine dedicated to beautiful people with terrible intentions.

Nevertheless, he looks every inch the future king. Caleb always thought that is rather unfortunate.

“Caleb,  you were photographed helping civilians instead of taking cover.” The King reminds him firmly.

“There was an elderly woman trapped near the window, I wanted to help.”

“You should have let the security assist her.’

“They were busy trying to prevent everyone else from being shot.” Caleb reasons once again.

“Caleb.” Another warning.

“She was old.”  Caleb stares back. “She needed help, so I helped  her.”

“You could have been killed.”

“But I wasn’t.”

“That is not the point Caleb.” The King says.

“It seems to be the rather important part from where I’m sitting.” Caleb says. He doesn’t wanna argue anymore but he can’t seem to keep quiet, his father never understands.

And then Andrew finally speaks.

“You always have to make everything about yourself.”

Caleb’s eyes move toward him. Andrew’s voice is calm, almost bored. “You see a camera and suddenly you have to perform.” Caleb raises his eyebrow.

“I wasn’t aware helping an elderly woman constituted a performance.”

Andrew’s face hardens. “Not everything requires an audience, you have no idea what kind of responsibility your behavior creates for the rest of us. You cannot even take accountability for it, how will you take the crown when it’s your time?”

Caleb leans back in his chair, there it is again, the speech that Andrew has been giving him for years.

“You know,” Caleb says lightly, “I don’t actually want the crown, and you don’t have to keep reminding me.”

“I’ll remind you whenever necessary, questioning your duty is not what is expected of a prince.”

Caleb’s smile fades slightly. What does Andrew know about duty when all his tactics have been to manipulate father into taking the crown.

“I think you’ve made your position quite clear, Andrew, I’d appreciate you saving the lecture for the next time.”

Andrew rises from his chair. The movement is so sudden it startles Caleb. And then he sees as his big brother walks around the table and stops in front of him. Caleb slightly stiffens.

“You are reckless.” Andrew says quietly. “And every time you make a mistake, someone else has to clean it up.”

Andrew bends slightly, bringing himself closer to Caleb’s face. One of the things that Andrew seems to do a lot is invade his personal space, to the point it sometimes terrifies Caleb and leaves an unsettling feeling in his stomach.

“If you don’t start taking these threats seriously, one day, you’re going to end up dead in a ditch.”

His hand comes up and his fingers close around Caleb’s chin, titling his face up, not hard enough to hurt, but enough to make a point.

Caleb instinctively leans backward, something cold crawls beneath his skin.

Andrew smiles. “People talk, you may not care but I do, especially when it's about you.”

For a second, neither move, Caleb sits still until Andrew releases him and steps back. Caleb lets out a breath he doesn’t know he is holding.

“Alright, Andrew, you’ve made your point” Matilda finally speaks, her voice calm yet firm. Her eyes move to Caleb.

Caleb hopes Matilda doesn’t notice his discomfort. He absolutely hates that he allows Andrew to have so much control over him. It makes his skin crawl, whenever his older brother gets like this.

Matilda is the eldest daughter of the Whitmore siblings, and there is something about her that makes people instinctively listen when she speaks. Long black hair falling over her shoulders, framing features so similar to Caleb’s that the resemblance is unmistakable. But where Caleb’s presence is charming and playful, Matilda is always composed.

She carries herself like someone who has spent her entire life learning how to survive rooms full of people who underestimate her. Had circumstances been different, she would be the next in line for the Crown.

Instead, centuries of tradition decided that being born a woman makes her somehow less suited to rule.

Matilda has never forgiven the institution for that. Nor has she allowed it to stop her.

The King trusts her more than almost anyone in the palace, she is his strategist when political matters become too complicated for simple diplomacy.

Andrew handles alliances but Matilda understands the monarchy from inside, out. And Caleb loves her for it.

“Caleb,” she says gently. “How did you get out?”

”What?”

”The museum. The security informed us that you were able to reach them on time, did you find the exit yourself?”

For a moment Caleb says nothing.

And then Paris returns to him, not the gunshots or the screaming, *him*. The black baseball cap, the leather jacket, a hand firmly closing around his wrist, and a deep voice telling him…

*Come with me*

Caleb’s fingers tighten unconsciously around his teacup. The thing is he hasn’t been able to stop his brain from thinking about the man who saved him that night. He could still faintly remember the scent, something warm and woody beneath a trace of smoke. He could still remember the way those dark eyes watched him, as though Caleb was simply….Caleb.

Nothing more, nothing less.

And then there was that hat, the one that he had taken it with him, technically stole it, now currently hidden underneath the drawer in his bedroom. Not exactly his proudest moment, but when he was making his way down that dark staircase alone, every nerve in his body was screaming. He kept his fingers wrapped around the brim of the cap, allowing to feel it, to keep him grounded. The hat was the proof that the man had been real, and not someone he imagined.

“Caleb?’

Matilda’s voice pulls him back, he blinks and then quickly recovers, smiling.

“Sorry, I… uh escaped through the roof.”

Matilda briefly glances at her father, before she looks back at Caleb again. “How’d you get to the roof?”

Caleb feels a bit cornered, because Matilda is asking too many questions now.

“Uhh.. you know..I guess I was lucky enough to slip away and found the exit on time.”

He doesn't meet Matilda’s eyes and she doesn’t look convinced but lets it go. Eleanor next to Matilda chuckles.

“You had quite an adventurous evening little brother, I’m kind of jealous.” She says playfully, lightning the mood and Caleb laughs with her.

“More like a suicidal evening” Andrew mutters. Eleanor rolls her eyes.

Matilda lets out a small sigh and smiles at Caleb. “For what it’s worth, the assassin’s dead.” She says.

Caleb’s head snaps toward her, “Wait… they got him?”

“One of the guards found him before he could escape, according to the reports, he was roaming the museum searching for you, when the guard got to him and shot him on sight.”

“Do we know which security personnel did it?” Caleb asks, he doesn't know why he asked that, perhaps he’s just hoping against hope that he can find something about him.

Caleb notices Matilda shares another look with her father before looking back at Caleb.

“We…have yet to find out, I’ll be having a meeting with the security team tomorrow morning and get the briefings of the incident.”

Caleb knows she’s bullshitting, the news doesn’t bring the relief he expected, because whoever that man on the rooftop was, he seemed terrifyingly certain that the assassin wouldn’t reach him.

And it still doesn’t answer Caleb’s question, as to why he had been there, where had he come from and why the memory of him, his lingering touch and his voice had stayed with him.

The King finally exhales and gets up from his seat. He’s been silent throughout the exchange, watching his children with an expression Caleb can’t quite decipher.

His father is often like this, quietest when he knows something. Caleb learned a long time ago, the silence from the king is rarely comforting.

“We are glad you are safe son, just please don’t do something foolish like this again.” His tone comes out soft and gentle.

Caleb gives him a small smile. “Thank you.”

“I am serious Caleb, this cannot happen again.”

“I’ll do my best.”

“No, you will do better. One more reckless decision and I will have to take drastic measures.” His father’s voice hardens. Caleb remains smiling.

“Understood.”

He can feel Andrew watching him, like always, Caleb refuses to look.

“Matilda, come with me. I need your opinion on the reports from Brussels.”

Matilda pushes her chair back and nods curtly. Before leaving she turns toward Caleb. Caleb looks at her and sees the soft smile on her face, comforting and reassuring.

“I’m glad you’re alive, little one.” She says. Caleb returns her smiles.

“I am not going anywhere.”

Matilda squeezes his shoulder. “See that you don’t.” Then she follows their father out.

Eleanor waits approximately three seconds before standing up, she comes around the table and bumps her shoulder against Caleb.

“Sooo, rifle shooting?”

Caleb  glances at her, a grin appearing on his face already. “You know I’ll never say no to that.”

“Good, let's get out of this dull place.” She pulls him up and hooks her arm through his.

Caleb happily lets her drag him. As they begin walking toward the garden, he feels it.

Andrew’s gaze follows him, watching, but he doesn't turn around. Andrew can sit in one place for hours if he wants to, and apparently staring at Caleb is an excellent way to spend an afternoon.

Weirdo.

He tightens his arm around Eleanor as they leave, and for once he wants nothing to do with the crown or assassination attempts and certainly nothing to do with his brother.

Some rifle shooting ought to quiet his mind for a while. But even then, inconveniently, there’s one thing he still can’t seem to stop thinking about.

The stranger in the black leather jacket beneath his trembling fingers.

Pair of dark eyes, and deep voice telling him to listen.

And the black hat hidden in the drawer, in his bedroom.

The rifle cracks across the grounds, Caleb lowers the weapon and watches the target swing gently in the distance.

“Bullseye.”

Eleanor glances sideways and snorts. “Show-off.”

“And you say that as though it's a bad thing”

“It is when you’re so insufferable about it.” Eleanor retorts, she’s lifting up her rifle and aiming at the target, when a voice comes from behind.

“Big brother.”

Caleb barely has time to turn before a blur  of dark hair collides with his side. He laughs, catching Cecily around the shoulders as she wraps both arms around him.

“Good heavens, where do you get all that strength in that little body of yours.”

“I heard you nearly got yourself killed.”

“Nearly?” Caleb chuckles. Cecily pulls back, narrowing her eyes. “I mean I survived so it’s all good.”

“Actually, I heard all about how you managed to escape through the rooftop by yourself, that must have felt like a movie”

“Tch, that’s what I said.” Eleanor intervenes from beside them, eyes still locked to her target.

“Are you sure you’re alright?” Cecily asks, looking at his face as if inspecting to find any injuries.

“I am perfectly fine.” Caleb reassures her, ruffling her hair as she tries to swat his hand away.

Cecily being the youngest of the Whitmore's has been spoiled accordingly, and everyone knows it. She’s charming enough to get away with most of the things and clever enough to know exactly when to use that charm.

Although Caleb gets along well with all his sisters, he’s especially been powerless against Cecily.

“I’ve been studying all morning. I have three chapters of constitutional history to finish and a tutor who apparently  believes that sleep is an optional human function.”

“That sounds dreadful.”

“It is dreadful, which is exactly why I have decided to drop out.”

“You’ve said that every week for the past six months.” Eleanor says, eyes laser focused on the target, as she aims with her rifle.

“And one day I shall mean it.”

Caleb smiles at his sisters.

For a while everything feels normal. No Assassins, or newspaper headlines, or suffocating dinners. Just his sisters and the familiar rhythm of their teasing.

“Caleb, what actually happened in Paris?” Cecily asks, expression changing. That’s the thing about her, she is always perceptive and notices things.

Caleb lets out a sigh, and fakes a smile. “I’ve already told everyone.”

“You said you got lucky, and that usually means there’s a version of a story you're not telling anyone.”

“Well that's the only version you’ll get out of me because it's the truth.”

“Hmm, you’re hiding something.”

“I am not, and you are annoyingly persistent.”

“I am the youngest, being annoying is my birthright.”

Eleanor laughs, as she joins them. “Cecily just drop it, I think it’s best we put this whole assassination shit show behind us.”

“Ugh fine….I’ll drop it, at least for now.”

“I think after everything, we deserve a celebration.” Eleanor suggests.

“Honestly I could use a drink, a strong one.” Cecily says.

“That’s your solution to everything.” Eleanor retorts, rolling her eyes.

“I nearly lost my brother to an assassin, I deserve a champagne.”

“Okay, I am afraid I have to support her logic.” Caleb agrees.

Eleanor sighs. “Fine, let’s go before the two of you become unbearable.”

++++

The lounge occupies an entire wing of the palace, tucked away from formal halls and prying eyes. Low lighting washes over deep velvet sofas and a polished marble bar stands against one wall, lined with crystal glasses and an impressive collection of drinks.

In the center, an open space serves as a dance floor, making the room feel less like part of the palace and more like a private club.

The royal family’s lounge is the closest thing any of them have to a normal night out. Caleb of all people can’t exactly walk into a London club without causing a minor diplomatic incident.

So they built their own version of one. A place where the siblings disappear when they want to forget they are royal for a few hours.

By the time Caleb arrives, music is already playing, while champagne bottles are popping and people are already on the dance floor. Looks like Cecily has invited half a dozen friends.

Caleb loosens the collar of his white dress shirt, finally feeling like he can breathe. “Much better.”

Eleanor hands him a glass. “To surviving Paris.”

Caleb raises his glass. “To surviving this family.”

They clink glasses together, as Cecily appears beside him, wrapping her arms around his. 
“There’s someone else coming.”

Caleb looks at her and frowns, Cecily’s smile widens.

“Good lord Cecily, what did you do now?”

“Don’t be dramatic, I am doing you a favor.”

“You….” Before he can answer, a familiar voice comes from behind him.

“Hello, Your Highness.”

Oh…of course.

Caleb turns. And there he is, tall, dark haired, unfairly handsome.

Landon Reed is the son of a senior parliamentary figure, who grew up around politics without ever seeming particularly interested in inheriting it.

Caleb chuckles half heartedly, as the man walks up to him. “You’re kidding me right?”

He’s dressed in a loose black shirt, with the sleeves rolled carelessly to his forearms, hair styled with enough effort to look effortless.

He is charming, smart, and kind,  and unfortunately for Caleb, very good at making him forget himself in the bedroom. They have never been serious, and always had a non exclusive agreement. Until a month ago, when Landon admitted that he had started wanting something more.

Caleb couldn’t give him that, and things just ended. At least for a while.

But now he’s here again, and he’s smiling, that oh so charming smile.

“It’s been a while, Caleb.” His voice is always a little bit softer when he speaks to him.

Caleb swallows.

“It has.”

Landon steps closer and takes his hand, turning it gently, he presses a kiss on his knuckles. Caleb’s breath catches in his throat.

“Good to see you alive and well, my prince.”

Caleb retrieves his hand almost instantly. “Don’t.” Landon’s smile softens.

“Don’t what?”

“Landon, why are you here?”

“It’s been a month, I hoped you at least missed me?”

Caleb rolls his eyes. “Tch. I was rather busy surviving psycho killer on the loose. Forgive me for not finding the time to miss your insufferably arrogant face”

Landon throws his head back, laughter bubbling out of him. “And you’re still impossible.”

“I prefer charming.”

Landon smirks, and steps a bit closer, Caleb doesn’t move, just lets him get into his space. “That too.” Landon whispers.

Across the room Eleanor and Cecily exchange delighted looks, and Caleb glares at them.

As the evening dissolves into laughter, and the music grows louder, Caleb manages to forget everything. Landon stays next to him at all times, though they just chat casually, catching up, until…

“I am glad you’re alive.” Landon says in a softer voice, Caleb smiles, holding onto his drink a little tighter.

“I’m not that easy to get rid of.”

“Hmm, to be honest, I’d rather not test that theory.”

Caleb smiles, this time genuinely.

“I wasn’t ready to lose you.” Landon says suddenly, seriously. Something about that makes Caleb’s chest tighten. “I just want you to know that Caleb.” Landon steps a little closer, their hands almost touching, resting on the bar counter.

“Thank you.” Caleb replies softly.

“You know I…I’ve been meaning to…” Landon starts and then lets out a breath as Caleb watches him patiently. “Just that, I don’t like how we left things off last time, what we had was good, wasn't it?” Landon asks, and Caleb doesn’t miss a hint of hope in his eyes.

“Landon...”

”I know, I know what we talked about, but I don't regret what we had Caleb.”

And for a brief moment Caleb sees the sincerity in his eyes and smiles softly. “Neither do I.”

Landon steps slightly closer, close enough that their breaths brush on each other's faces. “Then maybe we could try again?”

Caleb’s heart sinks, he opens his mouth to speak but nothing comes out.

Suddenly, flashes of a black baseball cap, and dark eyes come back to him, as a quiet voice whispers in his ear.

*You need to leave now. *

Caleb blinks as memories of that night vanish when he feels Landon’s hand gently squeezing his arm.

“Caleb?”

“Sorry.”

“You okay?”

“Yeah, I’m fine.” He pushes himself slightly away from Landon creating some distance and takes a long sip of his drink.

“Hey, I know you must be shaken after what happened at the museum, but I’m…”

”I said I’m fine.”

Landon goes quiet, and pulls away just a little bit as well, understanding that Caleb needs some space.

“Alright.” Landon’s voice is soft, he feels his hand still resting gently on Caleb’s arm and something stirs in his heart. Guilt perhaps? Landon has always been good, no rudeness or manipulation behind his actions, he genuinely cares. “Let me get you another drink.”

He turns.

”Landon.”

Landon suddenly stops and turns back to Caleb. Caleb hesitates for a second before he draws closer to him again, grabbing the front of his shirt’s collar. “Do you want to get out of here?”

Landon blinks at him a couple times, Caleb waits as a slow smile appears on Landon's face.

“Are you sure?”

Caleb nods. “Yes.”

And they slip away from the party.

Caleb doesn’t give himself time to reconsider, as he wraps his arms around Landon and kisses him. Landon responds immediately. They burst into his bedroom as he catches Landon’s hand and pulls him closer, toward the bed.

For a few precious seconds, Caleb lets himself disappear into something familiar.

No Crown, no assassination attempts or a mysterious savior telling him to listen.

He focuses on the hands around his waist, Landon’s mouth on his neck and the reassuring familiarity of someone he’s known before.

He kisses Landon harder almost desperately as they fall on the bed together. Caleb pulls him closer, his fingers fumbling impatiently with the buttons of Landon’s shirt.

Landon laughs softly against his mouth.

“Someone’s in a hurry.”

“Don’t talk.”

Caleb kisses him again, coaxing his mouth open to feel his tongue, he doesn’t want to think, he doesn't want to remember the rooftop or the strange warmth that had settled over him when the man stood between him and the gunfire, protecting him.

It’s been five days since the incident, five days since his return back to London, yet the stranger's voice stays in his head.

He wants something he understands right now, something that doesn't make his chest ache.

Landon kisses along his jaw, and Caleb closes his eyes.

*Focus. *

But his mind betrays him.

*I need to get you out of here. *

He forces his eyes open and Landon is still here, familiar and beautiful. So why does his heart feel like it’s somewhere else?

“Caleb?”

“I am fine….just, don’t stop.”

Landon smirks, and obliges, as he goes down to trail open mouthed kisses along his neck and Caleb closes his eyes again, focusing on the touch.

Caleb barely notices, as Landon reaches his hand out and pulls the bedside drawer open, and his hand disappears searching for condoms when something catches Caleb’s eyes.

The black baseball cap slips free and lands on the floor, Caleb freezes, every ounce of urgency disappears as his entire body goes still.

Shit, he forgot he had put it there.

Landon doesn’t notice, until his hand is back on Caleb again with a couple of condom packets.

“Stop.” Caleb gasps.

Landon immediately pulls away.

“Caleb?”

Caleb stares down at the hat, still lying carelessly on the floor.

*His hat. *

The one he so quietly took with him, and carried it down the staircase when he was terrified. The one that smells faintly of sandalwood and smoke.

Suddenly he can’t breathe.

He sits upright and pushes Landon away.

“Caleb…hey.”

”Wait.” Caleb’s voice cracks as he tries to catch his breath.

Landon’s expression changes instantly, he quickly pulls away from Caleb to give him some space.

“Caleb, take it easy, it’s okay.” Landon says gently and Caleb finally looks up at him. The man is looking at him all worried, and Caleb feels bad for him.

Landon gently touches his cheek, and Caleb flinches. Not because Landon had hurt but because the touch felt wrong.

*It’s not his. *

The realization scares him more than anything else. He gently removes Landon’s hand.

“I’m sorry.”

Landon looks at him for a moment then nods. “It’s okay.”

And somehow that understanding and kindness makes Caleb feel even worse.

“I thought I could.”

“You don’t have to apologize, it’s okay.”

Caleb looks up at him, there is disappointment in his eyes, but no anger or resentment. Landon gets up from the bed and reaches for his shirt to pull it back on.

“Let’s go back to the party.”

“You’re not upset?” Caleb asks.

Landon gives him a small smile. “Of course I’m disappointed, but I am not going to make you feel guilty for it.”

“Landon….”

“I’ll see you downstairs.”

At the door, Landon pauses and turns back to Caleb. “Take your time.” And then he’s gone.

The room becomes quiet and Caleb stares at his hands, his eyes go back to the hat on the floor, and he slowly reaches down to pick it up.

For several seconds he doesn't move and then slowly presses it against his chest, closing his eyes.

He doesn't understand why a stranger he’s met for perhaps ten minutes is living inside his head like this. He doesn't even know if he'll ever see him again.

And yet the warmth of his hand around his wrist, the sound of his voice, the way his body stood between Caleb and the world, he can’t seem to forget any of it.

Caleb doesn't know how long he’s been sitting here on his bed clutching onto the hat like a fool.

“God, this is ridiculous.”

He quickly shoves the hat back into the drawer.

Then, after a moment of hesitation, he reaches in again and pushes it all the way back, and slams the drawer shut.

Out of sight.

Out of mind.

At least that’s the idea.

Caleb falls backwards onto his pillows, and covers his face with both hands. He’s survived assassination attempts, his father’s lecture and even Andrew’s tormenting.

Yet somehow, none of it has managed to make him feel nearly as helpless as a man whose name he doesn't even know.

He groans into his hands realizing he’s quite possibly, completely screwed.
