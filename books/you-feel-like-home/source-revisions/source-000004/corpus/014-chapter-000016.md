It’s two in the morning when Siciliy’s sky opened without warning with persistent drizzle settling over the harbour and dampening everything around it. The rain is not heavy enough to send the remaining workers to seek shelter, but it has darkened the roads and the walls while the sea shifts beneath the night sky. Black water catches the glow of the navigation lights as ships signal their arrival and departure.

Sicily tends to grow colder at this hour, the ports remain quiet as the day traffic dwindles, tonight however, the usual activities at the harbour have been interrupted by an unexpected explosion as the workers have fled the dock after the sudden attack.

An hour after the explosion, Arian steps out of the car. Nothing in his expression suggests he has been dragged from the warmth of his bed though he is not particularly happy for having to leave the sleeping prince behind, which is a good enough reason to resent the interruption.

He would have rather stayed in bed and spent the remainder of the night with Caleb tucked warmly against his chest, but a call from one of his men had informed him about the explosion at the harbour which forced him out of bed and had him stand in the cold rain overseeing the aftermath of the attack.

He had barely managed to whisper an explanation in Caleb’s ear, promising to return in an hour, but Caleb had stirred at the loss of warmth, making a disgruntled sound, instinctively reaching for Arian which had taken a considerable amount of restraint for Arian to not climb back beneath the covers and pull him close again.

One of Arian’s men hurry forward with an umbrella, while Rowan approaches from the far side of the car, and holds out a sealed evidence bag containing a handgun recovered from the scene.

“He’s over there.”

Arian takes the bag, glances briefly at its contents, and passes it to the man beside him.

“Any word on the second one?”

“Nothing yet. We have men searching the perimeter and the service road. He got off the dock before anyone could reach him.”

“And him?”

”Still refusing to talk.”

Arian’s gaze moves down the length of the dock, two of his men stand beneath the floodlights, one on either side of a man kneeling on the boards. His hands are bound behind his back, his shirt stained with blood, and bruises already darkening the side of his face. He looks as though he has been given a few chances to reconsider his circumstances, and chose not to take it.

Arian walks towards him as he dismisses the man carrying the umbrella behind him. The rain settles across his hair and coat, as his gaze fixes on the prisoner, just as Arian crosses the distance, his eyes catch the crest embroidered on the man’s suit jacket.

The royal family’s insignia.

Arian studies the man for a second before speaking, “Who are you, and what business do you have on my property?”

The man lifts his head, his gaze moves over Arian’s face before settling into the silence. One of the guards behind him strikes the back of his head, forcing his chin down.

“Answer him.” the man grunts in pain, before he looks back up at Arian and gives a humorless laugh.

“I have no intention of answering anything.”

Arian tilts his head. “Then this should be a very short conversation.” He turns back to Rowan who has followed him down the dock. “Tell me what happened.”

“Two men. They came off a vessel shortly before the explosion and crossed the loading area without authorization. Our men intercepted them after the blast. One escaped, while we caught this one.” Rowan’s expression hardens before he looks down, lowering his gaze. “The explosion killed two of our men. The other two are injured, we managed to contain the damage but whoever arranged this knew the layout of the port.”

Arian’s expression doesn’t change. “Have the families been informed?”

“We are arranging it now.”

“Make sure they have everything they need.” Arian says, before he turns back to the prisoner, eyes narrowing. “What about the one who escaped?”

“We are searching for him.”

“Find him by sunrise, I don’t care how, check every street, and make sure the surveillance footage is on my desk in an hour.”

“Already underway boss.” Rowan inclines his head.

Arian reaches into his coat and brings out the hand gun, inspecting it like one would check a familiar object, then loads it with a practiced movement.

“Leave us.”

Rowan hesitates for a brief moment before signalling the other to move back. Arian crouches before him, bringing himself to eye level and smirks.

“Let me make this simple for you,” he says. “I don’t need you to tell me who you are, I’m not interested in that but I have a reasonable idea of who you work for and why you’ve come here.”

The man shifts uncomfortably while Arian remains unfazed.

“What I don’t know is how the man you work for arranged this attack, and what else is in planning. Those are the answers I’m interested in.”

“You’ll get nothing from me”

Arian stares without a reaction although there is a hint of faint amusement in his eyes, as he rises, brushing the rain from his palms. “You’ve been very loyal so far, I imagine your employer appreciates that.”

“I am not afraid of a bloody criminal like you.” The prisoner lifts his chin. “You think you can torture me until I betray my king? You’re wasting your time.”

“Torture?” he repeats, as though the suggestion has genuinely surprised him. “I don’t believe you understand how little of my time you are worth.”

“You see, the difficulty in working for powerful men is that you begin to believe their protection extends to every part of your life. You assume that because your employers know where you are, no one else can find out where you’ve come from.”

Arian lets the silence settle before continuing. “I know you have a four-year-old son, clinging to what little hope remains while receiving treatment for a terminal illness at the expense of the royal family. I know you’re drowning in debt, with no end in sight, and a wife who thought you were so little worth keeping that she left you for another man.”

The color starts to drain from the man’s face.
“It takes minutes for my men to piece together the life of a lowly guard who believes the Crown’s generosity comes from the goodness of the King’s heart.”

“You leave my family out of this.”

The response comes too quickly, as the guard's composure cracks, and beneath the pain and defiance, Arian can see the fear beginning to take hold. It’s almost disappointing how easily men like him break once you show them where they belong.

“I haven’t touched them.” Arian says. “I am only giving you the opportunity to understand the position you’re in before you make another mistake.”

“You wouldn’t dare.” the guard shouts.

Arian merely stands there and watches, almost disinterestedly, as the last shred of the man’s resolve crumbles, taking what remains of his dignity with it.

Arian finds no thrill in having to find the place where the man is vulnerable, and he has no particular interest in his family. Arian understands they are innocent of whatever has brought him to the harbour, but he needs to understand that he is not the only man here trying to protect someone that can slip away from his hand in a second.

After a moment of silence Arian speaks again, this time quietly. “Now, who sent you?”

The guard’s eyes move towards the darkened end of the dock, before his eyes settle on Arian again.

“His Majesty wants the Prince back.”

Arian’s expression changes, a slight shift, as his fingers tighten around the gun in his hand.

“The Crown knows where Prince Caleb is. His Majesty’s orders are to bring him home. We were told to make it clear that this would happen one way or another.”

“By planting an explosive in my harbour?"

“We were instructed to make you understand that this wasn't a request. Prince Caleb is to be returned to London, and King Andrew doesn’t care what it takes. If you refuse to cooperate, then we’ll bring him by force. If we have to break every limb in his body and drag him off the coast, we’ll do it.”

The harbour seems to grow very quiet. Arian had anticipated that Andrew would come for Caleb eventually. But this is something else, two of his men are dead, and his territory has been violated, and the royal guard stands before him, delivering the message with a brazen disregard for his own life, as though he has already accepted that tonight might as well be his last.

And the message is received,  it's never been about diplomacy, it’s a warning. A warning that the Conti organization is not beyond Andrew’s reach.

Arian regards him for a minute before finally nodding as though the man has confirmed something he had already suspected.

“Well, then, I’m sure he’ll be thrilled to receive a response to his little message, won’t he?” he says and raises his gun.

The guard’s breath catches. Whatever composure he managed to hold onto finally deserts him, his eyes darting to the weapon now aimed at him.

“Wait…please I can tell you more. I have names, contacts..”

“You should have thought of that before you came here.”

The gunshot cracks through the harbour.

The royal guard collapses against the wet boards, blood pooling around him, his final words lost beneath the sound of the rain. Arian remains where he is for a moment, looking down at the man whose loyalty brought him to the wrong side of the line.

A familiar quietness follows, one that Arian has never entirely grown accustomed to. The act of taking a life has become frighteningly simple for him, yet the silence afterwards stays with him, leaving no particular satisfaction in it.

“Rowan.” Arian calls, lowering his gun. His second is beside him almost immediately. “Seal every exit. Nobody leaves this harbour until we’ve accounted for everyone who was here tonight. I want every inch of the docks searched, every access point checked and surveillance cameras reviewed. Find the man who escaped.”

“Understood.”

“I want the full report by sunrise, I want every connection traced back to its source.” Arian says, as he hands the gun back to one of his men, and looks towards the water.

Andrew has made his intentions clear tonight, he won’t rest until Caleb has returned, meanwhile the Prince is currently still asleep in Arian’s bed, unaware that the world outside is beginning to close around him.

By the time Arian returns to the villa, he makes no attempt to go back to his room. Instead, he heads straight for his study, discards his jacket over the back of a chair, pours himself a drink, and settles behind his desk. Hours pass, and he barely moves. Specks of blood still stain his shirt, remnants of the moment he had put a bullet through the royal guard a few hours ago.

He has already made certain that Caleb is still asleep. The staff assured him that there have been no disturbances, but he can’t go up there right now.

Not when Caleb is blissfully unaware of the blood on his hands, of what those hands have done only hours ago. He cannot bring himself to walk back into the room carrying the night's violence with him.

There are things Arian understands to be necessary. Things he has done, and would do again without hesitation if it means keeping Caleb safe. But understanding that does not mean he wants Caleb exposed to any of it. Having him in his life is already becoming something of a necessity, an urgency Arian can neither reason with nor bring himself to resist. And somewhere beneath those feelings is the uncomfortable certainty that Caleb deserves someone better than him.

Someone who doesn't have blood on his hands.

But Arian is far too selfish to let him go now.

He remains in his study until the first traces of daylight creep over the mountains. At some point, he abandons his drink and turns to the security footage his team has sent over, reviewing it with frustration.

Frame after frame reveals nothing useful. And that, more than anything, unsettles him.

Because now he knows Andrew will not stop. He will exhaust every possible avenue to have Caleb brought back to London, and Arian has no intention of letting that happen.

The first thing Caleb notices over the past two mornings is the empty space beside him. It has become a rather irritating way to wake up.

For the better part of a week, he has grown accustomed to Arian’s warmth, to the heavy arm draped across his waist and the quiet reassurance of waking with someone beside him. Now, for two consecutive mornings, he has opened his eyes to find the other side of the bed empty and the sheets already cool, with Arian long gone.

It is not like Arian has completely disappeared. Every night, without fail he returns to Caleb’s room, sometimes Caleb is already asleep by then, and sometimes he is already awake, reading or pretending to read while waiting for the familiar sound of Arian entering. Either way, Arian would come to him, settle beside him and pull him close as though the distance throughout the day has been an inconvenience.

Every night, he presses soft kisses to Caleb’s neck and shoulder, his touch unhurried and tender, until the last of Caleb’s complaints dissolves somewhere in his throat.

Caleb always falls quiet beneath the warmth of Arian’s mouth that trails down his throat that by the time Arian settles beside him, he is already half-asleep.

But waking up the next day alone in bed is, however, becoming insufferable.

Whatever has claimed so much of Arian’s attention seems to demand every waking hour. He leaves before Caleb has finished breakfast and returns long after dinner offering a vague assurance that he had business to attend to. Caleb understands that Arian has responsibilities but that doesn’t stop him from missing him terribly.

Later in the afternoon, Caleb has just finished riding Nerone and is standing beside the stallion, one hand resting against the horse’s neck when he senses someone approaching.

Arian has discarded his jacket somewhere along the way, his shirt sleeves buttoned neatly at the wrists. He looks as composed as ever, though Caleb notices that he seems to have come straight here rather than stopping inside the villa first.

Caleb’s face brightens before he could help it. “Well, I wasn’t expecting to see you home before sunset, stranger.” He says looking Arian over with an exaggerated surprise. “Have you been dismissed from whatever mysterious business has been keeping you?”

Arian’s mouth curves. “Good afternoon to you, too.”

“Oh don’t be charming now. I’ve spent two days having breakfast alone.”

Arian raises his eyebrows. “Two days.”

“Forty-eight hours Arian, I’ve been counting.” Caleb gives him an affronted look, though a smile tugs at his mouth, as Arian gives a light laugh, and reaches out to stroke Nerone’s nose.

“I see you two are getting along rather well,” Arian remarks.

“He’s been very good company. He listens when I speak and has the courtesy to remain in the same room as me for more than ten minutes.”

Arian’s hand stills against the horse’s muzzle, he looks at Caleb, before catching his wrist and drawing him against his chest.

Caleb lets out a startled gasp, his hand coming up instinctively to rest against Arian’s arm. For a moment he can only blink at him, entirely off guard, as Arian’s arm remains around his waist, holding him close enough that Caleb could feel the warmth of him through the fabric of his shirt.

“Should I be worried about my horse taking away all your attention from me?”

Caleb smirks. “Oh, are we jealous of the horse now?”

“Perhaps I’ve decided I don't care to share your attention.”

Caleb’s smile softens. “Then perhaps you should consider that before abandoning me to the company of a particularly temperamental stallion.”

“An oversight on my part, I guess I’ll have to make amends.” Arian whispers lowering his head, and Caleb tilts his face up to meet him.

Their first kiss is gentle, almost unhurried. Arian’s hand settles more securely against Caleb’s waist while his other hand comes to rest along his jaw, his thumb brushing the skin just beneath his cheekbone. Caleb melts into the familiar touch with a quiet, satisfied hum, his arms sliding around Arian’s neck.

God, he missed him. He really missed Arian in the small, ridiculous way that is difficult to explain without sounding hopelessly enamoured.

Arian pulls back enough to look at him, and Caleb follows without hesitation, catching his mouth again before he could move away. That earns him a faint smile.

“Someone’s been waiting for me.”

“Don’t flatter yourself, I’m simply expressing my displeasure at your absence.”

“That is very convincing.”

Caleb pinches his side. Arian catches his hand, and brings it to his mouth, as he kisses his knuckles before returning it to his shoulder. The tenderness of the gesture makes something warm flutter beneath Caleb’s ribs.

Arian kisses him again, and Caleb abandons all thoughts of his surroundings and deepens the kiss, rising onto his toes, pulling Arian closer by the back of his neck, and Arian responds by tightening his arms around Caleb’s waist. He coaxes Caleb’s mouth open, pulling his bottom lip between his, titling his head to deepen the kiss until Caleb forgets his intention to complain about the past two days.

Nerone makes a disgruntled sound beside them, but Caleb barely registers it.

When they finally part, it is only because they need to breathe. Caleb remains close, his forehead almost touching Arian’s, his lips warm and slightly swollen. Arian looks equally reluctant to put any distance between them.

“Are you going to leave again?” Caleb asks softly.

The question escapes him before he could pass it off as a joke, his fingers curl around the back of Arian’s neck, as he searches his face with an expression that is more hopeful than he intends.

Arian’s gaze softens. “No.” he brushes his nose against Caleb’s. “I’m yours for the rest of the night.”

Caleb closes his eyes and hums in satisfaction. “Good.”

He kisses Arian again, a quick peck that immediately turns into another when Arian follows him. Caleb smiles against his mouth, then pulls back just far enough to speak.

“Then I suggest we take this upstairs and continue where we left off.”

“I think that’s an excellent idea, Your Highness.”

Caleb is already turning towards the villa, still holding on to Arian, when Arian catches his hand and brings him back for one more kiss. Caleb laughs against his mouth, delighted by the sudden affection, and lets himself be pulled closer.

They have barely taken a step when someone clears his throat behind them.

“Boss. My apologies.”

Arian closes his eyes briefly, and reluctantly pulls away to look at the man standing behind them. Caleb, who still has his arms around Arian’s neck is enjoying himself far too much to pretend otherwise, as he grins over his shoulder at the unfortunate man standing a few feet away.

“This better be important.” Arian says glaring at the man.

The man looks entirely unfazed by the interruption, half of the people at Conti household have grown accustomed to finding their boss in various states of affectionate distractions with His Royal Highness, and no one dares to question it.

“There is someone at the gates, boss. He is asking to see Your Highness.”

The smile fades from Caleb’s face, making way for confusion. “Who is it?”

“He claims to be an old friend.”

An old friend? Caleb exchanges a glance with Arian, whose expression has changed almost imperceptibly. The easy warmth from a moment ago has receded behind something more guarded and sharp.

“Were you expecting anyone?” Arian asks.

“Of course not. I don’t exactly have a social calendar here, do I?”

Arian turns towards the man again. “Keep him outside the gates. We’ll meet him there.”

“Yes, boss.” The man takes his leave as Caleb looks back at Arian, still trying to make sense of the interruption.

“Who on earth would come here for me?”

“I suppose we’ll find out.” Arian takes his hand again, fingers firmly closing around as he pulls him along with him. “Stay close to me.”

Caleb is already sensing the change in Arian, who seems more alert than relaxed as he was a moment ago. As they reach the gates, nothing prepares Caleb for the figure standing by them.

Dressed in a crisp black and white suit, his posture straight despite the long journey that has brought him here. His expression is soft, almost apprehensive, and when he looks up he sees Caleb approaching, the smile that spreads across his face is so familiar that Caleb feels his heart lurch.

For a moment he could only stare, and then the man takes a step forward.

“Your Highness…”

“Paul?”

The name leaves Caleb’s mouth as little more than a breath, and then he’s moving. He doesn’t remember letting go of Arian’s hand, or crossing the remaining distance. One moment he is standing beside Arian, and the next he is rushing towards the man who has been a part of his life for years.

Paul barely has time to react before Caleb throws himself at him.

The embrace is so sudden and enthusiastic that Paul staggers half a step backwards, catching Caleb around the shoulders as the prince wraps his arms tightly around him.

“I can’t believe it’s you,” Caleb breathes.

“You Highness ... .I can’t…” Paul struggles to draw a breath. “I can’t breathe.”

“Oh shut up, you can breathe in a minute.” Caleb only hugs him tighter.

Paul chuckles, and pats him lightly on his shoulder, before they pull away. Caleb laughs, but the sound is caught somewhere in his throat while his eyes began to sting, overwhelmed by the sheer impossibility of seeing someone from home standing in front of him.

Being surrounded by people who only knew him as a prince for months has made Caleb become accustomed to the unfamiliar rhythm of Sicily, to a house that is slowly becoming home and a man who has somehow become the centre of it.

But Paul belongs to a life that existed before any of this. A piece of his life that he misses dearly.

“No way,” he says laughing through the tears gathering in his eyes. “No way, it’s actually you.”

Paul’s expressions soften as he squeezes his shoulder. “It is, Your Highness. It is good to see you.” he gives a small formal bow, placing a hand on his heart.

Caleb shakes his head, still unable to quite believe it. “What are you doing here and why didn’t you tell me you were coming?”

“I was hoping to surprise you.”

“Well you have surprised me quite a lot. I am considering whether to be furious with you for not warning me that you were about to appear out of thin air.”

Paul laughs and Caleb finds himself laughing with him. He is so absorbed in the reunion that he doesn’t notice Arian approaching.

Only when Paul’s smile falters slightly and his posture straightens, does Caleb look up, still smiling. Arian’s face remains completely composed but distinctly less welcoming than Caleb might have hoped.

Paul steps back from Caleb, and tilts his head in a small nod. “Mr. Conti.”

“Mr. Walker.” Arian’s voice is polite enough to be impeccable and cold enough to leave no doubt about the distance between them. “To what do I owe the pleasure?”

“I’ve been in Sicily on business, sir. I thought it would be appropriate to pay His Royal Highness a visit while I was here.”

“And what sort of business brings you to Sicily, Mr. Walker?”

Paul pauses for the briefest moment.

“Paul, how did you know I’d be here?” Caleb asks suddenly.

“I mean Mr. Conti’s estate isn’t exactly a secret, I thought this would be the most likely place to find you, Your Highness.”

“How fortunate,” Arian remarks. Caleb glances at him.

He notices that Arian’s expression has not changed much, but there is something deliberate about the way he is looking at Paul, as though he is examining a piece of information that doesn’t quite fit yet.

“And what business have you been conducting here?” he asks.

Paul’s smile becomes a little more formal.

“Princess Matilda asked me to attend to a few matters following the cancellation of Prince Caleb’s diplomatic assignment after His late Majesty’s death.”

Caleb’s head turns sharply. “Is Matilda here?”

“No, she sent me because she couldn’t come herself.”

“Oh.” Caleb’s expression falls a little. “Is she well? How is everyone else? You must tell me everything.”

“In due course,” Arian interrupts. “I’m curious, Mr. Walker. I don’t recall Princess Matilda informing me that she has sent someone to Sicily on her behalf.”

Paul meets his gaze. “It wasn’t a formal arrangement with you, sir. I was handling matters on behalf of the royal household.”

“And yet you have arrived at my gates without prior notice, claiming to have come on business for a member of the royal family who isn’t here to confirm your account.”

The words are delivered without hostility, which somehow makes them more pointed. Paul’s jaw tightens briefly before he inclines his head.

“I apologize if my arrival has caused an inconvenience. That wasn’t my intention.”

“Paul, you haven’t caused any inconvenience,” Caleb says at once. He turns towards Arian, smile fading into a frown.

“Arian.”

Arian looks at him, as Caleb’s gaze holds a message that is unmistakable. Please stop.

He understands the look. He has become familiar with Caleb’s expressions that he can often tell what the prince is thinking about.

But understanding Caleb’s irritation does not mean he intends to abandon his questions.

Two nights earlier, someone had trespassed into his harbour, an explosion had killed two of his men, and the man they had captured was part of the royal security. Arian had spent the past two days tracing the attack, examining the security footage, and trying to determine how much Andrew already knows about Caleb’s whereabouts.

Now Caleb’s personal secretary has arrived at his gates, unannounced, claiming to have come to Sicily on unexplained royal business.

Arian was not born yesterday, while he would not accuse the man without evidence, particularly not in front of Caleb. But neither would he pretend that the timing is unremarkable. Paul could precisely be who he claims to be, an old friend who has travelled to see someone he cares about.

Or he could be something else entirely. Until Arian knows which, he intends to keep him where he could see him.

Caleb’s hand comes to rest on Arian’s arm, the touch is gentle, almost pleading. Arian looks down at it, then back at Caleb’s face.

He can see how much the reunion means to him, the happiness shining through the confusion, the faint embarrassment of having watched Arian interrogate someone he clearly considers a friend. Caleb has been lonely, Arian knows that and he knows how much he misses his sisters, his home and the familiar people who had known him long before Arian entered his life.

He isn’t going to take this from him.

He lets out a quiet breath and turns to Paul. “Where are you staying Mr. Walker?”

Paul seems momentarily taken aback. “Oh…at a hotel near the marketplace sir. I intend to remain in Sicily for the entire month, depending on how long the business takes.”

“You are welcome to stay here. I’ll have a guest room prepared for you.”

Caleb blinks. For a moment he looks from Arian to Paul as though he isn’t entirely sure he has heard correctly. Then his expression transforms, the remaining tension disappearing beneath a wave of relief and affection, his hand on his arm squeezes a little in gratitude.

“That’s very generous of you, Mr. Conti. Thank you.” Paul inclines his head respectfully.

Arian returns the gesture with a brief nod. “You’ll be shown to your room once you’ve settled in. If you need anything, speak to the staff.”

“Of course.”

Arian doesn’t offer him a warm welcome, or extends anymore conversation. He simply turns and begins to walk back towards the villa, leaving Caleb standing beside Paul with a look of unrestrained delight that Arian feels the faintest pull at the corner of his mouth despite himself.

For now, he has given Caleb what he wanted, a piece of home. If Paul is innocent, he would have nothing to fear from staying under Arian’s roof, and if he isn’t, Arian would much rather have him where he could keep an eye on him.

Caleb turns back to Paul and catches him by the arm, as he begins to steer him towards the villa.

“You have absolutely no idea how many things I need to ask you.”

“I suspect, alot.”

“Yes, you are not leaving my side at all for the rest of the evening.” Paul simply laughs and lets himself be pulled.

“You know, I almost forgot how intolerably organized you are.”

Caleb finds Paul in the guest room, unpacking his belongings. His jacket now hangs neatly in the wardrobe and the leather case that he had brought with him sits open on the writing desk.

Paul looks up at him and smiles, it fills Caleb with a familiar sense of happiness and he finds himself smiling back as he enters the room.

“Come and sit with me,” Caleb says, settling into the armchair beside the window.

Paul takes the seat opposite him. For a few minutes, they speak of familiar things: Eleanor’s latest musical obsession, Cecily’s school going well, Matilda’s relentless schedule. Caleb listens to it all eagerly, laughing at the stories.

Then Paul’s expression grows more serious,

“You Highness, there is something else I wanted to speak to you about, specifically about your return to London.”

The smile on Caleb’s face fades slightly. Paul folds his hands together. “Things have been difficult since His Majesty’s death. And King Andrew….he had to take an extraordinary amount of responsibility in a very short time.”

Caleb looks down at his hands. “He’s worried about you too.” Paul continues. “He wants you home, and beside him supporting the Crown.”

A familiar unwelcome guilt begins to settle in Caleb’s chest, he has spent months away from London, cut off from his sisters and responsibilities he had once taken for granted. Whatever happened between him and Andrew, whatever doubts he has about his brother’s intentions, he can not pretend that his absence has left the royal family unaffected.

“I do miss everyone.” he admits quietly.

“Then perhaps it’s time to come home.”

Caleb is silent for a moment. There is something else he wanted to ask for months, a question that followed him from London to Sicily and refused to leave him alone.

“Paul, can I ask you something?”

“Of course.”

“About Father,” Caleb hesitates for a minute. “How did he really die?”

Something changes in Paul’s expression, it is fleeting before his face settles again, but Caleb catches it.

“His Majesty died of a cardiac arrest, he’s been unwell for quite some time” Paul answers.

Caleb studies him. “That’s what everyone has been told.”

Paul's brow furrows. “Your Highness?”

Caleb looks away. He remembers Arian’s words at the airport in London, the quiet certainty with which he had told him that his father had been murdered. He has never forgotten them, nor has he found the right moment to ask Arian about them again.

“People talk,” Paul says gently. “There will always be rumors when someone as important as your father dies unexpectedly. But he had been ill. You must not let speculation make this harder for you.”

Caleb manages a small smile. “I miss everyone, and of course I want to come home, I have a duty to the Crown, regardless of what’s happened.” he pauses. “But I can’t simply leave tomorrow. Arian needs to be certain that it’s safe for me to return.”

“Mr. Conti has no obligation to keep you here any longer.”

Caleb’s smile falters. Paul leans forward, a new urgency entering his voice. “You have to understand, Your Highness, Andrew is King now and he is asking you to come home, why should it matter what anyone else thinks.”

“Paul.” Caleb’s voice is quiet, but the warning in it is unmistakable.

“I’m sorry, I didn’t mean to upset you. It would simply mean a great deal to all of us if you came home.”

Caleb’s expression softens. He reaches across the space between them and rests a hand on Paul’s shoulder. “I will come home eventually, I am not planning to stay in Sicily forever, I just need to make sure that I can return safely.”

“Just promise me you’ll think about it. I leave in a month and I can make arrangements, and we could travel back together.”

For one dreadful moment Caleb’s heart seems to drop into his stomach. He pictures himself standing at the gates of the villa, walking away from everything and everyone that has slowly become familiar, the life he has started to build…with Arian.

He can not imagine saying goodbye to him. Not after everything they have been through. But he could hardly tell Paul any of that.

So he smiles and nods, instead.

“I’ll think about it.”

Paul seems relieved. “Thank you, Your Highness."

Caleb finds Arain waiting in his room. He is sitting on the edge of the bed, his phone pressed to his ear. When he looks up, whatever he had been about to say on the phone dies on his tongue and he ends the call without another word.

For reasons Caleb can't quite explain, the sight of him there loosens something tight in his chest. As the tension and the heaviness from his conversation with Paul leaves him, easing him to breathe properly.

Arian smiles at him softly, as Caleb crosses the room and sits beside him with a tired sigh.

“Has Paul settled in all right?” Arian asks.

“Yes, he’s quite comfortable I think.” Caleb glances at him, offering a small smile. “Thank you for letting him stay.”

Arian looks down at the phone in his hand and sighs. “It seemed the best way to keep an eye on him.”

Caleb’s smile disappears. “What is that supposed to mean?”

Arian is quiet for a moment, then he sets his phone aside and turns towards him.

“Two nights ago, we had an incident at the harbour. Two men trespassed onto Conti territory from a ship. They killed two of my men before we could intercept them, one of them has escaped”

Caleb stares at him, struggling to understand what he was hearing.

“He was a member of the Royal Guard.”

For a moment, Caleb doesn’t move. Then he is on his feet, staring down at Arian in disbeleif.

“You knew this for two days and didn’t think it was something I should've been told?”

“I needed to establish what they wanted before I told you anything.”

“You needed to establish….” Caleb stops, a disbelieving laugh escaping him. “Arian, they killed two of your men, someone from the royal palace came here to attack and you thought I didn’t need to know?”

“I was going to tell you eventually, I just needed to be sure first if that was where the attack came from.”

“And now?”

“Now I know they were sent to bring you back to London. They made it quite clear they were willing to break every bone in your body to accomplish that.”

The color drains from Caleb’s face.

Arian rises and reaches for Caleb’s hands, taking them gently in his own. “They threatened my men, they threatened you. They made it clear that they have no intention of accepting your continued presence here. And now Paul arrives at my gates without warning, claiming to have come all this way because he’s worried about you.”

He holds Caleb’s gaze. “You can understand why I have to question this timing.”

“Wait a minute.” Caleb shakes his head and pulls away from Arian. “You cannot seriously be suspicious of Paul.”

“I am suspicious of everyone who arrives unexpectedly while someone is actively trying to hunt you down.”

“He is my secretary, Arian. He’s been in my life for years. He’s been with me long before any of this happened, and has never given me a reason to doubt him.”

“You don’t know that.”

Caleb flinches as though Arian has struck him.

“Oh, that’s unfair. You’re telling me that the men at the harbour were sent to threaten you because Andrew wants me home, and now you’re suggesting Paul is somehow involved?”

“Andrew wants to keep you where he can control you Caleb, yet you keep assuming that the person sitting on the throne had nothing to fear from you.”

Arian steps closer, the tenderness in his face from earlier is gone now. “You think because you’ve spent a few months here, the threat is over? That whoever tried to kill you in London has simply forgotten you exist? You know what your brother is capable of”

Something inside Caleb turns over. He thinks about that night in London, of the violence and confusion and how quickly everything fell apart. He had even considered the possibility that Andrew is involved in all of this, and had forced himself to discard such thoughts.

Now he is being asked to reconsider that his own brother might have wanted him dead.

“Why would he do that?” Caleb asks again, looking at him with something more wounded than anger now. “He is already king, I never wanted the crown, I’m the spare. I did everything to avoid being dragged into royal business and now you’re telling me Andrew might have orchestrated an attempt on my life?”

“I’m telling you, you need to stop assuming he wouldn’t.”

“And Paul?” Caleb demands. “Is that what you’re telling me about him too, that he’s part of some elaborate plot to drag me home?”

“You have reasons to trust Paul, I don’t.”

“That’s convenient, isn’t it? Everytime I ask a question, there is another secret I can’t possibly know about.”

Arian’s eyes narrow slightly. “Caleb…”

“No, that is your problem Arian. You have never been entirely honest with me. You and Matilda both have made decisions about my life, and decide what I can and cannot know.”

“I am trying to keep you safe.”

“Paul would never hurt me.” Caleb says sharply.

“I never said he would.”

“But you don’t trust him, you trust me enough to let me sleep in your bed, kiss me and hold me, but when I tell you that Paul would never betray me, you look at me as though I’m a fool who doesn’t understand the world he’s living in.”

“This isn’t what I think.” Arian says, reaching out for Caleb, but he pulls away.

“Then what do you think?”

“That you want to believe the best of people who have given you no reason to question them. But sometimes it gets you killed.”

Caleb laughs, thin and bitter. “But do you know what else can hurt me? Finding out that the people I trust most find it convenient to keep things from me in the name of protection.”

His voice falters, as he looks away for a moment blinking hard.

“And I hate that I have to ask you for the truth, I hate that I have to wonder what else you know that you haven’t told me.”

Arian’s jaw tightens, as his fist clenches on his side.

“Caleb,” he says quietly.

He reaches for him again, his hand settling gently around his arm. Caleb doesn’t pull away this time, though he doesn’t move closer either. Arian exhales and lowers his forehead to his.

“I am sorry.”

The words are so soft, that Caleb almost wishes he doesn’t hear them. They make it harder to remain angry.

“I’m sorry I didn’t tell you about the attack,” Arian continues. “I should have, you deserved to know.”

Caleb closes his eyes, feels Arian’s soft breath on his cheeks as he takes him into his embrace.

“I wanted to be certain of what had happened before I put another thing on your shoulders. I was sparing you the uncertainty, I didn’t consider that keeping you in the dark would be worse.”

“I don’t need you to make everything easier for me,” Caleb whispers. “I need you to trust me enough to let me face it with you.”

Arian’s forehead remains against his. “I know,” he says softly. He draws him closer, resting his cheek briefly against Caleb’s hair. Caleb lets himself be held, hides his face in Arian’s shoulder as his fingers tighten around the fabric of his coat. It is the worst part of loving someone, he thinks, how easily a single touch can undo all the anger you had worked so hard to gather.

For a little while, neither of them speak.

Then the moment breaks when the vibration from Arian’s phone fills the air.

Arian lifts his head and glances at the screen. His expression changes as he reads whatever has appeared there, and closes his eyes wearily.

“I have to take this.”

Caleb steps back, the warmth between them already beginning to fade.

When Arian drops the call he turns back to Caleb again and asks. “I’ll come back in an hour.”

Caleb folds his arms in and shrugs. “Don’t worry, take your time.”

“Caleb…”

“Just…we’ll talk later okay. You should go.”

Arian looks at the sudden distance Caleb has put between them, or the careful tone of dismissal in his voice.

He reaches for him, then pauses, giving Caleb the chance to step away, when he doesn’t, Arian rests a hand against the side of his face, his thumb brushing lightly over his cheek.

“I’m coming back.” he says quietly.

Caleb looks down briefly before easing into his touch. “I know.”

That was all, as Caleb watches Arian leave through the door, until his footsteps disappear down the corridor before letting his arms fall to his side.

The room starts to feel strangely quiet without him again.

The old industrial plant stands beyond the outskirts of town, just a hulking skeleton of concrete and rusted steel that was abandoned years ago. Arian steps out of the car and finds four of his security team standing guard just outside the iron gate. Rainwater has collected in the potholes surrounding the building as Arian walks up to one of his men.

“He's inside. We found the bastard in the downtown hotel at Palermo, he put us on a bit of a chase.”

“He’s been talking since we brought him in,” Rowan says. “Mostly nonsense, keep insisting we’re all dead men.”

“How reassuring.”

A single work light bulb illuminates the centre of the room, beneath it, a man is tied to a metal chair, his head hanging forward and his clothes filthy with dust. Unlike the man they had captured at the harbour, he is not wearing any Royal Guard’s uniform. He looks rather ordinary who can easily disappear into the crowd.

Rowan’s patience snaps, as he steps forward and drives the butt of his gun against the man’s temple. The laughter stops abruptly as the man’s head slumps to one side, and he swears under his breath.

Arian lifts one hand, stopping him before he could strike again. He has no interest in theatrics. He already heard the threats at the harbour, this man is simply another mouth repeating the same message.

The prisoner spits blood onto the concrete. “You think I’m afraid of you?”

“No.” Arian glances at the man’s bound hands. “I think you’re hoping I’ll be distracted by your courage long enough for you to forget how little of it you actually possess.”

“Wait…”
