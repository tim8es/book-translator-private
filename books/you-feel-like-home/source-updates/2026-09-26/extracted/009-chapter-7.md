Arian had accounted for everything.

The moment the first vehicle had stopped, and gunfire broke through, he knew leaving Caleb inside the convoy was no longer an option. Whoever had orchestrated the attack had already known enough to intercept a route very few people should have had access to.

So Arian had changed the plan. His men would remain around Caleb’s car, maintaining the appearance that the prince is still inside while Arian moved through the security line himself, get Caleb out through the rear of the formation and disappear with him into the alley before whoever was watching realized their actual target was gone. Then Adrien would bring the secondary vehicle around the extraction point, and he could move Caleb somewhere secure while the rest of the convoy held the street.

It was the perfect plan and the only best option available to him. And for several minutes, it worked.

He had successfully diverted Caleb into the alley unharmed, and in that moment Adrien’s voice had come through Arian’s earpiece only minutes earlier, assuring him that the secondary vehicle was already on its way.

Except now, there is no vehicle.

There is absolutely nothing, and Adrien is no longer responding.

Arian stands at the end of the alley staring across an empty road, his gun still in his hand, held low against his thigh. He can hear Caleb breathing behind him, slightly uneven from the run, but otherwise the street is silent.

Five of his men are already down, the original route has been compromised and now the extraction has failed. For several seconds, Arian has absolutely no idea what to do next.

The admission is unthinkable under any other circumstances, because there is always another route, another vehicle, another man he could call. Contingencies exist because plans fail, and Arian has built his entire life around ensuring he is never dependent on a single one.

But this is London, not Sicily. Someone had known Caleb’s movements, and knew to intercept.

“Arian?”

Caleb’s voice brings his attention sharply back to the street.

In that moment, he realizes they are exposed. Caleb barely manages a startled breath before Arian moves, catches him by the arm, and pulls him backwards into the alley. He doesn’t stop until Caleb’s back meets the brick wall several feet inside, safely beyond the direct line of sight from the road. Arian follows him into the shadows, one hand braced beside Caleb’s head while his body blocks what remains of the opening.

Caleb stares at him, startled by the sudden movement, but doesn’t protest.

He looks past Caleb towards the rooftops instead, forcing his mind through the possibilities. Someone approaching from the street he can handle. What he can’t account for is a shooter positioned above them, or how much information has already passed into the wrong hands.

"Do you think someone betrayed you?"

Arian's gaze drops to Caleb.

"Adrien?"

There is concern in Caleb’s face now, and something closer to fear beneath it, but no panic. He is breathing hard, his hair disordered from the run and his coat slightly crookedly across one shoulder, yet his voice remains steady.

“Either someone betrayed us,” Arian says quietly, “or someone got Adrien before he reached us.”

Caleb’s expression tightens. “Neither of those options are particularly comforting.”

“They aren’t supposed to be.”

“Who could even do this? We are in the middle of London.”

“Someone who already had access to your schedule, they knew enough about the route to stop us exactly where they wanted us.”

The warning earlier in the week had been contained, every report he had received suggested there was no continuing threat severe enough to justify today’s engagement, and when Caleb had asked him directly whether postponement was necessary, Arian had told him no.

He had been so fucking certain.

“I should have postponed the visit.”

Caleb frowns. “What?”

“I knew there had already been a threat. I should have changed the schedule regardless of what the intelligence said.”

“Arian…”

“I brought you out because I was convinced we’d contained it.”

“Hey, no we are not doing that.”

Arian stops, Caleb is looking directly at him.

“You gave me the choice, I decided to go, whoever did this tonight could have tried somewhere else tomorrow, or next week, or while I was doing something considerably less worthwhile. You can’t just decide that everything that happened tonight is somehow your fault.”

Arina doesn’t say anything for a while before he lets out a sigh, and closes his eyes briefly. “I know.”

“And we can’t just spend the rest of our lives cancelling everything because someone might decide to shoot at me, you know.”

“Our lives?” Arian raises an eyebrow. Caleb rolls his eyes.

“That is what you took from that?”

Despite everything, Arian almost laughs, the sound never quite escapes him but sees it anyway and smiles back.

For the first time since leaving the convoy, Arian becomes properly aware of how close they are. There’s barely enough room between them to call it a distance. Caleb’s back rests against the brick while Arian stands directly in front of him, one arm still planted beside his head, the other hanging low with the gun. Every breath Caleb takes shifts the front of his coat against Arian’s chest. Arian can feel the warmth coming from him despite the cold air, and can smell the trace of whatever cologne Caleb had put on that morning.

Caleb doesn’t appear bothered by the proximity.

If anything, he seems calmer inside it, but his eyes seem to follow Arian’s movements as well.

“I have a place here a few hours away, it’s secure, and I have enough people there that no one gets close without us knowing. I can take you there, but getting there unnoticed will be a problem.” Arian says.

Caleb’s mouth curve in a smile, one that Arian is very well aware of by now.

“That’s an awfully tacky way of wanting to take me home, and you haven’t even bought me dinner first.”

Arian stares at him.

Despite everything that has happened, Caleb Whitmore still finds the time to flirt with him in a dark alley.

And even after all this, Arain’s mouth betrays him first. “Don’t flatter yourself, Your Highness. Keeping you alive through an attempted assassination isn’t generally my idea of a date.”

Caleb’s eyes brighten. “That’s a shame cause those are usually the best kind.”

“Your standards are concerning.”

A quiet laugh leaves Caleb, and then neither of them say anything, their eyes remain on each other.

Caleb’s smile fades slowly rather than disappearing altogether, leaving something softer behind. Arain becomes conscious of his own breathing, of Caleb’s face only inches from his, of the faint warmth of each exhale against his mouth. Their surroundings seem to recede into something distant, the empty street, the dark windows overhead, even the gun still resting against Arian’s palm.

Caleb looks at him without the usual challenge in his expression, without the usual amusement or irritation. He knows the exact shade they become when Caleb is tried, and the way they narrow before some particularly irritating remark escapes him.

Tonight there is something vulnerable in them.

Arian hears himself ask, very quietly, “Are you scared?”

Caleb doesn’t answer immediately, instead he gives a small shake of his head.

“No.”

Arian waits as something shifts in Caleb’s eyes, and then feels him relaxing into his arms.

“A little.”

“I think that’s allowed.”

“You’re here though, you’ll take care of it, won’t you?”

There is no teasing, Caleb simply asks him as though the answer is already decided. Arian nods once.

“I will.”

“Without getting yourself killed in the process.”

Something inside Arian tightens. There’s fear in Caleb’s eyes, not only for what might happen to him, but fear for Arian.

Arian leans slightly closer. “That’s the plan.”

Arian feels Caleb’s hand before he sees it move, fingers curl slowly into the front of his jacket, gathering the fabric near his chest. There is no force behind it, but Arian allows the small movement to bring himself closer until the last of the space between them nearly disappears.

Their noses brush. Their foreheads touch next.

Caleb goes very still, so does Arian.

His hand comes away from the wall and settles over Caleb’s where it remains curled in his jacket. There is blood dried across Arian’s fingers, dark against Caleb’s skin, not his blood. One of his men had died with Arian’s hand pressed uselessly against a wound that had already taken too much.

Caleb closes his eyes. The trust in that small gesture nearly undoes him.

Arian can feel Caleb everywhere, the warmth of him through their clothes, the rise and fall of his breathing, the faint beat of his pulse beneath the hand Arian is covering. For once there is nothing clever between them.

They simply stand there, breathing each other in, knowing the other is safe and that’s enough.

“Promise me you won’t get yourself killed.” Caleb whispers.

Arian’s fingers tighten gently over his. 
“Promise.” Arian watches with quiet admiration, as Caleb exhales and completely melts under his arms, eyes closed, forehead pressed, as if he’s been seeking a moment of peace after everything that took place tonight.

Arian stays. He doesn’t know how long, perhaps only seconds, perhaps long enough for Caleb’s breathing to settle. Long enough that Arian becomes aware that if Caleb lifts his face even slightly, there would be almost no distance left between their mouths.

Several seconds pass, and then the moment breaks as Caleb opens his eyes and leans his head back against the wall.

“Wait…I just remembered something.”

Arian blinks, realizing abruptly where they are right now.

Caleb turns his head enough to peer towards the street, Arian shifts with him instinctively, still keeping most of Caleb’s body concealed behind his own.

“There’s the cafe….”

Arian follows his gaze towards a faded sign visible farther down the road. Caleb’s expression suddenly changes with recognition. “I know where we are.” Caleb presses his hands on Arian’s shoulder to create a bit of distance, not pushing, just enough so he can clearly look at the sign.

“There is an old warehouse a few blocks from here. We could go there until you figure out what happened to your extraction.”

Arian narrows his eyes, and follows Caleb’s gaze. “Why do you know about an abandoned warehouse?”

The corner of Caleb’s mouth lifts into a smirk. “I may have attended a few college parties there.”

Arian stares at him with a remarkably unimpressed look.

“What? Did you think royal debauchery is limited exclusively to palaces and champagne?”

“I was hoping your security had exercised better judgement.”

Caleb’s smile widens. “Okay Mr boring, I was twenty, and I was fun, unlike you.”

Arian shakes his head, some of the pressure in his chest finally loosening.

“Okay, how far?”

“Two, perhaps three blocks. We used to come in from the other side, but I’m fairly certain I can find it from here. I was usually drunk, but I am sure.”

Arian rolls his eyes. “That’s wonderful.”

“Ugh, would you prefer we stand here while you continue to judge my youth?”

Arian finally steps away from the wall, he checks the road once more, and then nods.

“Alright, stay close to me.” he says, reaching for his hand.

Caleb looks down at their joined hands for the briefest moment before his fingers close securely around Arian’s, neither comments on it, it almost feels natural.

With his gun low in his other hand, Arian leads him out of the shadows.

The warehouse looks smaller than Caleb remembers. Arian pushes open the heavy metal doors, the hinges protesting with a long, grating screech as rust catches against rust. The sound tears through the silence and echoes inside the building before Arian pulls the doors shut behind them.

The building itself is enormous, opening before them into one wide, cavernous space with high windows running along the upper walls. Moonlight spills through the dirty glass in pale strips, catching the staircase climbing towards an old upper level, its metal railings eaten through with rust until they are almost entirely red. Below, the weak light reaches little more than dust, scattered chairs and some abandoned furniture.

“Well, this place has seen better days.” Caleb says as he enters the empty space.

There had once been music here, hundreds of people, endless drinks, flashing lights, and enough questionable decisions in a single night to send the entire royal family into cardiac arrest, had they ever discovered how Caleb spent his college weekends.

Now there is only silence.

A few tables have been pushed against one wall, chairs scattered around them at odd angles. Somewhere deeper inside the building, water drips steadily from a leaking pipe.

They enter several feet inside, before Arian finally stops.

“Wait here.”

Arian walks towards the metal stairs present at the far end of the building, as Caleb remains where he was told. Arian disappears briefly to the upper side, Caleb can still see him checking the area, making sure there is no one else but them. Minutes later he reappears climbing down the staircase, the metal screeching beneath his weight.

Caleb looks around and finds a switch beside him, so he flicks it.

Nothing happens, he tries the second switch, and an old lamp hanging somewhere above the tables flickers uncertainly before bathing one corner of the warehouse in weak yellow light.

Arian walks up to him, and frowns at the light switch. “I told you to wait.”

Caleb rolls his eyes. “I survived being shot at tonight, I think I can be trusted with a light switch, stop getting so paranoid.” Caleb says as he walks past him over to a nearest table, and pulls up a chair, making it screech loudly on the floor.

Arian closes his eyes with a sigh, but says nothing otherwise.

Arian walks towards him. “It’s clear, no one’s been here recently. The upper floor is empty and there aren’t any other entrances apart from the ones we have already seen.”

“This place used to be spectacular. It looks so depressing now”

“Well for what it’s worth, it’s supposed to keep you alive for now.” Arian opens his coat and brings out both his guns, placing them on the table, checking for any missing bullets. “The security will maintain the convoy for as long as possible,  if whoever attacked us is still watching, they’ll continue to believe you’re in the car, that gives me enough time to arrange another extraction.”

It should reassure Caleb, but his mind returns briefly to the men lying on the road.

He pushes the image away.

Arian removes his coat next and drapes it over the back of one of the better looking chairs. Then he starts rolling his sleeves up.

Caleb stares.

Oh for fuck’s sake.

Apparently nearly being assassinated has done absolutely nothing to improve his priorities.

Arian folds one sleeve neatly towards his elbow, then the other, exposing the same tanned forearms Caleb has spent an embarrassing amount of time pretending not to notice.

This is neither the time, nor the place. Someone may still be trying to kill Caleb, and he’s here wondering what those arms would feel like wrapped around his waist.  His brain needs to develop some respect for context.

Caleb clears his throat before he speaks. “Any luck with Adrien?”

“Not yet,” Arias says, checking his phone. “I have already tried several times, at this point, I don't know whether he’s even alive.”

Caleb’s mind goes back to the dead bodies again, he looks down at the scratched surface of the table before asking quietly, “How many?”

Arian glances towards him.

“Your men.” Caleb hesitates. “How many were…?” he cannot quite finish, but Arian understands.

“Five.”

Caleb’s chest tightens. All of them dead, in the line of duty, dead because they had been trying to keep him safe. The thought leaves something ugly twisting beneath his ribs.

“I’m sorry.”

Caleb looks up and finds Arian watching him. There is something strange in his expression, not grief exactly, at least not in any form Caleb recognizes, but familiarity. As though this is not the first time Arian has stood among the dead and carried on regardless. Perhaps he has lost enough people that grief no longer knows how to reach his face, settling instead into the terrible calm front he wears so well.

“Whoever attacked was firing from somewhere outside our immediate perimeter,” Arian explains. “By the time we realized what was happening, five were already down. I couldn’t locate the shooter without leaving you exposed.”

He says it so calmly that it unsettles Caleb.

When the first gunfires were heard, Arian had changed entirely.

Caleb wonders how many times he has had to do that, how many people Arian has watched die over the years, or the lives he couldn’t save.

How many has Arian killed himself?

His gaze drifts towards the guns lying on the table but he keeps quiet. He’s isn’t certain he ever wants the answer to that.

It is surprisingly easy to forget what Arian is.

Their lives have become so tangled that sometimes Caleb catches himself thinking of him only as Arian. The quiet presence behind him at official engagements, and the insufferable bastard who deliberately provokes him simply because he enjoys watching Caleb react.

But Arian Conti is a criminal.

There is no romantic way around that fact.

His world exists beyond the boundaries Caleb has been taught all his life are supposed to matter, laws, governments and traditions, the structure Caleb’s family represents.

Arian’s world has its own rules. And there is blood somewhere beneath it and Caleb refuses to be naive about it.

Yet there is something about watching Arian work that makes the distinction more complicated rather than less. Nothing about him is reckless, even tonight, with blood drying across his fingers, every choice Arian made had been designed around getting Caleb out alive.

They belong to two different worlds, two different names, and two completely different ideas of power. Caleb had no idea where someone like him is supposed to fit into Arian’s life.

The thought hurts more than it has any right to.

“You should rest.” Arian says, suddenly bringing Caleb out of his thoughts.

Caleb looks around at their available accommodations. “And where exactly would you recommend I enjoy this rest?”

Arian gestures towards the chair behind him that he had pulled out rather loudly just a few minutes ago.

And Caleb lets out a sigh, despite himself he wordlessly lowers on to the chair and stretches his legs out in front of him. His body seems to realize immediately how exhausted he actually is.

And now he has no idea when he’s going home.

By one in the morning, Caleb has exhausted nearly every possible source of entertainment available to him. Though his phone had lasted longer than expected. He had answered a few messages and scrolled mindlessly through social media until accepting that even the internet could no longer save him.

With a long sigh, he drops his phone onto the table and lets his head fall back.

“You’re way too restless, stop moving so much and take a nap.” Arian says, not lifting his head up from his own phone.

Caleb lifts his own head again, offended. “We’ve been here for hours, I’m losing my mind.”

Arian remains perched against the corner of the table, apparently unmoved by Caleb’s suffering. He has spent most of the past hour alternating between calls with his men and cleaning one of his guns and seems perfectly capable of continuing both activities until sunrise.

Caleb has a little less patience than that. It’s already past one, and the adrenaline that had carried him through the first hour has long since faded, leaving behind exhaustion and an increasingly unbearable awareness that they are still stuck inside an abandoned warehouse in the middle of London.

“Tell me again why can’t we just make contact with the palace right now?”

“Not yet.”

“Why not?”

“Because I don’t know what we’re dealing with.” He places the gun down. “Someone knew your route tonight, they knew enough to compromise the convoy, until I know where that information came from, I’m limiting contact.”

“You think whoever tried to kill me tonight might be there too?”

Caleb doesn’t like that thought one bit, not when his sisters are there. Arian notices and quickly shuts it down.

“I’m saying we don’t make assumptions until I get you somewhere secure.”

Caleb sighs, as he gets up from the chair and leans back against the edge of the table. “You know, I spend half my life finding excuses to escape that palace. The moment I actually manage it, people start shooting at me, that offends me on so many levels.”

“Perhaps you should take a hint, and start following proper security protocols for once.”

“Oh no, you are not going to put the blame on me. None of it even makes much sense anyway, if someone is determined to destabilise the Crown, they are wasting an extraordinary amount of effort on the wrong brother. Andrew’s the one who’ll be King.”

Something shifts in Arian’s expressions. Caleb sees it for a brief second but decides it’s probably nothing.

“Not that I’m suggesting anyone redirect their murderous ambitions towards my brother,” he adds. “I’d prefer we all remain alive, but there is rather an obvious flaw in the strategy.”

He casually looks up, leaning slightly back on his palms resting on the table. “Besides, what does anyone gain from killing the spare?”

He obviously means it as a joke, mostly. But the words still land somewhere unpleasant inside him. He feels Arian watching him, he’s quiet long enough that Caleb starts to regret ever saying it.

“Is that what you think you are?”

Caleb looks over. “Technically? Yes. That is rather the point of having four children.”

“That’s not what I asked.”

“It’s hardly a tragedy, I’ve made quite a comfortable existence out of being unnecessary.” Caleb shrugs.

Arian’s expression changes, but there is no amusement in it. “You’ve clearly never paid attention to the way people respond to you.”

Caleb laughs softly. “I’m a prince, people respond to the title.”

“I don’t think those children cared much about your title today, half of them were climbing over you before your security team knew what to do with themselves.”

“Do you have a point?”

“The point is that you may not be the one inheriting the crown, but you’ve already managed something people with far more power spend their entire lives trying to achieve.”

Caleb swallows, as the smile he’s been wearing disappears slowly. “And what’s that?”

“They love you.”

There is no teasing in Arian’s voice. If anything, he sounds irritatingly matter-of-fact, casually cleaning his gun while making Caleb’s heart flutter as though he hasn’t just said something capable of throwing Caleb completely off balance. Hearing that from Arian does something warm to the center of his chest.

Caleb stares at him for another second, before deciding this conversation has become entirely too sincere for his continued wellbeing.

So his mouth tilts into a smile as he leans forward.

“Careful Godfather, keep talking to me like that and I might start thinking you actually like having me around.”

Arian smirks, finally looking up, Caleb’s heart skips another unwarranted beat.

“Don’t get carried away, I merely made an observation.”

“How disappointing.” he settles back again. “Tell me, are all Italian men resistant to my charms, or is this particular affliction unique to you, I should probably make a note before we arrive in Sicily.”

“Perhaps you should be making notes about the diplomatic meetings rather than how many Sicilian men you intend to charm.”

“I can multitask.”

“That remains to be seen.”

Arian returns to his phone, though the faint curve still lingering at the corner of his mouth rather undermines the indifference he is trying to sell as the conversation fades naturally into silence.

The warehouse becomes strangely peaceful despite the circumstances. Moonlight still filters through the high windows, while the weak lamp above them throws a warm pool of light around the table.

“Tell me about it.” Caleb asks after a moment of silence. Arian looks up.

“Sicily. I’ve never been there before, but I have heard it’s beautiful, although I suppose I’ll be seeing enough government buildings and conference rooms to develop a different opinion.”

For a moment Caleb thinks Arian might dismiss the question. But then he puts his phone away and speaks.

“It is beautiful. I’ve lived there my entire life, the coast is probably what you’d expect. Beaches, old towns, too many tourists in summer.” Arian pauses. “The mountains are quieter.”

“You prefer them.” Caleb says, not a question, because he can tell just from the tone of Arian’s voice.

"Yes. It’s peaceful when you know where to go, but Sicily isn’t what people imagine from photographs either. The families are still there, territory matters, and ports matter. People fight over both.”

“That must be exhausting.”

Arian gives a quiet scoff. “It’s not an easy business but people romanticise it. Our hands aren’t clean, once you’re born into the world or choose to enter it, getting out isn’t as simple as deciding you’ve had enough.”

Caleb considers that. “Sounds an awful lot like being born into a royal family. Being born with a silver spoon doesn’t exactly  mean you get to hand in your notice.”

Arian smirks. “The difference is that no one pretends men like me are saints. Governments have flags, titles and speeches about duty. We have guns and enough sense not to call ourselves honourable.”

Caleb laughs, but the words settle somewhere deeper than the joke.

Perhaps their worlds aren't as different then. Both are built on power, loyalty and decisions made by people most will never meet. The difference is that Arian's world makes no great performance of being righteous, while Caleb's has spent centuries dressing its uglier choices in ceremony and calling them duty.

And that is why his father had found it so easy to sit across from Arian Conti and negotiate in the first place.

Arian smiles. “But…none of that changes Sicily, the mountains are quieter, so it’s peaceful to just go there sometimes and forget about everything else for a while.” His expression softens almost imperceptibly. “When I was younger, my mother and I used to….”

He stops.

Caleb waits.

Arian’s eyes flick towards him, and whatever had opened for those few seconds closes just as quietly.

"You'll probably be too busy to see much of it anyway, you have a full schedule, so focus on surviving that first. " he says.

Caleb knows a quick deflection when he sees one, he could ask though.

The curiosity is immediate, almost painfully so, but he doesn't.

It is the first time Arian has ever mentioned his mother.

Come to think of it, Caleb knows very little about Arian’s life that doesn’t involve the Conti name, guns or whatever complicated network of power he commands. He has never heard him speak about his childhood, or what his mother or father was like, or whether there were brothers or sisters once.

A family.

The fact that Arian now stands alone at the head of that family answers enough that other members are no longer here and perhaps blood may have been involved.

Arian’s life exists somewhere darker, where violence carries its own authority and survival sometimes seems to matter more than legality.

Whether their worlds are meant to fit together or not, Caleb knows one thing with certainty is that none of it has made him afraid of Arian. He saw it tonight, when danger came for him, Arian moved towards it.

Something tightens in his chest as he looks at Arian.

“Maybe you can show me. The mountains you speak of.” Arian looks back, as the corner of his mouth lifts.

“We’ll see”

Caleb smiles. "I'll take that as a yes."

Arian opens his mouth, presumably to correct him when suddenly his phone buzzes, the sound cuts strangely through the quiet.

The conversation dies right there, as Arian takes his phone and glances at the screen. Something makes him pause as he stares at it for several seconds.

Arian notices Caleb’s gaze on him, and instead of giving him a chance to ask, he answers the phone. 
“What is it?”

Whatever comes through the other end wipes the last trace of amusement from his face. Arian turns and walks several steps towards the darker end of the warehouse, putting enough distance between them that Caleb immediately understood.

He doesn’t want Caleb to listen to whoever is on the phone.

The call is considerably longer than the others.

Caleb can barely make out Arian's voice from where he stands. Most of the conversation seems to be happening on the other end anyway. Arian says very little.

A quiet yes once.

By the time the phone finally lowers from his ear, several more minutes have passed and Caleb stopped pretending as if he wasn’t listening.

Arian doesn’t turn around, as Caleb waits.

Normally, Arian would already be moving, making another call, planning five steps ahead, but there is nothing.

The warehouse has become painfully quiet again, somewhere beyond them, the leaking pipe continues its slow rhythm against the concrete.

“Arian?”

Arian flinches, the reaction is subtle, and it shouldn’t unsettle Caleb as much as it does, but for one strange second he has the distinct impression that Arian had forgotten he was there.

Then Arian turns, and whatever Caleb was about to say disappears. He has never seen Arian look like this.

Like the color seems to have drained from his face, his brows are drawn tightly, the usual composure replaced by an expression Caleb cannot quite read, as though Arian has heard something his mind hasn’t managed to make sense of yet.

Caleb straightens immediately. “What happened?”

When Arian says nothing, Caleb pushes away from the table, crossing the space between them.

“Was that one of your men?”

His breathing is wrong, someone who didn’t know him might never notice it, but Caleb does. Each breath is a little shallow, a little too deliberate, and the longer Caleb watches him, the more uneasy he becomes.

Caleb stops in front of him. “Arian, what’s going on?” 
Arian’s eyes move slowly over his face, Caleb falls quiet.

Arian isn’t merely looking at him now. His gaze travels from Caleb’s eyes to his mouth, lower for the briefest moment before returning, as though checking something. As though Caleb might somehow have changed during the five minutes they spent apart.

Caleb takes another step closer. “Hey, look at me.” Caleb says in a softer voice, tilting his head slightly until their eyes meet properly.

“Did…did you hear from Adrien, is he dead?”

Arian takes a breath, and then speaks. “No.”

“No, you didn’t hear from him, or…”

“Caleb.”

There is something the way Arian says his name that stops him for a minute.

“Yes?

“I need to tell you something.” his voice comes out a mere whisper.

“Then tell me, I’m right here.” he says quietly.

Arian’s jaw tightens. Caleb’s gaze travels down to Arian’s hand clutching onto his phone so tight, Caleb thinks he might break it.

Caleb gives him another moment, but whatever is happening behind those dark eyes appears to be moving far faster than Arian can put into words.

Oh for god’s sake.

Caleb closes the remaining distance between them and places both hands on Arain’s shoulder.

“Whatever it is, just tell me.”

Arian swallows and looks down, closing his eyes, but Caleb is relentless. He doesn’t care about getting an answer anymore as much as he cares about whatever is happening to the man standing in front of him.

He hesitates only briefly before bringing his hands to either side of Arian’s face, titling his head up to meet his gaze. Arian allows it.

Caleb feels the tightness and the faint roughness along Arian’s jaw beneath his palms. They are standing close enough now that Caleb has to lift his chin slightly to hold his gaze.

“Talk to me.” he murmurs.

Arian’s hands come up to close gently around Caleb’s wrist, the touch is soft, careful that it does terrible things to Caleb’s heartbeat.

Caleb’s thumb moves softly along Arian’s cheeks as Arain’s grip on his wrist tightens.

“I’m here.”

Their eyes meet for half a second before Arian moves, and Caleb has just enough time to see him coming before every coherent thought leaves his head.

Arian’s mouth finds his in a kiss that steals his breath away.

Caleb freezes.

Every word dies in his throat, and for one suspended, impossible moment, he can do nothing but stand there and feel it.

Arian is kissing him.

His mouth is warm against Caleb’s, the kiss almost startlingly soft for something that came without warning. There is no teasing in it, none of the smug amusement Caleb has grown accustomed to, only a strange, aching urgency that steals the breath from his lungs.

Arian’s lips part just enough that Caleb feels the heat of his breath, the faint press of tongue against the seam of his mouth before it retreats, as if Arian is testing the edge of something he cannot stop himself from wanting.

Arian’s fingers tighten around his wrists, keeping Caleb’s palms against his face as though he cannot quite bring himself to let go.

Caleb’s heart slams painfully against his ribs.

He has imagined this more times than he would ever admit. He has wondered what that mouth would feel like against his, whether Arian would kiss with the same maddening control he applied to everything else.

The reality lasts barely five seconds.

It is still enough to obliterate every imagined version.
