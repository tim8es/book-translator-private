Chapter 9

Chapter Notes

For the first time in seven days, Arian can breathe. Standing on the balcony of his room, he looks over the estate, quieter, more serene during the night. The warmth and the softened sunlight that falls on the old stone walls, tones itself down, as the night grows colder.

Beyond the gardens, the mountains rise in dark, uneven layers beneath the sky, their peaks fading into the distance.

Arian takes a slow drink from the whiskey glass in his hand, and lets the silence settle around him. He likes it when the villa goes quiet and there’s nothing immediately demanding an answer from him. For the past seven days, he’s been investigating the murder and the disappearance of his men, the business with Marco, endless meetings with other mafia leaders.

And underneath all this, has been the death of the King looming on top of his head like a dangling sword. His death has changed the balance of things in a way Arian doesn’t fully understand yet. Andrew is king, which means Caleb is no longer simply the youngest son of a reigning monarch who happens to be under Arian’s protection. He is now the brother of a King, and a British prince living indefinitely inside a Sicilian estate belonging to the head of one of the most powerful criminal families in Europe.

It’s only a matter of time until the King would demand Caleb’s return.

It’s a political disaster waiting for the right moment to become one.

Arian stares into the darkness. He has known from the beginning that he can not keep Caleb here forever without consequences.

Arian doesn’t know Andrew personally, but he knows enough about what happened to understand the kind of man he is dealing with.

Andrew had dismantled an entire monarchy in a single night, removed his father with calculated precision and became King. None of it had been accidental, every move had been planned, and every piece had been put into place long before anyone realized there was a game being played.

It was almost a perfect execution to the plan.

Had Arian not moved quickly as he had, Andrew would have gotten to Caleb too.

Arian has spoken to Matilda twice since bringing Caleb to Sicily, their conversations had been brief and careful. She asked about her brother and Arian gave her updates, small updates that he’s fine, physically at least he’s safe.

He has not told her that one of his men had mentioned that Caleb had spent nearly two hours in the library one morning, without reading a single page. He had simply sat there, and stared out of the window.

He’s come across Caleb several times since returning to the villa, though never for long. Caleb has made quite clear he doesn’t want any form of interaction with him at the moment. At lunch, he didn’t appear at all, Arian also ate in his study surrounded by reports and correspondence. When they crossed paths in the corridors, Caleb looked away. Once Arian had caught him standing at the end of the gallery with a book in his hand, their eyes had met briefly before Caleb immediately looked down.

There is no point in forcing him right now, Arian understands that. Caleb is grieving and he is angry, Arian has given him enough reasons to be both.

So, he’s just going to leave him alone, for now.

He takes another sip from his glass when he notices some movements in his peripheral and looks down.

Caleb has stepped through the rear doors into the courtyard.

Arian’s expression changes, only slightly.

Caleb is wearing a sleeping gown with narrow golden stripes running along the edges. It hangs loosely around him, tied at the waist, his dark hair falling untidily over his forehead. He looks around the courtyard before walking towards the pool.

Arian remains still, he could call out but he doesn’t.

There is no need. The estate is secure from every side, the perimeter is monitored constantly and Arian has doubled the security around the property after bringing Caleb to Sicily. There is no road leading directly to the villa without passing through one of his checkpoints. Caleb is safe here.

Caleb stops beside the pool and, for a moment, simply looks into the water. Then his fingers move to the belt of his robe.

Arian’s eyes narrow.

Caleb loosens it, and the robe slips from his shoulders. Arian quickly looks away and lasts for perhaps three seconds before his gaze returns.

Caleb stands at the edge of the pool in nothing but a pair of dark shorts, and Arian’s fingers tighten around his glass.

Arian has held Caleb before. He knows the shape of his body beneath his clothes, he remembers the strength in his shoulder and the warmth of his skin beneath his hands. He remembers Caleb pulling him closer when they kissed, and the brief, dangerous moment when Arian forgot everything except the feeling of him.

He remembers wanting more, remembers exactly how much he wanted him. This should have been enough reason to look away.

Caleb is beautiful.

There is no point denying something so obvious. The world has been admiring him since he was old enough to be photographed. The press loves his face, his smile, the effortless way he walks into a room and makes people want to know him.

Arian does understand the fascination, but he has been close enough to Caleb to see it firsthand, that it is not simply the charm Caleb reserves for the general public, but the little things about him that most people never get close enough to notice.

Like the freckles scattered across Caleb’s face, the small mole high on his left cheekbone, the softness of his hair, which seems incapable of staying perfectly styled for more than an hour. His long lashes and those soft grey-brown eyes that change with the light.

His soft lips that Arian remembers against his.

Caleb steps forward and dives into the pool. Arian watches the water close around him, as Caleb surfaces a few seconds later and pushes his hair away from his face. Slowly gracefully, he starts to swim.

Arian has spent the entire week away thinking about Caleb. The back of his car, during meetings, late at night when the house he stayed in was too quiet.

Caleb reaches the end of the pool and turns before beginning another lap. Then, without warning, he stops, rolls onto his back and lets the water carry him. Something changes in his expression.

The confidence has disappeared, so had the amusement, for once there is no smile waiting at the corner of his mouth. He looks tired.

Not from swimming, but from everything else.

Caleb looks painfully alone. It’s the first time Arian has seen him like this. Not angry or sarcastic, just lost.

Arian lets out a sigh and turns away from the scene.

He has given Caleb enough reasons to hate him, he has no right to take this moment from him too. Especially because he knows what it means to have the need to be somewhere no one can reach you. Caleb has every right to be angry with him.

Arian’s mind had stopped working properly the minute he received the call from Matilda back at the warehouse. There had been no strategy, no careful consideration, and only one overwhelming need.

Get him out.

And then Arian had looked at him standing in front of him and realized something he spent months refusing to acknowledge.

He can not lose him, not now and not like that.

He was overcome with the need to touch, just enough to make sure that Caleb was alive, and warm and real.

The kiss had been instinct, nothing more calculated than that. He had not kissed Caleb to distract him, or manipulate him, he had not even known what he was doing until it had already happened. He had simply needed to feel him, that the same fate that had taken his father had not already reached him.

And perhaps in some selfish corner of his mind, he needed Caleb to understand it.

But Arian understands now how it could have looked from Caleb’s side.

His father had died, and Arian kissed him, what else was Caleb supposed to think?

In his own selfishness, he had needed Caleb close, and didn’t stop to ask what Caleb needed.

And there is no excuse for that.

Arian is beginning to realise that the years he has spent mastering restraint and control mean very little when Caleb is around. It is no longer just about keeping the prince safe, the thoughts he finds himself having, the things he wants, are becoming harder to contain, and he no longer knows how close he is to crossing a line he may not be able to come back from.

He wants Caleb in ways he has never allowed himself to want anyone before. The mere thought of it terrifies him.

He knows what he’s capable of doing to protect someone. He’s done terrible things for less, but Caleb has already changed the limits of what Arian is willing to risk. He has already brought Caleb to the one place in the world that is entirely his own.

Arian looks down at his glass and finishes the last mouthful before setting it on the railing.

Without looking back, he turns and leaves.

He doesn’t see Caleb open his eyes, or turn his head towards the balcony, noticing the empty glass sitting on the railing or the moment he  realized that Arian had been standing there all along.

Halfway through the third report, Arian hears footsteps approach his study. His pen stops across the page as Caleb walks in and stops right before the wooden table.

That in itself, is unusual enough to make Arian look up.

He has spent the better part of the week watching Caleb avoid him, and somehow he even developed an impressive ability to disappear whenever Arian entered a room. Now he’s directly walking towards him, head held high and expressions serious.

Caleb is dressed in the same suit he had worn when he left London, dark fitted and unmistakably expensive. Most of his clothes have been left behind in London.

Arian had intended to have someone arrange a wardrobe for him, and not yet found the right moment to bring it up.

Caleb, meanwhile, seems like he’s solved that problem himself. He is carrying his jacket in his other arm. Arian remembers him complaining the previous afternoon about the Sicilian weather, insisting that he is more accustomed to London’s cold.

Arian raises his eyebrow and waits for Caleb to speak.

“I would like to go out.”

Straight to the point.

Arian leans back slightly as Caleb continues before he could respond. “I want to see the markets around here, buy some new clothes, and I want to find somewhere decent to eat.”

Clearly the words are not phrased as a request, it’s an itinerary. Arian’s mouth twitches, as he places his pen down carefully.

“If you need anything, I can have someone bring it here.”

“No, I want to go by myself. I would like to see Sicily, not the inside of your house.”

And there it is. Arian had known it would come eventually.

He closes his eyes briefly and exhales through his nose.  Caleb is not a prisoner, and Arian would never make him one. He had never told the staff to keep him confined, never locked a door or demanded that Caleb ask permission before leaving his room.

But freedom and safety are not always compatible.

Arian knows that if Caleb goes out and is photographed, the media will have a field day with it. The tabloids would turn it into another story that would paint Caleb in a negative light, and it would be controversial enough to get people talking.

Arian sets his paper aside and gets up from his chair.

“Fine.”

Caleb blinks and Arian doesn’t miss the look of surprise on his face.

“We can go wherever you want, but not like this.” He says and gestures towards Caleb’s clothes.

Caleb looks down at himself. “What is wrong with my clothes?”

Half an hour later, Caleb stands beneath the covered entrance to the villa and wonders what exactly Arian considers an acceptable public appearance.

The answer apparently is an oversized black hoodie that was brought to him by one of Arian’s men, and Caleb is almost certain that it belongs to Arain. It hangs far too low from the shoulders, and the hood sits heavily around his face. He’s never gone out looking like this.

But Caleb realises it’s better not to complain, because at least he gets to go out, and that's what matters at the moment. He has spent way too much time wandering the same rooms, the same gardens and the same library last week and he desperately needs something else that doesn’t include the inside of this villa.

The front doors open as Arain steps out and Caleb forgets about his own hoodie.

Arian walks out dressed nothing like the man Caleb has spent the last few months seeing in London. Not a formal suit, just a dark t-shirt beneath a black leather jacket and black trousers. Caleb stares for a second longer than he’s meant to.

For one strange second, Caleb thinks of the man in Paris.

The stranger who found him in the middle of the gunfire, the man who stood between Caleb and death without hesitation, who had taken his hand and led him through the night and made Caleb believe for a little while that nothing could touch him.

Here, Arian looks younger, taller somehow and more like the man Caleb remembers from Paris and less like the Godfather he has spent months trying to understand.

It all stirs something painfully familiar inside him, but Caleb knows better now.

Arian is not some mysterious savior who had simply happened to find him in the middle of chaos. He is the Godfather of his family, powerful and dangerous, and someone who is here because he has a duty to fulfil, because the Crown asked him to assess an alliance and protect him.

And it’s a heart breaking realization that even after everything, seeing him like this makes his heart beat as though he were looking at that man again.

The only thing missing is the hat.

The one he had worn in Paris is still in London, sitting somewhere in Caleb's drawer. He never returned it, and Arian never asked for it.

Caleb's eyes linger for a second too long before he looks away.

One of Arian’s men approaches and hands him the keys to the car. The Ferrari waits beneath the entrance, black and low against the stone of the villa. Arian takes the keys and accepts the gun the man hands him. He checks it briefly before slipping it beneath his jacket.

Caleb looks around, and realizes there’s no one else with them.

“Are we going alone?” he asks.

“The key to staying unnoticed is keeping things simple, my men attract attention.”

Arian walks past him toward the car, and Caleb follows wordlessly as he climbs into the passenger seat.

He stares straight ahead as Arian starts the car, refusing to acknowledge the thought that has quietly slipped into the corner of his mind: this feels dangerously like a date.

He refuses to think about that.

The market is alive, the narrow streets are crowded with people, old buildings rising on either side in pale stone and faded colors, balconies overflowing with flowers and laundry hanging between them. Vendors call to one another across stalls filled with fruits, ceramics, fabrics, leather goods and food.

Caleb slows almost immediately, wandering without much of a plan. A book catches his attention, then a small painting, then a display of handmade ceramics. He stops whenever something interests him and moves on to the next.

Arian quietly follows. He doesn’t interfere or ask where Caleb is going, he simply walks behind him, close enough to notice when the crowd becomes too dense and far enough to give Caleb the afternoon to himself.

It is strange. Caleb had expected Arian to take control of the entire outing, instead he let him wander and somehow that makes Caleb more aware of him than if Arian had been giving him orders.

They pass a fruit stall when an elderly woman looks up, her face immediately brightening.

“Mr Conti, what a pleasant surprise.”

Arian stops as Caleb watches a woman reach for his hand and begins to speak rapidly in Italian.

The affection in her face is obvious, there is no fear or hesitation. Arian listens, answers quietly when she takes a pause. At one point, she laughs and touches his arm, then looks towards Caleb. Her expression changes with recognition, and Arian says something to her.

She smiles, and they continue forward.

Caleb is tempted to ask what she said, but Arian is already guiding him away from the crowd heading their way, one hand resting gently at the small of Caleb’s back as he steers him toward the stalls on the other side.

A few streets later, another man greets Arian, then a shopkeeper, then an elderly couple. People know him, not just because they are afraid of him, they know him because he is a man with influence who belongs here.

Caleb has seen Arain in London, present himself as powerful in a polished room, and behind closed doors. But here it’s different, here people know him, know his name and his family name. They stop him in the street, and Arian doesn't seem particularly surprised by any of it.

Caleb is suddenly brought out of his thoughts, when Arain’s arm folds around his waist and brings him close as the crowd passes in front of them. Their faces are suddenly close, too close.

Arian looks down at him. “Careful.”

Caleb swallows, as the crowd thins, but Arain’s hand remains at his waist for one moment longer before falling away.

Caleb steps back immediately and adjusts his hoodie.

By the end of the market, Caleb has bought far more than he intended, just small things like a couple of paintings, a few books, some scarves and plants. They wandered into some boutiques nearby and Caleb was able to buy some new clothes, though Arian had told him he’ll have a proper wardrobe arranged for him soon enough.

Arian has been carrying all the bags, and Caleb doesn’t ask if he needs help. He is accustomed to people carrying things for him, if Arian wants to do it, Caleb is perfectly happy to let him.

Later, they spot a small cafe at the end of the market, and sit outside. For the first time in days Caleb realizes he is properly hungry.

After lunch Caleb spots a stall selling cannoli and he orders one. He closes his eyes on the first bite, and makes a small delighted sound before he could stop himself. When he opens them again, Arian is smiling.

“This is the best cannoli I’ve ever had, I could die happy now.”

He is smiling with a mouth full of cream when he looks up and something in Arian’s expression has softened. Caleb’s smile immediately falters, but his heart skips a beat against his wishes.

He quietly grabs a napkin, avoiding Arian’s gaze as he wipes his mouth, determined to ignore the deep flush creeping high across his cheeks.

Arian watches the mountains darken as they drive back towards the villa. Caleb sits beside him, his head turned to the other side, and there is a calmness on his face that was not there in the morning.

He has spent the past week watching Caleb search for ways to occupy himself, with books, his horse, in the gardens, anything that allowed him to keep moving, but right now he seems the most content, simply to watch the mountains pass by.

Arian’s gaze falls back towards the road when Caleb turns his head.

“We should go to the mountains next time.”

Arian glances at him. “It will require planning.”

“Then we’ll plan.” Caleb leans his head back into the seat and adds quietly. “You promised to show me.”

Arian’s fingers tighten slightly around the steering wheel. The warehouse comes back to him, when Arian had mentioned the mountains, and told him how peaceful they felt in the midst of everything.

“We’ll see,” Arain says.

Caleb sighs, but doesn't say anything for a while, but then: “Thank you for today, I had a good time.”

Arian doesn't look back, he doesn’t trust himself to say much, so he nods as something eases inside him.

After days of silence, he heard Caleb laugh and smile today. He heard his voice again.

For now, it feels enough.

When they reach the villa, one of Arian’s men is waiting beneath the entrance. Arian has barely stepped out of the car when he approaches.

“Rowan, what is it?”

“Don Conti, an invitation from Mr. Novak.” Rowan says, presenting him a sealed envelope.

Arian recognizes the flashy golden seal and closes his eyes. He opens the envelope and reads the invitation, expression changing almost immediately.

Caleb who's been standing beside him notices. “What is it?

Arian hands him the card, and waits for Caleb to read it.

**To Arian, **

Word has reached me that you have acquired a British prince.

Naturally, I would like to see him. Bring him to dinner tonight.

He will be treated as an honoured guest.

You may come too, since apparently he cannot be left unattended.

**Pietro Novak**

Caleb looks up, eyebrows raised. “Wait, so your entire mafia circle knows I’m here?”

Arian says nothing, as Caleb gives a small disbelieving sigh. “So much for being discreet.”

“Our business remains ours but we don’t keep things from eachother.”

“Apparently not.”

“Peitro is one of the four leaders of the Sicilian families,” Arian continues. “He has been a part of this business for more than twenty years, there are certain things that remain transparent between us.”

“And now he wants to meet me. Lovely.” Caleb says

“Pietro is a bit meddlesome…complicated.”

Caleb raises an eyebrow.

Arian sighs quietly. “He’s a pain in the fucking ass.”

That earns him the smallest flicker of amusement from Caleb.

“Well then, I’d be honoured. Tell him we’ll be there.” Caleb says handing the invitation back to Rowan, before he starts towards the villa.

“Caleb, we really don’t have to go.”

Caleb turns with a smile but Arian sees the difference immediately. The openness from the car is gone.

“What? Afraid I’ll charm my way into your circle like I always do?” Caleb smirks. “Besides, you don’t get to spend months in London attending parties with me and exclude me from one of yours.”

He turns away before Arian could answer and leaves through the doors. Arian’s gaze remains on the doorway for another moment, realizing it is only a matter of time before Caleb wouldn’t be able to separate himself from his world.

He isn’t entirely sure anymore if he is protecting Caleb from it, or protecting himself from what it would mean to let Caleb into it.

The suit arrives in a black garment bag stamped with the crest of Arian’s tailor. Caleb stands in the centre of the dressing room and stares at it with a frown.

“Why does your tailor know my measurements?”

Arain, already half-dressed in a charcoal suit, doesn't look up from fastening his cuffs. His dark coat hangs over one arm.

“Your measurements aren’t classified information.”

“That doesn’t make me feel better at all.”

The suit is nothing too extravagant, dark coat layered over a deep maroon silk shirt, paired with dark trousers and a matching tie. It is unmistakably expensive, tailored to perfection and fits him effortlessly. As a Prince, Caleb is more accustomed to brighter tones and crisp white suits, but a private dinner at a mafia leader’s house seems to call for a different sort of dress code.

Not that he is complaining. Dark shades suit him quite well.

After he received his package, he dressed alone in his room, and decided to add a thin silver chain around his neck, leaving the top few buttons of his shirt undone. It was then Arian had knocked at his door, and came to ask if he was ready.

Arian looks just as unfairly good in dark colors. Dressed in black from head to toe, the Godfather looks every bit formidable as he does handsome. Caleb has seen him in countless variations of black by now, and yet it does nothing to lessen the effect where he is simply left breathless.

His attention returns to the tie that he’s been trying to fix around his collar for several minutes, but the knot has somehow managed to become worse with every attempt.

“You’ve been fighting with that tie for five minutes.” Caleb meets Arian’s eyes in the mirror and frowns.

“I have it under control.”

“Clearly.” Arian murmurs, as he moves away from the door and walks up to him, gaze lingering on him through the mirror. His eyes slowly travel over the reflection of him.

Caleb doesn’t move, as he feels the warmth of his body reach the space between his shoulder blades.

“May I?”

The question lands softly, and Caleb stills. He looks at Arian through the mirror for a moment before lowering his hands.

Arian reaches around him, his fingers closing gently around the ruined knot, and begins to loosen it.

The scent of warm tobacco and sandalwood surrounds Caleb, with something darker beneath Arian’s cologne. Caleb keeps his eyes on the reflection as those long fingers work patiently at his throat. Every single movement sends a quiet current down his spine.

There is something unbearable about being handled so carefully by the same hands that Caleb remembers, wrapped around his waist in the warehouse. The same hands that touched his face before Arian kissed him and changed the shape of everything between them.

Caleb looks up and finds Arian already watching him through the mirror, a smile touches Arian’s mouth as he finishes the knot.

The tie sits perfectly now, the dark silk falling neatly against Caleb’s chest, but neither of them seem particularly interested in it anymore. Their eyes remain locked in the mirror, and Caleb becomes painfully aware of how close Arian is standing.

He could feel the heat of him without being touched at all and he wonders why Arian isn’t moving away.

“Aren’t we getting late?” Caleb speaks in a low voice. “I’d rather not give your people to think the prince has little regards to other people’s time”

Arian tilts his head, just enough to bring his mouth closer to Caleb’s ear. “It’s not the sort of party where anyone cares when you arrive.”

Arian’s eyes meet him again in the mirror, amusement flickering in their depths.

“Not quite like the royal events you’re used to.” he whispers close to his ear. Caleb’s pulse begins to climb before he conceals it with a scoff.

“I’ve been to enough social events to know my way around men who make their living on the wrong side of the law”

Arian closes his eyes and leans closer. “I know, but there will be people who don't take kindly to royalty. Stay close to me.”

Caleb’s mouth curves, but there is no real amusement behind it. “Of course, my knight in shining armour, keeping me safe all night and locked up in your mansion all day.”

The words land exactly where Caleb intended, and Arian goes very still. Caleb sees the change in his reflection, the tightening around his eyes, the way something closes behind his expression before he smooths it away.

“You’re free to go wherever you want, Caleb,” Arian says quietly. “You’re not my prisoner, you’re safe as long as you’re here.”

Caleb looks down and smiles. “Of course,” he murmurs. “You have to keep me safe.”

Because it’s your duty.

Arian’s silence somehow hurts more than an argument would have.

His father is dead, The Crown is no longer standing between them, therefore there is no diplomatic obligation to fulfil, and yet, Caleb is too afraid to ask why Arian is still here and continues to speak of protecting him when the reason for it should have died with his father.

When Caleb looks back up at the mirror, Arian is looking at him with an expression he cannot read.

And then Arian moves closer.

Until his chest is against his back, his hands lift and settle gently against Caleb’s waist. Caleb melts into the touch instantly against his will as his shoulders loosen barely, his head tilts, exposing the line beneath his ear.

Arian’s eyes darken as his breath brushes the side of Caleb’s neck before his mouth touches the skin beneath his ear.

Caleb closes his eyes and exhales a small breath that seems to disappear into the space between them.

One kiss, a soft brush of Arian’s lips and Caleb’s pulse leaps violently beneath it.

Arian lets the kiss linger only long enough for Caleb to feel it, he pulls away only slightly before his lips touch his neck again, a little lower this time. When Caleb tips his head back to give him more access, Arian takes it as permission and trails his lips up gently, as his mouth brushes the curve beneath Caleb’s jaw.

Soft, tender kisses follow along the line of his jaw, each one breaking Caleb’s breath softly.

Arian has wrapped his hand completely around his waist, steadying him as he can feel the effect he is having. Caleb is suddenly aware of everything, the solid warmth of his chest, the slow movement of his mouth against his skin, and the quiet restraint in every touch.

It’s too much and not nearly enough.

“There’s really nothing I can say to take that anger of yours away from me.” Arian murmurs against the edge of his jaw, his voice rough.

Caleb’s hand finds Arian’s around his waist as his fingers curl around it, holding him there despite everything he has said, despite everything he is still angry about.

“You’ll have to wait a little longer for forgiveness.” his words betray him, as he presses himself more against Arian, allowing his mouth to move once more along the jaw.

“How long?”

“However long I want.”

Caleb’s breath stutters, when Arain trails kisses along the corner of his jaw.

“You don’t get to lie to me, force me out of my own country, keep my father’s death from me and then….” his words falter.

“Then what?” Arian asks softly.

Caleb swallows. “Then you kissed me and made me think it meant something.”

Arian’s lips stop moving.

For a moment, the only thing Caleb can hear is the sound of their breathing. Then Arian turns him carefully, his hands never leaving his waist. When they face each other, the sadness in Caleb’s eyes is raw and it shifts something in Arian’s eyes.

He looks at Caleb for a long moment, as though there is something he wants to say and knows no words will be enough.

“It never didn’t mean something,” Arian says at last, so quietly that Caleb almost misses it.

“I don’t believe you.”

“I know.”

Their faces are so close that every shared breath feels like a touch. Caleb’s hands close around Arian’s arms, fingers tightening against the fine fabric of his shirt.

Arian leans down, their noses brush, lips barely a breath away.

“I can show you right now what it meant to me Caleb.” Arian says, voice hoarse as if all the restraint in him is starting to slip away.

Caleb’s eyes drop to his mouth, his heart beating so hard, it almost hurts.

He leans forward, until his forehead rests against Arian’s. The smallest movement would close that last fraction of space between their mouths. For one terrible second, Caleb thinks he might do it.

He wants to, god he wants to.

Instead, he smiles.

“No.”

Arian's breath catches.

“No?”

“You don't have the permission.”

The words are quiet, but there is nothing uncertain about them. Arian’s arms tighten around Caleb’s waist, as he closes his eyes.

“For how long?”

“However long until I say otherwise.”

Caleb’s voice is barely above a whisper, yet his body betrays him completely. And then he sees something soften in Arian’s expression. There is no frustration, no attempts to persuade him, only a quiet acceptance that somehow makes Caleb’s chest ache more than resistance would have.

Arian steps back.

“Whatever you say, Your Highness.”

The loss of warmth is immediate and sharp enough to make Caleb sway. Arian’s hand slips from his waist, and Caleb hates how quickly he misses it.

But then Arian reaches for his hand and lifts it. Caleb’s eyes widen only slightly as his mouth brushes against his knuckles, the kiss lingers as warm lips press against his skin.

The simplicity in the action feels like a promise, so impossibly quiet and intimate.

When Arian finally steps back, something inside Caleb’s chest cracks wide open.

“I’ll wait downstairs.”

He picks up his coat from the chair on his way out and closes the door softly behind him.

The moment Arian is gone Caleb loses all feelings in his legs and he braces one hand against the dresser behind him. His face burns and pulse thunders everywhere Arian’s mouth had been moments ago.

He turns towards the mirror and looks at his reflection, before he presses his fingertips briefly against the place beneath his ear.

His chest aches with something dangerously close to surrender, and for once, Caleb isn’t sure he wants to fight it.

Pietro’s mansion rises above the Sicilian hillside like something built for royalty.

Vast, immaculate, and impossibly grand, its pale stone wall glowing beneath the warm evening lights. Cars line the circular drive beneath the entrance, each one more expensive than the last. Through the wall windows of the main hall, Caleb can see chandeliers burning overhead as elegantly dressed guests move between tables already placed for dinner.

There are at least sixty people inside the grand hall.

For the first time, Caleb finds himself walking into a room where he doesn’t recognise a single face.

He has spent his entire life attending social gatherings where people knew him. Ambassadors, celebrities, diplomats and businessmen. He knows how to handle these rooms, he knows the rules too.

Except, this time it's different.

These people know who Arian is, and  judging by the sudden quiet that falls over the entrance as they step inside, they know exactly who Caleb is too.

Conversations soften, heads turn, as people lay their eyes on them. Caleb feels Arian’s hand settle at the small of his back.

“Stay close.” he murmurs near Caleb’s ear.

Caleb for once doesn’t argue and leans slightly towards Arian.

“Why does it feel like I just entered enemy territory?” Caleb glances around before turning back to Arian.

“And why does everyone look as though they are deciding how best to cook me alive?”

Arian’s hand presses lightly against his back, urging him forward without a response. The have barely crossed the threshold when whispers begin to drift through the room.

“Is that the British Prince? With Arian?”

“I heard he’s been under Mr Conti’s protection for months.”

“On the late King’s orders, wasn’t it?”

Caleb keeps his expression perfectly composed as people start to whisper about his father. Years of royal training has taught him how to smile while people discuss him as though he wasn't standing three feet away.

Arian doesn’t seem affected by the comments at all.

Then a tall man in an immaculate black suit approaches them. He’s perhaps in his late fifties, hair white from its ends, slicked back neatly. His manner seems polished enough to belong in any European boardroom, as his sharp eyes fall on Caleb first.

“Well, I didn’t expect to see you here tonight. I thought you’d be too busy cleaning the mess you created at the Bellandi’s parlour.”

A faint smirk touches Arian’s mouth.

“That’s all been handled.” Arian says.

“Until Steffano decides handing Marco to him isn’t enough.”

“He can try.”

The man gives Arian a knowing look, his smirk deepens. “That’s precisely the sort of answer that gives me headaches.”

Caleb has absolutely no idea what they are talking about and he suspects he isn’t supposed to. And then he notices as the man’s attention shifts towards him, expression softening only slightly.

“Your Highness.” he inclines his head respectfully, and extends his hand. “Vincent Caruso. It’s a pleasure.”

The Caruso’s are one of the leading families in Sicily, second only to the Contis in influence. Caleb has heard the name before, though he knows little about the business that has built their influence.

He relaxes slightly and returns Vincent’s handshake with a smile.

“The pleasure is all mine, Mr. Caruso.”

“I hope you’ll forgive the unconventional hospitality. Pietro has been terribly excited about having you here.”

Before Caleb can answer, another voice appears behind them.

“Such a pity that none of us has been able to stop him once he gets started.”

An older man joins them, dressed in a tailored suit, his silver hair neatly brushed back. He places a hand on Arian’s shoulder in a gesture so familiar that Caleb notices the difference immediately. Arian doesn’t seem bothered by the gesture at all, he allows it, almost like it’s a normal occurrence.

The man turns his attention to Caleb.

“Enzo Barone, Your Highness” He inclines his head. “I must say, the air in this room changed the moment you walked through that door. We don’t often have royalty wandering into our little corner of Sicily.”

Caleb smiles politely.

“I imagine I am rather ruining everyone’s evening.”

“Not at all.” and then Enzo’s expression softens as he lowers his head.

“And please allow me to offer my condolences for your father. I am very sorry for your loss.”

For a moment, Caleb’s smile falters.

“Thank you. That’s very kind of you.”

Enzo nods, before turning his attention back towards Arian, eyes narrowing.

“Rizzo isn’t pleased about Marco.”

Arian’s expression remains unreadable. “Rizzo’s opinion is irrelevant.”

“He says Marco owes him a considerable sum.”

“Marco owed several people several things, it’s no longer his concern.”

Vincent chuckles next to them. “You’ve been back for a week and you’re already making new enemies.”

“I did what needed to be done.”

Enzo sighs. “What you did was reckless, if Steffano decides he isn’t finished, we’ll all have to deal with him.”

“I’ll handle it.” Arian says

“I’m sure you will. Though I hope you mean without blowing up an estate or two this time.” Vincent says.

Caleb’s eyebrows raise, though he figures remaining quiet is a better option for him right now. Arian's only response however, is a deeply unimpressed frown before he tilts his head in Enzo’s direction.

“I’ll handle it, Uncle Enzo.” he says more quietly. “You don’t need to worry.”

Enzo gives him a brief pause, and then nods. Just then a booming laugh erupts from somewhere behind them.

Caleb barely has time to turn before a tall man in a grey suit sweeps towards them, red pocket square, bright against his chest and dark sunglasses inexplicably perched on his face.

The man seems remarkably younger than Enzo and Vincent, perhaps in his early forties. Hair slicked back and white, he is quite handsome too.

“Oh, look at this!”

Pietro opens his arms and engulfs Arian in a hug.

Before Caleb has time to react, Pietro turns and wraps both arms around Caleb as well.

Caleb lets out a startled “Oof!”

“My God, Your Highness, what an honour, what an absolute honour.”

Caleb laughs despite himself, as he’s let go from the hug.

“The pleasure is mine…”

“I have been dying to meet you, I can’t believe this guy has been hiding you in his mansion all this time, without telling us.” Pietro says patting Arian back.

Arian closes his eyes briefly.

“Well then I hope I haven’t disappointed.” Caleb speaks politely.

“Disappointed? Never, you are much prettier than the photographs.” Pietro turns towards the hall with enthusiasm. “Oh and I hear you like art.”

Caleb suddenly perks up. “Yes, I do.”

“Brilliant, you must see my collection. Come with me.”

Before Caleb can say anything, Pietro takes his arm lightly, but then Arian’s hand catches his wrist from the other end. The movement is so immediate that it startles him.

Pietro looks at the hand around Caleb’s wrist and slowly at Arian.

“Oh for goodness sake Arian, let him go, I’m not kidnapping the boy. I am showing him paintings.”

Arian releases his hand but his gaze remains fixed on him. “Don’t go outside this hall.”

Caleb rolls his eyes. “I’ll be fine, stop being so paranoid.”

He says it quietly, but the room notices the casual way Caleb speaks to the Godfather. Caleb realises, perhaps too late, that nobody in this room has ever seen anyone speak to Arian Conti like that. And, nobody has ever seen Arian simply accept it.

“Come along, Your Highness.” Pietro grins, as he sweeps Caleb away towards the collection.

Arian watches them disappear into the crowd, only then does Vincent speak.

“What exactly are you doing?”

Vincent’s expression has lost its amusement when Arian turns to him. Enzo stands beside quieter now, but seems to carry the same sentiments.

“You brought a royal prince into our circle,” Vincent continues. “Why?”

“He is under my protection.”

“He was. You no longer have an alliance with The Crown because the King is dead.” Enzo corrects.

“And the new King? Andrew Whitmore is not exactly a man we should want looking closely at Sicily, you know that.” Vincent says.

Arian says nothing.

“If anything happens to that Prince here, the Crown won’t care about our arrangements, they’ll care about the fact that their son was under the protection of a Sicilian Godfather.”

Arian clenches his fists. “I understand the risks Vincent, you don't need to tell me that.”

“Do you?” Enzo looks at him with something close to concern.

Arian narrows his eyes.

He is quite accustomed to always being underestimated, and he hates being reminded that in rooms like this, Enzo still looks at him as though he were the young man who inherited too much power too early and needs someone older to tell him when he is being foolish.

Enzo’s gaze shifts past him, towards the hall.

Caleb stands beneath one of Pietro’s enormous paintings, listening intently as Pietro talks beside him. Caleb’s expression is attentive, smile effortless, and he seems completely composed.

Though Arian notices the faint tension in his shoulders, and the subtle stiffness in his smile. Caleb is good at pretending, but Arian recognizes the discomfort of a man who knows what it is to feel like an outsider in a room full of people who belong to a world entirely different from his own.

Arian realizes he’s been watching him for several seconds too long, which is why when he turns back, both Vincent and Enzo are looking at him, with almost unbearingly knowing expressions.

He exhales slowly. “Its not as complicated as you’re making it.”

“You served the late King Arian, if this goes wrong and Andrew decides he wants him back, we will all bear the consequences.” Vincent says sharply.

Arian’s expression hardens. “Andrew will not reach him.”

“You cannot guarantee….”

“I know precisely what I can guarantee, Vincent. I am aware of the political consequences.”

Vincent holds his gaze. “And you believe you can keep this from becoming a disaster then?”

“I believe I can keep Caleb out of it.” The answer comes too quickly.

Enzo watches him for a moment, before his expression turns grave. “Whatever your reasons for keeping the Prince here, don’t let your feelings get in the way of your judgement.”

“My feelings have nothing to do with this.”

Enzo gives him a look that suggests he believes absolutely none of that.

“If this goes wrong, we all burn.”

“I know.”

“Good.” Enzo lifts his glass. “And stop staring at the Prince so hard as though he'll disappear.”

Arian’s jaw tightens as Vincent laughs under his breath.

The collection is extraordinary. Pietro’s mansion currently houses paintings spanning several centuries, some displayed prominently beneath the chandeliers. Caleb finds himself lingering in front of a seventeenth century portrait, captivated by the delicate brushwork, while Pietro stands beside him, enthusiastically recounting the artist's history before moving on to another without waiting for him to finish.

“You really do love this, don’t you?” Pietro asks, watching him with an expression of genuine amusement.

“I do.” Caleb replies smiling.

“You have hosted some very notable auctions, I would love to see your collections, Your Highness.”

“I think that can be arranged, although I can’t promise any time soon, but one day.”

“That would be wonderful.”

Pietro is unexpectedly an entertaining host, and his enthusiasm for the collection is infectious. For a little while, Caleb has almost forgotten that he is surrounded by people whose names carry a weight he barely understands.

Then his gaze wanders across the room and settles on Arian.

He stands among a small gathering of men, a glass in one hand that he seems to have barely touched. The room seems to accommodate him naturally, as though his place within it has never been in question.

Caleb has seen him in London where he carried himself carefully, concealed. Here, among old families, Arian seems entirely at ease. Men twice his age listen when he speaks, and even those who disagree with him appear to choose their words carefully.

There is a quiet authority to him that Caleb has only glimpsed before, something that goes beyond his title or the reputation that precedes him.

This is his world, with all its danger and its unspoken rules, and Arian understands it in a way Caleb suspects he never entirely would.

It suits him rather unfairly.

Their eyes meet across the room for a few seconds, and neither seem inclined to look away. Arian’s expression eases into something softer, and Caleb’s heart beat skips.

Caleb smiles, not the practiced smile he offers strangers and photographers, but something more private. Arian's mouth curves faintly, and the warmth that blooms in Caleb's chest is both ridiculous and entirely beyond his control.

Pietro’s voice eventually draws him back.

“So, Your Highness, how are you finding Sicily?”

“Oh, it’s beautiful.” Caleb answers, still half distracted.

“And how is Arian treating you?”

Caleb glances at him. “What do you mean?”

“I mean, is he being a good host to you? Looking after you properly?”

“Oh…well he’s barely here, mostly off on business.”

Only after saying it does he realize how it sounds rather like a complaint about a partner who never comes home for dinner, almost too domestic. He clears his throat, suddenly very interested in the painting before him, but Pietro appears not to notice.

“Ah, then you’re not the only one who complains about his disappearance from time to time.” he waves dismissively. “Arian has always been terrible about it, Miss Valentina is forever complaining that she never sees him either.”

Caleb’s attention returns to him at once. “I’m sorry, who?”

“Oh Miss Caruso? She is Arian's…..ah speaking of, there she is.”

Before Pietro could finish, the doors at the far end of the ballroom opened. The sharp click of heels carry across the marble, drawing the attention of several guests. Pietro turns towards the entrance, his expression breaking into a broad smile. Caleb follows his gaze.

The woman who has entered is breathtaking. Dressed in a sweeping gold dress that catches the lights of the ball room, her dark hair gathered into an elegant knot, accentuating the striking lines of her face.

There is something self-assured in the way she walks, as she crosses the ballroom without so much as glancing at the people who have turned to look at her, but she seems to have only one person in mind.

Arian.

Caleb looks, as Arian turns towards her. For the briefest moment, something familiar softens in Arian’s expression, a flicker of recognition followed by the faintest hint of a smirk. It is enough to make Caleb’s stomach tighten.

But nothing prepares Caleb for what happens next, as she reaches Arian and steps into his space, catches the front of his collar in one hand, and pulls him down into a firm, unapologetic kiss.

Caleb goes utterly still.

Around him, the ballroom erupts in hushed murmurs and gasps, Pietro looking particularly delighted by the spectacle. Arian appears momentarily stunned, but he hasn’t pulled away, and the woman eases into the kiss as someone who has every right to be there.

“Miss Caruso must be delighted to finally reunite with Mr. Conti.”

“I heard they are finalizing their wedding next month.”

“Well it’s about time, they have been engaged for over a year.”

Every voice nearby delivers the final blow, offering a piece of information Caleb would have very much preferred not to hear.

Caleb stares in disbelief, his mouth falling open as the whispers settle into place.

*Engaged? *

He looks from the woman’s hand curled around Arian’s collar, to Arian himself, hoping against all reason that he has misunderstood something.

*Oh absolutely fucking not. *
