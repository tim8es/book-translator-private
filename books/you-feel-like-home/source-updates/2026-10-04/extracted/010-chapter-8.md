The bells begin before sunrise. By nine, London is dressed in black.

They toll from Westminster as the procession moved through streets already overflowing with people, their sound carrying above the low murmur of thousands gathered behind barriers and spilling into every road. Flowers cover the iron gates of the palace several layers deep. More have appeared outside churches, government buildings and beneath statues across the city, handwritten notes tucked between white lilies and roses beginning to wilt beneath the grey sky.

The rain has barely held off, but the clouds hang lower over the city, heavy and colourless, turning the morning into something that feels suspended between night and day.

The King is dead.

The announcement had reached the public seven days ago, released by the Palace shortly after six in the morning.

His Majesty King Whitmore passed peacefully in his sleep following a sudden cardiac event.

There had been no mention of the attack on the palace hours earlier, no mention of the security breach contained before it reached the residential wing, or the men detained and quietly removed before sunrise.

And there had certainly been no mention of the other attack that same night.

What remains is the grieving family asking for privacy and a nation mourning the loss of its King.

Andrew walks behind his father’s coffin with his head bowed.

Matilda walks beside him. Eleanor and Cecily follow several paces behind, both dressed in black, their faces partially obscured by small veiled hats. Cecily has been crying since she left the palace. Eleanor has not cried once in public, although Andrew had heard her through the wall the night she returned.

Matilda has done neither.

There are cameras everywhere. Andrew can hear them beyond the barriers, shutters firing as the procession passes. Somewhere above them the helicopters circle at the edge of restricted air space. Every major broadcaster in the country is carrying the funeral live, foreign networks have been doing the same since dawn. Heads of state, diplomats and representatives of royal houses from across Europe have filled in for the service.

The scale is appropriate. Andrew thinks. His father would have appreciated that.

He lifts his gaze briefly as another bell sounds across the city.

The last seven days have required an extraordinary amount of work.

There had been an Accession Council first, convened while the family mourned the loss of their father. The council has already proclaimed Andrew as King, followed by his first audience with the Prime Minister, statements to parliament, briefing with the Privy Council, and security meeting.

The Crown does not mourn, people do.

When one sovereign dies, another takes his place. A distinction Andrew has always understood.

He glances sideways, Matilda’s eyes remain fixed ahead. She has spoken to him only when protocol demanded it.

Perhaps he’ll deal with her later, more so because there is one contingency Andrew didn’t expect to deal with tonight.

Caleb should have been walking behind Cecily.

The absence has become impossible to ignore. Newspapers began asking questions by the second day, social media by the first hour. Why had Prince Caleb not appeared at the proclamation? Why had the King’s youngest son not been photographed returning to the palace?

Why the most publicly recognizable of the Whitmore siblings absent from his own father’s funeral.

The Palace had answered yesterday.

Following the sudden loss of his father, His Royal Highness Prince Caleb has withdrawn temporarily from public duties on medical advice. He is receiving private care and asks that his privacy be respected during this period of profound grief.

Almost an entirely useless headline, because Andrew still has no idea where his brother is.

He knows who has him. That much Matilda has failed to conceal.

Arian Conti.

The name sits unpleasantly at the back of Andrew’s mind.

The attack should have solved that problem. The intention had never been to harm Caleb. Andrew had been painfully explicit about that. His orders were clear: Remove Conti, recover his brother.

Instead five of Conti’s men were dead, the secondary extraction had collapsed exactly as planned, and somehow Caleb had still disappeared before Andrew’s people could reach him.

Matilda.

Andrew looks at his sister again. She must have arranged the airfield before the attack was even over. Impressive, how Matilda has always been sharp and steps ahead of him, but now her interference has become greatly inconvenient.

Andrew lowers his head as the cameras turn towards him again and the procession continues.

Caleb cannot remain hidden forever he thinks. And Arian Conti cannot protect him forever either.

By evening, the crowds outside the palace have begun to thin.

The silence has settled differently over the palace since the funeral, absorbing even the familiar sounds of  footsteps and closing doors until the building feels far larger than Andrew remembers it being.

The family gathers in one smaller drawing room rather than the Great Hall, for the hearing of their father’s final testament.

The curtains have been drawn against the fading light, lamps burn low beneath portraits of dead Whitemores, throwing warm pools of gold across polished wood and dark upholstery.

Cecily is curled into the corner of the sofa still dressed in black, her fingers wound tightly through Eleanor’s. Her eyes are swollen from crying, as Eleanor sits close to her, holding her throughout her pain.

Matilda stands at the window, quietly.

Some of the king's brothers and sisters have gathered into the room as well.

Andrew pauses at the doorway, his eyes land on Matilda first. She has changed nothing from the funeral except removing her hat. Her black dress falls to the floor, hands loosely clasped before her. She still looks as controlled, and calm as ever.

“Your Majesty.”

The solicitor rises and everyone else automatically follows. Andrew inclines his head and steps inside the room.

“Please” Andrew motions for everyone to sit.

Matilda finally turns from the window. Their eyes meet as the solicitor open the leather portfolio resting on his lap.

Their father’s personal testament had been revised several times, during the last decade, the most recent version executed less than a year before his death. Private holdings are separated from Crown property, such as trust funds, reserved only for his children.

Eleanor sits straighter as her name is read.

Their mother had owned a Bösendorfer Grand Piano built more than a century ago, passed through her family before eventually coming into the Whitmore collection. Eelanor had learned her first scales on it when her feet could barely reach the pedals.

Their mother left it to their father, now he leaves it to her.

The piano is accompanied by two private estates, stewardship of the Whitmore Arts Foundation and several collections connected to its work. The Late King has recommended that Eleanor be granted a permanent position within the Royal Cultural Council, ensuring her work extends beyond ceremonial patronage.

Eleanor wipes her eyes, and whispers something that sounds like, “Ofcourse he did.”

Cecily comes next.

She receives the coastal estates in Cornwall, a considerable personal trust and controlling stewardship of the Whitmore Maritime Foundation, whose private holdings include docks, shipping interests and international development projects across several countries.

Cecily gives a watery, disbelieving laugh.

“I told him once, years ago that I want to see every ocean in the world before I die.” she wipes her face with the heel of her hand. Eleanor squeezes her hand.

Matilda’s inheritance changes the atmosphere of the room.

She receives property, considerable wealth, their father’s private collection of correspondence and historical papers. All incidental, however the important part comes afterwards.

The King formally names his eldest daughter Principal Counsellor to the Sovereign, granting her a permanent place at the head of the Crown Council and requesting that every sovereign who follows retain her counsel for as long as she is willing to give it.

“His Majesty further wrote: To Matilda, who has spent her life beside the Crown without even asking to wear it, I entrust the duty of reminding those who do that, it was never meant to sit lightly.”

No one speaks.

Andrew closes his hand into a fist, as he watches something crack through Matilda’s composed expression.

Even in death, his father has found a way to place Matilda beside the throne. The thought is as troubling as it is infuriating, especially with the rift between them widening by the day. Whatever his father intended, Andrew knows one thing with certainty: Matilda’s presence at his side will only create complications for him in the future.

“And to His Royal Highness Prince Caleb Whitmore.”

The room seems to change.

Cecily lifts her head from Eleanor’s shoulder, as everyone waits.

Caleb receives the Richmond estate he had always preferred as a child, along with another property in Scotland and their father’s private residence outside Paris. The entirety of the King’s personal art collection passes to him, save the works already promised elsewhere. His existing trust is expanded considerably, several charitable endowments are placed under his patronage, including the children foundation Caleb has represented for years.

This is more than generous, anyone hearing it would conclude that their father favoured the youngest son more than anyone else.

The solicitor reaches the bottom of the page but then takes a pause.

Matilda notices. “Is something wrong?” she asks.

“His late Majesty’s testament refers to a supplementary sealed document concerning Prince Caleb.”

“What document, everything father wrote has been in that folder untouched.” she says.

“My apologies Princess Matilda, but that is unclear.”

The solicitor turns another page, although Andrew knows perfectly well there is nothing there.

“The corresponding document has not been located among His Majesty’s deposited papers.”

Andrew’s jaw tightens, he can feel Matilda looking at him, eyes sharp. Andrew looks back but gives her nothing. His sister has always possessed the irritating habit of recognizing when a puzzle is missing a piece.

“Searches are continuing through the archives, at present, however, we have no further document to execute.”

The search will remain futile, the documents don’t exist anymore, Andrew has made sure of that.

The remainder passes without incident, Andrew’s mind has already gone elsewhere. To the papers reduced to ash, the signature that no longer exists, and finally to Caleb.

As the night settles completely by the time the room empties, Cecily leaves first with Eleanor. The lawyers gather the documents into locked cases, as people disappear one by one, until two people remain inside the room.

Andrew by the fireplace and Matilda by the window.

“Congratulations brother.” her voice breaks through the silence as Andrew looks at her.

“For what?”

“You finally got everything you wanted.” she says with a faint smile that contains no warmth.

“We buried our father today, you’re tired.”

This produces an incredulous laugh out of her. “Don’t pretend like you ever cared about this family.”

Andrew’s expression hardens. “Everything I have ever done has been for this family. It is not my fault you never saw my efforts”

“Of course.”

“Someone has to think beyond their own feelings, Matilda. Father understood that, you used to. Unlike our dear brother Caleb who chose to disappear just because things got hard.”

Her entire face changes. “Do not speak his name.”

Andrew smirks, finally abandoning the pretense of the grieving son as he turns to face her fully.

“Still protecting him.” Andrew pushes away from the fireplace and takes a step closer to her. “You think I don’t know what you did?”

She says nothing and stays in her place even when Andrew gets closer.

“You put my brother in the hands of a criminal and smuggled him out of the country.”

“I put him somewhere you couldn’t reach him.”

Andrew tilts his head, Matilda may be a thorn on his side but he will have to eventually break her.

“You sound remarkably confident.”

Andrew takes another step, this time Matilda takes a small step back, her back meets the window but she holds his gaze firmly.

“Arian Conti, you really believe he can hide a British Prince forever?”

“As long as I am here, you will stay away from Caleb.”

“He is a member of this family, he has obligations.”

“Not to you.”

Something flares in Matilda’s eyes, sharp and defiant, a fire Andrew has always admired in her. Now he wants nothing more than to see that composure crack.

She has taken something from him, something that has always been his.

“You have always indulged him. Both you and father. Every scandal, every reckless decision, someone eventually has to remind Caleb who he is.”

Matilda stares at him and then smirks. Andrew sees something close to disgust.

Then she steps forward, no longer pulling away from him, but coming face to face.

“That’s the thing you never understood about him. He was never yours to bring home.”

The words strike somewhere deep but Andrew’s expression doesn’t change.

Matilda steps around him, and starts walking towards the door.

Then she stops and turns.

Andrew watches as she executes the precise, immaculate bow owed by a princess of the realm to her sovereign. When she rises, there is unmistakable mockery in her eyes.

“Your Majesty.”

The door closes behind her as Andrew stands alone.

Then his hand slips inside his jacket, the photograph has softened at the edges with age. He’s carried it for longer than he remembers.

Two boys stand beneath the trees in the palace garden, shoulder to shoulder. Caleb is perhaps nine, while Andrew cannot have been older than fifteen as they both grin at the camera. Arms around each other, laughing at something just before the photograph was taken.

Andrew runs his thumb once over his brother’s face.

Back then Caleb had been easier to understand, but somewhere along the way, his little brother had mistaken freedom for the absence of anyone telling him no.

And now Arian Conti had apparently convinced him that running was the same thing as being free.

His anger settles, because he is certain that Matilda is wrong about one thing.

Caleb will come home eventually.

Andrew slides the photograph back into his jacket and begins to walk towards the door.

When Caleb does, Andrew will make certain Arian Conti is no longer there to take him away again.

The streets at Palermo are narrower, emptied of the evening traffic. Somewhere beyond the old rising buildings, music spills out from restaurants, scooters cut through intersections, people drinking beneath striped awnings.

The car comes to a smooth stop outside the building with cream stone walls and discreet brass lettering above the entrance.

CENTRO ESTETICO BELLANDI.

A respectable name for a thoroughly disreputable place.

Arian steps out of the Bentley as two of his men appear behind him.

Dressed in black, everything cuts precisely enough that nothing shifts when he buttons up his jacket, including the weight of the pistol secured beneath it. A second weapon sits lower against his back.

The young man behind the reception desk looks up when the three of them enter and goes visibly still.

The front of Bellandi’s establishment is designed for discretion rather than luxury. There’s another woman in a white uniform sitting behind another desk, pretending not to recognize the man who has just walked in through the door.

The young receptionist speaks.
“Don Conti.” Arian gives him a single glance.

“Il signor Bellandi l'aspetta?”
“No.”

The young man’s expression falters a bit. “Posso chiamarlo…"

“Non serve.” Arian responds, and continues walking.

The man hurries from behind the desk. “Da questa parte, Don Conti.”

At least the boy is sensible. Arian follows him through a narrow corridor and past a door requiring a key that the receptionist produced quickly. Behind it, the performance of a respectable business ends.

A staircase descends beneath the building, and the air changes very quickly.

The clean citrus scent upstairs gives way to perfume, alcohol and smoke, as soft, mellow music plays somewhere below, low enough not to carry into the street. Red light bleeds beneath closed doors. Curtains divide alcoves along one side of the corridor, offering little privacy.

Arian hears enough as they pass. Laughter, a man’s voice, a woman crying in pleasure. The unmistakable sounds of people indulging themselves behind white sheer curtains and locked doors serving as exclusive private rooms.

He doesn’t look twice and heads straight towards the door situated at the end of the hall.

Marco Bellandi had always liked dressing vulgarity in expensive fabric.

This place had begun years ago as a legitimate massage parlour before Bellandi discovered that pleasure wrapped in silk sheets produced considerably better margins than wellness. Private rooms followed, then gambling. Slowly, women and men were brought in for wealthy clients, drugs for those who wanted them.

The place became an underground brothel disguised as a massage parlour where information traded over drinks and rich men came to seek pleasure.

Arian had tolerated it because Marco had once possessed something far more valuable than the building itself.

Four years ago, Marco had access to a small private freight terminal west of Palermo, owned by the Bellandi family for decades. Though it was nothing impressive beside the larger commercial ports, but strategically positioned, and privately controlled, it was useful enough for Arian. He wanted access to it four years ago, and Marco Bellandi had become the man worth knowing for him.

Arian had struck at the right moment, because Marco needed money and Arian had provided it. Though it soon created problems when Marco threw himself in gambling, placing him in debt to men considerably less patient than Arian. And because Marco had promised him unrestricted access to the terminal, Arian made sure the men who wanted him dead never got close enough to try.

It should have been simple. But Marco spent the next three years making things complicated.

He borrowed against businesses already drowning in debt, gambled money intended for repayments, used the Conti name to frighten clients and creditors without Arian’s knowledge. Then eight months ago, a dispute with one of the creditors ended with a dead body in a car outside Arian’s villa. There was enough evidence pointing towards Marco, and Arian had been forced to intervene before investigation reached businesses concerning his own.

Arian had cleaned that mess because it benefited him, and because he didn't want Marco dead, yet.

Marco, on the other hand, had mistaken it for affection.

They reach the end of the hall, a woman passes them wearing little more than a silk robe, her expression carefully blank. She recognizes Arian immediately and lowers her gaze.

Arian’s jaw tightens. He hates places like this.

Arian’s world isn’t clean, neither are his hands, but Marco has a particular talent for allowing everything he touches to rot and then expect someone else to remove the smell.

The receptionist stops outside a dark wooden door.

“È dentro.”

Arian glances at his men, and signals them to stay outside before going in.

Marco Bellandi sits there on a velvet sofa, sprawled beside a low table, crowded with bottles and glasses. His jacket has disappeared somewhere, his white shirt is wrinkled and open at the throat. A woman sits across his lap, barely conscious enough to hold herself upright. Her eyes are unfocused, her head lolling against his shoulder.

Another  young man lay lengthwise across the sofa, staring at the ceiling through a haze of smoke.

Arian stops just inside the doorway.

“Fuori. Adesso.”

The young man on the sofa sits up immediately, trying to cover his body with the sheets that were lying on the ground a second ago but the woman is too far gone to notice who has entered the room.

Marco laughs, and helps the woman to her feet. She stumbles almost immediately. The young man catches her and the two of them disappear through the door without another word.

“Arian, always so serious.” Marco exclaims.

Arian looks at him and waits until the door closes before he crosses the room and takes the chair opposite Marco.

Marco stares at him for a moment before recovering enough of his usual theatrics to spread his arms.

“Arian Conti.” He smiles broadly. “Christ, you could have called, I would’ve prepared a better company for you.”

“I am not here for the company Marco.”

Marco laughs again, weaker this time. “Still no fun.”

“We had an arrangement.”

Marco’s smile slips completely as Marco realizes what Arian is referring to. “Arian, come on, we talked about this…”

“I’m here for what I’m owed.”

“And you’ll have it, I gave you my word. I told you it’s been a difficult quarter” Marco is starting to visibly sweat, as he sputters out his responses.

“You had a difficult four years.”

“Arian…”

“You borrowed another two million.”

Marco stops. Arian watches the color shift in his face.

“You put two properties against it, and neither of those belonged to you entirely.” Arian’s voice remains conversational. “You used my name to secure an extension from Rizzo and said that Conti would guarantee the debt. Then you lied to Rizzo that I had approved it.”

Marco swallows. “I said you knew we were doing business.”

Arian gives no reaction to his excuses and continues. “You lost another seven hundred thousand in gambling, and when Stefano Greco and his partner came looking for payment, you threatened him with my name”

“No, no, no wait…. It was him, Arian, he was threatening me.” Marco says, almost pleadingly.

Arian tilts his head. “That sounds like your problem”

“We are in alliance, I had to bring up your name to keep myself alive, come on, you always have my back?”

Arian smirks and leans back into his chair. “I kept this building open when the bank wanted it. I stopped Rizzo from coming after you, and I dealt with Steffano’s partner when your stupidity left a dead man in a car and half the police force asking questions you couldn’t afford to answer. Every time you fucked up, you came to me.”

Then Arian leans forward, eyes dark and cold.

“Did you know the man you killed was Stefano’s lover?” Arian asks, the mockery in his voice almost pleasant. Marco goes still. “No?” His mouth curves. “Then I suppose it'll be quite the surprise when Stefano puts a bullet through your head himself before anyone else reaches you.”

Marco’s jaw tightens.

“Every single time I made a calculation that you were useful enough to be kept alive, and cleaned up your messes. I don’t think I can do that anymore Marco”

Marco looks insulted, as he stares at Arian in disbelief. Then he reaches out and grabs his unfinished glass of whiskey, draining it in one swallow.

“I thought we were friends.”

Arian says nothing, and Marco scoffs.

“Fine, business partners.” Marco shifts forward. “Listen, I’ve had investors pull out, the club hasn’t been making what it used to and the restaurants have been bleeding money. And then five months ago you disappeared off to London, nobody could get a hold of you.”

Arian’s expression doesn’t change.

“I had everything ready.” Marco continued quickly.

“You were supposed to honor the agreement you signed but you gambled it all away in my absence.”

“I moved it. I can fix this, Arian please, just give me five days. I’ll call in what people owe me, settle with you properly, every cent.”

Marco mistakes Arian’s silence for consideration.

“Come on.” he reaches for the bottle between them and pulls another glass closer. “Drink with me, you came all the way here, didn’t you? We talk and we always work it out.”

He pours as Arian looks down at the amber liquid.

“That was before you tried to sell the terminal you promised me.”

Marco freezes. All the performance disappears from his face.

“I…I didn’t, its not what you think.” Marco blinks rapidly, stuttering now.

“You approached a client for negotiations, for a better price.”

“They approached me.”

“You sent them the valuation.”

“I was just exploring options.”

“You sent them documents concerning your remaining interest and neglected to mention that Conti has first right to it.”

Marco’s mouth opens and no words come out. Arian almost smiles.

This is the part men like Marco never understand about him. Arian had known about the client Marco had approached before leaving for London.

He knew about the gambling two days before that. He also knew which creditors Marco owed.

Marco spent the last six months believing he was negotiating a better deal from a position he no longer possesses.

“You went behind my back.” Arian says.

“I was desperate, I wasn’t going to sell, it was just a backup”

Arian stays quiet, as Marco waits, his body shaking now.

But then Arian leans forward and reaches for the glass of whiskey Marco had  just poured for him.

“Five days.”

“Huh?”

“I’m giving you five days, and then I’m coming to take everything.”

Relief appears so quickly on Marco it is almost embarrassing.

“You bastard,” Marco breathes out a laugh. “For a second there, I thought you were actually going to kill me.”

Arian takes a measured sip as Marco rubs both hands over his face and then he begins to laugh loudly as he pours himself another drink, shaking his head.

Arian knows exactly what Marco would do tomorrow morning. He’ll call his new clients, and try to move whatever he believes he still controls. Perhaps even leave Sicily and sell the terminal anyway.

But none of it matters, because Arian has stopped waiting several days ago. He didn’t come tonight to negotiate, he came because after six years, Marco Bellandi will soon realize that his usefulness to Arian has expired.

“So.” Marco speaks, confidence returning now that he believes himself safe.  “London is where you’ve been for months.”

Arian lifts his gaze, but gives no response.
Marco grins. “Heard the whole city went to hell while you were there, the King dies, the Palace locks itself down, and apparently, you’ve been babysitting that pretty prince.”

Marco doesn’t notice the change in expression from calm to cold and continues talking.

“Whitmore, isn’t it? Black hair, face like something painted on a church ceiling.” He leans back. “You should’ve brought him here, half my clients would pay just to…”

Arian sets his glass down, a little too loudly, causing Marco to stop mid-sentence.

“Finish that sentence.”

The amusement disappears from Marco’s face.

“Christ, Arian.” he forces a laugh. “It was a joke, I didn’t mean anything.”

“You rarely do.” Arain’s gaze remains on him.

Marco swallows as Arian finishes his drink and stands up. He  adjusts one cuff before reaching beneath his jacket and draws out the pistol from its holster.

All the blood drains from Marco’s face as his eyes immediately follow the weapon.

Arian checks it with the precision with which another man might check his watch, he wipes a faint mark from the metal with his handkerchief, then returns the cloth to his pocket.

Arian has no intention of shooting Marco in this room tonight, but Marco doesn’t need to know that. He has already planned something far worse to dismantle Marco’s life and leave him wishing Arian had simply pulled the trigger.

“Five days,” Marco says.

Arian looks down at him and smirks. “Yes.”

Marco’s relieved smile returns when Arian slips the gun back behind his jacket.

“A presto, Marco.”

His men fall into steps behind him once he walks out the room.

Arian steps out into the cool Palermo night, the door closing quietly behind him. He has barely made it down the few steps when he sees the man waiting underneath the streetlamp.

Steffano.

Tall, young, unruly honey brown hair catching the faint light, dark green eyes fixed on the entrance behind Arian. A gun hangs loosely in his hand, his expression cold enough to make it obvious why he’s here.

Arian stops beside him.

“He’s yours.”

Steffano looks at him for a long moment. “Do not think handing Marco over to me makes me forget that you hid Julian’s body.” he says, his Italian accent thick and unmistakable.

Arian doesn’t react.

“I know,” he says.

“You knew I would come.”

“Eventually.” Arian’s gaze remains steady. “But Marco was never mine to kill.”

Steffano’s expression hardens.

“Consider this a peace offering, if you want, or don’t.” Arian continues. “If you believe I owe you a debt as well, you know where to find me.”

It is an invitation, if Steffano’s thirst for revenge does not end with Marco, he can come for Arian. Arian has no fear of whatever Steffano chooses to do with that invitation.

Steffano’s jaw tightens. For eight months, he’s been hunting Marco down, and for eight months Arian had kept the truth buried.

Now Marco has betrayed him too and Arian no longer finds his pathetic existence useful.

Steffano steps away from the pole, coming close enough to meet Arian’s eyes. “Then perhaps I will come for you one day too.” They stare at each other for a few more seconds, before Steffano walks past him, their shoulders brushing lightly as he disappears inside the parlour, the gun concealed beneath his coat.

Arian doesn’t take the threat lightly.

Steffano is not just a dangerous man, he is a grieving lover, and there is no doubt in his mind that grief would drive him to any length for revenge.

Arian gives him one final look before walking towards his car.

Perhaps a minute or two has passed when two gunshots tear through the quiet street, Arian does not look back.

He has asked two of his men to stay back and deal with the clean up. By morning Bellandi’s club will belong to someone else, and Marco’s remaining assets will already be moving into Conti hands.

Arian has lost nothing.

Marco had asked for five days, but he failed to understand that Arian had never promised to give them to him.

As the Bentley pulls away from the curb, Arian finally lets his head fall back against the seat. He’s been in Sicily for six days and has barely spent a few hours in his own home. There were men to replace, routes to secure, alliances to be reviewed, and questions surrounding Adrien’s disappearance need to be investigated.

And then there is the very clear matter of a very angry prince currently taking up residence in his home.

Caleb.

He had left Caleb at the villa scarcely an hour after they arrived. Caleb had not spoken to him during the flight, or at all for that matter.

Arian couldn’t blame him.

He had considered going to Caleb’s room before leaving that first night, considered explaining, but there wasn’t an explanation capable of making any of that better.

So Arian had done the only useful thing available to him.

He left.

Gave him space.

His men have been sending updates from the villa for several days. Caleb is eating, although apparently only when he remembers, sleeping badly, because oftentimes he was seen strolling in the garden at night, in general spending long periods outside. He has explored enough of the estate that yesterday one of his men had found him in a section of the western garden.

Apparently Caleb had also discovered the stables on his fourth morning.

Of all the horses he could have chosen, he had immediately become attached to Nerone. One of Arian’s oldest bay with a temper bad enough that his stable hands routinely refuse to ride him.

But of course, only Caleb would walk into a stable containing six perfectly agreeable horses and select the one most likely to throw him into a wall.

Arian stares out the window, buildings passing by as a smile appears on his face.

He’ll be returning home tomorrow, and he doesn't know what he could say to him at this point. He wants to know whether the anger has cooled enough that Caleb would actually look at him when he speaks.

Arian wants the opportunity to explain, but explain what?

Why Arian had known, the King had died and still used that moment to kiss him without any warnings, and continued to lie to him? There are no versions of those that make him look particularly good.

Caleb probably hates him, Arian thinks, as he watches the city disappear behind him.

And at the moment he has every right to.

It's been a week since Caleb arrived in Sicily. And by the sixth morning he has memorized the patterns adorning the carved ceiling.

Dark carved wood, patterns intricately designed into every panel, the details are so precise that he has spent enough mornings staring at them now that he can reproduce the whole thing from memory.

He has stopped looking at the time in the mornings. For the past week, he has spent hours lying in bed, staring at the ceiling and trying to make sense of his situation.

Sunlight has already found its way through the curtains. Unlike London, Sicily appears to have absolutely no respect for grief. Every morning arrives offensively beautiful and sunny.

The Conti estate sits high above the coast, built into the side of a mountain where the land drops towards an impossible stretch of blue sea. Mountains rise behind it, dark and uneven against the horizon, while lemon trees crowd the lower terraces and gardens surrounding the house. From Caleb’s balcony, he can see the coastline curling away in both directions, small towns scattered along distant slopes while the sea catches sunlight far below.

The villa itself is enormous.

Old stone walls warmed gold by the sun, arched windows, shaded courtyards, long balconies and more rooms than any single man could reasonably require. Caleb has stopped counting somewhere around thirty.

His diplomatic assignment has become somewhat redundant when his father died, his security team is dead or scattered, and his passport is apparently now under the jurisdiction of a Sicilian crime lord, and Caleb himself has been removed from Britain in the middle of the night without being consulted on any part of it.

So yes, he spent the entire week, walking around hallways, counting bedrooms. By the third day he discovered the library and the pool, and by the fourth, he discovered the stables.

Gorgeous, beautiful six horses are kept there, but Caleb’s eyes had immediately gone to a beautiful dark bay with a white mark between its eyes and precisely enough attitude to make Caleb fall immediately in love with it. Nobody had explicitly told him he could ride the horse, but nobody stopped him either, which Caleb had chosen to interpret as permission.

The animal has subsequently become the closest thing Caleb currently has to a friend in Sicily. This is partly because Arian’s security team appears to have taken a vow to never display human emotions.

And Caleb has tried. He has learned several names, and been asking questions, making jokes, attempting conversations over breakfast. He even flirted shamelessly with one particular handsome guard yesterday in the hopes of obtaining Arian’s phone number.

But nothing.

The man didn’t even blink. Caleb realized he’s never experienced such comprehensive failure in his life.

Apparently Arian hadn’t been lying. Italian men really are immune to his charms.

The thought of Airan immediately wipes the amusement from his face, and his mood becomes sour all over again.

Caleb throws the pillow aside and sits up.

Seven days, and the bastard has disappeared off the face of the earth.

They had barely spoken to each other after they landed. But in all fairness, Caleb hadn’t exactly encouraged conversation. He had spent the entire flight from London staring out the window and refusing to acknowledge the man sitting several feet away.

And Caleb intends to keep it that way for as long as necessary.

When they arrived, the car took them through enormous iron gates and finally into the courtyard of a house he had never seen before. Men had appeared from seemingly everywhere the moment Arian stepped out.

Caleb had been exhausted, angry and still wearing clothes from the night before. His father’s death existing somewhere inside him as an enormous fact his mind refused to acknowledge.

Arian had looked as though he hadn’t slept either.

He had told a member of the staff to show Caleb to his room, informed him that the house and grounds were at his disposal, and then, almost as an afterthought, mentioned that he had business requiring his immediate attention.

Caleb had assumed that meant a few hours.

But then, three days pass, then five and now a whole entire week.

Arian didn’t leave any message, didn’t call, he just disappeared.

Every morning Caleb had made the humiliating mistake of asking.
“Has Arian returned yet?”

And every morning some immaculately dressed man had given him some variation of the same answer.

Don Conti will return when his business is concluded, Your Highness.

He accepted it the first time, the second time he got annoyed, and by third, he had begun considering whether throwing Arian into his own swimming pool upon his eventual return would constitute an international incident.

Caleb sighs as he climbs out of bed.

His room is absurdly beautiful. It occupies a corner of the upper floor with a balcony facing the sea, its doors usually left open enough for warm air to move through the curtains. The furniture is old without feeling fragile, dark wood against pale walls, everything expensive in the understated way that suggests nobody living here has ever needed to tell another person what something cost.

The entire house is like that. It has probably been passed down through generations, yet everything is so impeccably maintained that there is no sense of age or neglect. There are no overt displays of wealth, either. The Contis have apparently been wealthy for long enough to have nothing left to prove.

Caleb understands that particular language very well.

By the time he takes a shower, dresses up and goes down, the villa is already awake.

Someone greets him in Italian as he passes through the corridor. Another member of staff wishes him good morning in English. Somewhere beyond the open doors leading towards the courtyard, Caleb hears voices and the distant sound of a car.

For half a second, his heart jumps. Then two of Arian’s men step out of the car, and no one else follows.

For a moment, Caleb wishes Arian would just come back, if only so he can stop standing there looking like a fool, as though he is waiting for him.

He is not.

Because Caleb is angry, furious actually. Arian probably knows it, which makes Caleb want to strangle him more for disappearing on him like this.

Arian had known his father was dead.

And still, he had stood there in that warehouse, touched Caleb’s face, kissed him, let Caleb pull him closer and kiss him again as though months of wanting him had finally broken something open between them.

Caleb pours the coffee, stronger the better. Anything to drown out the fucking heartbreak that had been tearing through him for an entire week.

He has spent five days telling himself that Arian’s absence is convenient. It gives him time to think and decide exactly how angry he intends to be when he eventually returns.

His eyes drift towards the newspaper folded beside his breakfast. There has been some version of his father’s face on the front page every day since Caleb arrived.

The funeral yesterday had been broadcasted around the world and Caleb had watched it alone.

He sat in Arian’s library with the television muted for most of it and watched his father’s coffin move through London while thousands of strangers stood in the streets to mourn a man Caleb had not been allowed to bury.

Matilda had looked composed as always, Eleanor and Cecily held each other's hands and Andrew had walked at the front.

Caleb had watched his brother become King from another country.

The headline beneath the photograph had mentioned Caleb too.

The Palace had announced that he had withdrawn from public duties on medical advice following his father's death and was receiving private care.

Caleb didn’t cry.

The last of his tears had fallen somewhere over the ocean, his face turned towards the airplane window, away from Arian, where he had let a few silent tears slip free out of sheer frustration.

But the moment they arrived in Sicily, something in Caleb seemed to have shut down. Because beneath all of the emotions remains one thing nobody has explained.

His father did not die peacefully in his sleep as the headline had suggested.

Arian had told him that much in a moment of anger, when Caleb had been relentless about not leaving.

The King was murdered tonight.

Caleb turns the coffee mug in his hand. He has tried not to think about who could have done it.

Unfortunately, his mind keeps offering him the same face.

Blond hair, green eyes.

Caleb quickly pushes the thought away. Andrew is many things, cruel when he wants to be, controlling, vindictive, obsessed with the crown to an extent Caleb has never understood.

But murdering their father?

No.

There has to be something else.

And perhaps Caleb would know what that something was if the one man who apparently possessed every fucking answer hadn’t vanished into Sicily without leaving so much as a means to contact him.

Caleb thinks sitting here and thinking about everything that is making him angry is futile. So, he finishes his coffee and heads towards the stables.

If Arian intends to remain missing indefinitely, Caleb will simply have to steal his horse.

++++

Caleb hears Arian before he sees him.

It’s late in the afternoon, Caleb is just returning from the stables, when Arian’s voice carries from somewhere beyond the entrance hall, low and unmistakable, speaking rapid Italian to one of the men beside him.

Caleb hands the reins to the stablemaster.

"When did he get back?"

"Ten minutes ago, Your Highness."

Caleb looks towards the house.

Excellent, He's going to kill him.

Then he steps through the archway, and there he is.

Arian stands near the foot of the staircase dressed entirely in black, his jacket open over a dark shirt, one hand tucked casually into his trouser pocket while two men speak to him. There is something different about him here. In London, Arian has always seemed contained somehow, as though he were deliberately making himself smaller within someone else’s world.

Here, he doesn’t have to. Here he looks completely at home.

And unfairly handsome.

Arian looks up and whatever he was saying, stops.

For a moment, neither of them move. Then Caleb sees something shift in Arian’s expression, just a softening around his eyes that Caleb probably would have missed some months ago.

His own stupid heart skips a beat against his wishes.

Arian dismisses the men around him with a few quiet words and crosses the hall.

“Caleb.”

Caleb tightens his grip around his riding gloves.

“Arian.”

Arian’s gaze travels over him, taking in his dishevelled hair, open collar, dirt smeared across his white trousers and, apparently, his cheek too cause he definitely spent an entire morning being thrown around the Sicilian countryside by an animal with questionable respect for authority.

The corner of Arian’s mouth lifts.

“I see you’ve acquainted yourself well with Nerone.”

Caleb stares at him and then scoffs.

“You disappear for a week and that’s what you have to say to me?”

Arian’s smile grows considerably more obvious.

Oh, fuck him.

Caleb steps forward. “So this was your grand plan, then? Drag me halfway across Europe against my will, and then promptly disappear with no way of contacting you.”

For some reason, Arian’s smile doesn’t falter.

“There were matters that needed my attention,” Arian says calmly. “After London…I needed to know our routes were secure, it couldn’t wait.”

Some of Caleb's anger falters despite his best efforts. It doesn’t help that Arian keeps looking at him like that, and it does irritating things to his stomach.

“Oh.”

“I should have told you how long I might be gone.”

Caleb lifts his chin up. “Well…yes, you should have.”

“And give you a way to contact me” Arian continues.

“Yes?…that too.”

Wait, Caleb is confused now.

Arian inclines his head. “Then I apologize.”

Well, that is extremely inconvenient. Caleb had prepared a proper argument in his head.

His heart is beating a little too loud in his chest because Arian is still looking at him, and he just apologized.

Is there something in the Sicily air?

He looks down and begins straightening his collar, and self consciously rubs his cheek, removing some of the dirt smeared on his face.

“Whatever, I don't particularly care what you do.”

Arian says nothing.

Caleb sighs and holds onto his riding gloves tighter. “I have somewhere to be.”

Arian glances towards the doors leading back outside. “With my horse?”

“He’s better company than most people in this house.”

“I’ve been told you’ve become quite attached.”

Caleb narrows his eyes. “You’ve been back for ten minutes, who’s already reporting on me?”

And that damn smirk is back again. Caleb turns before that smile can do anything else unfortunate to his poor relentless heart. He takes several steps towards the entrance but then stops.

The words come out much quieter than he intends.

“A note would’ve been fine.”

Caleb doesn’t turn, thinks he might not even get a response, so he continues. “A message, anything, just so I knew.”

Behind him Arian is quiet for a moment.

“Next time, I’ll leave one.”

Caleb’s chest tightens, he nods once.

And without another word, he walks away before either of them can make the moment any worse. Arian doesn't call him back, but Caleb can feel his eyes on him all the way down the corridor.

And irritatingly, impossibly, the villa feels a little less unfamiliar now that he's home.
