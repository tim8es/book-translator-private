# Style Guide — You Feel Like Home

## Scope

This guide describes the English source and the fidelity requirements for the Russian translation. It is descriptive and must not be used to invent traits not supported by the source.

## Narration

- Third-person, predominantly present-tense narration with close focalization. Chapters 1–3 stay close to Caleb; Chapter 4 opens in Arian's viewpoint and later returns to Caleb. Preserve viewpoint shifts rather than smoothing them into a neutral narrator.
- Narrative distance is close: perception, attraction, irritation, fear, and bodily reactions frequently blend into the narration without formal thought tags.
- Register is contemporary romance/thriller prose with modern royal-family and mafia elements. It moves between elegant public settings, action danger, family banter, and charged romantic tension.
- The prose often uses short standalone sentences or fragments for emphasis (`No.`, `Fuck.`, `Godfather.`), alongside long flowing descriptive sentences. Preserve this contrast and the source's pacing.
- Repetition is often purposeful, especially names, pronouns, brief fragments, and parallel clauses. Do not automatically replace repeated wording with synonyms.
- The source uses visual scene separators `++++`. Preserve them exactly and as separate blocks.
- Italics and bold are meaningful where present (internal emphasis, remembered words, newspaper headline). Preserve them.
- Some source paragraphs contain explicit internal blank-line breaks through `<br><br>`. Treat these as pacing breaks and preserve them rather than flattening the text.

## Prose tendencies

- Cinematic and sensory: light, clothing, architecture, movement, body language, and eye contact carry much of the atmosphere.
- Emotionally direct but not uniformly melodramatic; Caleb's humor and flippancy frequently cut through danger or royal formality.
- Dialogue-heavy, quick, and character-specific. Banter is important and should remain nimble rather than becoming literary or formal.
- Attraction is conveyed through close physical observation, interruptions in thought, irritation, and involuntary attention. Do not intensify it beyond the source or make implicit attraction explicit sooner than the text does.
- Action passages are clear and spatially concrete. Preserve who moves, who grabs whom, weapon direction, exits, and chronology.
- Royal/public scenes carry restrained formality, while private family scenes are looser and teasing. Maintain that register contrast.
- The source contains occasional grammatical awkwardness or punctuation irregularity. Translate intended meaning into natural Russian without using those irregularities as permission to rewrite or embellish.

## Character voices

### Caleb Whitmore

- Witty, talkative, impulsive, observant, and frequently irreverent toward royal expectations.
- Uses teasing, flirtation, sarcasm, and deliberately provocative remarks even under pressure.
- Vulnerability appears through bodily reactions, memory, and moments when humor drops away; do not make him more fragile or more heroic than the source.
- His speech should sound contemporary and agile in Russian, not aristocratically stiff unless the scene requires public formality.

### Arian Conti

- Controlled, economical, and difficult to read. His stillness and concise answers contrast with Caleb's verbal energy.
- In danger he is decisive and physically direct. In formal meetings he remains calm, neutral, and authoritative.
- Attraction and attention are often conveyed by observation rather than confession. Preserve restraint.
- Do not make his mafia role more theatrical, cruel, or romanticized than the text does.

### Andrew Whitmore / the King / royal family

- Public language is formal and image-conscious. Private family exchanges can be blunt, exasperated, or teasing.
- Preserve institutional vocabulary and hierarchy without turning ordinary family speech into courtly archaism.

### Eleanor, Cecily, Matilda

- Their dialogue with Caleb is familiar, teasing, and fast. Keep distinct lines lively without adding unsupported slang.

## Russian dialogue and punctuation

- Mandatory book-specific convention: format spoken dialogue with an em dash (`—`) instead of quotation marks.
- When narration follows the spoken line, use standard Russian dialogue punctuation and lowercase/uppercase according to grammar.
- Do not put English curly quotation marks around spoken lines. Quotation marks may still be used when the source is quoting a headline, title, written text, or a quotation inside speech where Russian conventions require them.

## Recurring translation principles

- Preserve `++++` separators exactly.
- Keep royal titles/institution terms consistent with `glossary.md`.
- Preserve the difference between `Godfather` as a mafia title and ordinary kinship language.
- Preserve modality and uncertainty (`seems`, `apparently`, `probably`, `maybe`) rather than turning impressions into facts.
- Maintain source paragraph movement and intentional fragments; do not systematically split or merge for readability.
- Natural Russian syntax is required, but meaning, viewpoint, tone, character voice, rhythm, and ambiguity have priority over isolated elegance.
