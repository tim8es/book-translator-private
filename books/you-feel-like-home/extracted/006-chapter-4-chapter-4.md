## Chapter 4: Chapter 4

Chapter Summary

The chapter opens in Arian’s POV, following the events of the polo match and leading up to his eventual meeting with Caleb.

Chapter Notes

See the end of the chapter for notes

The prince has always been beautiful. That is hardly an observation.

The Whitmores have spent generations being photographed, painted and discussed as though physical beauty is another hereditary privilege of the Crown.

Caleb simply happens to attract more attention than most.

Back in Paris, Arian had seen it with his own eyes. The way Prince Caleb carried himself with confidence, how the crowd had surrounded him all evening, young women, men twice his age, and even some elderly patrons who waited for the chance to exchange a few words with him.

That kind of grace is useful for a prince.

What Arian had not seen until this afternoon was Caleb’s horseback riding skills.

He has just returned from the polo grounds less than an hour ago. But apparently, not all of him has left.
Arian watches through his bedroom window, as the last of the evening fades beyond the grounds of his London residence. His jacket is discarded across the back of a chair that he doesn't feel like putting on again, but he needs to be in a meeting with other leaders in less than an hour.

His thoughts keep returning back to the prince.

The Prince had been different on that field.
Different from the night when he had been frightened and Arian felt it in the hand, gripping his arm whenever gunfire sounded too close. But despite the fear he still kept his head, still helped people escape, fought Arian when he believed him to be a threat, and somehow, in the middle of an assassination attempt, still found the audacity to flirt with him.

Today however, he looked entirely at ease, not to mention he rode well.

Riding has always been an unusual skill among British aristocracy, and Caleb has apparently been doing it since childhood.

But there was that streak of recklessness that came with it, one that the Prince is known for.

Caleb rode as though the possibility of falling had never occurred to him. At one point his older sister had stolen the ball directly from him and Caleb had shouted something after her. Judging by the look Matilda Whitmore had thrown over her shoulder, it had not been particularly princely.

Arian had almost smiled.

Caleb is not only reckless, he enjoys the attention as well.

Every time he made a score, he raised his mallet in triumph, laughed at something one of his siblings shouted from the sidelines, his face flushed from exertion, and dark hair thoroughly ruined by the wind.

Arian had watched, he had been there to observe, that was the point.

Caleb’s schedule over the coming months will put him in environments far less controlled than a private royal polo ground. Arian needs to understand how he behaves in crowds, how much attention he pays to his surroundings, whether he follows instructions, whether he is capable of recognizing danger before it reaches him.

But judging by how he was foolishly trying to save others in an open fire meant for him, the answers so far are: poorly and selectively.

Arian finally reaches for his drink, the whisky burns pleasantly on the way down.

Then, there had been that moment. Caleb had looked toward the royal enclosure and Arian had seen the recognition begin before the prince could possibly have been certain.

Even from the distance he could sense the tensed shoulder, smile disappearing, eyes narrowing. His attention had remained fixed on Arian for several seconds too long.

It’s been four months, and he remembers. Arian isn’t sure why that surprises him.

It was hardly the night one forgets, still when the king had turned to speak to him moments later, Arian had welcomed the interruption more than he cared to admit.

He had left shortly after the match.

He will see Caleb again tonight, properly. The thought has followed him home.

A knock sounds at the bedroom door, and one of his men enters.

“Mr. Caruso and Mr Barone are waiting downstairs.”

Arian checks his watch and curses under his breath. They are early.

He isn't particularly thrilled to join them in the boardroom tonight, but the meeting has been called for the sole purpose of discussing the predicament he has managed to rope himself into. Word travels quickly among the mafia families, and transparency has always been one of the few non-negotiable terms of their alliance.
Arian understands their scrutiny. Becoming the youngest Godfather among them had earned him more than his share of doubt over the years. Some still considered him reckless, others dangerously unpredictable. He had long since stopped caring about it.

“I’ll be there in five minutes.”

The man nods and leaves as Arian finishes his whisky.

Whatever thoughts he'd brought home from the polo grounds could wait.

He has business to attend to.

++++

Vincent Caruso is already drinking when Arian enters the room.

Enzo Barone stands near the fireplace, examining one of the paintings. Neither bother with formalities. They all have known one another too long to waste time on that.

“You look thrilled,” Vincent says. Arian takes his seat behind the desk.

“I am always thrilled to see you.” Arian replies in a straight tone.

Enzo chuckles as he joins them on the table, taking a seat beside Vincent. “Careful now, he might think you mean it.”

Arian gives him a look but says nothing. Out of the two, Enzo Barone is perhaps the only one who can afford the privilege of calling out Arian in the middle of a meeting and get away with it. The man is well into his sixties, he has been a fixture in Arian’s life long before Arian ever became Godfather.

When his father and brother died, leaving behind a fractured empire and far too many men eager to test the young Conti who inherited it, Enzo had been one of the few who stayed close, offering counsel when Arian needed it and knowing when to step aside when he didn’t.

“As much as I appreciate you Uncle Enzo, barging into my home the moment you heard I was in London, is already beginning to feel like a spectacular waste of my time.” he says as he grabs the bottle of whiskey and starts to pour himself a drink.

Enzo’s smile widens. “There he is.”

Vincent lifts his glass. “Always a pleasure being acquainted with you, Arian, which reminds me, you should call my daughter soon, Valentina thinks you’re avoiding her on purpose.”

Arian says nothing and continues to drink from his glass.

Enzo speaks. “Well now that we’re here, we can get to business. We heard from Palermo this morning, a few names moving around more than usual, the royal family specifically keeps showing up.” Arian doesn’t give any reaction but sits back into his seat.

“And while we are watching Sicily, apparently, you’re watching the Prince.” Vincent says.

“How are you suddenly letting the Royal Family get involved in our matters, and why did we have to hear it from somewhere else and not directly from you Arian?”

Arian looks at Enzo before reverting his gaze back to his glass. “The Crown has offered me a preposition I cannot refuse.”

“And the preposition being the head of the Conti family personally following one of their sons around Europe?”

“I’m not following him around Europe.”

Enzo’s mouth twitches. “You spent three hours at a polo match today.” Again, Arian gives no reaction but he knows the bastard can read him like an open book sometimes.

Vincent clears his throat, leaning back into his seat. “What I don't understand is why the Crown requested your protection for Caleb rather than Prince Andrew. Surely the future King presents the greater security concern.” and then his mouth twists dismissively. “Instead, they’ve handed you the reckless spare whose greatest contribution to the monarchy seems to be looking pretty for the camera and keeping the tabloids entertained.”

Arian’s fingers tighten around his glass. And then a cruel little smile settles across his mouth as he tilts his head a little.

“And yet, the reckless spare has managed to earn the loyalty of an entire country without a crown on his head.” he leans forward. “I’d be careful about confusing scandal with insignificance, Vincent. It makes you sound stupid.”

Vincent’s amusement fades.

Across the table, Enzo says nothing, though his eyes linger on Arian for a moment longer than necessary.

“So what exactly is the purpose of this?” Enzo asks, breaking the tense atmosphere.

“The prince has two diplomatic assignments in Sicily over the coming months. One of them keeps him there for an extended period. Whoever targeted him in Paris had connections reaching into territory the Crown doesn’t understand.”

“But you do.”

“Yes, the Crown can surround Caleb with a hundred armed men,” Arian continues. “It won’t make a difference if they don’t know who they are looking for.”

Vincent studies him. “And in return?”

“Political cooperation, fewer obstacles to our legitimate interests and access where we need it.”

“Legitmate interests.” Vincent scoffs.

“Would you prefer I put crime in the agreement?”

“At least it would be honest.” Vincent rolls his glass slowly between his fingers. “And the King trusts you with his son?”

“He trusts the arrangement.”

Which seems to make sense to both Enzo and Vincent. Men like them rarely build alliances on trust, it’s the mutual interest that lasts considerably longer.

“Still seems like an extraordinary amount of trouble for a spoiled prince.”

“Caleb is part of this arrangement, I don't intend on exposing him to any of our internal business matters, but should it come to this, you will treat him with respect.”

Enzo finally sighs and leans forward. “Then we have an understanding. Our alliance remains as it has always been, we stay out of each other’s affairs and offer support when it is needed.” His gaze settles firmly on Arian. “But Arian,.... if the royal family interferes with our business, there will be a war they’ll have to answer for.”

Arian holds his gaze and nods once. He understands, because Enzo has never been a man to make threats he doesn’t intend to keep.

The conversation moves on. By the time Vincent and Enzo leaves, night has settled fully over London. Arian remains in the study for several minutes before he goes back to his room and changes into his suit.

It’s almost time to head back to the palace and finally meet the Prince in person.

By the time he's there, he realizes one thing.

Arian has faced far worse. He has stared down men who wanted him dead and killed more than a few corrupt bastards in cold blood without losing sleep over it. Yet, nothing quite prepared him for stepping into the Great Halls of the royal palace and seeing the look on Caleb’s face, as though someone had personally kicked his puppy and stolen his horse for good measure.

And fuck, Arian had wanted to wipe that expression from his face immediately.

Instead, he had stood there in silence and watched the prince’s carefully constructed version of Paris crumble right before his eyes.

Present

Caleb has decided that Arian Conti is considerably easier to tolerate from a distance.

Three days since the fiasco in the main hall and unfortunately, the man seems determined to make everything difficult.

Caleb is sitting beside Eleanor on one of the benches overlooking the training grounds, riding gloves discarded between them after their afternoon session. The horses are already led back towards the stables, leaving the grounds relatively quiet under the warmth of the afternoon sun.

Across the field, a little distance away, stands the newest complication of Caleb’s life.

Arian is surrounded by members of Caleb’s security detail, speaking to them with the calm authority of someone who has apparently walked into the palace for only three days and decided everyone should listen to him.

Which irritatingly, they were. Caleb’s frown deepens. He can’t hear what Arian is saying from here, probably explaining the various ways one might be murdered in Sicily.

Perhaps there are diagrams,  Caleb hopes there are, because if he is going to have his life commandeered by a criminal organization, he would at least appreciate a professionally prepared presentation.

Beside him, Eleanor follows his gaze.

“So,” she says after a moment. “He is your personal bodyguard now?”

“No.” he replies, frown deepening, eyes never straying from Arian.

“The sort who waits outside the bathroom while you bathe.”

Caleb gives her a deadpan expression, Eleanor is watching him with an expression of such innocent curiosity that he immediately knows she is enjoying this immensely.

“He is not my bodyguard.”

“Hmm, father has brought in a remarkably handsome Italian mafia boss whose sole responsibility appears to be keeping you alive. I can see why you’re devastated.”

Caleb closes his eyes. “Eleanor, I would appreciate it enormously if you stopped speaking.”

“I imagine you would.” the woman has the audacity to cackle at his misery.

When Caleb opens his eyes again, Eleanor is still looking at Arian.

A very small part of him dislikes that for reasons he is choosing not to investigate.

“Where does Father even find these people?” Eleanor continues. “He spends most evenings complaining about parliamentary dinners and then suddenly produces a Sicilian Godfather from somewhere.”

“I’ve learned not to ask.”

“He’s always full of surprises.”

A brief silence passes before Eleanor leans back on the palms of her hands and speaks again.

“This is him, isn’t it?”

Caleb knows immediately what she means, but he keeps his attention firmly on the field.

“I don't know what you’re talking about.”

“The man who got you out during the attack.”

“Eleanor…”

“Come on, you think I am going to let this go? A mysterious stranger who somehow got you out of the museum full of armed men and whom you conveniently neglected to mention afterwards.”

Caleb scoffs and looks away. “I did mention him.”

“You said someone helped you, you failed to mention that someone looked like that.”

Caleb finally looks at her. “I wasn’t aware his appearance was relevant to the investigation.”

“Well I am looking at him right now, clearly it’s relevant enough.” Then she narrows her eyes with sudden interest. “Wait a minute….”

Caleb doesn't like that look, it’s the look she gives when she’s about to say the most unfiltered nonsense that he doesn’t have the patience for at the moment.

“You knew him.”

“What?”

“Like you genuinely knew him, like you had a moment with him, or talked to him.”

“I met him for approximately fifteen minutes.”

“And then said nothing about him for four months.”

“There was nothing to say.” Caleb defends.

Eleanor stares at him like she knows he’s bullshitting. Caleb shrugs.

“I barely remembered him.”

His own mind calls him a liar so loudly it’s almost offensive. Like he didn't spend four agonizing months wondering who the stranger had been.

Barely remembered him, yeah right.

“Hmm…right, your face says otherwise.”

“I’m not having this conversation anymore.”

He looks away, and his gaze lands on Arian again, just then Arian looks over as well, directly at him, their eyes meet.

Caleb’s stomach performs an entirely unnecessary little turn.

He looks away at once.

Beside him, Eleanor makes a small sound.

“Stop it.” Caleb warns.

“What? I didn't say anything.” Caleb frowns at her

Eleanor laughs softly and rests her shoulder against his. For several blessed seconds, she allows the matter to drop.

"I'm not going to lie, Caleb. He does look dangerous." Caleb follows her gaze despite every intention not to.

"He's a mafia boss, Eleanor. I imagine he's done one or two questionable things."

"One or two?"

"Perhaps several."

"Father clearly has excellent judgment."

“Or he’s really in some deep shit to be joining hands with a mafia family.” Caleb mutters.

Eleanor considers this. "I find the entire situation rather entertaining."

"Of course you do."

"Who would have imagined Father would eventually require the head of a crime family to keep our little Caleb under control?" She reaches over and pats him on the head.

Caleb recoils immediately, batting her hand away. "Would you stop doing that?"

Eleanor laughs. "Hey, I am older than you."

"By an entirely unimpressive margin. And you're becoming intolerable."

"And yet you adore me." She slips her arm through his anyway, settling comfortably against his side as though Caleb hadn't just insulted her.

"Don't you have a piano lesson?" he asks. "Surely Europe would be devastated if its resident musical prodigy failed to prepare for her next showcase."

Eleanor has played the piano since childhood and somewhere along the way has developed an intense love for music and performing. She was able to build a reputation by performing at showcases across Europe and has earned recognition beyond the usual fascination surrounding the Whitmore name.

Caleb has always admired that about her.

Eleanor shrugs.

"I finished this morning, and besides, this is considerably more entertaining for me."

"What is?"

"Watching you unravel over an attractive mafia boss."

Caleb gives her a flat look. “I am done with you.” he gives her a slight push in an attempt to budge her off but she sticks to him with a grin.

“Don’t think you can pretend with me, you’ve looked at him for like the sixth time while we are here having a conversation.”

"Have you been counting?"

"I became curious after the third."

"You desperately need a hobby." Eleanor grins and squeezes his arm.

Caleb rolls his eyes and looks at Arian again.

Seven.

Fine.

Perhaps Eleanor has a point. But really, what is he supposed to do when the man is standing there looking like that?

Arian is impossible to miss among the security officers surrounding him. He stands taller than nearly all of them, dressed in black shirt beneath a fitted dark waistcoat, his sleeves rolled neatly to his elbows. His arms folded across his chest, exposing tanned forearms that Caleb most certainly did not need to be noticing from this distance.

He still hasn’t come to terms with the fact that this guy who showed up in a casual leather jacket is the same guy who's standing there looking absolutely calm, and powerful.

There is nothing hiding him now.

No more a nameless stranger, just Arian standing beneath the afternoon sun as though he has every right to be there. He doesn’t look like the man who has been brought in to join the security team, he looks like the man commanding it.

Even from here, Caleb can see the quiet assurance in the way he carries himself. Arian never seems to fidget or shift unnecessarily. He listens when someone speaks, responds with measured words and somehow the entire group adjusts around him.

Caleb finds it profoundly irritating.

Across the field, Arian looks over again, and this time Caleb holds his gaze as the corner of Arian’s mouth lifts, barely.

A fucking smirk.

Caleb’s eyes narrow. Something tells him, Arian intends to make being unaffected extraordinarily difficult.

Diplomatic speeches have to be one of the most effective forms of torture invented by the monarchy.

Caleb has been staring at the same paragraph for nearly ten minutes.

It is both an honour and a privilege to celebrate the enduring cultural relationship between—

He scratches out enduring. Then stares at it, then writes it back in.

“Riveting…ugh, who even uses that word anymore.” he mutters to himself.

The study has fallen quiet save for the occasional scratch of his pen and the low crackle from the fireplace. Several folders lay open across the desk, filled with briefings for the upcoming visit to Sicily, economic partnerships, cultural initiatives, diplomatic dinners and ceremonial appearances.

All perfectly important and painfully dull.

Sicily doesn’t bother him, if anything Caleb would happily disappear there for several months under different circumstances. He loves traveling, finding art galleries hidden away from tourist streets, wandering through old buildings, discovering the history of places through the things people have created there.

It’s the diplomacy he hates.

The endless dinners, the speeches and always having to keep up appearances. It’s an exhausting political race towards a Crown he never asked for.

Sometimes, Caleb envies his sisters.

Eleanor has her music, she performs across Europe, building a reputation that belongs to her rather than merely to the Whitmore name.

Cecily is still studying and regularly announces her intention to finish University, pack a suitcase and disappear around the world before anyone could saddle her with too many royal obligations.

And Caleb has art.

Paintings, drawings, galleries and auctions, modern pieces that make absolutely no sense until one stands before them long enough, and classical works that have survived centuries simply because, someone, somewhere has decided they deserve to.

The charity auctions have been among the few royal engagements he genuinely loves hosting.

And then the attack in Paris happened.

Since then, his father has promptly banned him from hosting another one for the foreseeable future. Instead, there are more diplomatic assignments, more responsibility.

His father has made it clear several times that he wants to prepare him for the possibility that whenever Andrew is absent, Caleb would be expected to step into increasingly significant duties beside him.

The entire thing is ridiculous.

Matilda would make a better ruler than either of them.

She understands politics better than Caleb ever cared to. She actually enjoys the machinery of the Crown and has spent years quietly challenging traditions that should have died several generations ago.

But apparently, being born a woman remains enough to complicate the obvious.

For god’s sake, this isn't the nineteenth century anymore. The rules are stupid.

Not that Caleb particularly feels like telling Matilda that at the moment. He is still angry with her.

And Andrew…

Caleb frowns at the page. Andrew still hasn't returned from his assignment yet, which means he doesn't know about Arian.

Or perhaps he does…

Caleb wouldn’t put it past his family to have constructed this entire absurd arrangement behind his back.

He’s still lost in thoughts when a folder lands on his desk, startling him hard enough that his pen drags a line of ink across the page.

He stares at it, then slowly looks up.

Arian is standing on the other side of his desk. Caleb raises his eyebrow.

“You know, doors traditionally come with a rather useful feature called knocking.”

“I knocked, you didn’t answer.”

“Then perhaps you should have taken the hint.”

Arian ignores that and nods towards the folder. Caleb eyes it suspiciously.

“Your schedule for the next six months.”

Caleb stares at the folder before letting out a small chuckle. “I’m sorry?”

“It includes the Sicily assignment.”

Caleb leans back in his chair still smiling. “I don’t keep a schedule.”

“You do now.”

Caleb makes a face, the bastard is serious. Always serious.

“How exciting for me.” he mutters, but picks up the file.

There is something particularly infuriating about the calmness with which Arian speaks to him, no raised voice or visible irritation no matter how much Caleb tries to provoke him. He delivers instructions as though it has never occurred to him that Caleb might refuse.

Naturally, this makes Caleb want to refuse everything.

He opens the folder, and the first few pages are unsurprising. Flight details, official appearances, meeting and travel arrangements.

Then, Caleb stops.

He reads one section, brows drawing together.

“Absolutely not.” He's up on his feet. Arian simply stares back.
“What?”

Caleb turns the folder around at Arian’s face causing him to tilt his head back a little. He taps on the page.

Private engagements and unscheduled appearances: Principal protection will remain within immediate response distance during all public and private movements outside secured royal residences. Advance notice of location and attending parties will be required where reasonably possible.

“What exactly does all private movements mean?”

“What it says.”

“So if I attend a fitting, you follow me?”

“Yes.”

“Photoshoots, dinners, private dinners?”
“Yes.” again with the one word response and that infuriatingly neutral expression, Caleb will slam his head into a wall.

“What are you, my babysitter?”

“It is for your protection.”

“And I already have security.”

“And they will remain.”

“Then why exactly do I require you breathing down my neck?”

Arian steps closer and calmly takes the folder from Caleb’s hands. “Because your existing security understands royal protocol. I understand the people who tried to kill you.”

That shuts him up. Caleb hates when he does that.

"Your security team reacts to visible threats. My job is to make sure they don't become visible in the first place."

"And this requires following me into a tailor?"

"If the tailor wants you dead." Arian says matter of factly.

"My tailor is seventy-three."

Arian smirks. "I'll take that into consideration."

Caleb's mouth twitches. "You're insufferable."

"So I've been told….recently."

Caleb has the distinct suspicion that is directed at him. He reaches for the folder, Arian hands it over.

"I'll read it, but if I find anything I dislike, I'll change it."

Arian keeps smirking knowingly as Caleb notices a hint of scoff in it.

"We'll see." Then he turns and walks towards the door. "Goodnight, Your Highness."

The door closes as Caleb grips the folder between his fingers, the urge to rip it up is there but he controls it.

"Oh, I despise him."

Arian has been at the shooting range for approximately twenty minutes when he hears footsteps approaching behind him.

He feels the thud of the folder on the table almost in the same manner as he had done last night on the prince’s study table. Upon seeing Caleb standing there, he lowers down his pistol.

“Okay, so I have conditions.”

Arian sighs and places the pistol down on the table. One glance at the folder tells him Caleb has apparently spent the last hour waging a very colourful and deeply personal war against his security protocols. Sticky notes popping out from the seams and his handwriting scribbled across nearly every available inch of the pages.

"Of course you do."

That seems to offend him, but he inhales. "I spent an hour reading this, and regrettably, most of it is reasonable."

Arian turns towards him, a smirk tugging at his lips as he folds his arms across his chest. He doesn’t miss the way Caleb’s gaze briefly drops to his forearms before snapping back to his face, followed by a rather deliberate clearing of his throat.

"How disappointing for you."

"Immensely."

"And that still didn't stop you from annotating my notes?"

"I improved it." Caleb argues.

"You drew a skull beside section twelve."

"That one felt oppressive."

"It requires you to inform security before leaving a secured residence."

"Exactly, it feels like I am being kept a prisoner here, therefore it’s oppressive.

Arian exhales a sigh, and shakes his head. This is going to take a while.

"Fine," he says. "Let's hear your conditions."

Caleb visibly brightens "Alright then, first, you are not following me on dates."

"I am."

The brightness vanishes, and is replaced by a pout. Arian suddenly wants to look away.

"That is absolutely non-negotiable, you cannot follow me on dates." Caleb says again emphasizing on each syllable like he’ll have an aneurysm.

“I won't be sitting at the table.”

“No, I imagine it, I’m having dinner with someone and you’re lurking three tables away looking like you’re contemplating where best to bury the body.”

“I will not be three tables away. You won’t see me, and you won’t know where I am.” Arian says.

“You do realize that’s even worse.” Caleb tells him.

Arian can feel his patience wearing thin. Of all the clauses Caleb could have chosen to argue over, naturally, he had settled on the one that irritated Arian the most.

Not that Arian is particularly thrilled by the prospect of following the prince around on his dates either. But protocol is protocol. Wherever Caleb chooses to spend his time and whoever he chooses to spend it with, Arian is required to remain close enough to anticipate any potential threat.

“I’m not interested in who you sleep with, Your Highness.” Caleb’s eyebrows rise. “My concern is whether the person you’re with poses a threat to you. The clause says, I’ll keep my distance, and you won’t know I’m there.”

Caleb laughs, Arian senses a hint of frustration in his voice. “You are very reassuring.”

“Fine okay, but you remain invisible and you do not interfere.”

“Unless necessary.” Arian replies.

“Define necessary.”

“A threat to your safety, someone trying to kill you or attempting to drug you.”

Caleb gives him a bewildered look. “Hmm right.”

“Someone carrying a concealed weapon or whose background gives me a reason to believe they intend to harm you,” Arian continues.

“Okay, okay I got it.” Caleb says, waving his hand. “Wait…what do you mean by background?”

Arian says nothing, just waits until realization dawns on him.

“You are not conducting background checks on my dates.” Caleb says, he almost looks horrified.

“I am.”

“Arian, seriously, this is too much.”

Arian stills, only slightly. It is the first time he has heard the prince say his name so casually, without Conti or Godfather wrapped around it like some deliberate provocation. Caleb doesn’t seem to notice what he has done. He is too busy dragging a frustrated hand through his hair, exhaling heavily as though the security protocol has forced him to reconsider every decision that has led him to this exact moment.

"Alright, fine." Caleb said eventually. "But I never see them, you discover something embarrassing but entirely legal, you take it to your grave and I never hear about it.”

"Fine, next condition."

"Fine. Friends. I can go out whenever I please."

"Within reason."

"No, absolutely not. I go to clubs, private lounges, and parties. Sometimes without planning them, my social life does not require your approval."

"I don't recall asking you to seek it, you can go wherever you like. I need to know where you are."

“Okay, well you will keep your distance, twenty meters, where I can’t see you.”

“That can be negotiable, distance depends upon the environment."

“You’re awfully vague, do you know that?”

“A nightclub and a private dinner aren’t exactly the same security environment.”

Caleb’s mouth tightens, Arian shows a hint of smirk and he could already see how much the prince hates it.

“Fine, but if you insist on following me everywhere, I expect discretion, surely the Sicilian underworld has taught you some sort of mafia invisibility magic.”

Arian continues to look at him in a deadpanned expression.

“An invisibility cloak, perhaps? Secret tunnels?”

Arian rolls his eyes and waits.

He simply watches Caleb continue, apparently incapable of allowing a thought to pass through his head without immediately sharing it with the room. There must be something nearby he can use to shut the prince up. Preferably something effective enough to silence him without doing any permanent damage.

Because, the alternative is admitting that Caleb’s complete lack of a filter is becoming strangely endearing.

And Arian would rather shoot himself.

He picks up the folder, choosing to ignore Caleb.

There are more conditions.

No entering Caleb’s bedroom without knocking unless there is an immediate threat.

Reasonable.

No interfering with his friends unless they pose a legitimate security concern.

Also reasonable.

No cancelling personal engagements without explaining why.

Annoying, but fair.

Caleb wants the right to reject unnecessary changes to his schedule, Arian agrees, provided security related changes remain his decision. Caleb refuses, they argue, and Arian eventually decides to compromise.

“Goodness this feels like you’re running a dictatorship.”

By the end of it, the folder contains so much additional handwriting that the original document is barely recognizable. Arian will have to get a new print.

“Anything else?” Arian finally asks.

Caleb thinks about it, and Arian is immediately regretting asking.
“Actually, yes.”

“Of course.”

Caleb smirks as he leans forward against the table, closing some of the distance between them.

“I am agreeing to this because I understand why it’s necessary. Not because you ordered me to.”

Arian’s mouth curves. “If that helps you sleep at night, Your Highness.”

Then, because Caleb apparently believes he is the only one allowed to play this game, Arian leans forward too.

The surprise is immediate.

It flickers across Caleb’s face before he can hide it, his smirk faltering as the space between them narrows to something considerably less appropriate for a conversation about security protocols.

“But,” Arian adds quietly, “when your life is actually in danger, you’ll do exactly what I tell you.”

And Caleb says nothing, neither of them moves.

For several seconds, it becomes a silent content neither seems willing to lose.
This close, Arian notices things he has no reason to notice. The small mole on Caleb’s cheek, the faint scattering of freckles across his cheekbones, the light reflecting into those deep hazel eyes that otherwise appear a dark shade of brown under normal lightning, the way his lashes lower slightly when his gaze flickers over Arian’s face.

Irrelevant details, entirely fucking irrelevant.

And yet Arian finds himself committing them to memory anyway.

Caleb’s gaze drops, just briefly and Arian knows exactly where it lands. Something quiet and warm shifts between them, so delicate beneath the provocation that brought them this close in the first place.

Caleb is the first to break it.

He straightens abruptly, blinking once as though returning to himself.

“Fine,” he says.

Arian remains where he is for another second. His smirk returns.

“Fine.”

Caleb shoots him a look, but there is a faint flush climbing his cheeks now. Arian very graciously decides not to mention it.

Mostly because he isn't entirely certain Caleb is the only one who needs a moment.

Then Caleb’s mouth curves, just like always he has a way of pulling himself out of anything.

And then he extends his hand.
“Do we have an agreement then, Godfather?”

So we are back to nicknames again.

Arian looks at the offered hand, then at Caleb, before extending his own. Caleb’s palm is warm against his, his grip firm despite the softness of his skin. The contact lasts only a moment, perfectly ordinary in every sense and yet Arian remains strangely aware of it.

“Until you break it.”

“Tch, you have remarkably little faith in me.”

Pulling his hand back, Arian pushes both his hands inside his pockets and steps back, perhaps he needs to keep a distance with the prince for now.

“I met you during an assassination attempt and you tried to punch me.”

"I literally thought you were kidnapping me."

"And then you stole my hat."

Caleb freezes.

The humor between them quietens, replaced by an awareness that hadn’t been there a second ago.

Arian watches Caleb’s expression change, it’s subtle, the brief widening of his eyes, the way his lips part before closing again. For once, the prince who seems to have an answer for everything, has none.

Because they both know what the hat means.

Arian watches the faintest trace of color creep into his cheeks, then the prince turns and starts towards the exit.

“We’re done here.”

“Caleb.”

Arian doesn’t know why he suddenly called the prince by his name, but he stops abruptly. Silence follows before Arian lifts the heavily annotated folder.

“You forgot your masterpiece.”

Caleb glances back.

“Keep it, consider it a reminder of your limitations.”

Arian looks down at the page where Caleb has drawn a skull beside one of his security protocols.

When he looks up again, Caleb is already walking away. Arian exhales and then shakes his head.

Insufferable, completely, relentlessly insufferable.

His gaze remains on Caleb until he disappears through the doors. He doesn’t even realise there is a small smile betraying him before he could stop it as he stares at the little drawings the prince had drawn on the pages.

This arrangement is going to be a fucking nightmare.
