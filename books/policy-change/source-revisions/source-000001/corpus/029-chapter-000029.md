Shane concluded that there was absolutely *no way* to write something that would come off as professional and chill, and also likely to get a positive answer back.

He was going to have to call him. Cold.

Shane didn’t like calling people at the best of times. He preferred email, when it came down to it. A nice, brisk email, perfectly formatted and re-read a few times for grammar. If necessary, sent to his parents for a quick sanity check, depending on the level of seriousness. Then hit* Send.* And the person at the other end could read it at their leisure.

But he did not have Rozanov’s email. And he sure as hell wasn’t putting anything down in writing.

So, he waited until early evening, poured himself a ginger ale muddled with a lot of mint and lime, and hit *call* on Lily’s contact details, braced over his kitchen counter and heart rate pounding like he’d just done an hour of bag skates.

The phone rang and rang and rang and just before Shane was about to give up – relief warring with disappointment – it connected. He held his breath.

“Ilya Rozanov’s phone,” an accented-English voice said, a little breathless.

A woman’s voice.

“Hello?” she prompted, when Shane didn’t say anything.

His brain did some easy, unbearable math. This was the girlfriend. The woman he had seen in the photos. She was answering Ilya’s phone. Wherever they were, she felt comfortable enough to pick up his phone, a phone that said ‘Jane calling’ and answer it. Or maybe – even worse – Rozanov had told her to answer.

There was no way Shane *could* say anything. He actually, physically couldn’t speak. Hand shaking, he pressed the red call-end button and slid down into a crouch on the floor, where he burst into shocked tears.

For the last time, he promised himself as he sobbed into his hands. This was the last fucking time he cried over Ilya Rozanov.

After, exhausted, Shane put his phone in a drawer, took a couple of prescription sleeping tablets and took himself off to bed.

Three days later, Wiebe put both his hands on Shane’s padded shoulders in the tunnel before the game and met him eye to eye.

“You are going to get through this,” his coach told him, quietly. Or comparatively quietly because surrounding them was his team in various states of nervous preparation and just beyond that the low-level roar of the crowd. Boston-Montreal games were always sold out.

Shane nodded, though he thought his skin was going to vibrate from his body. He rubbed his lips together; they were smothered in a greasy ChapStick kindly donated by J.J. He had been picking at his lips, which were dry from all the deep breathing exercises he had been doing. “I am.”

“You are Shane fucking Hollander. Say it back to me.”

“I am Shane fucking Hollander.”

Wiebe’s eyes bored into him, uncomfortably. “More convincing.”

Shane summoned *something* from deep within. “I am Shane *fucking* Hollander.”

Wiebe leaned forward and knocked his head against Shane’s helmet and finally the eye contact ended. “You will get through this, Hollander, and be stronger for it. And after we win, we’ll go for a drink. I think you should tell me all about it.”

True to his word, after he’d done press, Wiebe had bundled Shane into one of the team cars but instead of going to a bar – which, if the objective was to have a quiet, private conversation, was an impossible feat in Montreal after a winning game – they went back to Wiebe’s.

They were welcomed by his wife just getting home from work, still in a smart dark pantsuit. Wiebe and she kissed in a perfunctory but affectionate manner. Having only seen Emily at social functions, this version of her with a slick-back bun and slightly worn makeup took Shane by surprise. She looked frighteningly competent.

“You know Emily, of course.”

Shane nodded and stuck out his hand. “Great to see you again, Mrs. Wiebe.”

Emily’s mouth twitched as she shook Shane’s hand. She had a very firm grip. “Shane. Please don’t call me that. I’m going to,” she looked to Wiebe and gestured up and down her body, “wash all this off. I would like a *very large* glass of wine.”

Wiebe grinned. “Gotcha.”

Like the Pikes, the kitchen of the Wiebes house was cluttered in the way that all family kitchens were cluttered, just like his parents’ kitchen had been as he had grown up. There were kids’ paintings stuck to the fridge. There was stuff *everywhere*. School books and tablets and toys on every available surface. This morning’s breakfast was still on the table and there was a cold French press in the middle of the kitchen island. It was the kind of clutter that would drive Shane insane but Wiebe ignored it and went straight to the wine fridge.

“What does Emily do?” he asked, studying one of the pieces of children’s art on the fridge. It looked like a family of stick figures on fire. Disturbing.

“She runs a management consultancy. They just acquired a new arm here in Montreal – bit of a hostile takeover – and is trying to get it off the ground.”

Shane was none the wiser. It sounded important. “Is that, ah, tough?”

“It’s a cesspool. She loves it.”

“Oh.”

Wiebe poured a large glass of red wine, arranged it – along with a small bowl of olives – in the middle of the island. He then grabbed a beer from the fridge and held up a Perrier to Shane. “This okay?”

Shane thought about it. “I’ll take a beer.”

Wiebe nodded, grabbed another beer, a large bag of Doritos from a cupboard and gestured for Shane to follow him.

“So where are the kids?” Shane asked as he was led out into a large backyard filled with children’s play equipment and – their destination – a sunken patio area with a firepit. He had no idea on the gender of the Wiebes’ children, or the ages, or, come to think of it, how many there were. But he knew there were some so it was a safe question.

“They are with Emily’s parents. One of the reasons we moved here was the proximity to her family; they’re in Ottawa.”

“Hey, I grew up there.” He liked when he had easy connections with people. It meant he could open a whole topic of conversation with Emily with whom, thus far, he had only exchanged banal pleasantries.

“I’m well aware. They’re both obsessed with you. Ottawa’s own Shane Hollander,” he repeated, as if quoting someone.

Wiebe dropped down onto one of the soft couches. He offered Shane a beer which was at least one of those light ones. “So. Tell me about you and Rozanov. I’m guessing not many people know about you two.”

Shane was feeling remarkably fine about this interrogation, given he’d been sobbing on his kitchen floor three nights ago. Maybe he had nothing left?

Or maybe – new thought – he was now so used to *telling* his secrets that he was getting inured to it. Used to be *keeping* secrets was all he did. Now he just blurted them out to everyone.

It was kind of freeing, actually. “Just you. I mean, assuming he’s not told someone.”

“Is that likely?”

“I don’t think so. His team would not be good about it, apparently.”

“Except maybe Connors. He was a green on the Excel table.”

“Yeah. Except maybe him. Though I always thought the guy thing was only half of the problem.”

“The other part being you’re rivals.”

“People would not be cool about that.” Shane took a sip of his beer and maybe it was psychosomatic, years of watching guys sip beers after a hard day, but he sighed with relief.

After all that build-up, the game had been fine. Well. Not *fine* fine. But manageable if he really, really just *crushed* all his feelings down. Rozanov had been professional, greeting him with a simple ‘Hollander’ which Shane had been able to return. He had looked like shit though. He had played worse. Halfway through the second period, Shane checked him against the boards, pretty lightly all things considered, and for a moment it had looked like he was going to slump to the ice.

“Something is really wrong with him,” Shane mused. He was worried.

“Yeah. I asked around. There are some problems with the room.”

“Oh?”

“Mmm, people are being cagey about it. They’re obviously trying to keep it under wraps.”

That tallied with a couple of questions Shane had got after the game. Pointed ones about Rozanov slipping in the scoring race neither of them had paid attention to for some months. That asshole from Sportsnet asked him about the tensions in Boston’s locker room affecting their game in that sneery way he had.

“You didn’t talk.”

Shane shook his head. “I tried calling. He didn’t answer,” he said shortly. And in the end Shane had wrung out all his emotions on the kitchen floor. Rozanov had moved on. Shane needed to work out how to do that as well.

Still. The worry… it niggled. Maybe this was what happened, when you loved someone. Even if they were no longer part of your life, you still cared.
