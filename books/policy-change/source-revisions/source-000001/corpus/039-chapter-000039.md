Her mouth rolled into an ‘o’ but before she could say anything Ilya leaned forward and chipped in, “And I’m bisexual.”

Clearly, with these two meagre pieces of information, and the fact that they were leaning against each other like books on a shelf, Farah then put two and two together.

Nevertheless, Shane felt it needed absolute clarity. “And we’re together,” he murmured and all the hairs on his body lifted as goosebumps ran down the back of his spine and along his arms.

It was the first time he’d said it. The first time he had told anyone properly.

It was fucking amazing. In the most terrifying way possible.

“Oh. Wow.” Farah took a moment to gather herself. “That’s… wonderful news,” she said carefully. “If you don’t mind me asking, how long has this been going on?”

They glanced at each other. “Ah, a few… years?” Ilya suggested, posing this more as a question for Shane to corroborate rather than a confirmation.

“Yeah. A few years,” Shane agreed readily.

“So, it’s not recent. And it’s… serious?” Farah prompted.

They looked at each other again, both wearing identical, wavering smiles.

‘Serious’, Ilya mouthed to Shane, wide-eyed.

“Yeah,” said Shane, bashful, “it’s serious.”

It was Saturday morning and Shane had been sitting in his car outside his parents’ house for about thirty minutes.

He had not told his parents he was coming.

He had not told Ilya.

He wasn’t sure why. Maybe it was because, even halfway here, he had been reconsidering this approach and he didn’t want to get Ilya’s hopes up. Not that Ilya had been pressuring Shane to tell his parents – almost the opposite. He had no plans to tell his dad (“Little point. He would forget. And he would murder me.”) or his brother (“He would blackmail me. Probably.”). But Ilya knew Shane’s relationship with his parents was different and sometimes, when they were together, he got a look in his eye anytime Shane mentioned them. Sort of… longing.

Wiebe’s voice kept coming back at him, not just his absolutely terrifying lecture about fallout but his confusion about Shane still keeping this secret from his parents.

He had been trying not to think about why he hadn’t told his parents. It was probably the same reason he hadn’t told Hayden. Hadn’t told J.J.

It was obvious, though, wasn’t it? He didn’t need to scratch too hard at that one. He was afraid. He was a coward.

For some reason, *this* secret was bigger than him being gay. Gay was— incidental. Gay was almost *inactive.* Ilya Rozanov was active. The active symbol of Shane’s gayness. It moved Shane from a theoretical construct to an entirely practical and practicing gay person.

A gay person who was *with* Ilya Rozanov. Not just any other man. Ilya. Rozanov.

The guy Shane had been compared to all his professional hockey career, the flip side to Shane’s wholesome coin. The guy who had been the main feature of his mom’s more colorful rants. The guy Shane had bitched about at team meetings with Hayden and the others, who he’d put down and performatively belittled, even when he was simultaneously messaging him to meet up, knowing full well he didn’t mean it.

It was like he had been two different Shanes since he and Rozanov had first hooked up. There was the Shane everyone knew and expected – the generational hockey talent, the role model for Asian kids, the captain and teammate and good son. And then there was the other Shane who did unthinkable, secret things, like dirty sex with a rival in anonymous hotels and lied to his loved ones.

Good Shane and Bad Shane.

But there had never been two Shanes. Just the one Shane who wanted to deny a part of himself. The part of himself that preferred men and sex with men and Ilya most of all. Wanting Ilya didn’t make Shane *bad*.

*Ilya* wasn’t bad, either. He was a generational hockey talent whose skills rivalled Shane’s, though he executed those skills differently. And *he* shouldn’t be a secret. And certainly not from the people who loved Shane the most.

With unearthly timing, Shane’s cell phone vibrated with a message from Lily.

Lily: *Serious*

Even in extremis, it made him smile. Since that call with Farah, they’d taken to sending this word to each other, once, sometimes even twice a day.

*Serious, *Shane wrote back and, after much deliberation, he added the little red heart emoji and pressed ‘send’.

Then he opened his car door and climbed out.
