“Hey, so we maybe need to catch-up,” Hayden said after practice.

Shane dragged himself out of a mental repetition of the variation of a continuous attack drill he’d been working on with both his wings in preparation for their game against Tampa. “Sure. Any reason in particular?”

“I had my 1-2-1 with Petrov yesterday and I think I need to talk about it with someone.”

He gave Hayden his full attention now. “That sounds… serious.”

Hayden made a face. “I dunno. Maybe just need to talk it over with you?”

“We can do that.” Shane smiled in what he hoped was a reassuring way and not – as he’d been told before – in a way that suggested he was pretty sure he was about to be disappointed.

Hayden affected a hang-dog expression. “Don’t look at me like that, man.”

Yeah, he might need to practice that one.

They headed to Murph’s, keeping it quiet in case the rest of the team saw this as an invitation to get a rare drink with their Captain.

Murph’s was, to Shane’s surprise, festooned with dangling glittery red hearts. “What the fuck,” he asked, as they slid into their usual booth at the back, Shane shuffling his way into the corner so that he wasn’t really visible. The staff at Murph’s were pretty good at keeping fans off his back but sometimes they slipped through. Not that there was anything wrong with that – Shane loved his fans – but sometimes a guy just wanted a beer with a friend. Or a ginger ale.

“Damn, you are so single. It’s nearly Valentine’s.”

“Oh yeah? Wow that came around quick.” He ignored the single comment. He was *busy* okay? The season was ramping up. They’d had All Stars – which Shane insisted on taking seriously, particularly since they’d had him up against Rozanov at every opportunity. And *he’d* had himself up against Rozanov at every opportunity – in more ways than one. Heh. “What are you doing with Jackie?”

“Dinner and a babysitter we’re paying through the neck for. Hey, Marcie,” Hayden greeted one of their usual waitresses, a pretty brunette with a nose piercing and a love of brightly colored lipsticks. Today’s was a really vibrant pink. Which would suit Shane because he was a *Bright Winter.*

Only not because he would never wear lipstick. Weird thought. He rubbed his thumb against his lips, recalling the unpleasant waxy texture. Jessica, his high school girlfriend, had also worn lipstick. And lip gloss. All of which Shane had hated.

“Afternoon, gents. What can I get you?” She smiled broadly. “Your usual, Shane?”

It was really a pleasure to be known. Shane smiled back easily. He liked Marcie. “Please.”

“And I’ll get a Blue. Thanks, Marcie.”

“No problem. I’ll be right back.”

They returned to the crucial matter of dissecting Hayden’s lame Valentine’s plans. “What did you get Jackie?”

Hayden picked up the Valentine’s menu, grimaced at it, and put it back down. “It’s good, actually. I got her a gold necklace with the kids’ initials.”

Translation: his mom had suggested a gold necklace with the kids’ initials. Still. This was actually pretty thoughtful. And he hadn’t left it to the last minute like last year. And it wasn’t a bouquet of really big daises like the year before that, which had apparently also been Bad. “Nice one, man. She’ll love that.”

“And a spa day for her and her sister. But this time,” Hayden said, pre-empting Shane’s mental dismissal, “I’ve agreed with my mom that whenever Jackie wants to do it, she’ll come and stay and take care of the kids.”

Shane followed that logic through. “But doesn’t that mean that Jackie will have to look after both your mom and her sister? Since they’ll both have to stay?”

Hayden studied Shane with a sort of slowly morphing irritation. First the irritation that Shane was questioning his plan. Then the irritation that he was probably *right* to. And lastly the irritation about whether or not he needed rethink this plan, which required brain capacity he did not have.

“Why don’t you just pick a week in June and I can help you babysit instead.”

“I can look after my own kids.”

“Can you though?”

“Fuck off, Shane.” Hayden laughed and ran his hands through his hair. He groaned. “You’re right. I’ll sort it out. I just want to do something nice for her. She does so much.”

“I know, dude. I know.”

Marcie arrived with their drinks, dropping coasters down and then placing their drinks neatly. “You want any chips or anything? Not you, of course, Shane,” she said, winking at him.

“I think we’re all good here,” Shane said, speaking on behalf of Hayden who had a habit of mainlining about a kilo of peanuts here – they would keep topping up his bowl – and they were *so* bad for you.

“Okay,” Hayden said, after Marcie had left and he’d taken a swig of his beer. “So it was a pretty normal meet. We talked about Jackie and the kids. He asked about Coach and the team. We talked about the game on Monday and about the roadie and then he asked about you.”

“Like, captain stuff?”

Hayden shook his head. “No. He asked if I’d seen any of the press after the gala.”

“Right.” Great. “Oh, wait, have you?”

“Yeah, man, Jackie follows all that shit. Plus, there were pictures of us there all dressed up and she likes to share them with our parents.”

“Of course. Sure. You didn’t mention.”

“Well, some of it was a bit weird, wasn’t it?” Hayden – nervously – took another swig of his beer. Shane mirrored him and gulped his ginger ale. “He wanted to know if the team had picked up on it.”

“Have they?”

Hayden shook his head again, this time more urgently. “No, I haven’t heard anything. Not that there was anything to hear. It wasn’t that big a deal. Only—I ah. Kind of said that it couldn’t happen.”

“I don’t follow. What couldn’t?”

“Like. He was saying it was frustrating. That that kind of behavior from the media and stuff was detrimental to people being, like themselves.”

WARNING, Shane’s brain went. WARNING.

“—and then I said I’m sure you didn’t care because you weren’t gay and even if you were, which I know you’re not, man,” Hayden said earnestly, “it couldn’t happen.”

Shane held himself very still. “What couldn’t happen, Hayden?”

“Being gay.”

“I couldn’t be gay?”

“No, just people generally.”

Shane was lost. Totally lost. “Hayden—”

Hayden looked up to the ceiling with frustration. “I’m not explaining this well. I mean, I know that’s, like statistically unlikely right? That there are *no* gay people in hockey.” He leaned forward eagerly, as if to get Shane to confirm that, yes, it was statistically unlikely. So Shane nodded, even though he really, really didn’t want to. He wanted to peel the skin from his body, create a Shane clone that would sit on front of Hayden for this conversation and *vanish* from the bar. “Yeah. So I said more that – if someone was gay, they couldn’t come out.”

“Couldn’t. That’s the word you used.”

“Yeah.”

“Not… wouldn’t?”

“Does it make a difference?”

“Um. I mean, sort of. Maybe some people,” not Shane-people, “*would* come out. If they were —” Not Shane. Not a generational hockey player. Not mixed-race. Not first line center for the Montreal Metros. Not casually fucking Ilya Rozanov.

“You think someone would if they had the balls?” Hayden asked with surprise, naturally not appreciating that he was stabbing Shane in the stomach here. Or in the balls.

“I mean. I guess. Anyway. What happened when you said that.”

“He asked what I thought the repercussions would be.”

“Right.”

“And I said the guy would probably be benched.”

Shane winced.

“Shit, I knew that was wrong.” Cringing, Hayden took another fortifying drink. “I’m such a fucking idiot.”

“You’re not—” Shane sighed and rubbed his face with his hands and then just left them there. He *was* an idiot. He could picture it so easily, picture Hayden’s golden-retriever face, his earnest desire to correct Petrov on hockey etiquette. “I don’t think they could bench you. Or whomever. Legally speaking.”

“No, but *emotional* benching.”

He peeked through his fingers. “Did you say that?”

“Kinda. I said it would cause problems in the room.”

“Oh boy.”

Hayden groaned and pushed his beer aside so he could lean his head against the table. “I’ve really fucked up, haven’t I?” he told the no-doubt sticky wood.

“Did *you* come off as homophobic here or was it more that the MLH is?”

“I don’t know. Maybe both? He seemed pissed.”

Fuck. Shane was going to have to fix this.

That night, Shane sent a very polite and professional email to Marlene asking for Petrov’s soonest availability. He signed it *Shane Hollander (Captain),* just in case Marlene needed reminding.

*Regarding?* she wrote back, no niceties, not even a ‘sent from my iPhone so apologies for the brevity’ signature.

*Regarding Hayden Pike*, he replied. Two could play at that game.

*Will he know what that means,* she asked, no question mark.

*Yes,* said Shane, even more pleased with himself. Suck it, Marlene.

Twelve hours later, Marlene emailed him a phone number. *Mr. Petrov said you can call him when you want. Do NOT share that number with anyone else. It is his PERSONAL cell. *She then wrote the whole email in French for him. Just in case he hadn’t understood.

What a baffling woman.

Obviously, Shane was *not* going to call Mikhail Petrov, billionaire, owner of the Montreal Metros whenever Shane wanted. That was insane. Instead, he composed a short text message, which he spent over an hour on.

Shane: *Hi, this is Shane Hollander. Please let me know when you are available for a call. Thanks.*

Petrov replied immediately. *Hi Shane Hollander. Give me twenty minutes.*

Shane stared. Did that mean he would call Shane? Or should Shane call *him* in twenty minutes?

Probably Petrov would call Shane.

He checked the timestamp. It was 9.02am now. So he had until 9.22am.

Shane replied, *Great. Speak at 9.22am (EST).*

Petrov sent back a laughing face.

Petrov didn’t call him until 9.56am which was annoying for a number of reasons, the main one being that Shane had come back from a run and had delayed showering in case Petrov called. So now he had been stewing in his own sweat for nearly an hour, writing and rewriting what he was going to say.

Shane was uncomfortably nervous. He wanted to clear up any issues with Hayden, of course, but he also wished to be as far away from the context as possible. If he could get through this conversation without the word ‘gay’ being used, he would count it as a win.

Shane’s phone lit up with the incoming call and his heart jumped. *Mikhail Petrov (Owner, Metros) calling.*

He answered, putting the phone on speakerphone so he could move about if he needed to. Bracing himself with his hands on his hips, he stood up straight. “Hi, Mr. Petrov, thanks for taking my call.”

“Hi Shane.” Petrov sounded amused. In the background, Shane could hear the clutter of what he suspected was crockery. “How can I help?”

“Ah. Yeah. Hayden Pike came to talk to me yesterday. He was concerned he’d given you a bad impression in your last catch-up.”

“He was?”

Shane’s eyes glanced at his notes. “Yes. He worried he came across as homophobic. He’s not. He’s just not the most eloquent guy, that’s all.”

“That’s certainly true. You can reassure Hayden that I didn’t think he came across as a homophobe. I did think he was fairly blasé about the toxicity of his chosen career and the casual way he threw about the anticipated discriminatory punishments a member of the MLH might face should he chose to be open about his sexuality.”

“Yeah,” said Shane, slowly.

“So you can imagine my concern.”

“I can imagine it, yes,” Shane repeated, because *concern* wasn’t on his notes and he needed to choose his words carefully. He imagined this was press and Petrov was that irritating guy from Sportsnet. “But, in Hayden's defense, that *is* what would happen."

“It would not be what would happen on my team, Shane.”

Shane winced, grateful that Petrov couldn’t see him do so. “I’d like to think so, too.”

“Shane, I have removed the GM, I have purged human resources, I cleansed the coaching staff and replaced them with people who are more aligned to Petrov 7s values.”

“Yes, sir.”

The moment seemed to call for a ‘sir’.

“Are you saying that even with new policies, and an overhauled support system in place, no one in my team would feel comfortable expressing their sexuality, should that be something they would choose to do?”

Shane pictured Rozanov’s mirth-filled face. *He wants to make hockey gay.*

“Shane? That’s a yes or no question.”

Sighing, Shane took a seat on one of his bar stools. “But it can’t be a yes or no answer.”

“Perhaps you could elaborate,” Petrov said coolly.

Elaborate?

Fine, Shane decided, feeling put-upon and irritated by the oversimplification of the culture he had been living and breathing for more than half his life. He could *elaborate*.

“Right. Well. Fine. You’ve done all these things. And they’re good and progressive things. I agree with everything you are doing. But— shit. It’s not a quick fix. It’s not going to change decades of— of the MLH touting this white, uber-masculine ideal. The qualifications for professional hockey do not include being open-minded and that goes from fucking Crowell down to the kit guys. You – we – have influence over the Metros but we don’t live or work in a bubble. You don’t have influence everywhere else and it’s the *everywhere else* that’s the problem now. Not just other teams who have shitty players and shitty management, who won’t trade for you if you’ve got a reputation for not playing the game the right way, but the fans. I *love* my fans but I also have a whole team who manages my social media because I get so much abuse despite being the best fucking player in the league. Meanwhile, my linemate and white, married friend Hayden gets memes and virtual pats on the back when we lose. I cannot conceive what it would be like if I—” *Don’t say it, Shane, don’t say it.* “What I’m trying to say, we aren’t an island. Gay, black, Asian, Latino, mixed-race, whatever it doesn’t matter. It’s not what the MLH promotes. It’s not what guys like Hayden have been raised under, even if they’re only passively absorbing behaviors like icing guys out in the locker because they don’t fit the mold. Half of them go along with it because it’s just what people *do.* It’s going to take more than diversity training and 1-2-1s to change that. It’s going to take —I don’t know. Starting the whole team from scratch. Uniting other teams in the league*.* A fucking figurehead or something who talks about it in more than a performative way and isn’t going to be shut down. It’s going to take… years.”

And then Shane, exhausted, ran out of words.

And Petrov said nothing.

The line was so silent that even the clinking of crockery and glasses had gone away.

Shane anxiously tapped the screen, just in case Petrov had hung up, but there was the counter, still ticking away.

“Um. Hello?” Shane asked, wondering if maybe he was fired.

“I’m here. I just wish I’d recorded you.”

He was fired. He was so fired. “Oh God, I’m really sorry.”

“No, no. I think that was the first honest thing you’ve ever said to me. Bravo. We will talk when I’m back. We have a lot to discuss.”

Shane didn’t tell his mom.

For as long as he had been playing professional hockey, Shane had downloaded every meeting he’d ever had with management to her. He’d replayed – heavily edited – conversations with coaches. With teammates. She often read the room better than he did and could see things from a different perspective, which was useful.

He had got into the habit of also calling her after most of his 1-2-1s with Petrov, unpacking what they’d talked about, wanting her evaluation – and approval – of everything he had said.

He did not do that this time.

In the first place, Yuna wouldn’t have understood why Shane wanted to clear up the situation with Hayden. She was fond of Hayden, though she had never really understood why Shane had decided he was his best friend. He got the feeling she didn’t think Hayden was very bright – which sure as shit this situation would have proved. She acknowledged he was a good liney. She cared about Hayden’s career but more in the sense that his career currently supported Shane’s career.

Whether Petrov liked him or not was irrelevant to Shane’s career so while she would have respected Shane's desire to right a wrong for his friend, she ultimately wouldn’t have approved of Shane intervening at the possible detriment of Shane’s own favor.

But that wasn’t why he hadn’t told her. This was about more than Hayden. This was about the fundamentals of the sport that his mother and to a degree Shane ignored in favor of the *good* things about hockey. That Shane was fucking good at it. That it mostly gave him joy. That it would pay him well and when – shudder – he retired, he would retire a young man with millions in the bank and a reputation that would live on.

But that *also *wasn’t why he hadn’t told her.

Shane hadn’t told her because – at the heart of it – Shane didn’t want Petrov to think Hayden was a homophobic asshole.

And that was important to Shane because he, Shane Hollander, was probably, definitely gay.

“Shane, I’m not sure if you’ve met Alex.”

Shane shook Alex’s hand, eyes immediately glancing at the bright pink hair. They had matched their earrings to the same shade. “Yes. You came and gave us that talk on the use of pronouns in the workplace.”

A talk that had been genuinely informative about an area of etiquette that Shane had found confusing but also the most excruciating experience of Shane’s life, surrounded as he had been by his teammates who had been extremely vocal about their opinions regarding the talk.

“And you were very attentive.” Alex raised their eyebrows, as if to suggest that this was not the case with the rest of Shane’s teammates, some of whom had audibly and rudely sighed and Wiebe had been forced to raise his voice for the first time.

“Some work to be done with the team there,” Shane acknowledged sheepishly though, frankly, the sighing had been the best of the scenarios he had imagined. He had been afraid someone might walk out. Or, worse, contest Alex on the usage of pronouns and mentioning that fucking liberal agenda again.

Alex smiled wryly and set their laptop down, fiddling around with the connections. “I think we’re here because there’s work to be done everywhere.”

Petrov snorted. “Take a seat, Shane.”

They had gathered in one of the smaller conference rooms that were dotted along the hallway down from the owner’s suite. Shane had signed some paperwork in this room, he was pretty sure. Though it had been decorated with more Metros stuff at the time and now was stripped back to rather more neutral tones.

And a really nice table, actually, with an intricate parquet design. He stroked the wood thoughtfully. Judging from the color and pattern, it was mango, which he’d been reading about. He had been paying rather more attention to furniture, now that he was in the process of building his own place.

He looked up and found both Alex and Petrov watching him stroke the table. They wore eerily similar expressions, heads tilted to the side in curiosity.

Shane sat back and cleared his throat. “Great table.”

The now familiar crinkle around his eyes indicated that Petrov had, once again, found Shane deeply funny. “I’m sure Marlene can find where we sourced it.”

“Oh, no, let’s not bother Marlene,” Shane said hurriedly, imagining being on the receiving end of more snippy emails.

“Shane," Petrov's eyelids fluttered at him, "are you afraid of my assistant?”

He scoffed, absolutely not going to be ashamed. “Any sane person would be.”

Alex laughed, a short, sharp bark. “He’s got you there, Mishka.” They opened their laptop as Shane pondered how you got ‘Mishka’ from ‘Mikhail’. “Okay. Let’s get started. I have a few slides.”

“Color me surprised,” said Petrov drily, which got him a glare. “What? You love a slide deck.”

They bickered with obvious fondness as Alex switched the screen to presentation mode and Shane wondered if maybe this wasn’t going to be as awkward as he had thought it was going to be.

That was right until the presentation loaded and Alex said, super casually, “So here is our plan for world domination, Shane.”

And projected on the screen was some intense grid with years horizontally and vertically there was a list of different departments – including HR, Communications, Management, the Team, Coaching Staff, Supporters. The text was super tiny but within each box was an initiative.

He squinted. He wished he’d brought his glasses.

“Well, not the world,” Petrov corrected. “This is *our* organizational transformation roadmap, for the Metros.”

Alex clicked on. A series of boxes on the furthest left column lit up in green. “We are currently here.”

Now that the boxes were green, Shane could read them better. “Introduce new DEI policy. Cascade policy. Introduce mandatory training. Restructure management and coaching staff— hey, does that mean you were already planning to replace Theriault?”

“Yes. He proved pretty early on that he wouldn’t align to our values. And would consistently undermine us to the team. Correct?” Alex asked Shane sharply, raising their eyebrows, as if daring Shane to disagree.

He couldn’t. “Yeah.”

Petrov tapped his pen against the table. “You never mentioned.”

“You never asked me.” Shane waved a hand at the look of complaint that crossed Petrov’s face. “Look, no, you don’t get it. That’s not what you do to your coach. Hockey is a hierarchy. It goes rookies, vets, assistant captain, captain, then coach. You don’t bitch about your coach to management unless you really mean it and you’re prepared to be punished if it goes wrong. And in the beginning we were just trying to work out if this—” He gestured to the screen “—was one of those performative initiatives the MLH roles out whenever there’s bad press or the real thing. It’s obviously the real thing. Everyone can see that now.”

“How do you know everyone sees it’s the real thing?” Alex wanted to know.

“There are chats. Chat groups,” Shane said hesitantly, really now feeling like he was breaking a fairly substantial bro code. “We have a big group one, which I police. Then there are various splinter groups. The team without me and just the As. One with just me, J.J. and Hayden. The rookies have one – that one usually lasts a couple of years so I know last season’s rooks still have an active group. That’s Peetie and Franks and Jessie, though he's gone back down to the AHL now." Not a loss, in the grand scheme of things, though Peetie and Franks had been upset. "And then there is another group that Comeau set up just after I got made captain which is more… selective.”

Alex and Petrov exchanged a look and then Petrov leaned forward like Shane was spilling a juicy secret. “Comeau thought he was going to get the C, didn't he?”

Shane grimaced. It wasn't a thought he particularly liked to dwell on. “Yeah, he was Anderson’s A. But the year Anderson retired, Rozanov was made Captain, the youngest ever, and we’re all about the rivalry here so.” Shane shrugged. At the time it had left a nasty taste in his mouth, coming second to Rozanov once again, and all for optics. But Shane had made his peace with it. He was a damn good captain. They would pry that C from his cold, dead hands.

“Yeah. I have a couple of guys in there who give me the occasional insight. I won’t tell you who. But I understand the tone is shifting. The dissenters have started to make noise about toeing the party line. Comeau doesn’t like it but he knows if he makes waves, it will not be good for his career. Not when there isn’t a coach to suck up to, not when he knows management is willing to make big changes.”

Alex shrugged and looked over at Petrov. “I still think we get rid of Cormeau.”

“Shane?”

Shane shook his head. “He’s an excellent defenseman. He and Drapeau are almost unstoppable.”

“I’m sure we could find another,” Alex insisted.

“Are we seriously talking about restructuring the team now?” he protested. These were not decisions that the captain made. And certainly didn’t discuss it without his coach present.

Petrov seemed to read Shane easily. “Do you want Wiebe here? I can assure you, he’s on board.”

“Wiebe knows about this? Fuck,” Shane muttered, sitting back in his chair.

“Shall we click on to the second chart?” Alex asked brightly. They didn’t wait for permission. The next chart was even worse in terms of density and this time Petrov stood up so he could stand closer to the screen. “This is my first draft of the Transformational Roadmap for the whole of the MLH. I haven’t come up with a catchy name yet.”

“Oh, yes, we need an acronym.” Petrov squinted at the screen. “How about TROL?”

Alex’s whole face lit up. “TROL? Oh, that could be fun.” They socked Petrov on the arm. “*That’s* why they pay you the big bucks.”

Shane was not going to be able to tell his mom about any of this.

It was gone nine by the time they wrapped up and Shane’s head was buzzing with new information and the anxiety that he had just stepped on a landmine which was on top of another landmine, which was on top of *another *one. He couldn’t shake the feeling that he was the entirely wrong choice to be involved in this operation.

For it was an operation. It was a *multi-year* operation that would probably continue until well after he retired from hockey.

His stomach rumbled, loud enough that Petrov heard it. “Should have fed you. Sorry. We really got caught up.” He checked his watch. “Want to grab dinner?”

Alex had slammed their laptop closed and left already, murmuring about being late to meet friends, so it was just Shane and Petrov standing outside the conference room.

Shane frowned, thinking about the photographs.

Petrov seemed to guess this was the direction of his thoughts. “Shane, I really don’t actually have paparazzi chasing me around most of the time.” He sighed. “But, you’re right, let’s not risk it.”

Now Shane felt bad. Petrov looked genuinely disappointed. “No, no, it’s fine.” It *was* fine. There was nothing going on between them. Petrov was basically his boss, for God’s sake. Plus, he knew that if he went home now, the buzzing in his head wouldn’t stop.

Shane was rewarded with those crinkling eyes. “We’ll go to TOKii.”

“Bribery.”

“You know it.”

Smiling, weirdly relaxed, Shane followed Petrov to his office, and then remembered the specter that was Marlene. She wasn’t at her desk. He peered into the little kitchenette, just in case she was hiding in there. “Please,” Petrov said, clicking things on his laptop, “she leaves at 6pm on the dot every day.”

That was a relief. “Aren’t executive assistants supposed to… assist you all the time?”

“I have others.”

“Others?”

“Two others, actually. One for GMT, one for EST. Marlene is really only here for the Metros administration which is not exactly time consuming, though she of course feels otherwise.”

Shane ambled over to look out of the window onto the ice as Petrov sank down into his office chair and began to reply to emails. The facility was shut down, so there was little but ambient light. Shane was patting himself on the back for the decision to accept this invitation to dinner. For one, the restaurant itself was a good choice. It was pretty upscale and at this time of night only a few patrons were eating. Petrov and Shane had been escorted to one of the more private tables in the restaurant, away from any scrutinizing eyes but not exactly hidden like they were actively hiding something. It didn’t feel subversive. For another, the conversation that edged around their earlier discussion was helping Shane with the buzzing in his head, almost like he was being regulated by Petrov’s openness.

Also, Petrov hadn’t done that annoying thing that nearly everyone did when there was any Asian food on a menu and assumed Shane knew anything about it. If anything, he seemed to be far more *au fait *with Japanese cuisine than Shane was and had basically ordered for them. They seemed to have very similar tastes and dietary requirements, which Shane found a relaxing change from eating with his teammates. He would never have come here with the guys.

A fleeting thought – that he might like to come here with Rozanov – whistled through Shane’s brain and was quickly dismissed as irrelevant.

Shane selected a piece of kinmedai. “Did you think about buying another team?”

“Not really. I wanted a French-speaking team so it was a choice between the Metros or the Centaurs and it was pretty clear that the Metros were on an upwards track whereas the Centaurs—”

“Are mediocre.” Which from Shane’s POV was pretty heartbreaking. They had been his team, as a kid. His and his dad’s. They’d been cruising at kind of middle-of-the-pack for a long time but there had been a couple of exciting years when he was a teenager when the Cens had hovered around the wild-card cutoff and he and his dad had been screaming fans in the audience at home and even some away games. They had never quite made the mark. A shame.

“Yes, not much of a choice. Plus, the Metros are an Original Six franchise, which counts for something. If we could make change here, that would make a bigger wave than if it was a less historically impressive team. And there was you, of course.”

“Me? Oh, fuck, not you, too.”

Petrov snorted. “What? You don’t enjoy being the Asian-Canadian poster-child for hockey? I notice you feature on our website on nearly every page.”

“Yup. Me and J.J. and Sharif and Nisha—”

The eye crinkle, which had been a regular feature of this evening, returned. “—who doesn’t even work here anymore. I also noticed none of the actual LGBTQI+ staff are included.”

Shane nearly dropped his chopsticks. “We have LGBTQI+ staff?”

Petrov’s eyes widened. “Yes. To start with, we have an openly gay Ops manager.”

The Ops team was not big so it didn’t take Shane long to narrow down this to one utterly unlikely possibility based on knowing for a fact Marie-Therese was married to someone in finance, Lance was engaged to an ex-puck bunny (at least, Shane hoped she was an ‘ex’). Which left… “*Bruce?*”

“You didn’t know?”

“Bruce is gay?” Bruce was massive. Shane was a big guy, but Bruce was a foot taller and about a foot wider. His hands were the size of dinner plates. He had a *beard*.

“There’s a picture of him and his husband on his desk.”

“He’s *married?* I thought that was, like, his brother or something.”

“Shane. It’s their wedding photo.”

Shane closed his eyes, beyond mortified. He was pretty sure he’d once said ‘nice photo’ to Bruce for lack of anything else polite to say. “He doesn’t wear a wedding ring,” he said weakly.

He heard Petrov huff out a breath and when he (bravely) opened his eyes again it was to find him muffling his laughter in his napkin. He appeared near tears.

Gently, Shane laid his chopsticks down, facing this humiliation head-on. “Look. You should know, I’m basically oblivious to all social cues.”

This seemed to make Petrov laugh harder. He tried to smother himself and then muttered, peering over the edges of the napkin, “Oh, I *know*, Shane.”

“Unless someone tells me *point blank*, I’m not going to get it. You should keep that in mind with your plans for world domination. I am not reliable. I need explicit instructions.”

Petrov mopped his eyes and took a shaky breath. But a thought clearly crossed his mind, and his sense of humor was reignited, and he had to bury his face in his napkin once more, shoulders shaking. “His *brother*,” he wheezed. “Shane. His *brother*.”

Okay. Shane was a good sport. He could see the funny side. “Take your time,” he said generously, picking up another piece of sashimi.
