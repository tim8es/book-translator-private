Very occasionally, Shane had a scheduled lunch with Wiebe. In that they both brought their pre-prepared meals into work and nuked them in the player lounge microwave and took them back to his office to eat. From this Shane had learned that Wiebe ate a lot of sweet potatoes.

They talked generally about the team for a bit, then drifted with the kind of comfortable casualness Shane had never had with Theriault to Wiebe’s personal life, his wife’s latest hostile takeover, and then naturally turned to Shane’s personal life. Because he had one now and Wiebe knew about it.

“How’s Rozanov?”

Shane had been expecting this. He hadn’t brought Ilya up since the last time they’d spoken, when Wiebe had put the fear of God in him. It had felt over the last couple of weeks as if Wiebe was giving him particularly loaded looks from time to time. That might all be in his head though. A lot of things were.

“Good.” Shane said nothing else for a moment, formulating the best way to respond. A way that sounded mature, adult, put-together, a whole host of things that Shane did not feel in any way shape or form about his relationship. But fake it ‘til you make it, right? “We’ve put some plans in place, like you suggested.”

Wiebe nodded and speared a piece of chicken. “Good.”

“And he’s met my parents. Also.” Shane cleared his throat, aware that this piece of news might not meet with Wiebe’s approval. “I know you cautioned me against this, but we had dinner with Petrov.”

His coach paused to take a sip of water. “So he knows.”

Desperately, Shane tried to read a tone in that statement but came up short. He rubbed a slightly damp palm on his thigh. “Yeah. I mean. He kind of already knew. He was just being, I don’t know. Discreet.”

Wiebe ate another piece of chicken. “And how did it go?”

“It was fine. I mean, I wanted him to meet Ilya, really, because Ilya is very *not cool* about Petrov.”

“In what way?” Wiebe said in a way that suggested he very much knew exactly what way.

“In a paranoid way.”

Wiebe winced sympathetically. “Yeah. I get that.”

Shane sat up straight. Now, *this* was what he was talking about. “You get why he might be?”

“Mmm.” A very vague hint of a smile pushed up the corners of Wiebe’s mouth. “I think I can take a guess. If my secret partner was constantly around an attractive, driven, wealthy colleague who was overtly and publicly friendly with them… I might also have some concerns.”

Shane exhaled. “Yeah.” It wasn’t as if Shane didn’t see that. On paper that made sense. It was just… Ilya was Ilya. And Shane was so gone on him that it was hard to see the bigger picture.

“I guess the question is whether or not his concerns are unfounded.”

A forkful of pearl barley balanced precariously in front of Shane’s mouth for a moment. “Oh my God. Yes. Totally— they’re unfounded. And I’ve reiterated that.” He blushed and quickly swallowed the mouthful.

“Then it’s about trust.”

“Yeah.” Fuck. “Yeah. We talked about that. He says he does trust me. And I believe he does. I mean, I think it’s more the circumstances, the secrecy, that bothers him? Gets him in his head.”

At least he hoped so. He’d been putting a lot of thought into it because he thought about Ilya a lot. Thought about the things that made Ilya tick. Or, in this case, *cross. *Ilya was endlessly fascinating to Shane. Only there was no playbook for Ilya; Shane was having to write his own.

Wiebe chewed a mouthful of his own meal silently for a moment. “Okay.”

Was that a *judgy* okay? Or just an ‘okay’ okay?

Shane tried to form his anxiety into words. “Do you really think it’s bad, if Petrov knows about us?”

“I think… no, I don’t think it’s bad. I think Petrov wants his mission to succeed.” He thought about it. “Realistically, I think it would probably be *more* helpful if his star captain wasn’t in a complicated relationship with another player. The noise would surely be more detrimental than positive, if you both came out as a couple. You being gay, fine.” Wiebe waved his fork around. “I can see that spinning well. But you and Rozanov? No.”

“But you think I shouldn’t have told him?”

“I think the fewer people that know the better. Particularly if you’re not planning on coming out any time soon as a couple.” He raised his dark eyebrows at Shane, who shook his head.

“Not until Ilya has some form of permanent residency. And not, like, immediately.” There was still Ilya’s dad to consider. The Russia of it all.

And whether or not Ilya was planning on staying in Boston. One of the thoughts he hadn’t voiced, naturally, was that if Ilya stayed with the Raiders – Montreal’s historically biggest competitor – the ‘noise’ about their relationship would probably be overwhelmingly negative. Almost any other team would be better.

But one day. Right? It didn’t need to be super public or anything. But maybe he’d like to walk down a street and not worry if their hands accidentally touched.

“The Raiders are performing better,” was Wiebe’s next comment.

Shane eased out a breath. “Yeah. They are. Now. He, ah. Came out to them. Before.”

Wiebe’s fork drooped in his hand. “You’re shitting me.”

“It was before we got back together.” Shane shook his head. He still couldn’t really believe it. “Caused a bit of a stir.”

“How bad?”

Shane was warmed by Wiebe’s genuine concern. “Well, it was nothing like here.” Because there was *nowhere* like here. The Metros were a unicorn of a team. “His— Marlow and he have been on the outs. They’ve reconciled since. Kind of.” Could it be a reconciliation when neither of them had acknowledged there had been an issue? Who knew? Ilya apparently wasn’t going to poke that bear, that was for certain. “So, it’s been getting better. I think his team needed time to process. Not that anyone’s talking about it.”

“Sounds shit.”

“He’s been pretty miserable.”

“I can imagine.” Wiebe ate another piece of chicken, with a cube of sweet potato. “Brave of him.”

“It was, yes.” Shane found a bittersweet smile. It had been brave.

Insane, though. That fucking Green Card couldn’t come soon enough.

“Neither. Right.”

“God. I always get turned around down here. Everything looks the same.”

Shane struggled to imagine *how* this Code of Conduct would be rolled out across the teams. Maybe Vancouver. But Toronto? Dallas Kent had *not* improved. If anything, he seemed to be doubling down on being an absolute shit stain. He was constantly fighting, spending a lot of quality time in the penalty box. Shane hoped so. “It would be helpful if Ottawa was less shit, though.” A better news story.

“Where?”

“We’ll do a little announcement. Probably in the New Year. Take some photos with the GMs. Post on social media, that sort of thing.” Their eyes were hopeful. There *was* a resemblance between Alex and Petrov. He was sure of it and couldn’t believe he hadn’t noticed it before.

Shane didn’t like to say *no* to people and the hopeful Petrov-esque eyes were enough to almost make him cave. But he felt that same sickening lurch he had felt when Wiebe had alluded to his usefulness for Petrov’s cause. His face, the face of Canadian MLH DEI. Forever and ever, amen.

“Ah. No. Thanks,” he said, firmly.

Alex looked at him for a long moment. “You sure?” they asked. “It would create better engagement on socials.”

He could feel himself wavering. That prickling urge to *be good*. To do what was asked of him.

But he stuck firm. “I’m sure,” Shane said, not sounding sure at all, really certain he had disappointed them and *hating *it. “Um. Sorry. Maybe next time?”

“Okay,” Alex said easily, patting him on the arm and giving him a small smile. “Thought I’d ask, though come to think of it, if you’d been there, we’d have had to include Boodram and it would really look like one of those shitty DEI posters then.” They pulled a face and changed the subject. “Hey, I heard Crowell wants to interrogate you. Has he tried yet?”

“No,” said Shane, his heart sinking still further, this time with the inevitability of a confrontation he seemingly couldn’t avoid. “Not yet. Should I— should I reach out to him, do you think?”

“Jesus, no. Maybe he’ll forget about you.”

“You think?” Shane asked hopefully.

“Erm. No. Probably not.” Alex grimaced and switched their laptop to their other arm. “Sorry. Try not to… worry,” they said, looking doubtful that Shane was capable of this. Which showed a remarkable understanding of him as a person.

Shane had to tamp down his own irritation with this response. Talking about Ilya’s family never exactly brought out the best in Ilya. It was difficult for Shane to relate but he *wanted* to know. He wanted to understand. He wanted, in some way, to be helpful.

It was immediately better seeing each other’s drawn, unhappy faces. They both began with wide-eyed apologies.

“Again, I am very sorry for talking to Petrov about your family and financial arrangements. I really overstepped the line, and I won’t do it again.”

Ilya nodded very seriously. “I am sorry I tried to tell you who you could talk to. That’s not fair. I am—” He really struggled, mouth twisting around, “I am *glad* you have people to talk to who you can trust. Even if it’s him.”

Shane had been thinking about this now that Ilya had let him in on what really bothered him about the conversation. “Would you have felt the same way if I’d talked to my dad? Or my mom?”

“Yes,” Ilya said immediately, though he seemed surprised by his answer. “Though— maybe not in the same way? Still not good. But different. I want them to think well of me.”

Which was, in a nutshell, why Shane had been hesitant to talk to them about this. So at least they were aligned on that front. Only he’d then walked straight into Petrov’s office without that consideration. Which was some kind of weird double standard.

“Okay. And that’s because… you think it looks bad?” he asked, seeking absolute clarity. “That you’re sending all this money home to your family?”

Ilya pursed his lips and thumbed his nose. “Not bad. Irresponsible.”

“But it’s literally the opposite,” Shane protested, baffled. Ilya certainly wasn’t the first international player to support his family, nor would he be the last. And, knowing Ilya as he did now, he had no doubt he was being exceptionally generous. And probably had been for his whole career. *That* was the problem. *More, more, more*, he had said about his brother, once. Shane *also* wouldn’t have known what to do if his family were constantly asking him for money and maybe not for good reasons.

“I send money because I can’t be there, Shane.”

“And you feel bad about that. Even though no one is grateful. Or kind. And you are probably earning more money here to support them than you would over there.”

“*Yes*.” Eyes bulging, Ilya muttered something in Russian.

Shane narrowed his own eyes. “It sounds like you’re being rude about me in another language.”

“I wasn’t being rude. I wouldn’t do that.”

“I think you’re lying.”

“Oh, has your app got to the chapter on chirping, then?”

No. He was still practicing basic greetings. Russian was *hard. *“So you were being rude.”

“No, I said—” Ilya sighed. “I said you were a stubborn donkey.”

Objectively that wasn’t really funny, but the bar was currently pretty low, so Shane’s mouth twitched. “Well, that’s not *nice*, is it?” he managed.

Ilya’s mouth also twitched. “I meant it with love.”

Shane wished for maybe the thousandth time they were having this conversation face to face and not over Facetime. He wanted to kiss him. But also slightly throttle him. It was two weeks until the Christmas break, which they *still* hadn’t talked about and if his mom asked him *one more time* if Ilya would be joining them, he was going to lose his mind.

“Maybe there’s a French expression you could use on me.”

“You want me to insult you in French?” Immediately, *immediately*, Shane knew what he would say, though he chirped in French about as much as he chirped in English. And all of it vaguely wholesome. “*Tête de cochon*.”

Ilya’s face wobbled, like he was momentarily caught between laughter and tears. He settled for a lopsided smile. “That sounds nice.”

“I called you pigheaded.”

“Ah.” The smile became less lopsided and then it melted away. Ilya’s eyes lowered. “I am— I am used to being a private person,” Ilya said, choosing his words carefully. “I do not talk about my business. Not even with Sveta.”

“No?”

“No,” said Ilya thoughtfully. “Not as much as you might think.”

Shane was relieved. He privately admitted he had worried about this. He had imagined that Ilya was having cozy chats with Svetlana on the regular, talking in Russian, with in-jokes that Shane would never understand. That she would have all the insights into Ilya’s life, past and present, the way Shane didn’t. Couldn’t.

Maybe there might be parallels here, about how Ilya felt about Petrov, and how Shane felt about Svetlana. Jesus. He really hated being self-aware.

“I like talking about you,” Shane explained. “I couldn’t at all before and it’s so much better now that people know. I wish I could talk about you with more people. I want people to know the good things about you. And I want someone else to talk to when things are hard and I don’t know what to do. When I want to help you with your problems, too.”

Someone like Petrov. With the best will in the world, J.J. and Hayden were *not* the kinds of friends who would offer sensible advice about Shane’s relationship with Ilya. There was too much baggage. At least Petrov, and Wiebe, could look beyond that – and see that Shane wanted to make it work with Ilya. That this was an adult relationship, not a passing fancy somehow linked to Shane’s lust for hockey. And they had experience, more experience than a bunch of twenty-something-year-olds who had big money but very little sense.

“Is it too hard?” Ilya whispered, plainly horrified by Shane's use of the word.

“*No*. It’s not too hard. Not remotely. I think it’s normal? I mean. Long-distance relationships are hard. That’s what everyone says.” What the hell did Shane know about ‘normal’? Everything he knew about relationships he had observed from his parents, who were pretty much perfect and hadn’t been separated from each other for more than two nights Shane’s entire life. “Couples argue. And I did something that hurt you. I’m not expecting you to just be fine with it. I was *wrong. *But you— you have a tendency to stop talking to me. And I find that difficult to handle. How can I help us if you won’t talk to me?”

Ilya groaned. “I know. I’m sorry, Shane. It is a terrible thing. I get so—” He waved at his head, long fingers swirling around as if to indicate the frenetic nature of his thoughts. “So much happening and I can’t talk right. And I don’t want to say things I don’t mean. Better I say nothing, I think?”

“I’m not sure about that. I’m not sure it’s better but I understand if you can’t find the right things to say.” Shane put a pin in that; something to talk about when they weren’t so wound up. “I didn’t know you felt so guilty about not being there. I guess… I knew some stuff but I didn’t know you felt it so badly. I should have worked that out. I’m sure I’d feel the same way and my relationship with my parents isn’t so complicated.” He rubbed his face. “I— I just wanted to, I don’t know, help, in some way. Protect you. I know that’s ridiculous. You can look after yourself. But I don’t like that your brother calls you up and yells at you and demands money and gives nothing in return. You don’t deserve that. You deserve so much better from them. I basically wish you never had to speak to him, or go back there, ever again.”

“Me, either.”

*So don’t*, Shane thought, knowing it wasn’t that simple. Nothing about this situation was simple and Shane couldn’t *make* it simple. All he could do was be sympathetic. That was his only job. “I’m sorry. It’s shit.”

Ilya nodded slowly. His eyes were suspiciously shiny. “I have money,” he said quietly. “They do not. And they are… my family. My blood. It is— that is what it is.”

“Okay. Okay.” Shane drew in a deep breath and let it out slowly. “Well. Hopefully this attorney can find a solution for you for at least the financial aspects.”

“Was good idea, Shane. Thank you. And him,” Ilya said begrudgingly.

Shane reached out to touch the screen, in lieu of being able to physically touch Ilya. “Can you— I’ve been wanting to ask. Do you want to come to Ottawa with me? For Christmas?”

And now Ilya nodded semi-frantically, his heart in his eyes. His fingers touched the screen, too. “Yes. Please. I want this very much.”

He had dinner at Hayden’s and Jackie’s that Friday after practice. He arrived early enough that the kids were still awake – keyed up by the imminent arrival of Christmas. Shane was introduced to the Elf on the Shelf, a weirdly long-legged doll who was dangled before him excitedly.

“So he… watches you,” he surmised, looking down at Jade and Ruby and then up at Jackie with concern. *What the fuck?* he mouthed to her but she just rolled her eyes and grabbed the oven mitts to get something from the oven.

“He tells Santa if we’ve been good,” Ruby said eagerly, totally fine, apparently, with having some kind of spy living in their house reporting on their every move.

“But he’s *very* naughty,” Jade told Shane, delighted. He was then treated to a play-by-play of the Elf’s actions. Apparently, while they were asleep, he carried out pranks in the night. Spilling flour everywhere. Saran-wrapping the toilet seat. Leaving them semi-threatening notes. He was shown some photos of the devastation on the twins’ iPad. Both girls cackled with delight, their fingers leaving fingerprints on the screen.

“This all sounds really counter-intuitive,” was Shane’s view. Shouldn’t the Elf be setting standards of *good* behavior? Who cleaned up after this little monster?

“What does counter-intuitive mean?” Ruby asked, saying the word slowly.

“Um. Good question. It means the opposite of how something should be.”

Ruby looked none the wiser from this explanation but Shane was saved from further effort by Jade grabbing the elf from her sister. “He’s sleeping in our room tonight,” she announced, running off with him. Ruby screeched and followed bare feet slap, slap, slapping on the wooden floors. Shane heard Hayden make some kind of protesting noise as they stampeded past him.

“It was Leanne’s idea,” Jackie said through her teeth. Leanne was Hayden’s mom. An otherwise totally normal woman, from what Shane had gathered the handful of times he had met her. “Apparently, they’re all the rage. All the kids have them at school.”

“It’s super creepy—hey, buddy!” Shane bent down to pick Arthur up as he ran into the kitchen ahead of his dad. “Oh, wow, have you grown?” He heaved him into the air and held him over his head. Arthur smiled gummily down at him, dark hair falling around his face.

“He wanted to say goodnight,” Hayden said. He had Arthur’s safety blanket tossed over one shoulder and some kind of sticker stuck to his forehead. It was the nanny’s night off.

“Night, Uncle Shane,” Arthur whispered shyly.

Shane gave him a squeeze. “Night, Arthur.”

“Need a hand, babe?” Jackie asked Hayden in an oddly sweet, high-pitched way, as she did something with the potatoes in the oven dish.

“No, no, I’ve got it.” Hayden’s voice was somehow tense, his smile tight. “Where’s the fu— the *damned* elf.” Hayden looked around the kitchen, almost as if he was afraid.

“The girls have him. We need him back,” she said with heavy emphasis. “He’s got some work to do in the laundry room.”

“Yeah, yeah, I *know.*” Again, a bit of a weird vibe there, Shane thought. “Gimme my kid.” Hayden held his arms out for Arthur. “Will be with you in a few.”

Giving his godson a smacking kiss on the forehead, Shane handed Arthur back to Hayden and the two of them trotted off, Hayden running through the story choices for that evening. He was a good dad, he really was. Just the right mixture of fun and serious. He wondered if *he’d* be a good dad. Ilya definitely would be, which was a thought that just— popped up. Alarmingly.

He could feel himself getting red in the face. Things were really bad if he was making himself blush.

“Um. Can I help?” Shane asked, having completely forgotten to offer and now needing to do something with his hands.

“Sure. Could you take the salmon out? It should be done.”

Shane did. Jackie’s salmon recipes always involved baking it in little baking paper parcels with different vegetables. She added white wine and butter to hers and Hayden’s but skipped it for Shane. Tonight it looked like they were having broccolini and asparagus. It smelled amazing. “Looks great, Jacks. Thanks.”

“Oh, jeez, your rice— it’s that pouch on the side.” She began plating up the potatoes onto hers and Hayden’s plates. They were golden and crispy and well seasoned. “Can you microwave it for a minute?”

He did so and then helped finish plating up, wrapping Hayden’s in foil as he was sure to be a few minutes longer with the kids. They sat at the table without him.

“So, how are things with you and Ilya?” Jackie asked brightly once they had settled.

“Good,” he said automatically. Then remembered it hadn’t actually been *that* good. “Actually, no, I fucked up a bit this week.”

As he explained to Jackie – editing out a fair bit of detail – her face moved into a series of sympathetic winces, and occasionally she even let out an ‘ooof’. It was kind of great, actually. It was ingrained in him to consider Hayden first when it came to his close friendships but, while she was Hayden’s wife, Jackie was *also* his friend. He kind of forgot that. Judging from her responses to this conversation, she would have been a great person to talk to this week. She didn’t have the same issues with Ilya that Hayden did. She *also* was maybe a touch more in touch with her feelings.

Maybe he should get more female friends in general.

“Sounds like you muddled through that okay,” Jackie suggested kindly when he finished.

“It does?” he asked eagerly, looking for validation from any source. Always. “Anything I should have done better, you think? I mean. Apart from talking about my boyfriend’s personal life with someone else.”

“No,” Jackie said thoughtfully, neatly cutting a crispy potato in half. “And there’s no real *better.* You’re learning to talk to each other, Shane, about difficult things. Hayden and I used to have some rip-roaring arguments after we started dating. It happens.”

“Wow, really? He never mentioned.” Actually, the way Hayden had told it, it had been nothing but smooth sailing the entire time. Like fated. Destined. From dating to engaged to married in a few months. *We’re soulmates, Shane. I just know it.* It had been – honestly – revolting.

Secretly, Shane felt a little like he and Ilya were soulmates, now. But it sure as hell wasn’t smooth sailing. More like rough seas. Maybe like those boats in the North Sea on the verge of sinking at all times. Crushed by the waves coming at them from every angle.

“Oh, boy, yeah.” Jackie took a sip of her water, eyes twinkling. “Did he tell you he bought a house for us without talking to me first? Before we’d even talked about moving in together?”

Shane nearly choked. “What? Nooooo.”

Unfortunately, Hayden made his entry then, clapping his hands together with the triumph of a job well done. He was smiling. “Okay, sorry about that. Kids are down, but probably not for long. What did I miss?”

“Jackie was just telling me about the time you bought a house without discussing it with her.”

Hayden’s blue eyes widened with alarm, the smile dropping from his face. He spun on his heel toward the fridge. “I’m gonna get a beer.”

“Did you get the elf back?” Jackie asked, again with that weirdly sweet tone.

“*Fuck. *No,” Hayden spat, slamming the fridge door closed and hurrying back out of the kitchen.

Shane ate a piece of broccoli, eyeing Jackie’s expression as she placidly cut up her salmon. She *looked* fine, right? It was just her tone. “Everything okay?” he checked cautiously.

“Yes, yes, all good.” Jackie smiled at him. It was almost normal. “Looking forward to the last roadie before Christmas?”

“I mean, kinda. Should be some— no, really, I just unloaded on you, what’s going on?”

Jackie was quick to brush it off. She took a sip of her water. “It’s just a small thing. It’s fine, Shane. It’s really no big deal. It’ll blow over.”

“Okay. Okay,” Shane repeated, a little embarrassed to have pushed and been dismissed. Even though he’d been right, there *was* something off.

Maybe Jackie felt the same way about him, that he was Hayden’s friend first. She probably had her own friends to talk to, too.
