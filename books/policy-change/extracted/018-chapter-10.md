## **Chapter 10**

Shane had been studying his team dynamics from more angles than he usually did. Before he’d started on this goddamn journey of self-actualization, he just focused on who was good at hockey, who needed a little more correcting, and who simply wasn’t worth his time, which was a shitty truth but it was a truth. There were twenty-three men in the Metros roster and he only had so much time.

Besides, Theriault had agreed with him.

But now Shane was looking at his team through a wholly new perspective which – ironically – was pretty similar to the old one just with new layers: who was a good person, who needed a little more correcting, and who simply wasn’t worth his time. Despite his hopes, that latter category did exist. He just didn’t know what to do about it yet.

There were maybe two people on the team that Shane could place in the ‘good’ bucket and that was, inexplicably, Peetie and himself. And he wasn’t always totally sure about himself – acknowledging he was gay didn’t make him some kind of diversity and inclusion savant. It just meant he had a bit of self-awareness now.

The ones who needed a little more correcting were a much larger bucket and that bucket was qualified by a willingness to be corrected or to open their minds. Things had settled a bit since the upset of Anderson’s retirement and Shane getting the C. There were certainly many guys in the team who respected him now, some who even *liked *him – Hayden and J.J. and the remaining rookies of the last four seasons. Sure, maybe he winced internally when J.J. used cocksucker as a derogatory term. Maybe he lightly chastised the rookies for sexist language. But they were otherwise willing to respect his opinion and would listen when he said ‘hey, maybe don’t wonder out loud if Deniz from catering gives good head, maybe that’s an inside-only thought’.

But those two buckets combined did not a whole roster make. In fact, it was barely a third.

Once Shane would have said that the heart of that problem, the main challenge when it came to ‘alignment’ to Shane’s vision for the team and Petrov’s values, was Comeau. Comeau was everything that Shane was not. Shane was well aware that his social abilities, or lack thereof, made him less appealing as a leader to some of the more outgoing, charismatic members of the team. Guys like Comeau, guys who threw money down at the bar and took the rookies out clubbing and acted as their wingmen on nights out and talked shit about *liberal-agendas* and *woke culture* and generally behaved as if they were God’s gift to mankind.

And normally Comeau was loud. Loud with his opinions and his jokes and his *let’s fucking do this, fellas. *It was the cliched kind of loud. He repeated things he’d heard without much thought about their impact. He was a white guy in a white sport and he had never been affected by any of the diversity issues that people like J.J. and Shane faced on a day-to-day basis and for most of his career that had been fine. No one had challenged his thinking.

But. That talk about trans people and Elaine and Elaine’s kid had really shaken him up. He’d been noticeably *quiet* after that seminar.

After, Shane had checked in with his moles about the team chat he wasn’t included in and been sent a single screengrab.

Apparently there had been no more conversation after that and later Shane had observed a noticeable chill between the two. It was J.J. who told him that Comeau and Elaine *had* gone on a few dates the previous year, but she’d broken up with him when things had started to get serious. The fact that Comeau had kept this pretty damn quiet was unheard of. Typically, his women were paraded around like it was a fucking dog show.

So Shane gently, almost reverently, placed Comeau into the middle bucket. The *could be corrected* bucket. And pondered what to do instead about Drapeau, who *was* still a problem, before remembering that, actually, there were people he could talk to about this now.

Shane had regular one-to-ones with Wiebe and they were increasingly comfortable. Nothing like the ones he used to have with Theriault where he’d sort of stood to attention the whole time, like a soldier on parade. That’s not to say Shane hadn’t liked that. Being in a hockey team was kind of like being in the army and Shane and Theriault had been working together for a few years so they both knew each other and Shane *knew* how to manage him. There had been some pleasure in that, weird as it was to say.

But Wiebe had now got a leather couch put in his office and this big beanbag and sometimes they had informal leadership team meetings in there, all squeezed in like they were back in Juniors and half the size. He had a fridge filled with protein drinks and snacks. And fruit – which Shane really appreciated because fruit was always allowed.

Shane sat on the couch, eating an apple, and stared out the window. “What’s your experience with team dynamics?”

Wiebe was leaning back on his chair, studying the whiteboard where they had mapped out next week’s game plan. He had one of those squeezy stress balls in his hand, a freebie from some supplier who wanted to sell them something. “Maybe you’ll have to narrow that down for me.”

“You played on different teams. Detroit and the Scouts, right?” Wiebe had been moved around a reasonable amount for only being in the MLH for eleven seasons. In between the stint with Detroit he’d been sent down to the AHL – not uncommon, exactly, for a mid-range player. “Then Chicago.”

“What an unsurprisingly good recall you have of my career, Shane,” Wiebe said drily.

Shane shrugged because of course he would have done his research, which he supposed was Wiebe’s point. “Were the rooms very different?”

“Oooooh, yeah.” Wiebe tossed the ball into the air and caught it a couple of times. “Chalk and cheese. Let’s see. Detroit. Captain was Storky. Stan Sorkin. Rock. Absolute rock. Couldn’t phase him. Humble. Wicked sense of humor. Took absolutely no shit from anyone. Room was fun, for the most part. Relaxed unless, you know, you were giving him shit.” He sighed kind of nostalgically. “The Scouts. Fuck. Oskar Krajnc. Absolute machine. Controlled. Smart as hell. But,” Wiebe laughed and looked at Shane, laugh lines in full display, “literally dead behind the eyes. Zero emotion. Terrifying. I mean, for me, at the time. Coming from Detroit, that room was *silent.* Took me a while to understand him. He wanted you to work hard and own your mistakes and get better. Not sure I ever delivered.”

“Really different.”

“Totally. And… Chicago was also different. Chicago was— not great. Clarke is an asshole.”

Shane inclined his head. “I’ve heard that.”

Wiebe pulled his feet off his deck and sat up, resting his elbows on the table so he could massage his temples. “He’s actually the reason I retired when I did.”

“Fuck, really?”

“Fuck, really. The most toxic locker room I have ever belonged to. Intense bullying, from the captain, from the As, from management. Even the fans were pretty toxic. There was incredible pressure on the team. The stress was unbelievable. If you didn’t put in two hundred percent you were basically ostracized. I hated it. Emily hated who I was when I was there. Barely saw the kids and even when I did I was so burned out I was a shitty dad.”

“Fuck. I’m sorry.”

“It’s part of the reason I was so taken in by Petrov’s offer. The idea that we could actually *do* something was, is, still pretty inspiring.”

“But you said the other two teams were kind of positive.”

“Yes,” Wiebe said slowly, “in a comparative sense. No team is perfect. None of those teams would have accepted a gay or bi player. In fact, it would have been worse in Storky’s team. It would have ruined the fun.” The way Wiebe said ‘fun’ was a little bitter.

Shane was maybe oversensitive to this kind of chat now so was really, really aware of what his face was doing and couldn’t work out how to make his face look appropriately sympathetic and not, you know, terrified.

“Krajnc would have been *is not hockey, do not give a shit*.” And Wiebe laughed again, this time more tiredly. He rubbed a hand over his face.

Aware that he had brought up quite a lot of disappointing memories for Wiebe, Shane approached his real reason for the subject, “Um. Follow-up question.”

“Sure.”

“Did they have different approaches for when the team wasn’t gelling? If there were maybe one or two key troublemakers who were affecting the chemistry or something.”

Wiebe raised his eyebrows. “They talked to their coaches, Shane.”

Shane rolled his eyes, which was the sort of shit that would have got him punished by Theriault. “What do you think I’m doing now?”

The squishy stress ball flew at Shane’s face. He caught it. Of course. “Who are we talking about?”

“Can we talk in hypotheticals?”

“I’m sure I could guess.”

“Hypotheticals. Come on.”

“Okay. Hypotheticals.” He tapped his fingers on his desk. “So Chicago crushed anyone who tried to step out of place, so let’s not use them as an example. But in Detroit… I suppose we did a lot of team building exercises. Not just drinks after games where alcohol might be a hindrance to behavior but charity work and family things.”

“We don’t do much of that. That’s probably on me.” Not having a family – not in the wife-and-kids sense – Shane hadn’t really ever prioritized it.

“You focus on other equally valuable things, Shane. And the charity thing isn’t actually your responsibility.” Wiebe reached for his in-tray and began shuffling things around. “Come to think of it, I got a memo a week or so— okay, so maybe two months ago.” He winced as he pulled out a sheet of paper. “About this very thing. Our new head of HR has been reviewing where we should put our efforts.”

He handed the sheet over to Shane who had to admit had some qualms about the kinds of charity work Petrov might consider relevant but was pleasantly surprised to see it was all quite manageable and community based. Children’s hospitals and food banks and school reading programs. Nothing out of the norm.

“You look surprised.”

“Yeah, thought it might be— I don’t know. That trans charity or something. Not that it would be *bad…*” Shane, grimly, made himself stop talking. He thought of Elaine’s wet eyes. And the vaguely hunted look on his team’s faces.

Wiebe picked up another squishy stress ball. This time it was bright pink. “I think they know our limits.”

“Maybe next year,” Shane said, meaning it.

“Maybe.” Wiebe slid the memo back into the in-tray pile. “I’ll have a think. About other things we can do.”

“Thanks, Coach.”
