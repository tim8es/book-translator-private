Duke walked toward the Batcave with steady steps, a faint sense of disappointment settling in his chest. It wasn’t the first time. Far from it. By now, he was used to finding Tim buried in work. Nothing lit him up quite like a complicated, tangled case.

Out of all of them, Tim was the one who preferred working alone—digging through evidence, cross-checking files, disappearing into reports and patterns—while the others favored patrolling the streets.

In that, Tim was a lot like Bruce.

And on some days, Duke hated that trait. More than once, he’d had to drag Tim out of the Cave by sheer force just to get him into fresh air. But on the other hand, Tim was the one who closed cold cases. The one who brought answers to victims who had long since lost hope in Gotham’s justice.

Duke stepped into the Cave and stopped at the staircase, leaning his elbows against the railing as he stared down at Tim’s hunched silhouette in front of the computer screens. The fact that Tim hadn’t even noticed someone entering said more than enough.

For a while, Duke simply stood there, watching him from behind, thinking about what about how many cases Tim managed to close this time, then he pushed off the railing and headed down the steps.

“Hey, Tim!” he called out loudly. Tim flinched at the sound of his voice, turning in his chair to face him and blinking up at Duke with tired eyes. Duke stopped a few steps away, crossing his arms over his chest and looking down at him with fake disappointment.

“We were going to Batburger. You didn’t forget, did you?” He arched a brow, trying to hide the faint smirk tugging at his mouth. Judging by Tim’s rumpled appearance and the way he looked slightly disoriented, Duke was sure he had absolutely forgotten.

“No,” Tim replied without hesitation. Duke snorted at the blatant lie, his gaze shifting toward the screen, and narrowed his eyes, looking at the familiar name.

“Is that the shooting case with a young victim? I thought the police didn’t have any leads. No witnesses.”

“Oh, they did,” Tim muttered, spinning back toward the monitors and immediately resuming his rapid typing. “Someone just paid them off.”

Duke’s brow lifted. “Paid them off?”

“Mm-hm. I accidentally found a surveillance camera in one of the cars where the shooting happened.”

“Accidentally?” Duke echoed, skeptical. Tim ignored that completely.

“And it turns out those idiots didn’t even bother to check everything properly. Embarrassing, honestly.”

“If they took bribes, they probably didn’t investigate at all,” Duke shrugged. There was a familiar sting of sympathy in his chest—for the victim, for the mother who had lost her child. But this was Gotham. They had seen worse. All they could do was try to make the streets a little safer for the innocent people still trapped in them.

“I’m sure Gordon will be thrilled to hear the case has new evidence, and that there are a few corrupt rats in his department,” Tim said with clear satisfaction, still typing without pause. Duke remained where he was, waiting for him to finish.

“By the way, why are you here?” Tim asked suddenly, but didn’t stop working even for a second.

“We were going to Batburger,” Duke repeated patiently, tilting his head slightly as he studied Tim’s profile. He saw the exact moment the excitement drained out of him, the spark from the case vanishing like someone had flipped a switch.

“Well, I hope you have a good time,” Tim replied flatly. Duke frowned.

“You promised you’d come with us.”

“I did?”

“You did,” Dick confirmed, stepping closer to the desk and leaning back against it, bracing himself on his hands as he watched Tim carefully. Something felt off. “Last time I asked, you said you’d join us next time.”

“Duke…” Tim exhaled wearily, finally pushing himself away from the screen. And now the face looking back at Duke wasn’t the mask he was used to seeing. It wasn’t Timothy Drake. It wasn’t Red Robin—the one always five steps ahead, confident in every plan and contingency.

No. This was just Tim.

Tired. Alone. Like he was trying not to take up space he didn’t think belonged to him.

Duke so rarely got to see him like this that he softened immediately. He dropped into the chair across from him and leaned forward, looking straight into Tim’s exhausted eyes, dark circles stark beneath them.

“Tim, what’s going on?”

He wasn’t going to back off. And Tim could clearly see the resolve in him.

Tim dragged a hand through his slightly overgrown hair, the strands brushing against his neck, then leaned back in his chair and stared up at the dark ceiling of the Cave, avoiding Duke’s burning gaze.

“It’s just…I don’t belong with them. With you.”

“Tim…”

“I’m not part of the family, Duke.” Tim pressed his lips together and shook his head. “Bruce made that clear. So did the others.”

“Even me, Tim?” Duke hadn’t meant to let the hurt slip into his voice, but he couldn’t hide it. He felt it every time Tim pushed him away. “You’re my brother. Maybe we’re not related by blood. But you’re my family. And I hope…I hope I get to be yours too.”

“Even if the whole world hates me?” Tim gave a faint, crooked smile, but the sadness in his eyes only deepened, and it only strengthened Duke’s resolve.

“Even if the whole world hates you,” Duke answered without hesitation. “You’re my brother. And nothing is going to change that.”

A quiet, almost fragile silence settled between them. Duke didn’t rush him. He let Tim sit with his thoughts for a while. They stayed like that: Tim staring up at the ceiling, unfocused, lost somewhere deep in his own head, and Duke simply watching him, offering secure and wordless support.

“I’m still not going,” Tim said at last. Duke knew, this time, he wouldn’t get him to come along. And he didn’t try. Because all he wanted was to spend time with his brother.

“Great. Then I’ll stay here too,” Duke said, leaning back against the chair, crossing his legs, a grin tugging at his face. “Got any soul-stirring cases on the table?”

“There’s a haunted house. People keep disappearing,” Tim replied, arching a brow, his expression unreadable as he studied Duke, who shrugged casually.

“Haunted house, huh? Not the strangest thing we’ve dealt with.”

“You sure?” Tim asked suddenly. There was a faint edge of worry in his voice. When Duke gave him a questioning look, Tim hesitated. He drew in a slow breath and reached for one of the folders on the desk, flipping it open a little too quickly. The papers inside shifted as his fingers lingered on the edge of the file, like he needed something to do with his hands. “I mean,” he began, not quite looking at Duke. “You could go with the others. You don’t have to stay here. With me.”

“I don’t have to,” Duke nodded, noticing Tim’s shoulders slump just a fraction. “But I want to, Tim. So let’s get to work.”

At that, Tim’s lips stretched into a small, genuine smile. A spark ignited in his eyes—the same spark Red Robin had when tackling a difficult case. But he was still Tim. Unlike the mask he wore at night, Duke could see the sincerity beneath it clearly, the quiet honesty Tim had always treated like a weakness.

“Thanks, Duke,” Tim said softly, a little of the tension leaving his shoulders. Duke gave him an easy, reassuring look.

“You can always count on me, Tim.”

The wind began to pick up, carrying the sharp scent of dust through the courtyard. Sand lifted from the ground, swirling into a thin, stinging haze that blurred the edges of the palace. One by one, the remnants of Ra’s men who were still on their feet started to retreat. They backed away from the courtyard without a word, without a backward glance, not even sparing a moment for the body of their master lying in the dust as it slowly grew cold.

The king had fallen by the hand of his own pawn.

But what they all had forgotten was that there had always been a gamemaster behind the chessboard.

Now all of them were staring into his blood-hungry eyes, where victory flickered openly, where triumph glimmered without shame. His hand remained extended toward Tim, possessively waiting for him to accept it. Tim kept his glance vacant, face blank, not even a muscle twitching.

Duke couldn’t read him. Couldn’t even begin to guess what was happening inside his head. Tim had always been harder to understand than the rest of them. He revealed only what he chose to reveal. No more. No less. And Duke had been one of the few allowed a glimpse beyond the surface.

But even that wasn’t enough now.

He didn’t recognize the brother standing in front of him.

“Come on, Timothy,” Klarion coaxed, curling his fingers in a beckoning motion, his dangerous eyes boring into Tim. Tim didn’t blink. He stood over Ra’s body, the dagger still clenched in his hand, its blade dark and wet from blood. “It’s time to go.”

And to everyone’s horror, Tim stepped forward without sparing any of them a glance. Duke’s stomach twisted hard, a tight, sick knot forming as he watched Tim’s slow, lifeless stride. Duke ground his teeth. He was usually the restrained one. He never attacked out of bitterness or impulse. He preferred to analyze a situation before making a move, but right now, there was no time for that.

He couldn’t let Tim slip away again.

Duke had already failed him once. On that rooftop. The moment Tim had asked him not to look for him. When, instead of pushing, instead of asking the right questions, Duke had left him alone with his own thoughts. Because if Tim had trusted him even a little more, Duke would have believed him. He would have helped.

Because Tim Drake was never wrong.

Tim Drake was never—

“Stop.”

Bruce’s voice was firm and commanding. Everyone froze, eyes snapping toward him. Even Tim halted, though he remained turned sideways to them, his face hidden behind strands of dark hair that the wind kept tugging loose, lifting and twisting as if trying to bare him to the world.

But Duke noticed the faint relief on Bruce’s face. He took several steps forward. His brows were drawn tight, his jaw set, but there was determination in his eyes. Duke held his breath, painfully aware that the others were watching too; the air was thick with tension: weapons were raised, fingers tight on triggers and hilts, yet none of it mattered.

What could they do against the Lord of Chaos?

His power existed far beyond their reach, beyond training or preparation. And Duke cursed himself again, bitterly, for not reaching out to Zatanna when he still had the chance.

“The deal.”

Bruce’s gaze moved from Tim, who flinched, just barely, a reaction so small it might have gone unnoticed, except that everyone saw it, to Klarion, who tilted his head with open curiosity, as if watching an entertaining performance unfold. Maybe that was all this was for him, Duke thought distantly. A spectacle.

After all, the Dark Knight stood before him without his mask, stripped of his identity. It was the dream of every Gotham villain—to see Batman on his knees, pleading for mercy for his son. And Bruce pleaded, not even trying to hide the desperation in his voice beneath that firm steel.

“Take me instead of my son.”

In that moment, everything went still.

Uncertainty gave way to shock. Jason slowly lowered his gun, his grip loosening as if the weight of it had suddenly become too much to bear. Beside him, Dick looked like a statue carved from stone, frozen in place as though Bruce’s words hadn’t fully reached him yet—like his mind refused to accept what he had just heard.

Damian’s face remained calm, unnervingly so. But unlike the others, his predatory gaze never left Tim. His shoulders were tight, drawn like a coiled spring, and though his sword had lowered, it was clear it could rise again in an instant.

And Tim…

Tim finally turned to face them.

In his brother’s cold eyes was something achingly familiar—something vile in its familiarity. That quiet, poisonous acceptance. The look that said I’ve given up. The look that said it’s fine. But Duke didn’t believe it.

And there it was again—that small, sad smile, the one Tim used to hide everything that hurt.

“It’s fine, Duke,” Tim said every time, slipping past questions, dodging concern. He said it easily, with such calm certainty that Duke had believed him at first.

They all had.

They had forgotten who Tim was.

Tim Drake changed masks like gloves, because that’s how he had been taught to survive. He had never been allowed to be himself. From an early age, he was thrown to the sharks, taught to spot weaknesses and manipulate them, taught to smile and pretend.

And they were the ones who failed to see it. Blinded by their own anger and pride. By their grief and desperation. They betrayed their brother, the one who had always fought for them without asking for anything in return.

Nothing, except belonging.

“It’s fine, Bruce,” Tim rasped. The sound of his voice made Duke’s hands curl into fists. His chest rose sharply, anger burning hot and fast, his muscles screaming to move, to grab his brother and shake him until the lie shattered.

Because nothing was fine.

Nothing was fine.

“Tim,” Bruce whispered, taking another step forward, his face heavy with grief, eyes dark with the weight of a father’s fear of losing his son. “I won’t let you go with him. I won’t allow you to leave. I—” He cut himself off abruptly, jaw tightening as if the words were tearing their way out of him. He drew a breath that sounded too shallow, too thin. “I can’t lose you again…”

“How touching,” Klarion chimed in, folding his arms across his chest, one finger tapping idly against his chin. His pupils swallowed the color of his eyes entirely, leaving them pitch-black, bottomless. A shiver of instinctive dread ran through Duke’s spine.

They all felt it. As if something ancient and wrong had stepped into the world with them. As if the Devil himself had come to watch their downfall.

“But the deal between Timothy and me has already been sealed,” Klarion continued lightly. “His soul is mine now,” his smile widened, dangerous and pleased. “A soul broken by those he trusted most.”

He moved without a sound, the ground barely stirring beneath his feet, and came to a sudden stop directly in front of Tim. In the next instant, his fingers shot up, clamping around Tim’s jaw. Sharp nails dug into flesh, forcing his head to turn away from the others and tilt up toward those black, merciless eyes, eyes that promised punishment before words could even form.

“To serve me without question,” Klarion murmured, looming over him, “a soul must be bound by oath.”

Tim didn’t resist. Didn’t flinch. He didn’t even lift the dagger still clenched in his hand. He remained like a puppet in Klarion’s grip, utterly drained, every movement weighed down by exhaustion and quiet surrender.

Klarion tilted his head toward Bruce, his grip tightening. Tim let out a faint, broken whimper—barely more than a breath—and everybody around them tensed, instinct screaming to tear Klarion away from their brother.

“So tell me,” Klarion finished softly, not hiding his amusement. “Why would I trade a soul that already belongs to me for one that would never truly kneel?”

“I’m willing to make a deal, Klarion,” Bruce answered through clenched teeth. Tim tried to turn his head toward him, but Klarion wouldn’t allow it, keeping him locked in place with an iron grip.

“You still don’t understand, do you?” Klarion’s voice turned syrupy, almost sing-song, and Duke felt his face twist with disgust. He wanted to hit him. To wipe that pleased smile off his face and take Tim back by force.

But they were powerless.

In games where magic ruled the board, without preparation or countermeasures, there was nothing they could do.

Even Batman had limits.

“I can’t make a deal with you,” Klarion continued calmly. “With any of you.”

Suddenly, thunder violently cracked overhead. The sound punched through Duke’s ears, leaving a high ringing behind, and Klarion’s words echoed in his skull long after they were spoken.

“And do you know why?” Those blood-hungry eyes slid back to Tim, still trapped in that unyielding hold. “Because my deal with Timothy includes more than just returning all the memories I took from him,” Klarion said in a delighted voice. “It also includes a promise that I will never trade his soul for anyone else.”

He laughed then—wild, unhinged, and laced with a cold, mocking edge. The sound slithered through the clearing, heavy and poisonous, snuffing out the last flicker of hope.

Jason’s shoulders slumped. Dick stepped closer to Bruce, placing a steady hand between his shoulder blades, his dark gaze fixed on Klarion with quiet fury. Damian moved sideways, circling, trying to come up behind the Klarion in a desperate attempt to save his brother.

What else did they have left? What could they do, except fight?

“What does it feel like,” Klarion went on mockingly, “to realize your own fledgling outplayed every single one of you?”

He leaned closer to Tim, invading what little space remained, peering straight into his eyes as if devouring him from the inside out.

“Oh, Timothy,” he purred, unable to hide his glee. “We’re going to have so much fun together. After all, Ra’s al Ghul was right. Your mind is your greatest weapon,” his smile widened. “Unfortunately for him, he couldn’t claim it. But don’t worry.” His fingers tightened, possessive. “You’re mine now. Your body. Your mind. Your soul. Completely mine.”

The wind began to drive the sand harder, higher. It scraped along the ground and climbed into the air, grains hissing against armor and skin. Above them, the clouds seemed to sag lower, swollen and heavy, as if they could sense the danger below. Shadows stretched unnaturally, crawling outward and curling around the two figures facing each other.

“And you will make me more powerful,” because all that mattered to him was power. Power, and chaos.

Damian’s silhouette slipped soundlessly behind him. Duke’s heart lurched as he watched the blade rise in a single smooth motion, aimed straight for Klarion’s throat—

—and then Damian froze.

His cloak snapped and twisted in the wind, fabric whipping violently, but the sword halted in midair as if caught in an unseen grip. Damian’s entire body locked in place. His eyes burned with fury, the glow intensifying until it nearly matched the vicious shade Duke had seen in Jason’s gaze when rage fully took him.

Klarion didn’t look alarmed at all. He turned his head slowly, unhurried, still holding Tim, then pushed him back to arm’s length without loosening his grasp. His gaze lingered on Damian, inspecting him with mild interest before he spoke, bored and dismissive.

“No, no. That won’t do,” Damian was hurled backward. He barely managed to stay upright, boots carving lines through the sand as his sword bit into the ground to stop his fall. He stared at Klarion with naked hatred.

“Don’t look at me like that,” Klarion added lazily. “You started it.”

The truth settled over them with merciless clarity.

They were all powerless. No matter what they tried, no matter how hard they fought, they could not save Tim.

“Hey, Dami,” the hoarse voice cut through the moment, pulling every eye toward it. Despite the claws digging into his cheeks hard enough to draw blood, Tim forced a small smile. He looked over Klarion’s shoulder, straight at Damian. “It’s okay. It’s okay, I promise.”

“Akhi…” Damian shook his head, clearly refusing to accept it. Tim spoke again, more firmly this time.

“You’re a great Robin. Don’t forget that, Dami, okay?” Tim said, and tears shone in his blue eyes, his voice wavering despite his effort to keep it steady. “We made a deal. And if you don’t keep your word, I’ll never forgive you.”

“What the hell!” Jason surged forward, guns snapping up, both aimed squarely at Klarion’s head. Klarion only lifted an eyebrow at that. Jason bared his teeth, jerking the barrels with a sharp, furious motion. “One deal after another. When are you going to stop playing these games, Tim!”

“Jay…” Dick tried to step in, moving away from Bruce, who still stood frozen in place as if lost in thought, but Jason shook his head violently, cutting him off and throwing him a blazing look.

“Not now, Dickwing! Not now!” His voice cracked, raw with desperation. “Think about it. Just think!” His voice cracked, raw and frantic, teetering on the edge of unhinged. Even Bruce tore his gaze from Tim, frowning at Jason, taking in the fury and anguish etched across his face. “He planned this so far ahead. Every move. Every step. Even the things that were damn near impossible to predict—especially after he lost his memory. He knew Ra’s plans. He knew about Klarion. He even anticipated you, Bruce, made sure you couldn’t swap his soul instead, because this gremlin…this idiot…has a self-sacrificing death wish the size of Gotham!” Jason slashed the air with one gun, his fingers trembling on the trigger, his eyes burning that toxic swamp-green.

“All this time, everything’s been going exactly how he wanted. All this time!” His voice cracked, rough and ragged, but beneath the fury there was a fragile, stubborn hope. “So…doesn’t that mean he left something behind? Something. Anything. A way out?”

And his words…despite the sheer madness coiled inside them, settled over everyone like a weight, forcing each of them to replay Tim’s every step. Every choice. Every sentence he had spoken, and every word he had swallowed instead.

“You said it yourself, Jay,” Dick murmured quietly, stepping closer and placing a steadying hand over his brother’s wrist, guiding it down along with the gun barrel that was still aimed at the Devil’s silhouette. “He’s a self-sacrificing idiot. He didn’t leave us any way to interfere with this deal,” Dick’s gaze flicked to Tim, who listened in silence, lips pressed into a thin, restrained line. “You’ve always protected us, haven’t you, Baby Bird? Always put our lives ahead of your own.”

Duke swallowed hard, his throat tight as he lowered his head and stared at his boots, now dusted with sand and blood. It was true. Tim had never cared much about his own life. That kind of detachment—this quiet disregard—was one of the warning signs of burnout or depression. And Duke could see it now. He could feel the weight of his own mistakes, the countless times he had left Tim alone, simply because Tim had asked him to. God, he’d been an idiot.

“But I promised I’d always catch you, Tim,” Dick said through a tearful smile, never taking his eyes off him. Blood was drying on Tim’s face, dark and cracked against his pale skin. “And you always said you believed me. Believed I’d catch you, Baby Bird.”

Believed.

The word seemed to hang in the air, heavier than the wind, heavier than the storm pressing down on them, mocking them without mercy.

“What do we need to do, Tim?” Dick stepped forward, fists clenching and unclenching at his sides, leather creaking softly with the motion. In every pair of eyes there was the same fragile spark—hope. Hope that Tim Drake had a plan.

“Enough!” Klarion barked at last, releasing Tim and taking a sharp step back. Tim immediately grabbed at the torn, reddened skin on his cheeks, wiping the blood away with his sleeve before doubling over in a dry cough that scraped painfully out of him. Klarion stared at him, his expression darkening. The smile vanished. The boyish mischief, the careless delight was gone. What remained was something colder. “You don’t have a choice, Timothy. We both know that.”

Dark magic pooled over his palm, thick and lightless, as if a piece of the abyss had been dragged into his hand. The air around it warped, swallowing what little light remained. Duke’s skin prickled instantly. A cold and invasive tremor ran through him, and his throat tightened as though unseen fingers had wrapped around it, squeezing the breath from his lungs.

“You’re not the one they lost anymore,” Klarion continued, glaring at him darkly. “Forget them and accept your fate, Timothy.”

And in that moment, Duke’s eyes widened.

“And if I stop being myself, then whatever’s left isn’t the thing you’re meant to save.”

The memory hit him like a strike to the chest.

Could it be—

No. No. No.

No, he couldn’t cling to something that small.

He couldn’t.

But what if…

“Tim!” Duke shouted, his voice scraping against the dryness in his throat, worn thin by dust and exhaustion, but Tim immediately turned toward him. That same empty look met Duke’s eyes—the one Tim had always used like a shield, a polished surface no one could break through. Duke didn’t know what to grab onto, didn’t know if there was anything left to reach, but he hoped.

God, he hoped.

“Tim, that night. On the roof. Do you remember what you told me?” He held his breath, lungs burning, as he watched Tim’s brows slowly draw together. A faint crease formed between them. A shadow of uncertainty passed over his face like a cloud drifting across glass.

That’s it.

That’s it!

Duke felt his whole body start to hum, a tremor running through his limbs as adrenaline flooded his bloodstream. His pulse pounded in his ears, drowning out the wind, the storm, everything but this moment. He clung to it desperately, like it was the only solid ground left beneath his feet.

“The night we saw each other for the last time,” Duke pressed on, swallowing hard, the taste of grit and copper on his tongue. “Do you remember what you said to me, Tim?”

“I…” Tim began, the word catching in his throat. He stopped abruptly, a flicker of unease crossing his eyes—confusion that didn’t belong on Tim’s face. His pupils widened, and he lifted his head sharply, dark strands of hair falling into his face, half-shadowing the tension in his expression.

But Duke still saw it. That fractured, glass-bright look.

“…I don’t remember.”

“This has nothing to do with the deal,” Klarion shot Duke a furious glare. Duke forced himself to relax, drawing in a slow breath before asking calmly.

“Doesn’t it? Care to remind us of what your deal was?”

“I return the memories for a soul,” Klarion began, lazily flicking his hand as if the matter bored him, but Tim cut him off.

“No. That was the second time when you said I would remember the deal after you returned my memories. The real deal was made earlier, when you offered it to…another me. I remember that.”

“That does not change the fact that you agreed to it, Timothy,” Klarion hissed venomously, his black eyes gleaming as they locked onto Tim. Tim looked lost inside his own mind, as though he were reaching for something just beyond his grasp and failing to hold on.

“No, it doesn’t. But the true deal includes the return of all my memories in exchange for my soul. A soul you cannot exchange for another one.”

“Idiot,” Jason muttered under his breath, and Duke couldn’t help but agree with him. But he also heard what he had been hoping—desperately—to hear.

“If he returned all your memories, then answer me, Timothy: what exactly did you say to me on that rooftop?” Duke pressed, feeling their chance hovering somewhere close, fragile but real. The others tensed, staring at Tim in anticipation. Tim looked as though his voice had been stolen, as if the words refused to form no matter how hard he tried.

“I’m not sure…” he drew out slowly, locking eyes with Duke before adding, in a softer, almost hesitant whisper, as if afraid of Duke’s reaction. “I barely remember anything about you…”

Duke’s chest constricted as if it had been ripped open and doused in fire and blood. Those words were Tim’s salvation…and at the same time, Duke’s worst nightmare.

“Charlatan,” Damian bared his teeth, still standing behind Klarion as he raised his sword once more. Steel and resolve burned in his eyes; he was clearly prepared to fight to the death if it came to that, because Tim’s soul wasn’t bound by magic. Klarion couldn’t go against Fate.

Jason immediately lifted his guns again, fury undisguised. “Step away from my brother, asshole.”

“Warm-up’s over. Time to spill blood.” Dick, still holding Slade’s sword in one hand, grabbed his escrima stick with the other.

Bruce stepped forward in silence. Darkness fell across his face, a shadow Duke had almost never seen. It was the nightmare of anyone who dared stand in the path of vengeance.

“You will pay for what you did to my son.”

“I didn’t take anything that wasn’t mine!” Black sparks crackled between Klarion’s fingers. His silhouette seemed to stretch taller, his presence swelling until the air around him felt heavy with power and danger. But Duke stood his ground, just like the others, ready to fight the Lord of Chaos if it came to that. “Timothy’s soul is mine. He agreed to the deal of his own free will!”

“You failed to uphold your end of the bargain,” suddenly, the familiar voice came from behind them. Every head swung around in alert.

Zatanna walked toward them with unwavering confidence. Her dark hair fluttered in the wind, and her sharp, piercing gaze was locked on Klarion. “We both know the contract is void,” she said evenly.

She stopped beside Duke, giving him a brief look and the faintest hint of a smile. “It’s good to see you.”

“Yeah. You too,” Duke exhaled, relief loosening something tight in his chest. He had no idea how she had found out about what was happening, especially since Bruce hadn’t contacted anyone from the League. But that question could wait.

Right now, only one thing mattered.

They were taking Tim home.

“Zatanna,” Bruce called.

The sorceress arched a brow at him. “Bruce?”

It was clear enough that, once this was over, they would have a long conversation waiting for them.

“How about we save the questions for later? We’ve got the baby bird here who needs help,” Jason said dryly, never taking his eyes off Klarion. Dick didn’t look away either, firmly holding the sword, his muscles tensed.

Zatanna gave a restrained nod and stepped toward the Lord of Chaos. His eyes burned with hunger for power. The air around him seemed to turn colder by the second, the wind rising and circling outward, keeping everyone at a distance.

Everyone except Tim. He stood close enough to grab if he tried to run, and it looked as though Klarion was holding him there, keeping him at his side like a prize he refused to surrender.

“Let the boy go, Klarion,” Zatanna said, her voice firm, the threat in it undisguised. “This will not end well for you.”

“Never!” Klarion ground out through his teeth. Thick, violent black energy crawled across his palms, coiling like a living thing, before he flung it straight at Tim. In that instant, the abyss seemed to stir in Tim’s eyes—dark, endless, and impossible to escape.

But there was no fear on his face.  He turned toward Duke, who stood frozen in horror, staring at that damn smile on his brother’s lips, and watching Tim mouth those same words, “it’s okay.”

And Duke—

Duke will kill him for that himself.

“Your soul will be mine!”

“Dleihs sih lous!” Zatanna’s spell shot forward instantly, aimed straight at the dark magic, and the air between her and Klarion ignited.

Her magic struck his in a blinding collision. Dark energy slammed into shimmering blue light, and for a split second the world seemed to fracture along that line of impact. The ground beneath them cracked with a deafening snap. Wind exploded outward in violent spirals, tearing at capes and jackets, forcing everyone back another step.

Klarion snarled, pushing harder. The darkness in his hands thickened, writhing like living smoke, laced with violet sparks that hissed as they met Zatanna’s spell.

Zatanna didn’t retreat.

Her hair whipped around her face as she lifted both hands now, her voice rising, another incantation spilling out in reverse. The blue light brightened, forming a barrier around Tim. Every time Klarion’s magic struck it, the shield rippled like glass under pressure, yet it did not shatter.

The clash became a storm.

Shadows lashed outward from Klarion’s side. Zatanna’s magic answered in arcs of silver and blue, slicing through the dark like lightning through a night sky. The temperature dropped so sharply that Duke could see his own breath, cold burning in his lungs as he refused to look away.

Tim stood at the center of it all, watching the chaos unfold with unsettling calm. As if it had nothing to do with him.

Duke tried to step toward him, but he couldn’t move.

It was like the air had turned to stone around his limbs, pinning him in place. All he could do was stand there, helpless, forced to watch.

“No! No! No!” Klarion’s roar tore through the storm of magic. In the next instant, Zatanna’s spell flared brighter, surging outward. Blue light wrapped around the dark magic, swallowing it whole. The darkness shrieked as it was compressed, unraveled, and hurled back. The backlash struck Klarion full force, throwing the Lord of Chaos several steps aside.

He steadied himself, dark eyes blazing as they locked onto the sorceress. There was no restraint left in him now—only raw fury.

“You were not supposed to interfere!” he spat. “None of you were supposed to interfere!”

“You lost this game the moment you offered Timothy that deal.” The same silver-blue energy still surrounded Zatanna, coiling around her like living light. She stepped toward Klarion slowly, raising her hand as if delivering a sentence. “In your hunger for power, you chose to defy Fate itself. And Fate does not look kindly on those who break their word.”

The wind howled one last time around them. But then, before Zatanna could finish whatever spell she was about to cast, a dark portal tore open behind Klarion.

“I’m afraid I don’t have time for this,” he said, lifting his chin with cold pride as he stepped toward the portal. But before he vanished completely, his black eyes fixed on Tim, and with undisguised hunger, he made his promise. “One day, your soul will be mine, Timothy.”

And then he was gone. Vanished without a trace, leaving behind a bitter weight in the air—unfinished business, and the sharp, metallic taste of bloodlust. Duke didn’t doubt for a second that the moment they returned home, the witch hunt would begin.

Klarion would pay for what he had done to Tim.

Duke personally wanted to tear his head from his shoulders.

“Akhi!” The alarmed cry made them all turn at once.

Tim swayed, and for a brief moment, he looked as if he were merely tired, but then his knees gave out. Damian, closest to him, caught him just before he hit the ground, lowering himself without hesitation. He didn’t care about the dirt or the blood soaking his clothes. Dropping to his knees, he cradled Tim carefully in his arms, easing his brother’s head onto his lap. With one hand, he brushed the dark strands away from Tim’s pale face. With the other, he touched his cheek, almost afraid to press too hard, whispering in a trembling voice.

“Akhi?”

“Don’t worry. It’s most likely the backlash from the broken deal. He’ll regain consciousness soon,” Zatanna said with steady confidence, though she was already kneeling beside them. She placed her palm over Tim’s chest and murmured something under her breath, words too soft and too quiet to catch. Her blue magic shimmered over Tim’s motionless body.

The others stood around them protectively in a tight circle. Like vultures ready to tear apart anyone foolish enough to come too close.

“His soul has endured a great deal,” Zatanna added with concern and sadness. At her words, Dick pressed his lips together, staring down at the ground. Duke caught the flicker of guilt on his face immediately.

Bruce lowered himself onto one knee beside Zatanna. He didn’t take his eyes off Tim’s sleeping face, as if he were afraid to blink. Afraid that this was all some fragile illusion. That he would wake up in the morning, and his son would still be lying beneath the ground.

He drew in a slow, unsteady breath, touching Tim’s cold cheek.

“This is my fault…” he whispered, grief weighing down every word.

“Bruce, this isn’t the time.” Zatanna shook her head sharply, her expression firm as she looked at him. “What matters right now is Tim. And, for the record, I have a lot of questions.”

“You have no idea. So do we,” Jason let out a rough, humorless huff, folding his arms across his chest. There was warmth in the way he looked at Tim in Damian’s arms, warmth edged with unmistakable bitterness. “He was always five steps ahead of us, with more than one ace up his sleeve. As usual, too smart for his own good. Right, Baby Bird?”

“Speaking of aces,” Duke cut in, turning his attention to Zatanna with a frown. “How did you know? How did you find us?”

For a moment, she simply looked back at him in silence. Then surprise and disbelief flickered across her face as her gaze swept over all of them, and she spoke, her voice edged with concern.

“I received a message from Tim.”

As if they didn’t have enough questions already.
