Tim couldn’t breathe. Couldn’t move. His body felt paralyzed, the cold press of the gun barrel against his skull reminding him that one wrong twitch, one misplaced breath, and Red Robin would be nothing more than a memory.

He parted his lips, trying to force out words—anything—but nothing came. His hands trembled uncontrollably, so he curled them into fists, clinging to the last fragile shards of courage left in him.

“I won’t ask again. What the hell are you doing here?” the voice of his brother cut through the silence, sharp and unforgiving, and something bitter curdled in Tim’s stomach.

No one would miss Timothy Drake.

“I…” his throat tightened as he squeezed his eyes shut, choking down the lump that threatened to suffocate him. His head throbbed, thoughts blurring into one another, collapsing into chaos. He couldn’t hold on to anything. The abyss was calling, sweet and inevitable. “I… Jason…”

And that was his mistake.

“What did you just say?” the words snapped out of Jason like a whip, raw and venomous, as if Tim had confessed to burning down all his ‘Pride and Prejudice’ editions. Tim hated himself for it. Why couldn’t he have just stayed silent? “What did you just say?!”

Heavy footsteps echoed closer, and then Jason’s boots filled his vision. The gun never wavered, now pressed directly against Tim’s forehead as he knelt helplessly, tears spilling soundlessly down his face.

“Look at me.” It was an order, and disobedience wasn’t an option. Tim slowly lifted his chin, his gaze meeting Jason’s sneer only for it to vanish in an instant the moment their eyes locked.

Because behind that mask of fury, in those toxic green eyes, something else flickered. Confusion. And desperation.

And Tim couldn’t look away.

Because this Jason… wasn’t his Jason.

His body was larger now, bulkier, closer to Bruce’s build. A scar cut across his cheek, old but unforgotten. There was no helmet, only the red hood draped over his head and a mask shadowing his face. Guns weighed heavily on his belt, and the faint outline of blades jutted across his back.

It was Jason. But… not Jason. Older. Sharper. More prepared. More dangerous.

Tim tried to breathe, but his lungs refused. He couldn’t look away from the man standing before him. He couldn’t move.

His wings had been broken.

“Baby Bird?” Jason’s voice shifted, softened into something almost fragile, as if he were speaking to a child. Maybe, in his eyes, Tim was exactly that. The familiar nickname—so wrong, so tender—sent a tremor down Tim’s spine. Jason never called him that. Jason never spoke to him with warmth.

Jason hated Tim.

He shut his eyes tight, praying this was just a cruel dream. But then warm hands cupped his face, grounding him. A metallic clatter echoed through the cave, sharp and final. The gun had hit the floor.

“No, no, no—don’t close those blue eyes, Baby Bird. Look at me,” this time Jason’s voice cracked into a plea. Every word trembled with fear, with a need that made Tim’s chest tighten painfully. Tim bit down on a sob, the taste of salt and blood filling his mouth, forcing himself to lift his gaze and meet the toxic green fire of Jason’s eyes once more. He could feel the tremor in Jason’s hands, the way his knees pressed into the cold floor, and it made his own body ache with tension.

Jason had dropped to his knees fully in front of him now, abandoning every trace of fury. Desperation flooded in, twisting his face into an expression Tim had never seen before—vulnerable, pleading, terrified. Each breath Jason took shuddered through him, and Tim felt the weight of unspoken words pressing down, as if Jason’s entire being were tethered to him, begging silently for him not to vanish.

“Tim? Tim? Is it you? Is it really you?”

All Tim could do was stare back, blinking through his confusion, because Jason looked broken, on the verge of falling apart, and Tim’s mind couldn’t make sense of it.

“…Jason?” The name slipped out in a whisper, fragile and uncertain. Jason flinched at the sound, freezing in place as though the single word had gutted him. For a heartbeat, it felt like their roles had reversed, and Tim no longer knew what to do or what to say. None of this made sense.

None of it made sense.

“Something’s wrong. Something’s—no. I’m not—” Tim’s voice spiraled into panic, words unraveling, and the world blurred before his eyes. He caught one last glimpse of that green gaze, wide with horror, before the darkness dragged him under completely.

“Poor, poor detective,” the voice was syrupy sweet, dripping with mockery, and the cold arrogance in Ra’s al Ghul’s eyes made Tim’s stomach twist. They were alone, locked away in a room that seemed to breathe with the shadows, the walls pressing closer, stealing what little oxygen he had left. “No one is coming for the wounded little bird.”

Fingernails dug cruelly into his cheeks, sharp enough to sting, forcing Tim to flinch against the touch. He tried to wrench himself free, but the heavy chains binding his wrists and ankles clinked mercilessly, a reminder of his helplessness.

“Don’t struggle, detective,” Ra’s whispered in his ear, his breath cold, his smile predatory, his eyes glinting like a blade catching light. “Soon, you’ll understand everything.”

Tim’s chest rose and fell, uneven and desperate, his pulse hammering against the metal restraints.

“In the end…” Ra’s leaned closer, his voice a serpent’s hiss, venom coiled in every syllable. “…a dead Robin cannot dream of being saved.”

The voices reached him as though muffled by thick cotton, distant and warped, their edges crackling like static in his skull. Every syllable stabbed at his consciousness, sharp and relentless, making it impossible to find peace. Tim couldn’t summon the strength to lift his head; his muscles refused to respond. He lay sprawled on something unyielding, the surface biting cold into his skin, sending shivers that raced down his spine and made his teeth chatter. Every nerve in his body screamed in protest, fire and ice coiling together, while his temples throbbed with a steady, crushing pressure that made even the faintest sound unbearable. Somewhere nearby, a pipe groaned and water dripped, each drop echoing like a hammer against bone, and through the haze of pain and disorientation, he managed to catch fragments of words drifting near, incoherent but sinister, like whispers crawling along the edges of his mind.

“…You’re not listening to me, Jason! He could be dangerous!”

Tim frowned weakly, confusion knotting in his chest. Dangerous? Who were they talking about? And why did Dick’s voice drip with such venom, a tone Tim had never heard directed at him before?

“Not listening? You’re the one who’s not listening!” Jason’s voice came back like a blade, raw with fury. “Look at him, Dick! Look at this kid and tell me with a straight face he stormed into the Cave to kill us all! He couldn’t even stand when I found him!”

The shouting only worsened the pounding in Tim’s skull, each word splintering into a migraine that left him clenching his jaw.

“It doesn’t matter,” Dick snarled, his teeth audibly grinding together. “You put us all at risk, Jay.”

Tim forced himself upright, his hands trembling as he groped along the wall for balance. He didn’t want to hear this argument, didn’t want to feel the weight of their words pressing into his ribs like knives. He wanted nothing more than to sink back into unconsciousness and silence, but there would be no such escape, not here, not with them so close.

“What was I supposed to do, Dick?” Jason’s voice cracked, breaking through the rage with something closer to grief. Tim’s eyes finally fluttered open, the light searing his vision until he blinked hard, forcing his sight to adjust. And then he froze. His breath caught.

Before him, separated by a cold and glass door, stood his brothers. They were locked in a vicious argument just outside the cell he was trapped in. His chest tightened as memories slammed into him like a tidal wave, and dread clawed up his spine. This wasn’t a dream. It had never been a dream.

“I took the DNA samples,” Jason spat, his tone a mixture of exhaustion and defiance. “I locked him in here. I called you. What more do you want from me?”

Dick’s lips pressed into a tight, grim line, his stormy eyes narrowing as he stared Jason down. For a long, silent moment, the room pulsed with hostility. Then, finally, Dick dragged in a sharp breath and exhaled shakily, running a hand through his hair with restless fingers, frustration radiating off of him like heat.

“You’re right. You’re right. I just…” Dick dragged in a sharp breath, visibly trying to steady his emotions. His next words came quieter, though no less heavy. “I just worry—for you. For all of us. We don’t know what we’re dealing with.”

“We’ll find out, Dickwing,” Jason answered, his voice softer now. He set a hand on Dick’s shoulder, his gaze weighted with a grief that seemed older than both of them. “Whoever—or whatever—he is, we’ll find out.”

Tim watched them in silence, every muscle locked, terrified to make even the faintest sound. But the moment he tried to push himself up from the floor, both sets of eyes snapped toward him. Jason’s stare was steady, searching, touched with something almost gentle. Dick’s, however, Dick’s eyes burned like blue fire, and the sheer intensity made Tim’s chest seize.

Dick looked at him as though he were the enemy.

“How are you feeling, Baby Bird?” Jason called, his voice deliberately lighter as he stepped away from Dick and moved closer to the cage. His gaze swept over Tim, carefully studying every shake in his limbs, every stagger of breath.

“Jason,” Dick snapped, baring his teeth in a scowl. His arms folded tightly across his chest, shoulders squared with barely contained fury. No, not Dick. This wasn’t his Dick. This one was older, harder, darker. The aura he radiated was heavy, predatory. To Tim, he looked less like a brother and more like a panther stalking prey, ready to strike the moment he stepped too close.

“He’s not our brother.”

Just four words. But they shredded Tim’s world into jagged, bleeding pieces.

The gaze that accompanied him burned into his skin, searing him alive, branding him as something alien, unwanted, unworthy. His chest felt hollow, collapsing under the weight of rejection and accusation, and a cold, hollow panic spread through his veins. He wanted to vanish, to slip into the shadows and dissolve entirely, anything to escape the firestorm raging in those blue eyes, the silent verdict that he did not belong.

Every instinct screamed to run, to hide, but the cell held him fast, mocking his helplessness. The shadows around him seemed to close in, and the air itself tasted of despair, heavy and metallic. Tim’s throat tightened as if the words themselves were strangling him. He was trapped—physically, emotionally, utterly alone in the faces of the people he had once called family.

“Dick,” Jason exhaled heavily, the weight in his chest audible in every word. Exhaustion lined his face, dark shadows beneath eyes that usually burned with fire and defiance. Tim had never seen him like this before, vulnerable in a way that made his bravado crumble. “If he were a clone, he wouldn’t look fifteen years old…”

Tim’s head snapped up, eyes wide. A clone? They thought he was a clone?

“We both know Ra’s is not above playing dirty,” Dick shot back, his voice flat, disdain sharp as a blade. His gaze stayed fixed on Tim, pinning him down like an insect under glass. Under that heavy stare, Tim felt like filth, like something that didn’t belong in the same air as them.

His heart quickened, panic twisting his insides. Ra’s name—always Ra’s name—was tangled in this somehow. But how? His memories were still a blur, scattered fragments of voices and names, echoes of something lost. He had nothing solid, nothing to hold onto.

He had nothing…

“Maybe. But what’s he gonna do, Dick? Hit us with those big puppy eyes until we drop dead?” Jason’s hands cut through the air with a sharp, sarcastic gesture. His words carried fire, but Tim could see the edge of worry beneath.

The computer broke the silence with a piercing beep. Results.

Jason cursed under his breath and moved quickly to the console, leaving Tim alone under Dick’s suffocating stare. And Tim stared back.

This Dick was different. His hair shorter. Faint lines marking his face. No trace of the boyish spark that had once defined him, only a hardened seriousness lingering in his ice-blue eyes.

He wasn’t wearing the suit, but Tim knew. He was looking at Nightwing.

They stared at one another, the silence stretching taut. Tim clung to it, waiting for the machine to decide his fate. Until they believed he wasn’t a clone, nothing would change.

He prayed Bruce would walk through the door. Bruce could fix this. Bruce would know what had happened to him, because Tim couldn’t even trust his own memories anymore.

But Dick’s silence was different. It wasn’t waiting. It was judgment. And Tim knew with sick certainty: if Jason weren’t here, Dick would’ve already had him in chains.

Tim’s thoughts spiraled deeper, clawing for logic. Another universe? A fracture in time? There was no other explanation.

But before he could sink further, Jason crossed the space in a few strides. His hand slammed against the panel beside the cell, and the glass door hissed open.

“Jason!” Dick’s voice cut sharply, his body twisting toward his brother, but Jason shoved a slip of paper in his face, brushing past him without hesitation. He stepped into the cell, where Tim still leaned weakly against the wall, barely upright.

“He’s not a clone,” Jason said flatly. His hands closed on Tim’s shoulders, steady and deliberate, pushing him out of confinement with surprising care. He handled Tim like something fragile, almost breakable, guiding him toward the med bay before lowering him onto a cot. “He’s hurt. He’s exhausted. You can interrogate him after I patch him up.”

“We still don’t know if he’s dangerous!” Dick snapped, voice rising toward a breaking point.

Guilt twisted through Tim’s chest. All of this was because of him. Even if he didn’t understand what he’d done.

They knew him. That much was clear. Which meant whether this was another universe or another timeline, Tim Drake existed here. But if Dick looked at him with that much caution…

What had he become here? A traitor? A villain?

Where was Bruce?

Tim needed Bruce. Only Bruce could find the answers he couldn’t trust himself to see.

Bruce would know how to fix this.

“Hey, Baby Bird, easy,” Jason’s voice softened. His hand pressed through Tim’s hair, sliding to the back of his neck and shoulders, rubbing slow circles. The gentleness burned more than the wounds. The presence was steady, grounding. Too steady. Too kind.

This wasn’t real. It couldn’t be real.

Jason Todd hated Tim Drake.

“Bruce won’t be impressed by this, Jaylad,” Dick said finally, his voice losing fire but not his stance. He stayed close, just behind Jason, ready to strike at the first wrong move.

Tim was surrounded. Outnumbered. Unarmed. Alone among familiar faces that weren’t his own.

“Then that’s Bruce’s problem,” Jason muttered, unfazed, eyes scanning Tim’s injuries with grim focus. His frown deepened. He looked worried—worried for him. If Tim weren’t so tired, he might have laughed at the absurdity of it. But laughter wouldn’t come in this situation.

“His clothes are soaked through. Drenched in blood,” Jason’s tone sharpened, throwing a glance over his shoulder. “Dickwing, grab something from Gremlin’s wardrobe.”

Tim froze, eyes snapping wide. Damian’s wardrobe.

His heart stopped cold.

And before Tim could demand answers, Dick beat him to it.

“You do realize he’s not going to appreciate that, right?”

Jason didn’t even look up from rummaging through the med kit, his tone sharp with impatience.

“Got a better idea, Dickwing? Or should we just let him parade around in shredded, blood-soaked rags?”

Dick rolled his eyes, muttering something under his breath before throwing Tim one last, warning glance. Then he turned on his heel and stalked out of the cave.

The moment the sound of his footsteps faded, Jason’s shoulders eased, like he’d been holding a weight the entire time. He let out a long breath and finally looked at Tim. That stare—steady, intent—was enough to make Tim’s skin prickle.

“Don’t mind him,” Jason said, crouching down to Tim’s level. His voice softened as he uncapped the antiseptic. “He’s been… on edge lately.”

The sting of the liquid burned across Tim’s wounds. He bit his lip, but the pain was nothing compared to the void twisting in his chest.

“We all have,” Jason added. “It’s been a rough time.”

“You look… relaxed,” Tim heard himself say before he could stop it. The words slipped out too fast, too unguarded. His heart lurched, and he snapped his mouth shut, eyes darting nervously to Jason.

For a second, Jason froze. Surprise flickered in his eyes. Then, slowly, the corner of his mouth tugged upward into something that resembled a smile—sharp, boyish, unsettlingly genuine. For a heartbeat, he almost looked like Jason Tim remembered.

“Guess I do…” he murmured, turning back to dress the wounds. But the smile died as quickly as it came, his face shadowed again. Whatever warmth had been there drained, leaving something darker in its place.

“I’m glad you’re here, Tim,” he said at last. And the way he said it made Tim’s stomach twist.

No.

No, Jason wouldn’t say that.

Jason wouldn’t look at him like that.

Jason wouldn’t—

Tim’s chest constricted, each breath coming sharper, tighter, like the air itself had turned against him. His heart slammed violently against his ribs, frantic, desperate, as if trying to claw its way out.

The echo of new footsteps cut through the cave. Tim’s blurred vision shifted, latching onto the shape approaching from the shadows. Familiar. Solid. Steady.

Bruce.

Bruce was here.

Bruce would fix this.

Bruce always fixed everything.

For one fragile second, when blue eyes locked with his, Tim felt his lungs release. He could breathe again. He could believe.

And then the illusion shattered.

In a blur of cape and fury, Bruce was suddenly upon him, his hand clamped tight around Tim’s throat, driving him back against the medical cot. The impact rattled through Tim’s bones. He gasped, clawing for air, but the grip was iron and merciless.

The storm in Bruce’s eyes was unrecognizable. Not the calm, impenetrable certainty of Batman, this was raw, violent, a tempest that answered to no god. All the hope Tim had clung to splintered, drowning like a ship torn apart and dragged into the black depths of the sea.

“Who are you?” Bruce growled, the words rumbling out like thunder. No warmth. No hesitation. No recognition.

Tim’s vision blurred at the edges. Spots danced. He tried to force sound past his lips, but his throat convulsed, choking under the pressure.

Somewhere beyond the ringing in his ears, Jason’s voice cut through—hoarse, desperate, cracking with panic.

“Bruce! Bruce! Stop! It’s him—it’s Tim!”

But the words fell like stones into the void, never reaching their mark.

Bruce’s face was a mask of rage, lips curled back into a snarl. His grip only tightened. And when he spoke again, the words carved themselves into Tim’s chest, cruel and final.

“He’s not my son.”
