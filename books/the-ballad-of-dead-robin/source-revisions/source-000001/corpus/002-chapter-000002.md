# Chapter 1: Robin who never learned how to fly

The first thing he saw was sunlight—blinding, unnatural sunlight that didn’t belong in Gotham. It poured down like judgment, too bright and too warm.

He flinched, throwing an arm over his face, a strangled groan escaping his throat as sharp pain lanced through every nerve in his body. He wanted to collapse back into unconsciousness, but the migraine clawing through his skull refused to let him go. Nausea curled in his gut like a fist.

He dropped to his knees, body folding in on itself, trembling as bile surged upward. The taste of acid hit the back of his throat.

“God, this is pathetic…” he rasped, wiping his mouth with the back of his sleeve, the stench of vomit clinging to the fabric. His thoughts were a haze. Nothing made sense.

He didn’t know where he was. He didn’t know how he got here. He didn’t know anything. Except for one thing. One name.

Tim Drake.

That was all he had.

There was nothing, just a blinding flash of light, and then silence. A silence so deep it felt like someone had wiped his mind clean, erasing everything, leaving only a jagged scar behind as if to mock his helplessness.

Tim clenched his jaw, grinding his teeth as panic began to scrape at the edges of his mind. He tried to grab onto something—a memory, a name, a reason—but the more he searched, the harder his heart pounded, every beat laced with fear.

Tim Drake.

That name rose from the static like a hand reaching out from drowning water. He was Tim Drake.

And then, another name followed.

Red Robin.

Before he could breathe, before the fear could settle into focus, a voice cut through the silence behind him.

“Welcome to your new world, Detective,” it was cold and too familiar, and Tim froze, eyes snapping open. Slowly, with dread pooling in his chest, he turned to face the figure behind him.

The man standing behind him was carved from a nightmare he remembered too well. Dark hair streaked with silver. A long, emerald cloak that whispered with authority. A posture like stone. And eyes, piercing, predatory, and burning with quiet madness, making Tim feel uneasy.

He felt like he was cut piece by piece.

The gaze of a madman.

Then there was a flicker. A name, unbidden, whispered through Tim’s fractured mind like a ghost.

Ra’s al Ghul.

He knew that name. Knew the weight it carried. Knew the cost of hearing it.

But something was wrong.

Just details, nearly invisible, but they scraped against his instincts like razors. Ra’s al Ghul looked broader. Maybe it was the cut of his suit, tailored like armor, elegant and inhuman. Standing above him now, lips curled in a predator’s grin, Ra’s looked less like a conqueror and more like a businessman moments before closing a hostile takeover. But Tim wasn’t fooled.

Ra’s al Ghul didn’t negotiate. He consumed. A serpent in skin, always slithering forward, always finding an angle to strike.

“I must admit,” Ra’s murmured, voice smooth and distant, like silk wrapping around a blade, “I was hoping you’d be… older.”

His eyes raked over Tim like a tiger circling its prey. And maybe that’s exactly what Tim was.

A shiver crawled down Tim’s spine, cold sweat clinging to his back. He clenched his fists, trying to hide the tremor in his fingers.

Something was wrong.

Then the words truly hit him.

Older.

Tim’s breath caught. His brow furrowed as a new spike of pain stabbed behind his eyes. He blinked through the ache and took a long breath through his nose, forcing his thoughts into order.

It didn’t matter how he got here. Didn’t matter what Ra’s wanted. The fact remained:

Ra’s al Ghul was standing in front of him. And Tim wasn’t going to fold.

“Didn’t think you'd forget me so quickly. Memory failing in your old age?” His voice was cracked, breathless, but the smirk was sharp as he tried to look brave. He pushed himself to his feet on shaky knees, barely holding steady. His heartbeat thundered in his ears, wild, hummingbird-fast. He swallowed, lips cracked and dry, tasting the edge of fear.

Ra’s raised an eyebrow, a flicker of something—curiosity, amusement—passing across his face like a shadow.

“Bold,” he said coolly. “But perhaps that’s for the best.” Then his smile widened, too much for Tim’s comfort. “Discipline is forged through suffering. And it will be my honor to break you, Timothy.”

Tim took an unconscious step back, reaching for his Bō staff, only to find nothing. His belt was gone. His weapons, stripped. His stomach dropped.

Ra’s saw it, of course, he did. His grin stretched to something grotesque, eyes alight with something ancient and unholy. Demons danced in his pupils, waltzing to a tune only Ra’s could hear, something baroque, something cruel. Something like Vivaldi, if Vivaldi composed war hymns.

Then he felt it. A shift. A ripple of movement behind him and his instinct screamed. Tim threw himself forward just as a sharp blade sliced the air beside his cheek, close enough to feel the wind it carved.

He twisted, heart hammering in his throat. He stared straight into the mask of another monster.

Slade Wilson.

Tim was surrounded. A madman and a killer, and they both came for him, while Tim was alone, unarmed, bleeding, and completely in the dark about how he even got here.

“Hah. This bird’s got good reflexes,” Slade Wilson said with a low chuckle, sheathing his sword with the casual ease of someone who didn’t expect a fight to last long. He glanced at Ra’s with a half-interested tilt of his head. “You think he’s the one?”

Ra’s didn’t answer at first. He was still watching Tim with that same sharp, glinting stare, eyes that studied, dissected and devoured. There was no shame in the hunger behind that gaze. And in that moment, Tim wished the ground would split open beneath him, if only to swallow him whole and spare him from the weight of those eyes.

“If he’s not,” Ra’s finally said, voice low but firm with amusement hidden behind it, “he won’t survive.”

It wasn’t a threat. It was a promise. And it wound around Tim’s ribs like a slow-growing vine of dread.

He needed to run. He needed to move. But under the eyes of these two predators, both watching him like a hawk, Tim couldn’t even breathe properly, let alone plan an escape.

Come on, Tim. Think. THINK.

“The boy has potential,” Slade mused, almost to himself. “Let’s hope it’s not wasted.”

There was a bite of mockery in his tone, enough to make Tim’s jaw clench. His tongue burned with the urge to say something sharp, but this wasn’t the moment for pride.

He scanned the area, looking at familiar rooftops. A gargoyle statue jutting like a sentry into the skyline.

Gotham. He was still in Gotham.

Relief hit like a punch to the chest. But it brought another question with it, one far more dangerous.

What the hell was Ra’s doing in Gotham?

And more importantly, how did Tim end up here with him?

“I’ll handle it,” Ra’s said with finality, chin raised like a general already planning his next conquest. Tim’s breath caught, panic nipped at the edge of his vision, turning the corners of his sight black. He forced it back, anchoring himself to the skyline, to the shape of the gargoyle. He knew this city, knew it like his own fingers. Every alley etched into him like scars. Every fire escape, every shadowed shortcut, every bolt-hole tucked between forgotten brick.

Gotham was full of secrets. And secrets were Tim’s specialty.

“If it’s settled,” Slade said, pulling out a phone and starting to type a message, “shouldn’t we be heading back?”

Tim felt time slipping through his fingers. His hand inched toward a hidden seam in his suit, one only he would know was there, stitched beneath the sleeve, invisible unless you were looking for it. Fingertips brushed against familiar texture.

In the distance, he could hear the low thrum of a helicopter. This was it. He had one shot. No second chances.

“Don't be stupid, boy,” Slade muttered, disinterested, almost bored. But Tim didn’t listen. In a single motion, he hurled smoke pellets at the ground, a sharp hiss of pressure and light exploding around him, and then he moved, ignoring the trembling in his legs, ignoring the fire that lit up every nerve in his body.

He ran, full-speed, toward the edge of the rooftop as shouts of surprise rang out behind him. He had no weapons. No grappling hook. No backup.

Just his will to survive and the sheer, blinding terror pushing him forward.

The moment his foot touched the edge of the rooftop, he leapt, the cool rush of wind whipping through his hair, tugging it wildly to the sides. In the next heartbeat, his feet landed on solid ground, but he lost his balance, collapsing onto his knees and pressing his palms against the grimy rooftop. Without hesitation, he sprang back up, eyes fixed ahead as he sprinted toward the next building.

Behind him, the distant thrum of a helicopter grew louder, but all Tim could see was the statue looming ahead.

Just a little more. Faster. He needed to be faster.

Another jump, this time, he landed hard on his side, his breath ripping from his throat in a quiet cry. He clenched his jaw against it.

Later. He didn’t have time to cry now.

The Gargoyle’s head loomed close by, Ra’s presence pressing down on him like a personal shadow that refused to release its grip. Yet Tim moved swiftly, scrambling up to the statue as if it were a lifeline, gripping its cold stone like a last chance at salvation. His trembling fingers found the right claw and twisted it carefully.

A hidden hatch slid open beneath the statue’s wing, revealing a yawning darkness. Without hesitation, Tim plunged into the void, swallowed whole by the complete blackness below.

The hatch slammed shut behind him, and Tim knew—he was safe.

For now.

He had no phone. No communicator. No way to reach the Batcave. Even if he did, he doubted anyone would come.

He wasn’t family.

Bruce made that painfully clear the moment he took Tim under his wing. Dick never truly saw him as a brother, always choosing Damian instead.

He was alone.

Robin who never learned how to fly.

Jason was right. Maybe he really was just a cuckoo bird, an intruder in a nest that would never be his.

Tim swallowed the lump in his throat, shaking away the crushing thoughts, and gripping the cold wall for balance, he descended the stairs, trusting his memory to guide him.

He didn’t understand what Ra’s wanted from him. He couldn’t recall the last days of his life. But Tim was stubborn, damn him if he’d let Ra’s manipulate him again.

“So… what now?” Slade tilted his head, eyes fixed on the rooftop edge where the boy had vanished into shadow.

Across from him, Ra’s al Ghul sat cross-legged in eerie calm, fingers laced together, eyes closed as though meditating in the middle of war.

“We let him go,” Ra’s voice was silk over steel, serene and dangerous. Slade blinked, the faintest furrow of confusion crossing his brow.

“Wasn’t the plan to bring him to the League?” He sat opposite the Demon’s Head, eyes narrowed as he studied the older man’s face.

Ra’s was an enigma. He always had been. And some part of Slade, seasoned as he was, still hated that he could never quite read him.

“That was the plan,” Ra’s agreed, lifting his chin, a slow smile spreading across his lips. “But tell me, Slade, who breaks the bird more perfectly than Batman himself?"

His voice was low and almost reverent, like a priest speaking of divine sacrifice. In the depths of his stare, a dark and hungry spark ignited. It was an obsession.

Slade exhaled through his nose. Even he felt a flicker of reluctant admiration for the boy Ra’s had chosen.

Timothy.

A boy was dancing just beyond their reach, because Ra’s didn’t need to chase him.

Not yet.

“He’ll come to me himself,” Ra’s murmured, voice thick with quiet certainty. “Once he realizes he was never one of them. Once the illusion of family shatters—” He smiled wider, teeth glinting in the dim light. “—Timothy will come crawling to me for a purpose. And I will be the one to give it to him.”

Tim moved like a ghost through Gotham’s shadowed arteries, slipping between alleyways and rooftops until he finally stood before what should have been safety, home, the Cave, the one place that had always welcomed him back, no matter how bloodied or broken he was.

He placed his hand on the biometric scanner, expecting the quiet affirmation of belonging.

Instead, he was met with silence.

No whir of recognition. No subtle green light.

He tried again, slower this time, his fingers trembling as if already knowing what was coming.

Access Denied.

Not a glitch. Not a delay. Just rejection.

A hollow sort of disbelief bloomed in his chest, crawling up into his throat like smoke. His lips parted, but no words came out.

Did they lock me out? Just like that?

The system that had once known his pulse, his print, his voice, had erased him without hesitation.

But instincts, honed by years of surviving betrayal and disappointment, took over. His hands moved with mechanical certainty, muscle memory overriding the ache in his chest. He bypassed the security field using an old subroutine, one that only he knew how to exploit, buried so deep in the Cave’s network that even Bruce hadn’t caught it in his last audit.

The entrance opened, but as Tim stepped inside, relief never came.

It was the Cave, yes. The med bay, the rows of suits, the eternal blue glow of the Batcomputer, it was all here, all familiar, all exactly where it should be.

And yet—

It wasn’t right.

There was something… off. Not just updated tech or subtle renovations. No, this felt deeper. Like walking into a replica built by someone who remembered the pieces but decided to upgrade the design.

Tim couldn’t remember exactly when he’d last stood inside the Batcave, time blurred at the edges, but surely it hadn’t been long enough for everything to feel this… foreign.

Could so much truly have changed since his last visit?

Without meaning to, he found himself drifting toward the weapons vault, each step slow and hesitant, his body pulled forward while his instincts screamed to stop. He didn’t want to blink. He didn’t dare. Because some part of him feared that the image before him might vanish if he looked away, like a mirage finally giving in to the heat.

Normally, all their weapons were stored here, primary loadouts, spares, backups upon backups, each piece meticulously catalogued and in its rightful place.

But as Tim crossed the threshold and his eyes scanned the wall, his breath caught.

The space where his gear should have been… was empty.

Gone. Utterly.

“What the hell…” Tim muttered under his breath, spinning sharply around and heading toward the suits. There, lined up in cold, silent rows, were all armors from Robin to Batman. Even the Red Hood suit stood there, mocking him with its silent presence, an unspoken reminder that this place belonged to everyone but him.

But the Red Robin suit? It wasn’t there.

His fingers twitched at his sides, reaching out for something that wasn’t there, as if by sheer will he could summon it back. But all he felt was the suffocating realization sinking in like ice through his veins.

They had removed him.

As if he’d never belonged at all.

And in that hollow moment, staring at the ghost of a legacy that no longer acknowledged his existence, Tim realized that this wasn’t just about a missing suit.

They had finally gotten rid of him.

This is what you deserve.

You were always just a replacement.

His knees buckled before he realized he was falling. The impact echoed through the empty space, louder than it should have, like the Cave itself was announcing his failure.

He stared forward, dry-eyed at first, until the truth finally broke past the numb shell he’d wrapped around himself. They had forgotten him. Or worse.

They’d chosen to forget him.

His lips parted in a breathless, soundless laugh, more an exhale than a voice. So, this was it. Not a betrayal by enemies, not a fall in battle, but a quiet deletion.

And in the growing silence, surrounded by the suits of those who had taken root in this place, who belonged, Tim Drake realized he didn’t.

He never had.

The pressure behind his eyes grew until it spilled down his cheeks, hot tears slipping across his skin before he could even try to stop them. But he didn’t wipe them away. He didn’t move at all.

He simply sat in the dark, small and silent beneath the weight of a world that had kept spinning.

And just when the ache threatened to turn into a scream, he heard a click. A sharp and cold pressure at the back of his skull froze him in place.

A gun.

He didn’t need to turn around to know the shape of the threat. Didn’t need to guess who would hold him at gunpoint in this cave that no longer saw him as anything but an intruder.

A voice growled behind him, low and familiar, spitting venom through gritted teeth.

“Give me one reason not to pull the trigger.”

Tim didn’t flinch. He didn’t scream. He didn’t beg.

He just breathed in and waited.

Because maybe it was the ending he deserved. Perhaps being killed by his own brother was his fate all along.

How tragically fitting, yet unbearably cruel.

In the end, Red Robin never truly learned how to fly.
