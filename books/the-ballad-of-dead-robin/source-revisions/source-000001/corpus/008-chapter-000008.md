The streets blurred past, smeared into color and motion. Maybe it was the speed Jason was pushing the car to, maybe it was because Dick couldn’t force his mind to latch onto anything. His thoughts were too loud, his mind too heavy. And he was surfing in the deep waters of his anger and guilt.

“Dickwing,” Jason finally muttered after a long stretch of silence. Dick slowly lifted his head as if it weighed too much, blinking at him in confusion. Jason’s eyes were narrowed, his expression carved in stone. In daylight, the scars on his face were stark and pale, stretched in thin ridges that caught the light like seams pulled too tight.

When had they last seen each other in the sun instead of under the city’s shadows? When had they last been a family—a real one?

Dick studied the familiar features that somehow felt distant now, as if time had made strangers out of brothers. A dull burn curled in his chest; he swallowed against the bitterness of it.

His brother didn’t deserve this. None of them did. Not Jason. Not Tim.

Both of them had worn the ‘R’ because of him. Both had followed the path he’d laid. But in their story, Robin only ever had one kind of ending, because no matter how strong they were, lonely robins always died. Especially the fledglings, the ones just learning how to fly.

Dick should have taught them. No. He had been obligated to teach them how to fly, how to survive the fall. How to hide from predators. But instead, he ran. Like a coward. Left them to whatever fate picked them apart.

And for what? Pride? That same damn pride that had killed his brothers long before death ever reached them, because he was so focused on his own grief. So focused on his anger towards Bruce…

“…Wing, if you’re planning another round of self-flagellation, do me a favor and give some warning next time, so I don’t waste my breath talking to a brick wall,” Jason’s annoyed voice finally cut through the noise in Dick’s head. He sucked in a sharp breath and scrubbed a tired hand over his face. The situation was already eating at him, clawing under his ribs. “…Duke said he lost Tim near the edge of Crime Alley.”

“Duke?” Dick blinked, startled. He hadn’t even told him about Tim yet, but apparently someone had beaten him to it. Dick would bet his entire money it was Damian.

Even if Bruce had officially adopted the kid, after everything that happened, Duke had enrolled at Gotham University and moved into the dorms. He wanted space. Wanted distance after what happened to their brother. And Dick couldn’t blame him.

They’d all forgotten what family meant.

“…Come on, Big Bird—don’t zone out on me,” Jason snapped his fingers in front of Dick’s face, the sharp sound cutting through the drone of traffic as the car tore down the road. “We can panic later. Right now, we need to find the baby bird before any other predator does.”

And Dick heard the fear in Jason’s voice. That familiar fear, hidden beneath a layer of sarcasm. But there was still one thing he needed to know.

“And then what?” Dick asked. When had everything gotten so complicated? He felt lost. They’d already crossed too many lines, already abandoned all protocols, blindly searching for answers.

“First, we convince the kid no one’s gonna hurt him,” Jason muttered, eyes narrowing as if replaying their first encounter and Bruce’s behavior. Dick couldn’t blame the…other Tim. He wouldn’t have tolerated it either. And yet—

“Don’t call him that, Jay,” Dick exhaled, drained, the plea slipping out before he could catch it.

“What’s wrong, Dickwing? Feeling guilty?” Jason’s jaw clenched, irritation tightening his voice. He jerked the wheel sharply into a narrow alley, and Dick—unprepared—smacked his temple against the window. The pain shot down his neck and he hissed through his teeth.

He raised a hand to his head, ready to retort, but froze halfway.

Jason continued driving in silence. His cold and unreadable stare fixed on the road ahead; the passing streetlights flickered across his face, carving brief shadows under his eyes. But Dick saw it. Saw how Jason’s eyes, usually tinted with that toxic Lazarus green, were clear now. Clear and painfully blue. Wet with the threat of tears he refused to let fall.

Jason Todd didn’t cry anymore.

After he came back as Red Hood, Dick hadn’t seen a single tear from him. Even when they buried Tim, Jason only stood there, silently staring at the casket. Empty-eyed. Blank face. Like his soul had just been lowered into the ground with his brother’s body.

Dick remembered it vividly. Too vividly. He knew he’d carry that memory to the end of his days, returning to the grave again and again, asking for forgiveness no one would ever grant.

“Jay,” Dick called softly, catching how Jason’s fingers tightened around the steering wheel, knuckles whitening, his gaze fixed stubbornly on the road ahead as if afraid to look anywhere else—afraid of what the others might see on his face.

“Little Wing,” Dick tried again, because he had to fix this somehow. “If I could… I would never have let him go alone that night. If I’d just believed him…trusted him…”

“We all made mistakes. Tim’s death is on all of us,” Jason rasped, voice hoarse, as if forcing each word out cost effort. He bit the inside of his cheek, holding back a tremor or a sob, and Dick’s heart ached seeing this. “But I was the one who nearly killed him with my own hands.”

“You were under the Lazarus,” Dick cut in immediately. The answer earned him a low, poisonous laugh that had nothing humorous in it.

“And instead of taking it out on Bruce, I picked a kid. A kid who looked at me like I was some kind of hero. Pathetic, isn’t it?”

Dick had no answer. Because no matter how much Tim admired him, Jason had always been his Robin.

“Just a little more and you’ll make a hell of a Robin,” Dick grinned, watching Tim sprawled on his back on the mat, chest rising and falling fast. His clothes clung to him with sweat, hair sticking up in every direction. But his eyes burned with determination and grief.

“You think Jason would be proud of me?”

“We should contact Zatanna,” Dick finally said. They still didn’t have a plan—something that was beginning to grate on him. After a beat, he added anxiously. “Honestly, we should’ve done that a long time ago.”

“And when exactly should that’ve been?” Jason shot back, and Dick could hear fury in his voice, that made his own anger to flare up. “When the kid was half-dead in the cell? Or when Bruce nearly killed him himself? Or—oh, wait—this morning, when Bruce said flat-out he didn’t trust him? Don’t get me wrong, Dickwing, but Tim’s got zero reasons to trust any of us. Calling Zatanna behind his back wouldn’t fix that.”

“He needs answers. Same as we do,” Dick argued. He knew with certainty that the real Tim would’ve jumped at any chance to figure out what was happening to him.

“We’re talking about Tim, Dick. Do you really think he’s just going to trust us and Zatanna? Even if our Tim knew her, we don’t know what this Tim remembers. We don’t know if he even knows who she is. We don’t know a damn thing, because instead of waiting—just waiting—and talking to him, you and Bruce decided to break him down first! And now he’s gone, because he’d rather figure things out alone than trust us! And honestly, who could blame him?!”

“We follow protocol for a reason, Jay!” Dick snarled, palm slamming against the car door. The sharp crack echoed in the cramped space, making the air vibrate.

“To hell with protocol if it means losing Tim all over again!”

“We already lost him, Jason! God damn it, we already fucking lost him!” Dick’s voice shook. A shiver rolled through him—anger and grief tangled in his chest like barbed wire, leaving his hands trembling, breath hot and broken in his throat.

“I know,” Jason whispered suddenly. The word barely reached him over the hum of the engine. “I know, Dick. Believe me.”

He lifted his chin, inhaling deeply, steadying himself before turning the wheel and pulling into an empty side street. The tires hissed softly over wet asphalt. He stopped the car, killed the engine, and finally faced Dick head-on.

Dick could see the exhaustion carved under his eyes, the grief sitting heavy in the tense line of his shoulders. But his hands stayed locked around the wheel, like he might tear off down the road at any moment.

“And you don’t have to agree with me,” Jason murmured. “But answer one thing. Just one.”

Silence stretched, so taut it felt like it might snap.

“Why do you think Bruce didn’t follow protocol?”

The question hit like a bullet. Dick froze; breath trapped in his lungs. Bruce always followed protocol. Bruce always did what he believed was necessary. Bruce was the most paranoid person they knew. Bruce never—

“…For all his hatred toward…this Tim,” Jason grunted in a low voice, so similar to Bruce, “somewhere deep down he wants to believe his son is still alive. And if Zatanna can send Tim home—wherever the hell he’s from—Bruce loses that last flicker of hope to have his son back.”

There was truth in Jason’s words. An undeniable truth. And Dick couldn’t find a single argument against it.

Maybe Dick wasn’t the only one desperate for redemption.

“It’s suffocating,” Dick murmured, sinking back against the seat. The worn leather groaned beneath his weight. He stared out at the dilapidated apartment building across the street—its walls cracked, windows fogged, balconies sagging. Gotham always looked ready to crumble. But looks were always deceiving. Gotham had survived invasions, villains, war and death. It had survived the worst of them, even when they didn’t survive each other.

For a long stretch, they sat in silence, simply listening.

The distant voices of passing-by. A dog barking somewhere behind the building. The city was breathing around them like a restless animal.

Eventually, Jason broke the quiet.

“Why don’t you trust him?” he asked. There was no accusation, just exhaustion.

“Because I already made that mistake,” Dick replied, barely louder than a sigh.

“But he’s not a clone, Dickwing,” Jason exhaled. The words were weary, almost brittle. Dick’s gaze darkened at that, his brows contracting into a sharp, venomous glare as the words left him like poison.

“Ra’s can sink low enough,” he hissed, “if it means breaking us,” turning his head away, without any doubt he added.  “If I lose another brother, I’ll burn down everything in my way, and then I’ll burn myself. I swear it.”

Dick walked down the hallway, worry settling deep in his chest. A heavy unease gnawed at the back of his mind—an instinct he kept swatting away. Tim was with them. They’d found him.

It had been weeks since they’d carried his wounded, starved body back from Nanda Parbat. Bruce hadn’t left his side for days; same as Dick. Jason had returned to the manor too, hiding in the library, pretending he didn’t care. But Dick caught glimpses: Jason trailing behind Tim through rooms, glancing at him for long moments, as if wanting to say something, only to turn away, jaw clenched, faking disinterest as soon as Tim looked back at him.

Duke’s warm and youthful smile had returned, and he and Tim spent late evenings gaming together like real brothers. Damian lingered nearby like a silent shadow, observing them without a word, but staying close.

Everything looked right. Everyone was safe. And Dick should have felt relief.

But something inside him refused to settle.

Tim acted as though nothing had ever gone wrong between them. As if there had never been betrayal, hesitation, or doubt. As if he’d forgotten. Or worse—as if it hadn’t mattered to him at all.

But Dick remembered. He remembered the look in Tim’s eyes when Damian first walked into the Cave in the Robin suit. That betrayal was burned into Dick’s memory in a way he knew time would never fix.

His footsteps were muted as he moved down the corridor, only just back from patrol—boots barely skimming the wooden floorboards. The manor was quiet at this hour. A board beneath him creaked—sharp, splintered sound cutting through the silence—so Dick paused, holding his breath until the noise faded into stillness.

Before collapsing into bed, he wanted to check on his brothers. He did it every night. So did Bruce. So did Jason. They were all terrified of losing each other again.

Dick reached for Damian’s door, the one closest to Bruce’s, far from the others, and eased the handle down, opening it slowly to avoid the telltale groan of old hinges. The wood still complained in a soft scrape. He leaned in, eyes adjusting to the dim, and glanced toward the bed—

—then froze.

In the pale spill of moonlight, the dagger pressed to Damian’s throat glinted silver. Damian lay perfectly still, eyes open, not even attempting to struggle, as if he’d already accepted his fate. His green eyes were locked on Tim—on the boy leaning over him. And this time, in those blue eyes, Dick saw nothing.

No pain. No hatred. Not even the flicker of anger that could have driven him to this.

Because this was never their Tim.

They had been so desperate to believe they’d saved him, so eager for hope, that they let themselves be fooled. Let Ra’s manipulate them.

When Tim’s clone lifted his head to meet Dick’s gaze, his face remained utterly lifeless. Hollow. A doll made of flesh.

How could Dick have been so blind?

Heavy footsteps approached from behind. The clone’s hand twitched—just enough for the blade to dig deeper against Damian’s throat. Dick felt his heart drop into his stomach, his lungs searing with stolen air.

“Forgive me, Akhi,” Damian whispered, and before Dick could move, a gunshot split the silence.

The blade clattered to the floor. The soulless and still body followed right after.

And that night, Dick nearly lost Damian too because of his own weakness. Because truth was too painful to accept, and hope for birds was deadly.

That night, they all learned this lesson.

His back pressed against the door; breath caught in his throat as he stared at the shards scattered around Damian’s feet. Even now, he hadn’t been able to hurt him. Even now, cornered by a predator, Tim could feel the ache of his hunger, even if that hunger meant Tim’s own death.

He should have run. He should have hidden. He should have screamed. Anything. But his body refused to obey. The door behind him was locked—Damian had calculated Tim’s steps.

A faint crunch of glass broke the silence. Damian took a few steps forward, each one slow and deliberate, eyes fixed on Tim. Those sharp green irises pinned him in place, cold and steady enough to send a prickle of goosebumps down his arms.

This Damian frightened him. Not because he was angry, but because he wasn’t. He was calm. Quiet. Deadly. And he no longer looked at Tim as an opponent. That should have comforted him, and yet something about it only made the dread coil tighter in his stomach.

“…Forgive me, Akhi, but I can’t let you run again,” Damian’s voice was even, controlled, but Tim caught something frayed beneath the surface. Something close to desperation. But he didn’t have the time to parse feelings. None of that mattered in the picture forming before him.

He needed facts. He needed the truth. He needed to know the colors of the scene he’d been painted into.

“How did you find me?” Tim asked. This place was in no way connected to Tim Drake. He’d been certain the other him erased every thread that could tie his name here.

Damian tilted his head, lowering his chin and peering up at him from beneath his lashes. The motion was almost cautious—almost shy—and confusion flickered across Tim’s mind. Where had that come from? Where was the heir to the Demon’s Head? Where was Batman’s real son? Where was the child who once screamed at Tim that he didn’t belong in their family? That he didn’t deserve to be Robin?

“My clothes,” Damian said suddenly, eyes locked on Tim as if weighing how to swallow him whole.

Tim pressed harder against the door, nails digging into the wood, heart hammering like it would tear out of his chest. He cast a quick glance toward the window, the faint sunlight glinting off the edge of the sill.

“Your clothes?” he repeated, voice tight, barely above a whisper.

“The clothes you’re wearing,” Damian confirmed. Tim frowned, feeling a cold spike running down his spine. He clenched his sweatshirt to his chest, the fabric bunching beneath his fingers as unease crept up his throat under that unblinking stare.

Damian lifted his chin, stepping forward. The floorboards groaned beneath his boots, a slow, deliberate protest with each measured step, shrinking the space between them. Shrinking the gap between Tim and the window.

“There’s a bug sewn into them.”

Tim’s mind froze. His chest rose as he tried to inhale, but the air felt heavy and thick, refusing to enter his lungs. Panic clawed at his throat as he realized the simplicity of his mistake.

Idiot. How had he missed something so obvious? How could his vigilance have slipped so completely? He had hidden from cameras, avoided every person, erased every trace, but he hadn’t accounted for this.

“You…you sewed a bug into your own clothes?” Tim breathed, shaking slightly, trying to untangle the thought that raced through his mind.

It was his slip-up. He was too careless. Too impulsive. Batman would be disappointed in him. He already was. But Damian, who hated control, who defied Bruce at every turn, doing something like this? That was unthinkable. Especially given how meticulously Damian handled his belongings. Only Alfred was ever allowed to touch them.

Damian pressed his lips together, brow furrowed. Just like before, Tim thought, a flicker of memory cutting through him. The faint click of his boots against the floor echoed in the hallway.

“There were reasons,” Damian said, voice steady, yet heavy. Each word felt like it carried the weight of a warning: don’t push further, don’t pry, or you’ll uncover truths you aren’t ready for.

But he was Timothy Drake. Red Robin.

“So…you were kidnapped once, and Bruce decided to play it safe?” Tim spat, bitterness dripping into his voice. Because he had never mattered to them to do this, even after he had been kidnapped as Timothy Drake so many times.

“No,” Damian replied, stepping closer. The floor creaked under the weight, a soft rhythm that matched the thudding in Tim’s chest. Three meters separated them, and Tim instinctively stepped aside, trying to create any space he could. Damian’s gaze followed him, but he didn’t move back. “They kidnapped you. And I put the bug in there myself. Because…” Damian’s voice faltered. A shadow of doubt flickered across his face, just for a moment. This wasn’t the Damian Tim had known. “I thought you had come back. I was afraid of losing the family.”

But Tim was never a family.

He could feel the air around them chill, the faint draft from the window brushing his shoulder. His pulse raced, sweat prickling at his hairline, breath shallow. Every instinct screamed to move, to flee, but his legs felt locked in place. He was trapped, and yet, he couldn’t look away.

With every word Damian spoke, more questions swirled in Tim’s head. He couldn’t see the connection. Or perhaps it was so unreal, so impossible, that his mind simply refused to grasp it.

But through it all, Tim caught the most important detail—he had been kidnapped. Most likely by Ra’s. And he had returned…or had he? Was it really him, or just another strategy of Ra’s game? This went beyond anything Tim had faced before.

“Who else can track us?” Tim demanded sharply, trying to cut through the fog of confusion. If Damian had found him, then the others…

“No one,” Damian shrugged, calm but firm. “No one knows about the bugs. Maybe Pennyworth. But only I can track them.”

“Bugs?” Tim’s eyes went wide. How many were there? How many on him were right now? His hands trembled as he nervously slipped them under his sweatshirt, running them along the seams, feeling for any hidden devices.

A soft, mocking chuckle broke the silence. Tim froze and then slowly lifted his gaze from his sweater, catching the faintest smirk on Damian’s face.

And in Damian’s eyes…there was a flicker of tenderness, and grief. Too much for Tim to bear. He averted his gaze, ashamed to see it, ashamed of how it tore at him. His mind was playing tricks on him. This was not his Damian.

Tim felt lost. His thoughts tangled like a knot he couldn’t unravel. He didn’t know what to believe anymore. He wanted to go home. He wanted to be left alone.

“Don’t worry, Timothy. There’s only one on you. At the back of the collar,” Damian said lightly, almost casually. Tim’s hands immediately moved to the nape of his neck, fingers brushing against the faint, almost imperceptible bug.

“The others…in the clothes of the rest,” Damian added. Tim frowned.

“If they don’t even know about it, what is the point in them?” His nails dug into the fabric, trying to pry the bug free, but each attempt was futile. It was stitched in. Sewn in tight.

“Because I did it for you,” Damian said. Tim didn’t notice him approach until he was just a step away. A jolt ran through him. He fell still, dropping his hands and lifting his gaze. Damian had grown. He was as tall as Dick, maybe even slightly taller. Tim felt a pang of jealousy.

Slowly, keeping his hands visible, Damian pulled a small, sharp blade from his pocket. It caught the dim light, glinting like a sentence passed. Here it is. Right now, it could cut his throat. This could be it. And for the first time, that thought brought Tim a strange, chilling sense of calm. He was ready.

“When I thought we had brought you back, I stitched bugs into the clothing of anyone who gave it to you. You often wore Todd’s red sweater, saying it was the warmest,” Damian continued.

No. Tim didn’t remember that. He couldn’t. It couldn’t have happened.

The blade slid toward his throat, carrying a cool whisper of metal against skin. Tim shut his eyes, finally letting out a breath. Finally, he will finish his job, he thought briefly. But the calm he hoped for never came.

Damian’s hands slipped behind his collar in one fluid motion, cutting into his sweater and pulling out the bug. He held it out to Tim, who felt his pulse stumble, shock rooting him to the spot. His fingers moved automatically toward it, as if in disbelief, eyes darting first to the small device, then with hesitation to Damian as if the whole thing were some cruel joke.

“Why?” Tim whispered, barely moving his lips.

Damian tucked the blade away and stepped back, keeping his hands visible.

“Because it’s the only way I can convince you I’m on your side,” he said simply.

“Why should I trust you?” Tim’s teeth clenched, baring his fangs. Damian wanted him to trust him? Preposterous.

Damian’s gaze hardened, reminding Tim who was a predator here. Damian straightened his shoulders, adopting the soldier’s stance, hands clasped behind his back, as though mocking Tim.

“Because otherwise you’re alone, Akhi.”

Every word resonated in Tim’s mind, echoing again and again, like a broken record.

The truth had always been worse than lies. Tim shuddered at how sharp it cut this time. Damian pressed an invisible dagger into his chest, letting him bleed as the voices of his family taunted him in his mind.

But Tim had long accepted this truth. Cruel as it was, he had embraced it the moment he became Robin.

It had always been his condition.

He straightened his back, stretching to his full height, trying to appear taller, stronger, but no matter how hard he tried, Damian still looked down at him, making him feel small.

“Why should I trust you, heir of the Demon’s Head?” Tim ground his teeth, and a fire flared in his chest. Damian flinched at the words, fists clenching, but what came next snuffed out that fire, leaving only ruins and the bitter tang of ash behind.

“Because I killed Ra’s al Ghul. I killed my grandfather… for you, Akhi.”

Room fell into dead silence. Tim’s knees felt weak. His heart pounded so loud it almost drowned out the distant city sounds—the faint hum of traffic, the clatter of shutters in the wind, the drip of water somewhere in the building. Yet in that moment, all of it fell away.

Tim blinked, tasting iron on his tongue, the scent of sweat and tension hanging in the air. He swallowed hard, the sound loud in the quiet room, and asked, almost hopelessly.

“Then why is he alive?”

Damian’s dark pupils swallowed the green of his irises, and a palpable sense of danger radiated off him. Tim instinctively took a sharp step back, knees trembling, heart hammering as if it wanted to escape his chest. Every nerve was on edge.

“Because Deathstroke saved him,” Damian cut fiercely, and Tim could feel disgust and annoyance in his words. “He threw him into Lazarus.”

It seemed killing Ra’s al Ghul was almost impossible.

“I will kill him again if he ever touches you, Akhi.”

“Where could he have gone?” Dick exhaled, leaning against the roof of the car. The metal was cold beneath his palms, helping him to ground himself. He squeezed his eyes shut. They had no idea. No lead. Nothing.

“We should loop in Barbara,” Jason said. He stood by the hood, idly rolling the car keys between his fingers—metal clicking against metal in a steady rhythm. “I doubt we can handle this without her.”

When it came to Tim, plans and strategies were pointless.

For a moment—just a moment—Dick almost considered calling Clark. Almost. But the Justice League was out of reach. Bruce had grown cold and more distant after Tim’s death; whatever softness he had rotted away. He pulled back from the League, showing up only when he had no other choice, refusing to let any of them step foot in Gotham.

And Dick…wasn’t any better.

None of them knew how to handle the loss. And because of that, their ties to the other heroes had frayed, then snapped.

Dick didn’t feel like a hero anymore. After Wally’s death, maybe he should never have come back to this life at all.

Maybe their fate had been written long ago, inked in blood on paper, and the youngest birds, the ones who had barely learned to fly, always died first.

“Dicking,” Jason called. Dick lifted his head, turning toward him with a questioning look. “Looks like our grumpy gremlin found our chaos gremlin,” Jason said, flicking his chin toward his phone.

Dick shot upright instantly, snatching the phone from his hand. His breath caught in his throat when he read the screen:

Timothy does not wish to see you. I will keep him safe. We are going to breach Father’s system.

Dot. Dot. Dot. Dry facts, sterile words, and Dick felt his heartbeat spike painfully in his ribs.

“What do you mean they’re going to breach Bruce’s system?!” Dick demanded, eyes wide with disbelief and fear. Jason plucked the phone back from him, shrugging with grim ease.

“I assume we’ll find out soon enough.”

He watched the training from above, frowning as he studied the assassins of the League. Only the strongest survived here—any sign of weakness meant death. Names were meaningless. Nicknames were forbidden. Every movement, every strike, every silent landing was honed to lethal perfection.

The League was like a shadow stalking its prey—appearing from nowhere, disappearing just as quickly. Each assassin moved with the precision of a predator, muscles coiled, senses alert to the slightest sound. Even Slade, a killer in his own right, couldn’t help but admire the deadly efficiency of Ra’s army. And yet, he cared little for this display of power or the games of control behind it. He had his own mission.

“You look grim, Deathstroke,” Ra’s said, stepping beside him. His expression was icy, hands clasped behind his back, the weight of his presence filling the space with heavy authority.

Slade crossed his arms, keeping his face unreadable.

“This takes too long, Ra’s.”

“Patience, my friend,” Ra’s replied smoothly, a dangerous glint in his eyes. “Soon, we will both have what we seek.”

Even as Ra’s smiled sharply, despite his unshakable confidence and madness, Slade couldn’t shake the gnawing doubts already taking root in the pit of his stomach. They both knew how slippery the birds were.

Five years had passed. Five years, and he still hadn’t reached his goal. Perhaps he and Ra’s were playing the same game, but they were rolling different dice, moving their figures on the same board but in different directions.

“Your plans for an heir are none of my concern,” Slade said, turning to face him, staring straight into the madman’s toxic eyes. “I hope you remember that, Head of the Demon.”

“Don’t worry, my dear friend,” Ra’s replied smoothly, his cloak swishing as he turned away. “You will have your bird. After all, who better than I to understand the desire for a true successor?”

Ra’s left the hall silently, leaving Slade alone. A faint, predatory, and crooked smile curved the corners of his lips, and a dark and low laugh escaped his chest.

Ra’ thought Slade was playing by his rules, but the mercenary had other plans. And it was for this reason that he had saved the Demon when his grandson drove a blade through his heart.

Because Ra’s al Ghul was supposed to die at the hand of another bird. A bird that very soon will be his.
