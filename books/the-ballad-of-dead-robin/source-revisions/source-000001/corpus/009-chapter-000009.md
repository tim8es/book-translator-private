# Chapter 8: I'm Sorry, Robin

Chapter Notes

When everything is falling apart, at least I got my AO3 account.

The price of trust is too high.

Tim watched the entrance of the mall from the parking lot, scrutinizing everyone who came and went. A woman with a stroller. Young. Wearing second-hand clothes. No ring. Single mother. Then a man in athletic wear and sunglasses. Well-off. Owns a business. Probably real estate. Watch on his right wrist. Left-handed.

Time passed like this until he noticed Damian emerging from a more discreet side entrance on the right, hiding his face under a cap and carrying two bags of belongings.

Tim’s heart skipped a beat, and he pressed himself deeper into the seat, hoping, in some impossible way, to dissolve into the shadows with him.

A mistake. It was a mistake. He should have run the moment Damian left him alone.

The driver’s side door swung open, and in the next second, the bags landed on his lap, jolting Tim out of his focus.

“What’s this?” he asked, pressing his lips together and narrowing his eyes at Damian, who started the car without a word, steering out of the parking lot.

“Clothes. Food. First-aid kit,” he said evenly. “Your wounds reopened.”

How he knew, Tim had no idea. He himself had only realized after leaving his apartment that his bandages were soaked. But he was wearing dark clothes—it shouldn’t have been visible.

Damian wasn’t supposed to see his weakness.

“I don’t need this,” Tim said dryly, grabbing the bags and tossing them back. He was certain Damian would react in anger, call him a child, or just leave him somewhere in the middle of the road. Tim almost…hoped he would.

But Damian just kept driving silently, not uttering a single word. He was simply following Tim’s instructions like a guard dog, and the thought struck him as absurdly amusing. Tim twisted his lips into a mocking smirk, letting his sarcasm spill freely.

“Who would’ve thought that Damian Wayne, the true son of Batman, would follow the orders of someone he considered unworthy to even bear the name Robin so…selflessly.”

He hoped for a reaction. He held his breath, half-expecting a blade at his throat, but Damian didn’t even glance at him, keeping his eyes fixed on the road. And it confused Tim. It infuriated him. Tim couldn’t figure out what the hell Damian wanted from him. He slammed his hand against the door, knuckles whitening, the force reverberating through his arm. His voice tore out, laced with frustration and venom, as if every scream could batter the walls down.

“Just say something!” Despite the fury in his words, he could hear the underlying desperation in his own voice, and it made him nauseous.

“Don’t let them see your emotions, Timothy. They are your weakness.”

But Tim was on the edge of hysteria. Doubts twisted into a knot in his mind, tangling tighter with every passing second, refusing to unravel.

“You always had something to say before,” Tim snapped, digging his nails into the flesh of his legs through the denim of his jeans, feeling the muted sting that, somehow, helped suppress the rising hysteria. Damian’s gaze flicked to the movement, and that familiar crease appeared between his brows, betraying his displeasure. But instead of anger or disdain, Tim saw a foreign, almost uneasy concern in those green eyes. And across his face flickered a trace of regret.

“I used to envy your skills,”—this wasn’t the kind of admission Tim had hoped to hear. Not at all. And suddenly, a sticky fear wrapped around his body, sending shivers through him. “My grandfather always said the strongest survives. I felt threatened.”

“Threatened?” Tim echoed, incredulous, unable to grasp how their conversation had twisted into this kind of confession. Damian nodded.

“You were stronger. You were smarter. You’re still smarter than all of us,” he paused, and Tim’s mind began spinning with questions. Could he have been exposed to some toxin? Or was this an illusion? Was someone manipulating his perception? Maybe this was all just a dream? But Damian looked too real. And no matter how hard Tim tried, the pain in his leg didn’t help him wake up. “I’ve always admired you, Akhi. That’s why I tried to prove I was better. I wanted to become the same kind of Robin as you. But I didn’t know I could become one without killing my predecessor. That’s how you became Robin. After Todd’s death, proving your abilities to yo…my father. I wanted to become the same kind of Robin as you.”

And Tim understood. Truly understood. Because he had always dreamed of following Jason’s steps. Becoming a hero he would be proud of.

They had all become victims of this bloodthirsty legacy.

“I regret letting my grandfather manipulate me. Forgive me, Akhi. Forgive me for my weakness.”

And Tim had nothing to say. Damian had been just as much a pawn in Ra’s al Ghul’s hands as he.

And perhaps, his Damian was, too.

They hadn’t even made it through the manor doors when Bruce’s silhouette emerged at the top of the staircase, carved in shadow, his broad shoulders rigid and posture impossibly still, radiating authority. His eyes bore down on them like blades, heavy enough to pin them to the floor.

“I told you to keep an eye on him,” his voice was cold and distant. It wasn’t anger. No. It was disappointment. And for a split second Jason felt fifteen years younger, felt the old instinct curl up in his gut—the instinct to explain himself to Batman. But he wasn’t Robin anymore. And he didn’t bother to mask his scowl; his lips curled back to bare his teeth.

“Sure you did. You dumped the slipperiest kid on us and vanished like a coward,” he saw Bruce flinch, just a twitch, barely there, and it was intoxicating. “Should I remind you he walked right out of your house? If I were you, Bruce, I’d take another look at your security system.”

“Jay…” Dick murmured beside him, a warm and grounding hand settling on his shoulder, but Jason shook it off, stepping forward. The air between him and Bruce crackled, their breaths fogging faintly in the cool hall. Bruce stood a few steps above them, clearly having come down from his studio, the light behind him casting sharp angles across his face.

“No, Dick.” Jason’s voice snapped like a struck match. “He doesn’t get to blame us for losing Tim. Not when he did everything in his power to make Tim never want to set foot in this house again.”

Jason was furious. Beyond furious. If Tim wouldn’t defend himself against Bruce, then Jason would. He owed him that much.

“You expected him to sit still and behave? Bad news for you, Brucie. You never really knew your son.”

“He’s not my son,” Bruce cut in, stepping down onto the final stair and stopping right in front of Jason.

They were nearly the same height, but Jason still felt Bruce’s presence trying to crush him flat, like a silent hand pressed to the back of his skull, trying to force his head down. It only fed the fire roaring in his chest.

“Was he ever?” Jason snarled. His vision tinged sickly green around the edges, as if the manor lights themselves warped with his heartbeat. The air tasted metallic, bitter with adrenaline. His pulse hammered against his teeth, fingers curling so tight his knuckles cracked. “You taught him how to fight. Taught him how to handle weapons. Taught him not to trust a single living soul and to follow nothing but his instincts.” Jason’s voice dripped venom, each word sharpened to a blade. “Funny how you forgot to teach him how to be a son!”

He shoved Bruce with an open palm hardly. The impact reverberated up Jason’s arm, and Bruce staggered, boots scuffing against polished wood. He didn’t block. Didn’t even raise a hand. He just stood there and took it.

Jason stepped in again, closing the space with a predator’s certainty, fisting the front of Bruce’s shirt. The fabric bunched beneath his fingers, warm from Bruce’s skin, and Jason dragged him close enough to breathe the stale coffee and burnt ozone smell of the Batcave on him.

“And you forgot to teach him,” Jason hissed through clenched teeth, every syllable a slow puncture wound, “that the worth of his life isn’t measured by what kind of Robin he was.”

Jason pressed right into the wound, without hesitation and without mercy. He saw the pain flicker in Bruce’s eyes, the way his mouth thinned into a hard line and his brows dipped, as though the words physically landed a punch.

Jason snorted. He jerked Bruce closer by the shirt, then shoved him away. Bruce stumbled back, heel catching on the stair, and only avoided falling because his hand snapped out and gripped the railing.

“You want to drown in your own misery and grief? Be my guest. But don’t you dare lay a finger on Tim unless you’re ready to lose your head,” because Jason would gladly draw a weapon for his baby bird. And if that meant threatening Batman himself, so be it.

“It’d be great if everyone’s heads stayed right where they are,” Dick exhaled as he approached them, footsteps soft as ghosting cloth on marble. This time, though, he kept distance between himself and Jason, but he shot Jason a warning look first. Jason answered with a disdainful snort and folded his arms across his chest, as if closing armor plates over his ribs.

Then Dick’s gaze shifted becoming darker, and burning with danger, carrying the weight of something that made Jason’s skin prickle. It was the cold that held Jason’s anger by the throat, and it began to settle, reluctant but trapped.

“I don’t agree with Jason completely,” Dick continued in a low but firm voice, not looking away from Bruce. “And I still don’t trust this Tim. But I’m also not going to stand by while your choices tear this family apart, Bruce.”

Jason wrinkled his nose at that and turned away with theatrical exaggeration, missing entirely the faint upward curl at the corner of Dick’s mouth—the quiet amusement at his performance.

“Besides,” Dick added, tone cooling once more, “Damian is with Tim right now. You trust your own son…don’t you, Bruce?”

And in those words there was a challenge, as if Dick had simply been waiting for Bruce to make a mistake. Jason caught the flicker of surprise in Bruce’s eyes.

“Damian?” Bruce breathed, disbelief scraping his voice raw. And who could blame him? Maybe Damian was the only one who never pointed a gun at Tim, never tried to keep him in a cage, never attacked him screaming that he wasn’t family. But remembering their history, remembering Damian’s attempts to rid himself of Tim entirely, the idea didn’t make sense. It just didn’t fit in the picture.

“He remembers him,” Duke’s voice unexpectedly cut in from above. He appeared at the top of the stairs in casual clothes, descending quietly. His presence eased the room immediately. Jason’s shoulders unclenched a fraction, Dick’s tension dropped from his frame, and the deep crease between his brows softened. Duke stopped a few steps from them, gaze sweeping across all three weighing the situation. “He doesn’t remember me,” Duke continued calmly. “And he has no reason to trust me.” A beat of stillness. “He doesn’t have a reason to trust any of us.”

“Except for the gremlin?” Jason arched a brow, disbelief written clear across his face. His voice had bite, but his shoulders stiffened as if bracing for an answer he might not want.

Duke shook his head once, fixing Jason with that calm, steady gaze of his—the kind that kept them all tethered to what little sanity they had left.

“I doubt he trusts him,” Duke said. “It’s more like…a coping mechanism. Tim knows what to expect from him. Or he thinks he does. An illusion of control.”

He spoke clinically, but midway through his explanation the line between his brows deepened and his jaw clenched. His gaze hardened on Jason.

“I rewatched the footage,” Duke continued, breath drawing tight. “When you put a gun to his head, he didn’t even try to resist. And what he said after—” Duke’s throat bobbed as he swallowed, eyes flicking away for a second. “I can’t be certain, but it reads like passive suicidal behavior.”

“So he’s with Damian because he’s hoping he’ll finish the job?” Jason’s voice cracked at the end, the question rasping out of him. A cold ripple crawled down his spine, and his hand flexed against his thigh as if trying to shake it off. They could have lost him. They could lose him again.

Dick didn’t look any better. His face had drained of color, lips parted as though the breath had been punched out of him. His hand hovered aimlessly at his side, reaching for something grounding, before falling uselessly.

Bruce, meanwhile, went completely still. The muscle in his jaw jumped once, twice. His eyes unfocused, staring through Jason like he was replaying every frame in his mind, searching for evidence, for reasons, for the moment he failed. And Jason hoped he would find it.

“It’s not that he wants it,” Duke finally said, exhaling slowly. He shifted his weight and leaned against the banister, fingers dragging tiredly through his hair. “It’s what he expects. That’s different.” He paused, gaze lowering to the polished floorboards. “And if I’m right, I’m the last person who should go after him.”

There was a shadow of frustration on Duke’s face, and Jason understood it well. Tim and Duke really had been like brothers, even if only for a short time. They were close enough in age to share the same interests, the same problems and insecurities. Tim’s antisocial quiet and Duke’s quiet empathy were two sides of the same coin.

Just like Jason and Dick.

Where Jason’s anger burned hot and violent, Dick’s was colder than the peak of any iceberg.

“And who among us should go to him, then?” Dick asked softly, still looking half like a ghost. Knowing him, Jason was sure Dick was trying to deny everything he had just heard. Logic and emotion never mixed, and his brother was a perfect example of that proof.

Duke’s gaze slid toward Bruce, and for a moment time froze.

“Oh, for fuck’s sake…” Jason muttered, shutting his eyes. He could already feel in his bones how badly this could end.

“You can’t be serious, Duke,” Dick said, sounding just as unwilling. But Duke only lifted a shoulder in a quiet shrug, looking not even remotely disturbed by the implication.

“You don’t have to believe me,” Duke said, still staring at Bruce, “but I’m telling you what I see.”

Bruce finally unfroze, meeting Duke’s gaze with the same sharp intensity, dissecting every word.

“Tim won’t trust any of you—don’t even hope for that. But Tim relies on instinct first, long before he relies on emotions. And he knows that out of all of us, Bruce is the one most capable of helping him. He also knows that, to Bruce, he was just a soldier, which puts Bruce in the only position where he has never betrayed Tim.”

A flicker of pain crossed Bruce’s face, brief but unmistakable. Jason didn’t feel any satisfaction this time. If anything, every word Duke spoke twisted something inside him, scraping raw nerve over bone.

“Dick, the first brother he ever claimed, betrayed him. Jason, the one he looked up to as his hero, tried to kill him. He has no memory of me.” Duke’s voice dipped, shoulders tense, fingers unconsciously curling against the railing. “That only leaves Damian and Bruce.”

“In simpler terms,” Bruce muttered, his voice frayed at the edges, “positive emotions were drowned out by negative memories. But when there are  no positive emotions to begin with…”

“…He has nothing to lose,” Duke nodded.

Jason’s head spun from the flood of thoughts. He only wanted to get his baby bird back and make sure he was okay.

“It’s simple math. Multiply a plus by a minus and you get a minus. But multiply a minus by a minus and you get a plus.”

“How did we even get to math?” Jason grumbled, rubbing his temples. A headache was beginning to press harder and harder against his thoughts. Lifting his gaze to Duke, he asked, “How sure are you Tim won’t bolt the second he sees Bruce?”

“Not at all,” Duke replied plainly, still feigning indifference, though Jason noticed his eyes darken and his muscles tense. Calm on the outside, maybe, but only on the surface. “But if Bruce keeps a cool head, I’m confident we have a chance to bring him back without using force.”

Jason clenched his fists. He hoped…God, he hoped they wouldn’t have to chase Tim down. Because Jason already knew who would lose that game.

“We need Zatanna’s help,” Dick announced suddenly, and Jason caught his sharp look at Bruce, whose face had gone blank. “We don’t even know if this is magic or tech-related situation.” Dick tilted his head, considering, then added more quietly. “For someone so vehemently against this Tim being here, you’re not even trying to send him back where he belongs. Kind of hypocritical of you, Bruce.”

The air grew tense, and Jason could practically see sparks crackle between them. But just as Bruce opened his mouth, probably to offer some excuse for his inaction, his phone blared with a security breach alert.

Bruce frowned at the screen, then lifted his chin, and Jason immediately recognized the weighted stare hiding behind the mask of the Dark Knight. It made all of them tense on instinct.

“Someone breached the security system at Wayne Tower.”

Jason suddenly relaxed and exchanged a quick look with Dick. Maybe this was their chance. Dick returned his cold gaze to Bruce, grudgingly admitting,

“This is our shot.”

But even as they reached a silent agreement, Jason couldn’t shake the dread gnawing at him—Duke’s words still echoing.

If Tim wasn’t fighting for his own life, then Jason sure as hell would fight for him.

He hadn’t saved his baby bird before, but he could at least save this one. Even if it meant starting a war.

Tim stared intently at the building, not seeing any drastic changes. Maybe a few more cameras—reasonable enough, considering it was the Wayne Tower. He had always told Bruce that his security system needed an upgrade.

“Why are we here?” Damian asked suddenly, his gaze tracking the tower that technically belonged to him.

“There’s something I need,” Tim didn’t mention the video, nor the pieces of information he was after—those stayed with him. A temporary alliance did not equal trust. “I need a phone,” he added, turning toward Damian.

Without a word, Damian unlocked his phone and held it out. Tim frowned at that, but didn’t comment. If Damian wanted to play the good older brother, Tim was perfectly willing to play along. The game would end sooner or later, and he intended to be ready when it did.

A phone wasn’t the ideal tool for breaching Bruce’s system, but it was enough to slip in unnoticed. All he needed was ten minutes. Fifteen at most.

His fingers moved automatically, lines of code spilling across the screen—an override designed to swap out the surveillance feed for older footage, stitching it seamlessly into the system. Thirty minutes of buffer before anyone noticed. Even if they did catch the tampering, Tim would remain invisible; the script would erase itself in thirty minutes, leaving nothing but a clean slate.

Handing the phone back to Damian, Tim stepped toward the tower’s entrance, eyes tracking the security guards stationed inside—the very ones he’d have to slip past.

“Timothy,” Damian called. Tim stopped sharply, shooting him a questioning look, only to be met with a raised brow of mild amusement. “You didn’t forget who’s in front of you, did you?”

Despite the hint of mockery, the words lacked that familiar arrogance—the old proclamation of being Bruce Wayne’s rightful heir.

“You’re coming with me?” For a second, Tim froze, unsure how to react. Then he shook his head subtly, pushing emotion aside. Later. Information came first.

“Of course, Timothy,” Damian said. He stepped close, settling his cap over Tim’s hair. His palm brushed down Tim’s spine lightly, and Tim felt his breath hitch, forcing himself not to flinch, before Damian gave a gentle push forward. “Just stay behind me.”

Through the shade of the cap, Tim watched Damian’s posture shift—relaxed but commanding, shoulders loose, footsteps deliberate. When they approached the guards, Damian didn’t even look at them.

“He’s with me.”

No one so much as twitched. Without a single question, the guards stepped aside and let them through.

They headed straight for the elevator. Tim stood rigid beside Damian, waiting, counting the seconds, until the doors finally slid open. He couldn’t shake the sickening feeling crawling beneath his skin; without his suit and without any weapon, he felt exposed, stripped bare, open to any attack from any direction.

If Damian noticed his paranoia, he didn’t comment. He just walked inside as if it were the most mundane thing in the world.

The moment they were enclosed in the elevator, Tim hit for the top floor. Or almost the top. You could only reach the uppermost level from there. He could only hope the Tim in the video meant this place. Doubt gnawed at him—the thought that the tower might have changed, that the layout wasn’t as familiar as it looked. Even if it resembled the building he remembered, there was no guarantee they were identical.

The numbers climbed in steady increments, each soft beep hitting like a pulse against his skull. Time stretched thin, and the realization struck him with icy clarity: he was trapped in an elevator with Damian. Damian, who had tried to kill him. Who wanted him dead.

Tim dragged in a slow breath, trying to gather his thoughts before they scattered. He could feel eyes burning on his back, assessing him, but when he risked a glance, Damian just stood there, silent, taking his place behind him.

Like a guard dog, Tim thought bitterly. Or a hellhound waiting for the command to strike.

The moment the elevator doors slid open, Tim bolted out, relief washing over him as he realized everything was exactly where it should be. It was a bar. That bar—the one where Tim always ordered coffee. Everyone who worked here knew about it, but since it was nearly at the very top floor, most preferred the cafés lower down.

Tim, however, often enjoyed the quiet and calm up here. Especially when he wanted to hide from everyone.

Now there were only a few people inside, speaking softly over their coffees at a table. God, what Tim wouldn’t give for that right now. Shaking his head, he stopped by the wall and carefully scanned the room.

Southern harbor. That was the code. And Tim had found the right place. But where was the flash drive?

Tim’s gaze swept across the space, catching on the windows that stretched across the entire wall.

Harbor.

Could it be…?

Tim stepped forward, sensing Damian follow right behind him without missing a beat. The view from the window overlooked the harbor, but Tim couldn’t see any clues nearby. Not on the floor, not on the ceiling. Not even a single plant stood anywhere close. Just a few tables and chairs, and it would be stupid to hide anything where it could be moved.

Southern harbor. But why south? Could it be a double code? Tim bit down on his lip, trying to figure out where to look.

Think, Tim. Think.

South. South. Why South? Why not encrypted coordinates?

A compass always pointed north. Always north, unless this was a puzzle where south was north. Which meant—

North was South.

Tim spun sharply in the opposite direction, fixing his gaze on the wall lined with shelves stacked with newspapers and a few decorative statues. Interior design. And even if the items weren’t the same as the ones he remembered, the shelves were still in their place.

He approached them, fingertips gliding over the wooden texture, carefully tapping along the inside of one shelf, then another. Still nothing. Disappointment and confusion pricked at him, but when he reached the last shelf—the one at the very bottom crammed with old newspapers—he felt a softer patch on one side. Plywood.

No one would replace rich wood with plain plywood.

Tim extended his hand toward Damian, not even looking at him and muttering barely above a whisper.

“Blade.”

Without a word, Damian handed him the dagger, standing close and watching carefully as Tim began cutting into the shelf. They were hidden from prying eyes behind the couch and the plants, but even if someone had noticed them, Tim doubted anyone would dare question Damian Wayne.

Still, he had no intention of lingering here.

Cutting through the plywood and prying out a section, he immediately spotted an outlet. He opened it without trouble, noticing right away that the wires had been cut. Sliding his hand inside, he felt with relief the the flash drive he was looking for, and pulled it out, rising to his feet. A faint smile touched his lips, but it vanished the instant he remembered the time.

“We need to go,” he said, turning toward the elevator, and froze.

Bruce Wayne was standing directly in front of him, his gaze unreadable yet burning into Tim, who stepped back, instinctively reaching for his neck, feeling phantom touches of fingers on his skin. Bruce noticed. Of course, he noticed the mistake. Tim swallowed, lowering his trembling hand, the other clenching the blade tightly.

He couldn’t get away from Batman. He knew that perfectly well.

“Tim,” Bruce said, sternly, taking a step toward him. Tim stepped back. In the next moment, a broad back blocked his view. Damian. He shielded Tim from Bruce’s gaze, and Bruce stopped, frowning down at his son. “Damian.”

“Father.”

It was like a circle of predators, each one circling the other, waiting for the first move. Damian stood unmoving, clearly maintaining his role as the protective brother. But Tim had no patience for that. On his shoulders rested the weight of the death of the Tim they knew—their Tim. So why couldn’t they just leave him alone? He wasn’t a family, for fuck’s sake!

“I think we need to talk,” Bruce said, his gaze sliding past Damian’s shoulder directly to Tim, who instinctively clutched the edge of Damian’s jacket, pressing himself tighter against his back. Surprise flickered across Bruce’s eyes, and Tim understood. He, too, was startled by his own reaction, but between the two of them, Damian inspired some small shred of security.

“Timothy doesn’t want to talk to you,” Damian said flatly, straightening to his full height, chin raised in a cold, defiant line. “I told Todd and Grayson to stay out of this.”

“That’s not how this works, Damian,” Bruce exhaled wearily, rubbing the bridge of his nose. Tim’s eyes darted to the staircase beside the elevator. “He’s not—”

Bruce didn’t get to finish. Tim bolted, slipping past Damian. Damian lunged to grab him, but the fabric of Tim’s shirt slid through his fingers, and Tim’s heart hammered as he sprinted down the stairs.

“Timothy!”

“Stop!”

Tim knew. He knew he had little time.

He barreled down the stairs, leaping over multiple steps at once, every muscle coiled for speed. The flash drive was shoved deep into his pocket, his other hand gripping the blade like a lifeline. He grabbed the railing, pushing off it to gain momentum, hurtling forward faster.

Footsteps thundered behind him. Angry and frustrated voices followed, but he couldn’t make out the words.

He knew there was no place for him among them.

He knew they hated him.

He knew he could trust no one but himself.

“You’re not alone, Robin, because I’m with you,”

Tim was gasping for air. His ears rang. His wounds screamed, bleeding through the bandages and soaking into his clothes.

He kept running—down, down, down—stumbling over the stairs more than once, nearly pitching forward face-first before catching himself on the railing at the last second. But the next moment, a door on the floor below swung open, and Jason stepped out, his eyes immediately locking onto Tim.

Tim froze between two levels, mouth open and dragging in air, frantic eyes scanning for any escape. But there was none.

Above him stood Damian, and behind him, the silhouette of Bruce loomed closer.

Below stood Jason.

He was trapped between the floors. Between the bats.

“Kid,” Jason breathed, taking a step up toward him. Tim’s lungs seized. Cold steel ghosted against his throat, biting into the skin without mercy. His body trembled, thoughts collapsing into a single spiraling loop with no exit. Memories flashed before his eyes like jagged shards, and Ra’s cold, echoing voice rang through his mind.

“They betrayed you. They abandoned you. They killed you, detective. So what are you clinging to now?”

Tim saw the toxic green swirling, felt the widening horror in the eyes before him, and barely recognized his own voice as it rasped hoarsely from his lips.

“One more step and I swear you’ll be dragging a corpse back to the Cave.”

Because Tim Drake was tired.

Tired of proving he was worthy.

Tired of doubting himself.

Tired of doubting them.

He was so fucking tired…

I am sorry, Robin, I couldn’t fight for you.

Because in the end, he was alone.
