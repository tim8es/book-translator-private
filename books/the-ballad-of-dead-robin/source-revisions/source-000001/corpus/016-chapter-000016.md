There was only one thought in his head. Make it in time. He had to.

He tore through Ra’s men like a predator let loose, carving a path of blood behind him. Blow after blow. His escrima sticks flashed, slick and darkened with red, mirroring the colour of his suit. The air rang with the crack of bone and the dull thud of bodies hitting stone. He didn’t hear Bruce shouting orders behind him. It didn’t matter.

He kept moving forward, silent, relentless, like a soldier on autopilot. He had one mission.

Tim.

He had to bring Tim home.

Dick forced his way through the crowd, leaving the others behind. His gaze snapped upward, and there he was. Tim’s familiar silhouette, back slammed against a window high above. Dick’s jaw clenched as he yanked out his grappling hook. The moment glass spiderwebbed and shattered, the moment Tim’s body tipped backward into empty air, Dick fired.

The line went taut, and he was airborne in a heartbeat.

He caught Tim around the waist and pulled him hard against his chest, the impact knocking the breath from them both as they swung. Wind screamed past them. Dick could feel Tim’s heartbeat—fast, frantic, real—vibrating through his own ribs. He could see the shock in those blue eyes, the same eyes that had haunted his dreams for the past five years.

But this wasn’t a dream where Tim fell and never got back up.

This time, Tim was real. His baby bird was alive.

And Dick would burn the world down before letting that change.

“Looks like I owe you a few flying lessons, Baby Bird,” he said, forcing a careless grin that didn’t reach his eyes.

Inside, his blood boiled—cold, vicious fury coiling tight in his chest. His gaze flicked upward to Ra’s, standing at the shattered window of his study, shirt torn by the wind, staring down at them with naked displeasure.

Dick touched down on the ground but still kept a firm hold on Tim. He had no intention of letting his baby bird out of his sight while they were surrounded by assassins. He had no intention of ever losing sight of Tim again.

They stood in the middle of the training grounds. The space was littered with obstacles—training dummies, broken stone, scarred platforms—and from the corner of his eye, Dick also noted a weapons storage area, racks of steel glinting dully in the open air. Dust hung low, disturbed by the recent chaos, and the smell of blood and iron clung to the cold mountain air.

His gaze snapped back upward, to where Ra’s had been standing. He was gone.

Dick tensed instantly, pulling Tim closer, his arms tightening around him. Tim’s fingers dug into Dick’s suit, nails scraping against the armor as he tried to push him away.

“You can let me go now,” Tim breathed, exhausted when he failed to break free, lifting his head to fix Dick with a stubborn, defiant look.

“Hm,” Dick hummed, scanning their surroundings with sharp focus. Gunfire cracked behind them, knives rang as they met steel, but he knew the others could handle themselves. His attention slid back to Tim, only now, there was no smile on his face. His voice was steady, edged with steel and determination. “No.”

In an instant, Tim’s body went rigid. That familiar, glacial calm flashed through his eyes—the look that hid calculations three steps ahead. Dick could feel the tension coiling under his hands, the readiness to break free by force. He knew how slippery his baby brother could be.

But unlike the rest of them, Tim had no suit. No weapons.

He was exposed and vulnerable in the lands of al Ghul.

“Jay!” Dick shouted, glancing over his shoulder just in time to see Jason send one of Ra’s men flying several meters back, the impact echoing across the training grounds. The moment Jason heard his name, he snapped his head up, scowling as he yelled back.

“What? I’m busy, Dickwing!”

“I need you,” the words tore out of him like a plea. And for a heartbeat, it felt as though the world itself stalled.

Jason stared back at him, rigid, as if he didn’t quite believe what he’d heard. Then he squared his shoulders and, in one brutal motion, cleared two assassins from his path. He was at Dick’s side a second later, back to back, guns raised toward where Bruce and Duke were finishing off the last of Ra’s men.

“What happened?” Jason demanded, glancing toward Tim. “The Baby Bird is okay, right?”

Tim didn’t answer. He only flicked an empty look in Jason’s direction.

Something in Dick’s chest tightened. Doubt crept in beneath his ribs, feeding on the silence, on the way Tim stood too still, as if he’d already drifted somewhere far away.

“Watch him,” Dick said, pushing Tim toward Jason, who hadn’t been expecting it. He startled and instinctively hooked an arm around Tim’s shoulders, pulling him close while his hands stayed locked on his guns.

“Dickwing!” Jason snapped, genuine disbelief in his voice. “You’re not leaving me alone with him, are you?”

Dick couldn’t help the sharp, almost cruel smile that tugged at his mouth. He lifted a brow, asking in a mocking voice.

“What—afraid you can’t handle one small Baby Bird?”

“Him?” Jason shot back, glancing down at Tim again, fear written across his face. Tim ground his teeth, jaw tightening. His blue eyes were clear—too clear, like an open sky scrubbed of clouds. And in that clarity, Dick saw the thing he feared most. Acceptance.

“We both know,” Jason said quietly, voice dropping, “that even the two of us wouldn’t be enough to keep an eye on him.”

Catching the edge of a shadow moving toward them with steady purpose, Dick pulled out his escrima sticks, still slick with blood. He turned his back to his brothers and spoke in a hard, cold voice that left no room for argument.

“Don’t interfere. He’s mine.”

All playfulness vanished from his face. He took several steps forward, physically placing himself between Tim and Jason and Ra’s. The Head of the Demon stopped a short distance away, his indifferent gaze slowly sweeping Dick from head to toe. But the moment those Lazarus-colored eyes slid toward Tim, something stirred in them—something more alive. Something rotten. Something terrifying.

It was madness.

Dick felt the anger take hold, felt it coil tight in his chest. His ribcage rose and fell faster, breath turning sharp and heavy, his feet carrying him forward before he even consciously decided to move. He positioned himself directly in front of Ra’s, blocking his view of Tim entirely. His grip tightened around the escrima sticks, one of them lifting, levelled straight at Ra’s.

“Look at me, Ra’s,” Dick hissed, his voice venomous, like a panther drawing back its lips before the strike. “Today, I’ll be the one who kills you.”

God, how he wanted it—wanted to sink his teeth into Ra’s throat, to tear it open and feel the life drain from him, to watch him die slowly.

For everything he’d done to his brother. For all the pain he’d inflicted. For every ounce of grief he’d forced his family to endure.

Dick forgave many things. Dick rarely allowed the smile to slip from his face, but sometimes…sometimes nothing could stop him when his bloodlust grew stronger than his morality.

They had all forgotten that Robin was born of rage and hatred. And Dick was ready to remind them about this.

Ra’s lips curved into a crooked smile, one that stripped away the last illusion and showed Dick the monster beneath the skin. And if the monster stood before him, then Dick was ready to become the devil himself.

“Your naivety is admirable,” Ra’s drawled, his gaze flashing past Dick’s shoulder, sharp and calculating. “But I’m afraid I don’t have time for this.”

And Dick—

Dick stopped waiting. Nothing held him back anymore.

He lunged forward, pushing off the ground with explosive force. The world tilted as he shifted his balance midair, twisting into a fluid flip, his heel cutting through the air as he aimed a brutal kick straight for Ra’s face. Wind roared past his ears, his cape snapping like a whip behind him. Ra’s barely managed to evade, twisting aside at the last second and bringing his sword up in a flash of steel.

Dick didn’t even wait to land properly. The moment his boots hit the ground, he was already moving again—strike after strike, relentless, violent, the crack of escrima against metal echoing across the training field. The air smelled of blood and dust, of sweat and ozone, every impact vibrating up his arms as he pressed the attack without mercy.

Ra’s drew his sword, shifting into a defensive stance. Dick dropped low in a full-body slide, aiming to take out his knees, but his escrima sticks rang sharply as they struck against the blade instead. The impact sent a brutal vibration up his arms, rattling bone and muscle alike.

Dick bared his teeth as he sprang back, rolling one escrima stick in his hand, shaking it out as he forced feeling back into his fingers, loosening muscles numbed by the blow.

“I’ll admit,” Ra’s began, his tone almost thoughtful now as he studied Dick with renewed interest. He gave a lazy flick of his hand, recognition glinting in those Lazarus-coloured eyes. “Your skills are impressive. But you didn’t catch Deathstroke’s attention for your abilities alone, did you?”

Dick didn’t flinch, but inside, something twisted hard and tight.

“Slade?” he hissed, venomous, like a snake poised to strike. “So he is involved.”

Ra’s threw his head back, and the sound that followed was madness—laughter spilling out, echoing across the training field, sinking into stone and steel alike.

Dick glanced over his shoulder, relief cutting through the tension when he saw Tim still locked in Jason’s grip. Jason stood solid, weapons raised, finger tight on the trigger, ready to fire at the slightest movement. Around them, bodies littered the ground—Bruce and Duke had nearly finished clearing the field.

Only Ra’s remained.

“Oh, he’s more than just involved,” the Head of the Demon said. His voice spread through the space like a web, trying to catch its prey. “He was very interested in my plan. And I’m certain he’d be delighted to see you.”

“Pity the feeling isn’t mutual,” Dick tried to smirk, but his eyes stayed cold, flat, and predatory. He straightened, lifting his chin as his muscles went loose and ready, every tendon coiling with lethal intent. The voice in his head grew louder, sharper, drowning out everything else.

Kill him.

Kill. Kill. Kill.

“But I’ll deal with him later,” Dick promised. His teeth bared as his spine curved, body folding into itself like a panther on the brink of a killing leap. “You took my brother. You tortured him. You made us believe he was dead.”

He dug his back foot into the ground, stance wide, arms spreading slightly at his sides. A rough, feral growl tore from his throat, echoing back to him, feeding the fury burning in his chest.

“And for that,” he snarled, “you’ll pay, Ra’s.”

Dick lunged forward, but instead of jumping, he dropped low, folding down to the ground in a blur of motion. Ra’s was caught off guard. Dick drove his escrima stick up into his chin with everything he had. Fast. Precise. Merciless.

Ra’s lifted his sword, trying to block, but he was too late.

Through the haze of his fury, Dick watched with grim satisfaction as blood spilled from Ra’s mouth, dark and wet against his lips. But he didn’t pause. Didn’t speak. He just kept attacking.

From below. From the side. From above.

Ra’s was fast. Ra’s was a master of sword-wielding. But Dick wielded rage, and it burned hotter with every strike.

One escrima stick stabbed into the ground. Dick planted his boot on it and used it as leverage, snapping a kick toward Ra’s face, who barely managed to twist away. Still, Dick didn’t slow. Didn’t hesitate.

All he could hear was metal—steel shrieking against steel, the crack of impacts vibrating up his arms, rattling his bones. He moved lightly, instinctively, his entire focus narrowed to Ra’s alone.

And that was why he didn’t notice the new shadows.

They silently slid in from every side, closing around him, cutting him off, separating him from the Head of the Demon, pushing Dick back.

“You’ll have to forgive me, but our little dance doesn’t interest me much,” Ra’s smirked, rubbing his jaw with the heel of his palm. And Dick—

Dick snapped into white-hot fury.

“I’ll tear you apart.”

The words were low, feral, dragged out of him as he surged forward, turning on Ra’s men with blinding speed. They weren’t ready for his fury.

Dick felt nothing resembling fatigue. Whatever limits he’d once obeyed had burned away. His suit dragged at him, saturated and obscene, heat still trapped in the fabric as it smeared against his skin. It pulled where it shouldn’t, stuck where it shouldn’t, every movement accompanied by the wet reminder of what he’d already done. His hair hung in his face, heavy and dark, sweat and blood matting it to his brow, streaking down into his eyes. Noise existed only at the edges of him. Somewhere behind him, familiar voices cut through the chaos—shouts, orders, a scream.

Dick didn’t hear them.

His mind was drowned in vengeance. In agony. In agony, he finally allowed himself to unleash.

Kill.

Kill. Kill. Kill!

The voice whispered. Beckoned. Laughed each time another of Ra’s men hit the ground with a dull, final thud—bodies folding wrong, collapsing without resistance.

“You thought—”

A strike. Blood splattered across his mask, hot droplets streaking his vision red.

“—that I—”

Another blow, bone crunching under the force.

“—would forgive you—”

His elbow snapped backward into someone behind him, the impact reverberating up his arm.

“—for my brother’s death?”

His body moved on instinct alone—feral, ruthless—finding obstacles and tearing through them as if they were nothing more than dolls.

“Not in this lifetime, Ra’s!” Dick roared. “Do you hear me? I will get to you!”

But when he lifted his gaze to where the Head of the Demon had been standing, Ra’s was gone.

Dick’s eyes narrowed dangerously as he scanned the training field, breath tearing in and out of his chest. Jason had released Tim but kept him tucked behind his back, firing at anyone who dared get too close. Duke stood on the other side, mirroring him, shielding Tim with his own body.

His brother was safe.

They were all safe.

And that—that was the only thing keeping Dick from losing himself completely.

However.

Dick saw how Bruce reached Ra’s.

They traded blow for blow, steel and force colliding, and Dick watched grimly as Bruce struck again and again, his grip tightening around his escrima sticks. Bruce was strong. Bruce had trained them all. Bruce was brilliant, always calculating weaknesses before his opponent even realized they existed.

But Bruce was holding back.

Dick could see it. Those fractions of hesitation. Those strikes that landed without intent, without follow-through—touches instead of breaks. Pulls instead of kills. Tim didn’t deserve this. After everything Ra’s had done, after everything he had taken, he didn’t deserve mercy.

Kill.

Kill. Kill. Kill.

“I could lend you a sword,” a voice suddenly murmured by his ear. Dick turned slowly, predator’s gaze locking onto the startled interest on Slade’s face. He was watching Dick closely now, almost eagerly, holding the sword out to him, hilt first.

“It’s sharpened,” Slade continued smoothly. “I assure you—after a strike from it, even Ra’s al Ghul won’t rise from the dead.”

Something inside Dick screamed.

A distant, desperate warning clawed at the back of his mind, begging him to wake up. But Dick had no intention of waking—not when rage and grief had been poisoning his thoughts for years, not when they finally had somewhere to go.

Without a word, he seized the hilt. The weight of the weapon settled into his hand immediately—solid, unfamiliar. Dick wasn’t used to fighting with a sword. That was more Damian’s domain. But Dick knew a few tricks.

And more importantly—

He knew what he had to do.

Before turning toward Ra’s, his cold voice cut through the noise around.

“You’re next, Slade.”

He didn’t see the self-satisfied smile spread across Deathstroke’s face as he replied lightly, almost sing-song.

“Undoubtedly, Boy Wonder.”

Everything around Dick swam.

A relentless pounding hammered at his temples, each beat echoing like the clash of blows. Through the blood smeared across his mask, he stared straight ahead, the world reduced to blurred shapes and dark red stains. He didn’t look back. Didn’t think. He tightened his grip on the sword, one escrima stick still locked in his other hand.

The moment Bruce noticed him—saw the blade in his grasp and the unmistakable alarm flash across his face—Dick was already moving.

He lunged at Ra’s.

“Nightwing!” Bruce’s voice cracked, sharp with panic. Nothing could stop him now. There was no path back.

Kill.

KILL. KILL. KILL.

“You will feel the full fury of Robin,” Dick hissed through clenched teeth, the words barely leaving his lips as he never took his eyes off Ra’s, driving himself forward—faster and faster, harder and harder, each strike fueled by a growing, feral rage.

Ra’s fought back, but Dick didn’t slow for even a fraction of a second—damn the sharp pain screaming through his wrist from the sword’s weight, damn the exhaustion and the fire ripping through every muscle in his body. Sand burst upward with each violent movement, scraping against his boots, choking the air. The echoes of steel and impact rippled outward across the training grounds. His ears rang, a high, piercing whine, but the voice was louder.

He deserved to die.

“Why won’t you just stop?” Ra’s snarled at last. Any trace of a smile had long since been stripped from his face, replaced by raw irritation. His gaze flicked over Dick’s shoulder, his brow furrowing as his voice rose. “What are you waiting for, Deathstroke? Weren’t you planning to take this bird for yourself?”

“I was,” the voice came from behind. For a split second, Dick’s body locked up, and the knot of dread in his chest cinched even tighter. “But I’ll let him do what he needs to do first.”

“You’re betraying me?” Ra’s narrowed his eyes, something dark and dangerous flashing there. Dick stood frozen, like a statue, trying to force Slade’s words through the fog in his head.

Did that mean Tim had only ever been bait?

Did that mean Tim had suffered because of him?

“You know me, Ra’s. I don’t work for anyone. I care only about my reputation,” Slade didn’t bother hiding the mockery in his voice—he was savouring every second of it. Dick still stood in front of Ra’s with the sword in hand, feeling his entire body go numb, like ice creeping under his skin. “I’ll take my fledgling after he unlocks his full potential.”

Gunshots cracked behind them, sharp and deafening, followed by shouted curses. Out of the corner of his eye, Dick caught a fast-moving silhouette, blades flashing as they cut straight for Slade. Slade barely managed to leap back in time, the strike slicing air where his throat had been a heartbeat before. He scowled.

“You’re interfering again.”

“You ran,” Damian’s voice answered calmly, almost bored.

Dick wanted to laugh. A raw, hysterical sound clawed at his chest because suddenly everything slid into place. Every missing piece. Every lie. It had all been obvious, laid out right in front of them, but they’d been blinded by grief, drowning in it, unable to see anything beyond the pain.

A heavy hand clamped down on his shoulder.

Dick snapped his head around, coming face-to-face with Bruce’s mask. Bruce was looking at him with that tight, controlled restraint that always set Dick’s teeth on edge. Dick let out a venomous huff.

“What?” he sneered. “Decided to finally take this seriously, Bruce?”

It wasn’t a question. It was a challenge. And Dick saw Bruce’s lips press into a thin, hard line, felt the weight of that burning stare even through the mask.

“Leave this to me, Dick,” Bruce’s hand tightened on Dick’s shoulder, hard enough to sting. But this time, it didn’t sound like an order. It sounded like a plea. A plea Dick couldn’t just obey.

He drew in a deep breath, his chest tearing open with it, lungs burning like they were packed with broken glass.

“To you?” Dick snapped. “For what—so you can let him walk away again? Like you always do?!”

Because Slade didn’t matter. Bruce didn’t matter.

This was about his brother. About his baby bird, who deserved to see his tormentor die. And Dick was ready to be the one to carry out that execution, even if it was only to satisfy something ugly and selfish inside him.

“I promise, I’ll deal with him.”

Dick wanted to believe Bruce. He wanted to lower the weapons, to step aside and let his father handle it. But one look at Bruce’s face told him the truth.

Bruce wouldn’t cross that line. He never could.

Not for Jason.

Not for Tim.

“You won’t do it, Bruce,” Dick said, his voice sorrowful, though threaded with a faint, almost inaudible softness. Slowly, he raised a hand to his bloodied mask, peeling it off, letting his eyes meet Bruce’s with quiet understanding. “You won’t do it because if you did…Batman would cease to be who he is.”

His gaze flicked to Jason, standing near Damian. When their eyes met, determination flashed in that toxic green, and Jason gave the faintest nod, loading his pistol with fresh rounds.

Dick turned back to Bruce, now also taking off his cowl, who looked at him with a fatherly plea, as though silently trying to stop his son from marching into war. But Dick couldn’t stop. He couldn’t. Because war always demanded sacrifices. And Dick was ready to be that sacrifice if it meant safety for his family.

“And it’s okay, Bruce. But I’m asking you…don’t interfere. I beg you, don’t stop me. Or else—”

He tightened his grip on the sword, feeling dark hollowness in his chest when the words left his mouth.

“I’ll have to step over you.”

And he was ready to do it. It was the least he could do for his brothers.

“How…fascinating,” Ra’s interjected, his voice a silk-wrapped threat. Dick tilted his chin, letting his dark bangs fall as he stared at him with his bare face. Ra’s responded with a predatory smile, cruelness written on his face. “Want to kill me, birdie? Well then…let’s try. After all, Bruce Wayne isn’t unaccustomed to burying his sons.”

Dick’s hand slid off his shoulder, and in that instant, the massive figure of Batman stepped forward. The air seemed to shift, thickening around them. Dick’s breath caught, a flicker of uncertainty crawling across him as he studied his father’s back, the familiar breadth of his shoulders tense, unyielding.

“You’ve taught me a lot, Ra’s al Ghul,” Bruce said, his voice calm, almost eerily serene. Time seemed to pause. Dick’s grip on his weapons tightened, knuckles whitening, muscles coiled like springs ready to snap. His chest heaved with ragged breaths, a pulse hammering in his ears that drowned out everything else. Ra’s predatory smile faltered for the briefest instant, his eyes narrowing, calculating, but behind the mask of composure, there was a flicker of unease.

“Now I’ll teach you what happens to those who lay a hand on my sons.”

Dick stood like a pillar, rigid and alert, as Bruce lunged first at Ra’s. Behind him, the clash of metal and the thud of fists echoed—Slade was trying to break through to Bruce, clearly intent on pulling him away from Ra’s, but Damian and Jason intercepted, blocking his path.

“Nope. You’re not invited,” Jason said coolly, but everything else about him screamed coiled violence. His shoulders were set, weight balanced on the balls of his feet, guns held low but ready, fingers loose on the triggers like he was only a breath away from pulling them.

Duke remained behind, exhaustion tugging at his limbs, but he planted himself firmly beside Tim, scanning the surroundings with unyielding focus, keeping their baby bird safe. Tim’s gaze never left Bruce. He watched with an intensity that seemed to lock every moment into memory, face unreadable, blank expression. Dick couldn’t guess what thoughts flickered behind those steady blue eyes, and that uncertainty gnawed at him.

Tim was always a few steps ahead, and now he simply stood, unblinking, absorbing the unfolding chaos. Observing as if everything went according to his plan.

Dick exhaled heavily, a mix of relief and quiet elation flooding him as Bruce’s fist slammed into Ra’s' nose with a sharp, resonant crack that echoed across the field.

“How dare you!” Ra’s shrieked, the last remnants of humanity stripped from his voice. His pupils were black pits, the irises a vivid, toxic green that sent chills crawling down Dick’s spine. He raised his sword again, striking at Bruce with brutal precision, his attacks merciless and ruthless, as if reason itself had been devoured by the madness of the Lazarus Pit.

Bruce could only retreat, narrowly dodging each vicious blow, until one strike forced him to the ground. Dick’s heart skipped. He wanted to rush forward, to stop Ra’s, his muscles tensed, every nerve screaming action, but he hesitated. A cold, paralyzing fear wrapped around Dick, sinking deep into his bones, and he knew, with a pit in his stomach, that he wouldn’t reach Bruce in time.

“I created you, and I will destroy you, Bruce Wayne,” Ra’s declared in a flat and indifferent voice, lifting his sword with deliberate slowness.

“Bruce!” Dick’s desperate and animalistic shout tore from his throat. His body finally obeyed, springing forward, muscles coiled like a predator, but no matter how fast he moved, the distance was terrifyingly wide.

“Father!”

The blade gleamed inches from his vision, and Dick’s world slowed to a surreal, stretched-out frame. Every detail sharpened: the glint of the metal, the tension in Ra’s arm, the shadow on the floor. It felt like time itself had frozen, and Dick’s heart hammered so hard he feared it would burst.

Then, suddenly, Ra’s froze, eyes wide, and Dick mirrored him instinctively, unable even to draw a breath. Fear held him tight, icy and relentless, as if the world had narrowed to that single, suspended moment of imminent disaster.

“W-what…?” Blood ran over his lips, and he doubled over in a choking cough. Bruce rose to his feet, and Dick followed suit, both watching as Ra’s twisted in agony, collapsing to his knees.

And in that moment, Dick’s eyes locked on Tim. He stood behind him like a shadow, cold and empty, looking down at Ra’s crumpled form, the blade still buried in his chest. Ra’s trembling fingers clawed at the wound, his white shirt instantly soaking in blood. His dark eyes held nothing but disbelief. Through another jagged cough, he managed to rasp, “Detective…”

Dick’s stomach knotted with horror as the Demon Head fell forward, face hitting the ground. His eyes remained open, but all life seemed to have drained from them. No one could survive such a loss of blood.

Tim stepped forward slowly, kneeling near Ra’s body. His hand closed around the hilt of the dagger, and with a single swift motion, he pulled the bloodied blade free. His face remained a mask of indifferent calm, a coldness that made Dick recoil.

“I am Red Robin,” Tim’s voice came, quiet but unwavering. Everyone froze, their attention riveted on him. Tim’s gaze never left Ra’s lifeless form. “The king has fallen at the hand of his own pawn. How…tragic.”

The air felt thick with the echo of the words, the metallic scent of blood heavy, and the quiet menace in Tim’s posture made him seem not just dangerous, but hollow, a vessel emptied of anything human, filled only with purpose.

All the anger drained from Dick, leaving only regret and horror.

“Baby Bird,” Jason stepped up beside him, looking at Tim with the same sorrow. Tim, however, seemed not to see them at all, his eyes fixed on the dagger in his hands. The scene was so wrong, so unnatural, that Dick would have done anything to rewrite it, but he couldn’t.

Spilled blood couldn’t be brought back. Nor could memories be erased.

Suddenly, a clap of hands rang out, and everyone tensed. Everyone, except Tim, who simply lifted his chin toward the sound.

“Magnificent! What a performance! What emotions!” Klarion perched atop a pile of Ra’s fallen men, eyes gleaming like a cat’s as he surveyed each one. “So elegant. I am…thoroughly impressed with you, Timothy.”

Dick watched Klarion warily. He felt Jason tense at his side, saw calculations flicker behind Bruce’s eyes. Damian was the first to step forward, his gaze locked on Tim.

“Akhi,” he called urgently, and Dick could hear a tremor in his voice, but Tim kept ignoring them, as if the word never reached him at all. In that moment, Dick’s heart seemed to stall, a hard knot lodging in his throat that he couldn’t swallow. It felt like Tim was slipping through his fingers again, fading just beyond his reach, and Dick could do nothing to stop it.

Klarion lazily rose to his feet, tilting his head as he extended his hand toward Tim.

“Come along, Timothy. It’s time to honor your side of the bargain.”
