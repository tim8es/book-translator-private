Tim understood perfectly that their arrival would not go unnoticed. Ra’s eyes were everywhere. Always had been. Tim knew what he was capable of—especially now, when desperation drove him to hunt Tim with such single-minded resolve.

They all knew what Lazarus did to a person. How it unmoored the mind, stripped away balance, peeled fragments from the soul until there was nothing left to anchor it.

How many times had Ra’s bathed in that poisoned sanctum, clawing his way back to life, to youth, to relevance?

The air of Nanda Parbat pressed in on them—thin, cold, steeped in incense and old stone. Mountains loomed like silent sentinels, their snow-capped peaks veiled by drifting mist. Wind whispered through carved arches and prayer flags, carrying the scent of ash and ancient rituals long since soaked into the walls. This place remembered blood.

Tim scanned their surroundings, a faint frown pulling at his brow, before turning his gaze to Damian. Damian noticed immediately and gave a small nod, lips drawn into a tight line. He looked tense.

Tim’s fingers flexed at his side.

“Too quiet,” he murmured, eyes fixed on the distant silhouette of Ra’s property.

The training grounds lay abandoned—an open expanse of stone and sand, undisturbed and lifeless. That alone set Tim’s nerves on edge. It wasn’t Ra’s way. The League trained relentlessly, from dawn until exhaustion blurred into night, discipline carved into bone and reflex. Ra’s did not tolerate pieces on his board that missed their turn.

And silence, here of all places, was never accidental.

There was only one explanation for this.

They were expected.

Tim ground his teeth, tightening his grip on the bo staff until the worn handle bit into his palm. He wasn’t wearing the suit. He wasn’t even sure it still existed. He hadn’t found it anywhere, which meant Bruce had either burned it or thrown it away.

It didn’t matter anymore.

“Dami,” Tim called, forcing his tone into something thoughtful, almost casual. “Do you think we should burst in through the front?”

The lack of guards outside didn’t mean there wouldn’t be any inside. If anything, it meant the opposite.

Before Damian could answer, a smooth and amused voice slid in from the side.

“I wouldn’t recommend that. I’d be happy to escort you myself.”

Tim spun on instinct, staff snapping up defensively, every muscle coiling tight. But he didn’t even get the chance to bare his teeth at Slade. Damian moved first.

He stepped in front of Tim without hesitation, fast and sharp as a drawn blade. Steel flashed, both katanas in his hands, crossed just enough to block Tim from view. His stance was low, grounded, protective in a way that made Tim’s chest tighten painfully. Damian’s shoulders squared, his back straightening like a shield raised on pure reflex.

Tim noticed everything in that split second.

The way Damian’s jaw clenched. The way his grip shifted, adjusting for a real fight. The way his breath slowed, controlled, as if fear had been folded inward and locked behind discipline.

Slade approached at an unhurried pace, a predator savoring the moment, hands resting casually at his belt.

“So tense,” Slade drawled, eyes flicking past Damian, trying, Tim realized, to look through him. “What’s with the hostility, demon spawn? I’m certain your grandfather will be delighted to see you.”

Damian didn’t move a single step, silently making it clear that he wouldn’t hesitate to spill blood if it came to that. He straightened to his full height, feet planted firmly against the stone beneath him. With a single flick of his wrists, he rotated the katanas, drawing the blades closer along either side of his body, their sharpened edges now angled toward Slade.

Tim froze instantly, tightening his grip on the bo staff, one end pressed into the ground to keep himself steady.

He knew that stance.

Reverse grip.

How stupid. Idiot.

Tim wanted to shout at Damian. Wanted to smack the back of his head. Anything. Because that position gave him no advantage in a fight.

None—

Except defense.

He was protecting you, the thought cut through him.

“You’ve changed, demon’s spawn,” Slade said with an open, unapologetic smirk. Still, he didn’t move from his spot, craning his neck over Damian’s shoulder, trying to get a look at Tim. “Not even a word? You used to be much more talkative.”

Tim caught the tension coiling in Damian’s muscles, saw it in the rigid line of his shoulders, felt it in the air like a drawn wire. He stepped forward and laid his palm over Damian’s right wrist, gently but firmly pressing the blade down.

“Timothy,” Damian said, restrained irritation flashing through his voice. Another time, Tim might have welcomed the sight of Slade losing his head. Another time, he would’ve let it happen. But right now, he had a mission.

“Lead the way,” Tim said, flicking his hand toward the estate.

Only when Slade turned away with a low chuckle—his back to them, as if to prove they posed no threat at all—did Tim follow. Damian moved in his wake, a silent shadow at Tim’s back, radiating a restrained, blood-hungry fury that made the air feel heavier with every step.

Slade had a hand in his death, but he was only a dark knight in Ra’s al Ghul's game.

And Tim would use him to his advantage, just another piece on the board, to bring the king down.

“Lost in thought?” A light jab to the side made Duke flinch, jerking his gaze up to Jason. Fake surprise flickered across Jason’s face as Duke scowled and rubbed at his ribs.

Bruce and Dick sat up front, locked in a heated argument. Bruce looked as immovable as ever, but after all this time, Duke could read the tells—the tightness in his shoulders, the way his jaw set a second too hard. Batman might have been sparing with words, but he always drew clear lines. Dick, on the other hand, kept talking without pause, gesturing sharply, frustration snapping through every movement as if he was ready to jump out of the jet.

“Relax,” Jason drawled, boredom coating his voice. “They’re just trying to figure out who’s going after Ra’s head.” Duke frowned. Sometimes it was impossible to tell whether Jason was joking or was dead serious, but the darkness in his eyes made one thing clear: he had no intention of staying on the sidelines. “So what’s going on in that head of yours, Dukey?”

Duke exhaled and sank back into his seat, staring up at the ceiling. The low vibrations of the vehicle hummed through the frame, a steady, almost soothing pulse. If not for the angry voices up front, he was pretty sure he could’ve fallen asleep.

“It’s just…something doesn’t add up,” he said finally, rolling the events over and over in his mind.

“Doesn’t add up?” Jason leaned forward, chest shifting closer even as he stayed sprawled to the side. Now all of his attention was on Duke, and Duke wasn’t sure how to put what he felt into words.

“I don’t know. But all of this—” he waved a hand vaguely, encompassing everything, “—it feels like someone else’s plan. Like we’re just following instructions.”

He just couldn’t tell whose instructions they were.

Jason arched a brow, flicking a glance toward Bruce and Dick. They were speaking more quietly now, voices lowered but still tense, clearly not paying the two of them any attention. Jason looked back at Duke.

“Ra’s?” he asked warily. Duke shook his head.

“I’m not sure. There are too many inconsistencies. And Klarion, and Slade…they’re all playing their own games. I just don’t know which one we’re actually in.”

Jason’s jaw tightened. “And Tim?” His voice dropped, roughened, like he was afraid of hearing the answer. “Which game is Tim playing?”

Duke didn’t hesitate. He had only one answer.

“He’s playing his own match,” he said quietly. “I just don’t know what the cost of winning is.”

Tim watched the door swing open with a pounding crash, his heart stalling in his chest. The familiar stone walls rose before him at once, and in the same instant memories surged back, crawling up his spine and breaking into a cold sweat.

“Poor, poor Robin.”

“No one is coming for you.”

“They left you. They all forgot about you.”

“Run, detective. You won’t get far anyway.”

The weight of a heavy hand on his shoulder made him flinch. Tim took a step back and turned in raw fear to Damian. Damian’s expression had gone feral in an instant.

All the green had vanished from his eyes, swallowed by blown pupils fixed on Slade’s back. His jaw was locked tight, cheek muscles twitching, shoulders coiled like a predator about to spring. He looked ready to tear forward, to spill blood without a second thought.

Tim pressed his lips together and slapped Damian’s hand away from his shoulder. The grip loosened slowly. Tim swallowed hard, forcing down the knot of panic lodged in his throat, and pulled his mask of calm back into place.

“We don’t have time for this,” he said evenly.

They both knew what he meant. This was a mission. Another operation. Emotions were a liability—something that blurred judgment, something that got you killed.

Tim drew a deep breath, gathering his resolve, and stepped over the threshold into walls that felt disturbingly familiar. He pushed the fear down, deeper and deeper, until it dulled into nothing more than white noise in the back of his mind.

“Will there be any time at all, Timothy?” Damian’s voice suddenly brushed past his ear. Tim had to gather every shred of his will not to snap at him, continuing stubbornly after Slade, who hadn’t looked back at them even once.

“What are you talking about, Dami?” Tim asked, keeping his mask firmly in place. He still avoided Damian’s gaze, now fixed on him with intent, and Tim didn’t know what to do with that attention. He wasn’t used to being noticed. He wasn’t used to being seen.

Tim Drake had always been a ghost.

“Akhi, I may not have shown it before, refusing to acknowledge you,” Damian began with grave seriousness, matching Tim’s pace with an effortless stride, and it irritated Tim more than it should. It annoyed him that Damian was taller. That he could keep up so easily, without even trying. None of this was right. None of it felt right. “But we both know you have a plan. A plan that stretches at least several moves ahead.”

Stone walls slid past them, torchlight flickering along Damian’s sharp profile, carving harsh lines into his face. His voice was low, controlled, but there was tension coiled beneath it, vibrating through Tim’s bones.

“So answer me, Timothy.”

This time, Tim looked at him.

Their eyes met, and Tim felt like he was staring into something burning, something alive with chaos, as if darkness itself was being born behind Damian’s gaze. His expression was honed to a razor’s edge: jaw tight, eyes unblinking, every inch of him demanding the truth.

“Will there be time?”

And Tim—Tim wanted to lie.

He wanted to reassure Damian, to tell him that everything would be fine, that there would be time. But the words died in his throat, because there was so much pain in Damian’s eyes. So much naked desperation that Tim felt like he’d been dragged underwater—lungs burning, chest tight, unable to breathe without choking.

Damian’s face shut down in an instant. Whatever flickered there vanished, locked away behind discipline and steel. As if he’d come to a decision, he gave a barely perceptible nod and turned his gaze toward Slade, who stood by the door, watching them with open curiosity and something close to fascination.

“Let’s go, Akhi,” Damian said in a hollow tone. And all Tim could do was move.

His legs felt numb and unresponsive as he followed him toward the door. Toward fate itself, smiling through layers of stone, beckoning him closer with patient inevitability.

Slade’s lips curled into a predatory grin as he reached for the handle. He pulled the door open and leaned in just enough to murmur, almost kindly.

“Don’t worry, little bird. I’m sure the big bird is already on his way.”

Tim felt his entire body go cold. That was it. That was what he had been afraid of.

He was almost out of time.

Dick didn’t wait for anyone. He bolted from the jet, eyes locking onto the adjacent aircraft—the one Tim and Damian had used, since it belonged to Bruce. As expected, it was empty.

Dick ground his teeth, pivoting toward the horizon, scanning for any trace of their presence. Dust stirred lightly under the wind, but there were no signs of a struggle, no wreckage, nothing to suggest they’d been attacked or in danger. Nothing—except for the immortal maniac, the dark witch, and the killer.

“What’s the plan?” Jason called out, his voice teasing as he sauntered up. Dick arched a brow, feigning surprise.

“Since when do you care about plans?”

Jason shrugged, twirling his pistol between his fingers with a casual smirk.

“Just curious how many I’ll get to take out along the way.”

“How about staying unnoticed?” Duke interjected, halting a few steps behind, arms crossed over his chest. His eyes flicked skeptically to the gun in Jason’s hands. “The less noise we make, the faster we find Tim and Damian.”

“Duke’s right. Attention is the last thing we need,” Bruce growled as he stepped out of the jet. The wind tore at his cape, snapping it open with a violent crack, fabric howling like a wounded thing as it whipped and lashed behind him, heavy folds beating against his legs in restless flutters. Dick shot him a distrustful and skeptical glance.

During the flight, a new conflict had grown between them—an argument about who would go after Ra’s. Bruce didn’t want anyone near him. But Dick wasn’t about to let that slide.

Ra’s would pay. He would pay with every fiber of his body—for Tim. For Jason. For Damian.

Dick let them down. This was his redemption. Ra’s would become a symbol, a warning for anyone who dared lay a finger on his family.

“Looks like your plan just sank,” Jason said suddenly, grinning ear to ear. But there was fire in his eyes, little devils dancing tango that made even Dick’s gut twist with unease.

As they hesitated, weighing their next move, Ra’s forces emerged from the shadows of jagged cliffs and narrow ridges, moving with the silent patience of predators. They crept in from all sides, the sound of soft boots against stone and the whisper of wind masking their approach.

Dick didn’t wait for Bruce to speak. He drew his escrima sticks, shoulder to shoulder with Jason, matching his grin with one of his own—calm, but carrying the frozen fury of a soul that had known loss.

“How about a little game, Jaybird?” His voice was even, but under the surface, there was an electric pulse of adrenaline.

Jason smirked, raising his weapon as Ra’s men stepped forward, blades glinting faintly in the dim light, reflecting off the rocky terrain.

“Start counting, Dickwing.”

The door slammed shut behind them, and Tim felt his heart trying to claw its way out of his chest. Thud. Thud. Thud. Each beat came faster than the last, loud in his ears. Nausea rose in his throat, rotten and bitter, as his body locked under the weight of that predatory gaze fixed squarely on him. One look at the man’s smile made Tim want to turn and run, to flee without looking back, but he had no right to. He had to stay.

For them. For himself.

“I’m glad to see you again, detective,” Ra’s said calmly, rising from the table and taking a single step forward. His posture was relaxed, hands folded behind his back, as if this were nothing more than a pleasant reunion. Standing before him, Tim felt like a worm. A slug, small and fragile, something that could be crushed underfoot without a second thought. Ra’s gaze slid to Damian at Tim’s side, and something sick and gleaming flickered in his eyes. “Damian.”

“Grandfather,” Damian lifted his chin in open defiance, his eyes burning with disgust as they locked onto Ra’s. A wave of relief washed over Tim’s skin, loosening his lungs, allowing him to breathe again.

“I didn’t expect to see you again,” Ra’s drawled, still watching Damian closely. “Especially after our last meeting.”

The air between them thickened, heavy with unspoken violence, and in that moment, Tim felt like prey caught between two apex predators.

“I didn’t expect to see you again either, grandfather,” Damian replied, placing deliberate emphasis on the last word as he arched one brow. Tim caught the faint curl of Damian’s lip—barely there. He was holding himself back. “Especially after I killed you.”

Tim felt the sudden cold spill through the room. Tension coiled tight, thick enough to choke on, and everyone froze—statues locked in place, waiting for the other to make the first move.

Ra’s did not look the least bit disturbed. If anything, he seemed entertained.

Tim tightened his grip on the bo staff, knuckles whitening as Ra’s dark gaze slid back to him. It felt like being pinned in place, like prey caught under a shadow far larger than itself.

“Amusing, isn’t it, Timothy?” Ra’s said lightly. Tim flinched at the sound of his name. Ra’s noticed, and his smile deepened. He looked satisfied with the reaction he brought out of Tim. “You came here with Damian,” Ra’s continued, voice smooth as poison, “who never bore you any brotherly affection, to do what? To kill me?” He tilted his chin, still not moving an inch, yet Tim had the unmistakable sensation that Ra’s was growing larger—his presence swelling, devouring space, herding Tim backward into an invisible corner. “Or have you finally decided to accept your destiny, detective?”

“Destiny?” Tim snorted despite himself, forcing false courage into his spine as he lifted his gaze and stared straight into the devil’s hollow eyes, hatred burning hot enough to steady him. “If by destiny you mean becoming your puppet, then I think I’ll find myself a different fate.”

The word seemed to linger in the air between them, even as the shadows along the walls stretched closer, listening.

“A puppet? No, Timothy, you misunderstand,” Ra’s stated, his smile stretching wide, baring his teeth in something closer to a snarl. He looked less and less human by the second, and Tim wasn’t even sure there was anything human left inside him anymore. “You are a weapon. The most dangerous, and the most elegant, weapon in my hands.”

Ra’s lifted his palms, sweeping them toward the window, beyond which sprawled the lands belonging to the ancient al Ghul legacy.

“Just imagine how much we could achieve together once I rid you of everything that binds you,” Ra’s continued in a smooth and relentless voice. “There will be no pain. No compassion. No hope.”

Each word turned Tim inside out.

Your mind is your greatest weapon, Robin.

“Don’t you want to stop feeling?” This time, he stepped closer, halting only a meter away, looking down at Tim as if he were already something owned. “Don’t you want to stop hoping?”

Dad, please, save—

Stop feeling? Stop hoping?

No more pain?

Tim did. God, he wanted it more than anything.

He was so tired. So unbearably tired of being human.

“You know exactly what I’m talking about, detective,” Ra’s murmured, his voice coating Tim’s ears like warm honey, thick and suffocating. Tim could only stand there, staring at a point just beyond Ra's' shoulder—unblinking, unmoving, silent.

Stop feeling.

The thought echoed, hollow and tempting, as everything else began to fall away.

“Just imagine how easy it will be once you cast aside every feeling that interferes with your thinking,” Ra’s said softly. A heavy hand settled on Tim’s shoulder. Even through the fabric, fingernails dug into his skin until it burned, until pain became grounding and somehow distant all at once.

Stop feeling.

“Why fight for those who betrayed you?” Ra’s kept whispering like the devil. “Why die for them, when you could take all the power in the world into your own hands?”

He leaned closer, lowering his head to Tim’s ear. His breath brushed against Tim’s skin—hot, intimate, invasive—and Tim flinched despite himself, every nerve screaming.

“All you have to do is agree to be mine,” Ra’s sighed in deeply, “and I will help you rid yourself of all that pain.”

Stop feeling.

The words sank into Tim like shards of ice, scraping through the hollow caverns of his chest, where exhaustion had already left every nerve exposed. Each echo felt like it pressed deeper into him, twisting his insides, leaving him half-detached from his own body, as if he were watching himself from somewhere far away, incapable of stopping the ache.

“One word, Timothy.”

Ra’s invasive voice slipped into his mind, winding around his thoughts like silk threads pulled tight. Each syllable pressed deeper and heavier.

“One word, Timothy.”

Tim wet his lips. His mouth felt dry, his tongue thick, as if even air resisted him now. His hands shook, his pulse thundered in his ears, but he forced the sound out anyway, crushing the tremor in his voice.

“Dami—”

Steel sang through the air.

A blade flashed, and in the next heartbeat, Ra’s was ripped away from him, forced back with cold metal at his throat. Damian moved like a released spring. There was no hesitation in him, no doubt, no room left for mercy or restraint.

Ra’s was cornered.

And yet—he smiled.

Not a flicker of fear crossed his face. His eyes burned brighter, sharp with delight, drinking Tim in as if the blade at his neck were nothing more than an inconvenience.

“I should have known you would be harder to break,” Ra’s said calmly, almost fondly. There was naked, sadistic pleasure in his gaze now, his attention fixed on Tim while he deliberately ignored the katana threatening his life. “But that’s quite all right. We have time.”

Time.

And in that moment, despite the assassins hemming him in. Despite the walls soaked with blood. Despite the raw, animal terror clawing at his ribs—

Tim laughed.

The sound tore itself out of his chest without warning, loud and fractured, filling the chamber until it bounced off stone. It wasn’t clear whether it was hysteria or joy anymore—maybe both, tangled together so tightly he couldn’t tell where it was coming from.

He folded in on himself, clutching his bo staff as it braced against the floor, his shoulders shaking. Tears blurred his vision, streaking hot down his face, and through them his mouth twisted into something sharp and mocking.

Joy. Joy. Joy.

There it is.

“You—!” Tim choked, laughter spilling over him again, harsher now, wilder, until his throat burned and his lungs screamed for air. “You think you have time. God—ahahah—you think you have time!”

His voice broke, still laughing, still smiling through tears.

“Imagine that. The great and powerful Demon’s Head,” Tim rasped, lifting his head just enough to look at Ra’s through watery eyes, “fooled so easily by a witch boy.”

The laughter echoed again, thin and dangerous, and for the first time it didn’t sound like fear at all.

But his laughter died as abruptly as it had begun. The manic smile, however, stayed carved into his face, looking rigid and wrong. Tim felt insane—felt alive—as he watched Ra’s, whose brows drew together in irritation, the earlier amusement draining from his eyes and curdling into something uglier. Anger. Real, unfiltered anger.

“What are you talking about?” Ra’s hissed, baring his teeth. Tim tilted his head back, bracing both hands on his bo staff. In an instant, every muscle in his body went slack.

It was like being wrapped in a blanket. A wave of calm rolled over him, comforting, lulling him, pulling him downward—into the deep, dark quiet of an ocean floor. And for the first time, Tim didn’t fight it. He let it take him. Let it cradle him.

That same faint smile lingered on his lips as he spoke again, his voice light, almost amused.

“You forgot something important, Ra’s,” Tim said softly. “When you play a game, you should know who your enemy and who your ally is.”

He leaned forward, hair slipping loose and falling into his eyes, and stared straight into the Demon’s Head through the shadows.

“You don’t have time,” Tim continued, his tone bright with mockery. “Because I made a deal with the Lord of Chaos.”

His smile widened, just a fraction.

“You’ve already lost, Ra’s.”

Ra’s entire stance shifted.

Something dark and possessive coiled around him, an invisible pressure that made the air feel thicker. It reminded Tim of the madness he’d once seen in Jason’s eyes when he came back as the Red Hood—raw, untethered rage. Fury boiled in Ra’s veins; Tim could swear he saw them swell along his neck as his jaw tightened.

With a sharp, violent motion, Ra’s seized his green cloak and tore it free, flinging it to the floor. He was left in a white shirt, already creasing at the shoulders as he moved. His other hand snapped to the sword at his waist. He stepped back, just far enough to clear Damian’s katana, and drew his blade in one smooth pull.

Steel sang as it left the scabbard.

Ra’s bared his teeth, eyes alight with feral rage, and leveled the sword straight at Tim.

“If I cannot own you,” Ra’s said coldly, “then no one will, Timothy.”

This was Lazarus madness.

And now Tim could see it clearly—Ra’s had lost himself long ago, hollowed out by the Pit, driven by a single, consuming impulse: the hunger for power.

Damian moved instantly, trying to put himself between Tim and Ra’s, but at that momen,t Slade stepped forward. His sword came up, aimed at Damian’s chest, forcing him back a step as Slade spoke with lazy disdain.

“Stay out of it, brat. This doesn’t concern you.”

Something flared red-hot in Tim’s mind. His attention snapped to Slade, who had lingered in the shadows until now, watching, observing, but Tim didn’t have time to chase that thought.

A blade flashed across his vision.

Instinct took over. Tim twisted aside at the last possible second, the edge of the sword slicing through the space where his face had been a heartbeat earlier. Behind him, steel collided with a ringing crash, the sound sharp and brutal, echoing through the chamber.

“Eyes on me, detective,” Ra’s growled as he surged forward again.

Tim could only parry the blows, each impact shuddering through his arms, the force of Ra’s fury vibrating straight into his bones. Every strike carried the full weight of the Demon Head’s rage, as if Ra’s were trying to crush him by sheer will alone.

Tim wanted to see Ra’s fall.

He wanted to see the moment realization dawned—the instant Ra’d understood he’d been outplayed. Tim deserved that much.

But beneath it burned something darker.

Vengeance.

For the years of torture. For the madness Ra’s had dragged him through. For everything he had taken from Tim.

Tim stopped defending.

He broke into a sharp, brutal offensive, and they collapsed into a violent, fiery dance. Steel clashed again and again, sparks skittering across the floor. Out of the corner of his eye, Tim caught glimpses of Damian, locked in his own struggle, trying to disarm Slade. They pressed into each other without restraint, blades flashing, boots scraping as they fought for every inch of ground.

Tim flicked the switch.

A blade snapped out from the end of his bo staff. He twisted his wrists and slashed.

The edge carved across Ra’s face, tearing a long, vicious cut from his right cheek up toward his left eye. Blood welled instantly, dark against pale skin.

“You will pay for that, detective,” Ra hissed, his voice shaking with fury as he lunged again.

Tim leapt backward, vaulting behind the table as Ra’s sword came down in a savage arc. He barely deflected the strike, the impact rattling his teeth. A high-pitched ringing filled his ears. His heart slammed against his ribs, frantic and wild, his pulse drowning out thought as it thundered in his temples.

There was no room left for fear.

Breathing hard, Tim felt his strength begin to drain away. Ra’s seized the moment.

He closed the distance in a single, predatory stride, flinging his sword aside. It hit the floor with a sharp, ringing clang that echoed through the room. One hand snapped around Tim’s bo staff, wrenching it uselessly aside, while the other clamped around Tim’s throat, fingers digging in until skin broke. Ra slammed him back against the window, glass rattling violently behind his spine.

“Akhi!” Damian’s broken and scared voice tore through the air, but it sounded distant—far away, muffled by the roaring in Tim’s ears.

Tim clawed at Ra’s wrist, nails scraping, striking blindly, fighting with everything he had left. It did nothing. His lungs burned. Black spots burst across his vision, swallowing the edges of the room.

Ra’s voice, however, remained terrifyingly clear.

“Perhaps you truly were meant to die that first time. Then accept your fate, detective.”

There was a sharp crack.

Before Tim could understand what had happened, the grip on his throat vanished. His body lurched backward, weightless and then he was falling.

Falling and falling and falling.

Cold wind tore through his hair. The sky spun above him, clouds smearing into pale streaks. Time stretched. All Tim could do was close his eyes and surrender to it.

To the end.

But then—

Strong arms locked around his waist. He slammed into a warm chest, breath knocked from him as he was hauled close. The smell hit him all at once—blood and metal, smoke and sweat. Tim’s eyes flew open, and through the blur he caught a familiar profile.

Dick.

His suit was torn, streaked with dark, drying blood that glistened along his jaw and soaked into his collar. A cut split his brow, blood tracking down the side of his face, but his grip didn’t falter. His expression was feral, eyes burning with a focus that left no room for mercy. A crooked smile cut through the blood.

“Looks like I owe you a few flying lessons, Baby Bird.”
