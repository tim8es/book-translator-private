He couldn’t breathe. The crushing grip around his throat pinned him to the cot, iron fingers digging into flesh, choking out the last scraps of air. Tim clawed uselessly at the hand holding him down, his nails scraping against unyielding armor, but Bruce’s grip never faltered.

Those eyes—eyes he had trusted, eyes that had once been his anchor—now burned with unrestrained fury. And beneath that rage, Tim saw it: the hollow grief, the loss that had turned Batman into something darker, into Gotham’s living nightmare when Bruce finally stopped holding back after Jason’s death.

His vision blurred, stinging at the edges as black spots swam before him. His chest convulsed with the desperate need for air, and when he thought the darkness would finally swallow him whole, the pressure vanished. Bruce’s hand tore away, leaving Tim crumpled on the cot, gasping, coughing so violently it felt as though glass had shredded his lungs. A pitiful sound broke from his throat, a whimper, cracked and raw.

He hated himself for it. He was pathetic. Weak.

“What the hell, Bruce?!” Jason’s voice sounded through the ringing in Tim’s ears, harsh and furious. The aggression in it made Tim flinch, his whole body recoiling as he squeezed his eyes shut. He didn’t want to be here. Not in this place. Not under their eyes. He didn’t even understand what had happened to him, but already he wasn’t sure he wanted to know the answer.

Maybe in every world, in every timeline, Tim Drake was destined to be nothing more than a cuckoo, an intruder in the robin’s nest.

“Why isn’t he in a cell?” Bruce’s cold growl rumbled through the cave. The sound alone raised goosebumps on Tim’s arms, a visceral reminder that whatever Bruce saw when he looked at him, it wasn’t family.

“Because he’s human!” Jason snapped, and the desperation in his voice dragged Tim’s gaze sideways. Even through the blur, he could see it, Jason’s face, pale, terrified, his toxic-green eyes wide with something dangerously close to grief. “He’s human, Bruce. Not a clone!”

Bruce’s brow furrowed, suspicion carved deep into every line of his face. His eyes buned with distrust. It only made Jason’s temper flare hotter. With a sharp movement, he snatched the sheet Dick had left behind, the DNA results crumpling in his fist, and shoved it into Bruce’s hands.

“Here. Take a good look,” Jason spat, his voice low and venomous, leaving Bruce to wrestle with the evidence while he turned back to Tim.

Tim was still gulping air in ragged, desperate breaths, each inhale rattling like broken glass in his chest. Jason stopped a step away, his gaze flicking over Tim with an intensity that bordered on protective fear. Slowly, almost as if he was afraid Tim might break beneath the touch, Jason reached out, his hand settling on Tim’s trembling shoulder.

“You holding up, Baby Bird?” Jason’s voice softened into something dangerously close to concern.

But Tim had no strength left for words. He only lifted his head, his breathing finally steadying, and looked up at Jason with empty eyes, void of fight.

Tim was exhausted. Too exhausted to remember what had happened. Too lost to understand where—or when—he even was. All he knew was the crushing weight dragging him down. He was simply… tired.

And Jason saw it.

His hand slid down Tim’s back, following the fragile line of his spine. Jason’s eyes narrowed, a predator’s squint edged with something unspoken, anger, maybe, or sorrow, as his lips pressed into a thin, dissatisfied line.

“You’ve always been this skinny, Baby Bird?” His words were a growl, but the undertone wasn’t mockery. It was worry.

Tim’s chest hitched, his breath stuttering as he shook his head. Knees buckling, he folded in on himself, resting his forehead against them. He didn’t care anymore. Let them throw him back in a cell, lock him away, treat him like an enemy. It didn’t matter. Nothing mattered.

“Jason.” Bruce’s voice cut through the silence, no longer sharp, no longer dripping with rage. This time it carried something else, something almost desperate. “We have protocols. We don’t know his intentions.”

“You really think Tim Drake, the kid who worshipped you from the moment he saw you, who became Robin just so Batman wouldn’t drown in his own grief, who kept coming back to me no matter how hard I shoved him away like some lost hatchling, you really think he could ever hurt us? Really, B?”

It was impossible to tell if Jason’s voice dripped with sarcasm or sorrow, but the answer didn’t matter. His eyes locked onto Bruce’s with a fire that wouldn’t waver, even as Bruce stood rooted in place, that crumpled scrap of paper clenched tight in his fist.

“Because let me remind you,” Jason pressed on, his words gaining weight, grinding out of him like gravel, “Tim was the only one who believed you were alive when the rest of us buried you. He was the only one who didn’t quit, who went against the League itself.” Jason’s throat worked as he swallowed down something heavier than anger, his chest tightening with every word. And then, softer, almost breaking. “Tim was the only one still fighting for you… when you’d already given up on yourself.”

Silence crashed over the cave, oppressive, suffocating. It rang in Tim’s skull like a cruel echo, pressing against his temples until every heartbeat hurt.

He wanted to sleep. To sink into oblivion where none of this reached him. A bitter, fleeting thought flickered through his mind: maybe he should have let Bruce’s hand keep tightening around his throat. At least then he wouldn’t have to listen to Jason’s fury, to Bruce’s silence, to the unbearable truth threading through every word.

Jason’s words cut through him like broken glass, splintering something raw inside. Forgotten fragments clawed their way back to the surface of Tim’s mind, leaving behind nothing but a pounding migraine.

“Jaylad,” Bruce exhaled, his entire frame still coiled like a predator ready to strike. Jason immediately shifted, planting himself between Bruce and Tim, his broad shoulders eclipsing that burning, hate-filled gaze. For that, for just that moment of protection, Tim almost felt relief, grateful not to be pinned under Bruce’s eyes that stripped him down to nothing.

“He’s not Tim.”

The words detonated in the silence.

And then came laughter.

Sharp and hysterical. The sound cracked through the cave like glass shattering on stone. It rolled and echoed, high-pitched at first, then breaking apart into sobs. It was madness, uncontained, and Tim realized with horror that it was his. That awful sound belonged to him.

He could feel Jason’s presence looming close, tense and rattled, but the man didn’t touch him, didn’t dare. As if one wrong move would splinter Tim beyond repair. And somehow, that restraint made the hysteria worse.

Jason. Jason, who had once hated him enough to spill his blood. Jason, whose hands had almost ended his life. Now he hovered over him like some fierce, desperate mother bird guarding her wounded hatchling.

The irony broke something inside. His laughter twisted into choking sobs, his ribs aching with every shuddering breath. Tears blurred his vision until the cave was just a smear of shadows and stone.

“Tim… Tim…” His name, over and over again, torn from Jason’s throat, trying to anchor him. But it only made the spiral sharper, more suffocating. He couldn’t stop. He couldn’t breathe.

He was breaking.

“Tim!”

Rough hands yanked him upright by the shoulders, forcing him to meet a pair of piercing blue eyes, eyes that shimmered, deep within, with that sickly Lazarus green he had seen in his nightmares.

Jason held his gaze, unflinching, studying every twitch of Tim’s face. For a fleeting second, the storm inside Tim stilled. And then his lips curved into a too wide smile. A smile born of madness.

“Why not Replacement? That’s what you called me, isn’t it?” The words slipped out unchecked, his tongue clumsy, his grin stretching wider, while his eyes remained hollow and dead. “Red Robin. Outcast. Ghost. Pretender. The Unloved Son.”

Behind Jason, Bruce flinched. The smallest crack in the mask. Tim saw it, and it only fueled him. He didn’t care what they thought of him anymore, not Jason, not Bruce, not Dick, whose quiet steps now echoed across the cave until he came to stand at Bruce’s side, face shadowed with suspicion.

But Tim couldn’t stop. His voice rose, frantic and cutting, until every syllable was another blade against himself.

“So what’s changed, huh?” His gaze locked on Jason again, manic and wild, the broken smile twitching on his lips. “Because either I’ve fallen into another universe… or I’ve finally lost my mind.”

And then his eyes snapped to Bruce. Cold. Sharp. Like a knife catching the dim light.

“Because I was never Bruce Wayne’s son. You told me so yourself.”

He’d hoped for Bruce’s anger, a strike that would black him out and end the pain. He braced himself for the blow, eyes already closing, when Jason moved. But not in the way Tim expected.

Jason folded him into a hug, one arm across his shoulders, the other cradling his head to his chest. For Tim, it wasn’t salvation so much as another cage; warm breath, too-tight arms, a hold he couldn’t force his way out of. Jason’s chin rested on his head.

“I’m sorry, Baby Bird. I’m so sorry,” he repeated, voice breaking, each whisper tightening like a band. The grip only grew firmer. Pain lanced through Tim’s ribs at the pressure, and he flinched, but he couldn’t find the energy to struggle.

From somewhere close, he could feel Jason’s fast and uneven heartbeat, and then he felt tears hot against his hair. If this were real, if this were true, Tim told himself, then this was a nightmare. A hallucination. His mind was playing cruel games, showing him things he’d never been given.

“Jay—” Bruce tried then, his voice softer, almost pleading now instead of furious, but Jason didn’t look up. He barely heard Bruce at first. Dick stepped forward, laying a careful hand on Jason’s back; the contact made Jason finally lift his tear-bright eyes.

“Jay… this isn’t our Tim,” Dick said quietly. But then, as if the air itself broke, Dick’s voice folded into something harder and final. “Our Tim is dead. Baby Bird is dead, Jay.”

Tim folded like a puppet with its strings cut; his forehead pressed flat to Jason’s chest, palms falling limply to his knees. The world narrowed to the sound of his own breath and the hollow echo of that verdict.

“If I’m dead,” he said, flat and distant and frighteningly calm, “then you won’t mind giving me a bullet to the head.” The sentence slid out even-keeled, as if he were asking for directions. He felt Jason’s muscles tighten, heard Dick give a small, involuntary flinch, but Tim could no longer pretend. The ache under his ribs was louder than anything else. He wanted only one thing... “…To disappear. One shot, and I would be gone forever. I wouldn't be a bother anymore.”

Broken wings cannot be mended.

He didn’t know what he was hoping for anymore, did he want Jason to snatch a gun and end it all with one clean crack, or the brutal finish he’d seen in nightmares when his throat was sliced? If he could choose, he’d take the second choice. Maybe knowing he was dying, feeling the heat of his blood and the slow black around the edges, would scrape him out of the hollow nothing that had grown inside him.

A sudden, violent crash cut through the cave like a gunshot, and every head snapped toward Bruce. The sound echoed off stone and metal, leaving a ringing silence in its wake. Bruce’s face was unreadable, jaw clenched so tight a vein bulged at the temple, yet his eyes bored into Tim with a clarity that felt like accusation, as if Tim had sold his soul to the devil and there was nothing left inside worth saving.

There was nothing left to give.

“Don’t you dare,” Bruce whispered dangerously, stepping around the overturned chair. “Don’t you speak like that.”

In that instant Tim remembered why Gotham feared the Dark Knight. Standing before him was the stripped-down core of that terror: anger braided with grief, the raw bitter edge that had forged Batman when the man behind the cowl had been hollowed out by loss. Up close the figure was almost familiar, Bruce in a white shirt, tie loosened but present, sleeves rolled, the austerity of the suit replaced by workmanlike severity, but the face wore new lines. Fine wrinkles at the corners of his eyes and heavy bags hinted at too many sleepless nights. Under the rage, exhaustion sat like a fossil in his eyes.

Engulfed by the whirlwind of his emotions, Tim forgot where he’d come from, or indeed which world he even belonged to.

“I would never harm my son,” Bruce said, each word hammered out like law, voice iron and steady. “Not for anything.”

The statement wasn’t consolation. It was a verdict, delivered from a man who had already set himself above mercy and pity, who had learned to do what had to be done and made peace with the cost. Tim heard it and felt nothing but the echo of his own emptiness.

“But my son is dead,” Bruce’s voice broke the room like an accusation. “I tried to bring him back with the Lazarus—” He cut himself off, lungs burning as he drew a ragged breath, spine straightening as if he were bracing against wind. “It was too late. I buried him myself. I watched the grave fill. I checked the coffin ten times to make sure it was him before I closed it. I went to that place every day for years, hoping it was some awful dream.” His fingers curled until knuckles blanched; old pain showed in the flat plane of his jaw. “No matter how much I hoped, he stayed beneath the soil.”

The words struck Tim like a slap to the face. The air went thin and sharp.

“Whoever you are, you are not him.” Bruce’s eyes were empty of mercy when he finished.

“Enough.” Jason’s rough and sudden voice exploded. The softness that had held him seconds ago snapped like a frayed rope. “You’d better shut your mouth, Bruce, before I do it for you.”

Dick’s expression folded into disapproval, a hard line of displeasure, and he stared at Jason with something like reproach. Jason didn’t look away. He stood between Tim and Bruce, protective, furious, unyielding, refusing to be swayed.

“Jason, he could be another assassin sent by Ra’s!” Bruce’s voice cracked like a whip, reverberating off the stone walls. The accusation made Tim flinch. Instinctively, his fingers clutched at Jason’s shirt like it was the only thing keeping him tethered to the earth. Dick noticed, the narrowing of his brows, the hard press of his lips betrayed the calculation behind his silence. His gaze was sharp, thoughtful, the way it got whenever a new case unfolded before him.

“We already proved he’s not a clone, Bruce!” Jason snapped back, his hands slicing through the air with every syllable. But Bruce didn’t yield. His face was dark, his eyes cold.

“Ra’s is capable of anything. He knows our weaknesses. He knows how to use them.”

Jason bared his teeth in a humorless grin, the sound closer to a growl than a laugh. He turned his back on Bruce, grasping Tim’s wrist with surprising gentleness as he hauled him up from the cot.

“Come on, Baby Bird. Alfred’ll help me put some weight back on you,” Jason muttered, grabbing the folded clothes out of Dick’s arms without so much as a glance. He tugged Tim toward the shadows of the exit.

But Bruce’s commanding and firm voice followed them.

“Jason. We need to interrogate him.”

Jason froze mid-step. His shoulders tensed, and for a long second he didn’t move. When he finally turned, his glare burned hotter than the Lazarus Pit.

“No, Bruce,” he snarled. “He’s been through enough.”

The silence that followed was jagged. Jason’s grip on Tim’s hand tightened, not possessive, but protective, like he was daring Bruce to try and take him away.

“I know damn well this isn’t our Tim,” Jason went on, his words rough with something heavier than anger. “But that doesn’t mean he deserves our hate.” For a moment, his expression softened, his gaze flickering to Tim as if grounding himself in his presence. “Because if you’ve forgotten, Bruce, our anger, our indifference—” Jason’s voice cracked, breaking the air like shrapnel.

“That’s what killed Red Robin in the first place. And I don’t want to lose another one.”

Tim couldn’t feel his own body. He moved through the shadows like a ghost, thoughts scattering in a thousand directions, fragments of memory and fear colliding in his mind. The cold, hard floor beneath his boots pressed into him with every step, and the faint hum of fluorescent lights above echoed through the hall, making each footfall sound impossibly loud. He followed Jason on autopilot, every shadow a potential threat, every sound a jolt to his already frayed nerves. Every few seconds, Jason glanced back, eyes sharp and searching, as if afraid Tim might vanish into the darkness.

“What’s on your mind, Baby Bird?” Jason’s voice was tentative, a soft attempt at conversation that felt almost fragile. Tim lowered his chin, avoiding the gaze entirely, letting the tension coil tighter in his chest.

Jason let out a heavy, frustrated sigh.

“Don’t worry about Bruce. He… grief’s clouded his judgment. I’m not excusing him. Not for a second. But this… none of this is your fault. I hope you understand that, Tim.”

Tim didn’t feel it. The words slid off him. Inside, there was only emptiness, a hollow ache that swallowed every flicker of reassurance. His thoughts rattled like broken glass, cutting and scattering in every direction, and his chest felt like it had been hollowed out. He couldn’t grasp anything solid, anything real. His silence spoke volumes, and Jason knew it.

For a moment, Jason hesitated. Then he reached out cautiously, letting his hand rest lightly on Tim’s shoulder. Not too tight, not too forceful, just enough to anchor him, to remind him he wasn’t entirely alone. Tim flinched at first, muscles tensing, but he didn’t pull away. The warmth of Jason’s hand was a strange, alien comfort against the cold emptiness crawling through his skin.

“I’m sure Alfred’s cookies can at least lift your mood,” Jason said, softer now, a flicker of worry breaking through the roughness, his thumb brushing lightly over Tim’s shoulder in a subtle rhythm, almost like a heartbeat. The gesture was simple, but to Tim it felt like a lifeline thrown across a stormy sea.

They moved quickly down the corridor, Tim following Jason like a shadow, but when he caught sight of the kitchen and the familiar figure within, he froze. That single pause forced Jason to halt as well.

“What’s wrong?” Jason frowned, but Tim couldn’t find the words. He didn’t even know what he was feeling. Perhaps it was Bruce’s earlier presence that made him question whether he even deserved to be here, but for some reason, he couldn’t cross the threshold, his gaze locked on Alfred, who had turned at Jason’s voice, towel in hand.

“Master Jason?” Alfred’s usual calm smile appeared, but it vanished the instant his eyes fell on Tim, who stood motionless, statuesque, afraid even to breathe. “Master Tim…?”

Alfred, always composed, rarely betrayed emotion, but now, for the first time in years, a shadow of genuine anguish darkened his eyes, and his lips trembled, as if holding back tears he could not shed. He dropped the towel and took a careful step toward Tim, never breaking eye contact, as if he feared that blinking would make the boy vanish like a ghost.

“Master Tim…?” he repeated, disbelief thick in his voice. Tim remained rooted to the floor, silent, and unable to move.

Jason loosened his grip, allowing Alfred to close the final steps between them. Alfred slowly raised a hand and pressed it gently to Tim’s cheek, wiping away the dried streaks of tears.

“Is it really you?” Alfred’s voice cracked, a mixture of hope and desperation. At that, Tim could no longer hold himself back. His legs moved before his mind could catch up, and he launched into Alfred’s embrace, inhaling the comforting scent of baked bread and chocolate, feeling a warmth he hadn’t dared to imagine in years.

One of Alfred’s hands rested on his back, steady and grounding, while the other tangled carefully in his hair, soothing him with gentle strokes. Tim closed his eyes at last, letting himself sink into the comfort, letting the weight of fear, loneliness, and exhaustion melt away, if only for a moment.

“Shh… shh, my boy,” Alfred murmured softly, barely holding his own tears. “It’s alright. It’s all going to be alright.”

And this time, he let himself sink into the sweet embrace of darkness.

“You know, detective,” Ra purred, his voice dripping with menace, “they say when a human’s pain becomes unbearable, they begin to beg for death.”

He loomed over Tim, predatory and unyielding, his eyes glittering with a merciless delight. Fresh blood squelched beneath his boots, yet he stepped through it as if it were nothing, leaving a crimson trail behind him. He tilted his head, studying Tim with cold curiosity. “I wonder… how long will you endure, detective? How long before you finally plead with me for your life to end?”

Without warning, Ra yanked Tim up by the hair, forcing his face toward him. Their eyes locked, Tim’s burning with defiance and hatred, Ra’s widening into a grin that sent shivers crawling down his spine. The predator’s sadistic pleasure was palpable, an invisible weight pressing down on every nerve.

“Don’t worry, detective,” Ra said, almost gently, his grin never faltering. “I won’t let you die… not yet. You see, I need you alive.”

Then came the first strike: a searing pain shot through Tim’s thigh, white-hot and unrelenting. He screamed, a soul-piercing sound that echoed off the walls, ricocheting through the cold, dark chamber. His body jerked violently from the pain, muscles spasming, breath caught in his throat. The air was thick with the metallic tang of blood and the oppressive heat of fear.

Ra’s boots tapped deliberately on the floor, each step like a hammer against Tim’s sanity. His shadow stretched over Tim like a dark promise, and with every measured, deliberate movement, it felt as if the room itself were closing in, walls narrowing, the ceiling pressing down.

Another strike, and the sharp, agonized cry tore from Tim’s chest, bouncing in the empty space. The pain radiated, a cruel symphony of fire lancing through flesh and bone, and yet Ra’s gaze never wavered, drinking in every moment, savoring every flinch, every wince, every tremor.

“They left you… The poor, poor Robin. No one is coming to save you.”

Tim jerked upright, gasping silently, his mouth opening in a scream that didn’t make a sound. The coppery scent of blood hung in the air, phantom pain shooting through his limbs, yet when he opened his eyes, he realized it had only been a nightmare. He was lying in bed, tangled in blankets, his body slick with sweat.

Outside, darkness pressed against the windowpane. Tim knew sleep was impossible now. That pain, that terror, they had felt too real. These weren’t memories he recognized. He didn’t remember… not really.

And what did he remember?

Desperation clawed at him. He grabbed handfuls of his hair, curling his knees to his chest, trying to quiet his frantic heartbeat. His breaths came in ragged, uneven gasps, fingers twitching as if betraying the turmoil inside. He needed a distraction.

With a sharp movement, he threw off the blankets and slid from the bed. Bare feet hit the floor, silent on the cold wood as he edged toward the door. He paused, scanning the hallway; the house was still. No creaks, no whispers, no one to wake.

He slipped outside, the night air biting at his skin as he stepped onto the concrete path. A shiver ran through him, but he pressed onward, needing the chill to sharpen his scattered senses. He moved along the path toward the garden, drawn by the promise of fresh air, yet the shadows seemed to twist and shift, teasing his mind.

As he rounded the hedges, he stumbled back instinctively, about to retreat indoors. But then familiar green eyes pierced him, and Tim froze, every nerve screaming as though he were being dissected alive. But then a whisper reached his ears.

“Akhi?”
