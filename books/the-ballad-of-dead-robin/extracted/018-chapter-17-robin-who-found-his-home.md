# Chapter 17: Robin Who Found His Home

He stood before the grave, staring blankly at the name carved into the stone. Timothy Drake.

Rain poured down in relentless sheets, drenching him completely. His clothes clung heavily to his body, soaked through, and the hair beneath his hood was plastered to his forehead. Still, he did not move. He did not dare take even a single step away.

Something ugly was stirring inside him. Something he had never felt before. The unfamiliar feeling held him in place, refusing to let him turn away from the grave, as if whispering in his ear:

Look.

Timothy Drake.

He should have been happy. There was no one left now who could reach for what rightfully belonged to him. So why…?

Why couldn’t he look away?

Lightning split the sky, a blinding flash that illuminated the words etched into the headstone. They gleamed for an instant in the cold light cruelly, driving the blade deeper into his heart as if determined to carve it out entirely, leaving nothing but an empty hollow behind.

And then his own voice echoed through his mind.

“I’m the real Robin! And you’re nothing but a pretender!”

He watched with undisguised satisfaction as Timothy’s smile vanished at once, the light in his eyes draining away until they looked hollow and empty. A cruel smirk curled across his lips as he raised his katana and leveled the blade at the pretender’s chest—at the boy who wasn’t even trying to fight back.

Weakling.

Damian would show him what a true Robin was supposed to be.

“Step aside and stop getting in my way!”

He was ready to strike. Ready to fight to the last breath for what rightfully belonged to him. But Timothy only kept looking at him, and something about that lifeless stare unsettled Damian for the briefest moment. The feeling crawled under his skin, unfamiliar and unwelcome.

His lips peeled back into a snarl as he tightened his grip on the katana’s hilt.

And in that very moment, Timothy simply smiled back at him. He nodded once, yet his eyes remained just as empty as before, and Damian could not shake the strange unease that had begun to settle in his chest.

“I won’t get in your way anymore,” Timothy said quietly, as if it were the simplest thing in the world. “So don’t worry, alright? Robin is yours now.”

Thunder cracked above his head, and Damian suddenly tasted something salty on his lips. He lifted a hand, touching his own cheek, and stared in confusion at the drops of tears on his palm.

Al Ghuls did not cry. Damian had never—

He touched his cheek again, realizing the tears were flowing without stopping. The letters on the headstone began to blur, swimming before his eyes, and he felt so strangely lost, so beaten, standing before the grave of the boy whose death was supposed to make him happy.

It was supposed to…

So why?

Why? Why? Why?

Why did he feel guilty?

Suddenly, quiet footsteps sounded behind him. Damian stiffened instantly, hurriedly trying to wipe every trace of tears from his face.

“Dami?”

Grayson stopped a short distance behind him. Damian could feel his heavy and searching gaze on his back. Forcing himself to stand tall, he straightened his spine and pulled his shoulders back with rigid pride before turning to look at him from beneath the shadow of his hood.

“Richard? What are you doing here?” He would not admit that his voice had faltered. Damian watched the figure behind him with a dark frown.

Grayson let out a quiet sigh and stepped out of the shadows, coming to stand beside him. His eyes were dark, his lips pressed into a thin line. For a while he simply stood there in silence, just as Damian had, as if he too could not bring himself to move.

Then he lifted his head and turned toward Damian, and his lips curved into a faint, fragile smile.

“Apparently the same thing as you,” he said, swallowing hard. Regret had settled in his eyes, so thick it made Damian feel sick. In those glassy blue eyes he could see his own reflection, and the sight of it filled him with disgust. Disgust at himself. At what he was feeling. He hated it. “I came to ask for forgiveness.”

Damian drew in a sharp breath, his jaw tightening as he turned away from Richard, hiding his face beneath the hood entirely.

“I’m not here for that,” he said firmly, his fingers curling into fists, his teeth baring in a faint snarl.

But in front of him there was only the rain-soaked earth and the gravestone bearing Timothy Drake’s name.

So who exactly was he trying to convince?

Grayson?

“Really?” Richard let out a quiet huff, but there was none of his usual surprise in it. His voice sounded weary and broken.

Thunder rumbled again in the distance, and the rain grew heavier. Damian could feel the soles of his boots slowly sinking into the wet earth, the mud giving way beneath his weight, but he didn’t move. He remained standing in the exact same place.

As if he wanted to be buried alongside Timothy.

“Then why do you run here every night?”

The question made Damian snap his chin up sharply, his eyes narrowing with irritation as his teeth ground together.

“You were watching me?!”

“You thought I’d let you out of my sight?.. Any of you…after that?” Richard gestured weakly toward the grave, his voice heavy with grief as he spoke. “…After what happened to Tim?”

And Damian…Damian understood. He understood him. Because he had been doing the same thing.

He checked the rooms before going to sleep. Made sure everyone had returned from patrol. He couldn’t shake the constant feeling that his family was in danger.

His family…

The feeling was painfully familiar.

He remembered it. He remembered it well.

It was the same feeling he had when he lost his mother. The same feeling he had when he lost his family.

“I couldn’t protect him,” Richard exhaled heavily, the weight of his regret laid bare in his voice. “His blood is on my hands, because I betrayed him when he needed me. I failed him as an older brother…”

Brother…

Damian had lost his family.

Damian felt guilty.

Damian no longer understood who he was.

Yet as he stared at the name carved into the gravestone, fresh tears gathered in the corners of his eyes. His lips trembled, and in a quiet, unsteady whisper he spoke, standing like a soldier over the grave of a fallen warrior.

“I’m sorry.”

The wind rose sharply, tearing the hood back from his hair. Damian dropped to one knee in the mud, the wet earth sinking beneath him. He pressed his fist to his heart as he bowed his head—a warrior’s surrender before the dead.

“Forgive me, Akhi,” he whispered hoarsely, feeling salt on his lips. “I am to blame…”

The first thing he felt was warmth.

It was unfamiliar, yet so gentle and comforting that, for a brief moment, he allowed himself to surrender to it, letting his body relax. His mind fell quiet, and for the first time in what felt like forever, the silence no longer pressed down on him.

In his mind, he imagined snow slowly covering the roads in a soft white blanket. Snow was beautiful—cold, yes, but he had always loved winter. It was the one time of year his parents always came home for the holidays, even if it was only to keep up appearances at some charity banquet.

The entire horizon stretched before him beneath a white, endless quilt, and Tim kept walking. And walking. And walking. His footprints trailed behind him in the fresh snow.

The blizzard brushed gently against his hair and cheeks.

Strange, Tim thought. He wasn’t cold.

Somewhere nearby, there was a faint rustle.

Tim’s eyes snapped open. He pushed himself upright instantly and turned toward the sound, meeting the heavy, piercing stare of deep green eyes that seemed determined to burn straight through him.

The blanket slipped from his shoulders, and Tim quickly caught it, blinking in confusion as his fingertips brushed the soft fabric. He stared at it as if spellbound, tracing the delicate texture, completely ignoring Damian’s presence—even though Damian continued to bore into him with that intense, unwavering gaze.

Tim himself didn’t understand what was happening to him.

Fragments of everything that had occurred flashed through his mind, one after another, yet he couldn’t find the strength to feel anything about them.

As if…

As if everything had happened exactly the way it was meant to.

“Timothy,” Damian called quietly.

Tim slowly lifted his chin, drawing in a deep breath before answering with an easy, almost careless smirk.

“Long time no see, Dami.”

Damian clearly didn’t appreciate his tone. His brows knit together, and his lips pressed into a thin line. Tim himself wasn’t entirely sure where this strange confidence was coming from.

Or maybe he was.

“Is that really all you have to say, Baby Bird?” The voice came from above.

Tim raised his gaze toward the staircase that led up from the Batcave and spotted Jason’s dark silhouette, who stood there with his arms crossed, like a hunting dog guarding the entrance—making sure no one got in.

Or out, Tim thought uneasily, swallowing with a flicker of apprehension.

Jason was still wearing the Red Hood gear. Damian hadn’t changed either—he was dressed exactly the same as the last time Tim had seen him. So not much time had passed since Tim lost consciousness. Otherwise, Alfred would have absolutely forced them to change.

“Give him a minute to process it, Jay.”

Tim watched warily as Dick stepped out of the shadows beside Jason, leaning his elbows against the railing. With a narrowed gaze that reminded Tim of a panther lazily watching its prey, he spoke in a drawn-out tone while looking straight into Tim’s eyes.

“We’ll have time for that conversation.”

And something told Tim that the conversation wasn’t going to end well for him.

Jason responded by dramatically throwing his hands up in the air, turning to Dick with open disbelief.

“Seriously, Dickwing? Has life not taught you anything at all? He’ll run the first chance he gets, just to avoid this conversation!”

“He won’t run,” Damian interrupted calmly. Tim could hear the certainty in his voice, and something about it made him uneasy. “And if he does,” Damian continued without hesitation, “I’ll follow.”

“That reminds me of something,” Jason muttered darkly, without even a hint of surprise. “Sounds a lot like the beginning of the story of James Moriarty and Sherlock Holmes. I don’t think Gotham would survive that.”

“You’re calling me a villain?” Tim lifted an eyebrow boldly, not bothering to hide his surprise. Had he ever thought about taking over the world? Sure. Who didn’t?

But never seriously. It had always been more like a few idle sketches—plans drawn up just to chase away boredom.

“A villain? No,” Jason sighed, rubbing the bridge of his nose in tired frustration. “A criminal mastermind? Absolutely.”

Beside him, Dick let out a quiet huff, still watching Tim without looking away. Under that unwavering gaze, Tim felt like a painting on display, studied from every angle.

But then his thoughts flickered back to the deal with Klarion.

The playful mood that had settled over him vanished instantly. His expression tightened, the color draining from his face. Trying to steady his racing heartbeat—its rapid thud echoing painfully in his temples—Tim wet his lips and asked hoarsely,

“So…what about the deal?” he asked.

But when the only answer he received was a series of blank stares, Tim’s fingers began to fidget nervously with the edge of the blanket, twisting the fabric between them. He drew in a slow breath and tried to explain.

“I mean…I remember Zatanna. But the deal…it didn’t actually…?”

He couldn’t finish. The words lodged in his throat.

He was afraid to hope. Afraid that the ground beneath him would vanish again. Afraid that all of this was just another illusion woven by a witch.

He was afraid to trust his own eyes.

Memories of betrayal from every single day in Ra’s captivity rose unbidden in his mind—nights when he had fallen asleep hoping the nightmare would end.

He used to dream. Dream that he was Robin again. Dream that he was flying beside Dick. Dream that Bruce smiled at him, telling him he was proud of his work. That Jason sat with him, teaching him about literature. That Damian showed him drawings—pictures where their entire family stood together.

Including Tim.

But morning always came. And Tim would wake again in chains, surrounded by cold stone walls that seeped into his bones and made him shiver.

Because those dreams had only ever been that—dreams.

His foolish, naïve dreams that one day he might become part of a family. But every time, those dreams shattered. Every day he prayed for Bruce to come for him. Every day he believed they would save him.

And every day, he was disappointed by his own stupid hope.

“Magic does not favor those who fail to keep their word. And even a Lord of Chaos cannot defy Fate.” The female voice echoed through the cave. Tim lifted his gaze again toward the entrance. At the top of the stairs stood Zatanna, with Bruce and Duke behind her.

She began descending the steps, the sharp echo of her heels ringing through the suddenly quiet cave. Tim watched her as if spellbound, realizing just how much had changed over the years.

Zatanna looked stronger now. More confident.

Her dark hair had grown longer, falling smoothly over her shoulders, and her clothes accentuated the elegant lines of her figure. When she reached the medical bay where Tim sat, she rested a hand lightly on her hip and gave him a soft, reassuring smile.

“The deal is void,” she said gently. “You’re free, Tim.”

You’re free.

Free.

He was free.

And at the mere thought of it, tears gathered at the corners of his eyes. Because…

What was he supposed to do now? Who was he? Who did they expect him to be?

Tim Drake was free.

But every mask he had ever worn had already been stripped away, and he no longer had the strength to put on another one.

“But I do have a few questions, if you don’t mind,” Zatanna said seriously. Tim gave a faint, absent nod, his gaze drifting somewhere past her as if he were looking straight through her. He wasn’t sure he had fully understood what she had said.

Somewhere behind him, the monitors droned steadily. A metallic clatter rang out from the equipment, louder than the voices in the room. The sounds echoed strangely in the cave, hollow and distant, and Tim found himself drifting in them, catching only brief glimpses of Zatanna’s moving lips.

But he couldn’t understand a single word. It was as though his mind refused to listen.

He was free.

But Tim Drake had never been free.

He had always been bound by expectations, by the reputation of the Drake family. Later, as Robin, the weight of responsibility never left his shoulders. He had put on the mask to help Batman survive his grief. He had become Red Robin because he was the only one who could find Bruce when everyone else had given up, when he himself had been left completely alone.

Tim Drake had never been free.

Someone had always expected something of him.

Be obedient.

Be composed.

Be smarter.

Be older.

Stronger.

More patient.

Be the ghost in other people’s lives.

“…Timothy?”

Someone’s warm hand closed gently around his, but Tim remained sitting there in silence, feeling completely defeated. There was no strength left in him for anything. It was as if, in a single instant, all the life had been pulled out of him—like a body thrown into the ocean, left to drift and drift and drift, staring up at a blindingly bright sky.

“…Timothy, can you squeeze my hand?”

Squeeze it? Of course, he could. Compared to everything else, it was such a small thing.

He tried to curl his fingers, but his body refused to obey.

His hand remained still.

He couldn’t force himself to move. He couldn’t squeeze the hand holding his.

A sharp wave of panic flooded his mind. Suddenly, it felt as if he were trapped inside the spinning wheel of his own thoughts, like a hamster running in endless circles. The exit was there somewhere, but he couldn’t stop long enough to reach it.

His lungs began to burn. Only then did Tim realize he hadn’t been breathing.

He tried to inhale, but a rough, broken cough tore from his throat, scraping painfully against the dryness there. Still, the breath wouldn’t come.

Tears welled in his eyes from the helplessness of it. And in the middle of that rising panic, his body finally jolted awake. His fingers tightened suddenly—hard, almost painfully—around the hand holding his, and he leaned his full weight against it, clinging without a thought for the discomfort it might cause.

Another warm hand moved gently through his hair.

Tim froze for a moment at the unfamiliar touch, his body going still from the unfamiliar gentleness, then, slowly, he relaxed.

“Breathe, Tim. Just breathe. Don’t rush,” the quiet words finally began to reach him through the haze in his mind. Tim tried to focus on them, forcing out a shaky breath before attempting to inhale slowly through his nose. “That’s it, Chum. One more time.”

Tim kept breathing in uneven, ragged pulls. The burning in his lungs slowly began to fade, and the wave of panic that had seized his body started to recede. Gradually, other sensations returned.

He felt the tremor running through his limbs. A large hand continued to stroke gently through his hair, steady and patient, offering comfort so unfamiliar he barely recognized it.

Someone draped a blanket more securely around his shoulders, and Tim found himself relaxing despite everything. In that moment, all the strength drained out of him at once.

When Tim finally lifted his gaze, he saw Damian’s worried face in front of him. The familiar crease had settled between Damian’s brows—the same one Tim remembered from years ago.

Damian was crouching before him, looking up, studying Tim’s face as if searching for something only he could see.

Caught in that fragile moment of calm, Tim slowly raised his free hand. With his index finger, he gently poked the crease between Damian’s brows and tilted his head slightly to the side, a soft smile touching his lips.

“Don’t frown,” he chuckled. “You’ll end up like Bruce.”

For a brief moment, surprise flickered across Damian’s eyes. But he quickly composed himself, arranging his face back into its usual serious expression. Still, the familiar crease between his brows finally softened.

“As you say, Akhi.”

And the whole situation felt so absurd. So absurd that Tim couldn’t help it—he laughed.

But unlike before, the sound that escaped him now was lighter. Softer. Almost like the laughter of a child.

He hadn’t even noticed when the others came down, but their confused expressions were hard to miss now. Tim snorted under his breath and waved a still-trembling hand vaguely at them.

“This is all kind of funny, don’t you think?”

“I think we’ve got very different definitions of funny, Baby Bird,” Jason said. But the relief in his voice was impossible to miss.

“Tim, how are you?” The hoarse voice sounded from above him, and only then did Tim realize who had been standing beside him all this time, gently stroking his hair. Bruce. He was looking down at him with poorly concealed worry.

In response, Tim pressed his lips together and nodded uncertainly, going still again, almost afraid to move.

“I’m okay,” he insisted. He was quietly grateful that his voice didn’t shake. But the look on Bruce’s face made it clear he didn’t believe him. Neither did the others, who were now watching him with open disbelief—as if silently asking, who do you think you’re fooling?

“Of course you are, Chum,” the corners of Bruce’s mouth lifted in faint, surprised amusement, but the concern still flickered in his blue eyes as they remained fixed on Tim. He ran his hand through Tim’s hair once more and drew in a slow breath. “Now you are,” Bruce spoke so softly his voice was barely audible. “Now you’re home.”

And it sounded more like Bruce was trying to convince himself of that. So Tim didn’t try to correct him.

Because…

Was this really his home?

“Tim,” Zatanna called gently. She stepped closer, stopping beside Damian, who was still crouched in front of Tim without moving from his place—like a guard dog keeping watch.

Or rather, a hellhound, Tim thought absently.

“Tell me,” she continued, “do you remember how you recorded that message for me?”

Tim frowned thoughtfully, searching through his memories.

They were chaotic—like someone had broken into his mind, thrown open every cabinet, and scattered the files across the floor, leaving him to sort through the mess and piece them back together.

He remembered a lot.

He remembered recording the video onto a flash drive. He remembered hiding it. He remembered the deal. He remembered the prison in Ra’s dungeon—the cold stone, the chains biting into his wrists.

He remembered his death.

But it seemed that between the moment he recorded the first video and the moment he found Bruce, there was a black hole in his memory. Only faint echoes surfaced in his mind—fragments that refused to fit together into a single picture.

“No, I don’t remember,” he said slowly. Then he took a deep breath and straightened his shoulders, sitting upright the way his mother had taught him. Calculations raced through his mind—possible outcomes, probabilities, patterns—and pushing down the uncertainty clawing at him, he looked at Zatanna with calm, analytical seriousness.

“Based on what I do remember, I can assume that…the first me—” he paused briefly. It felt strange to say it out loud, because it was him. And yet…it was as if his mind held three different sets of memories. “—recorded the message around the same time when he talked to Duke,” he continued, his voice carefully controlled. “Assuming Bruce wouldn’t involve the League in this, I would’ve encoded the message on the second flash drive. I wouldn’t want anyone interfering with my plans too early,” he gave a casual shrug, not noticing the way the looks around him darkened. “I’d set a trigger, so that when someone found the second flash drive and opened the file on it, the hidden message would automatically be sent to you and then erase itself from the drive. That way, if it fell into the wrong hands, they wouldn’t discover anything about you.”

Tim finished confidently. Then, after a moment, he added more thoughtfully,

“That’s only a theory. I remember the first and the second videos, but I don’t remember how I recorded the message for you. So I can only assume I did it before I recorded the first video,” he paused slightly. “Because I barely remember anything from before that.”

He finished speaking, and the cave sank into a dead silence. Tim saw the glances they exchanged. He saw the disbelief and unease lingering in their eyes. He stayed utterly still, not betraying a single reaction.

Just as his mother had taught him.

Just as Bruce had taught him.

“I don’t even know what scares me more,” Jason finally broke the silence, raking a hand roughly through his hair, his expression troubled. “The fact that he talks about it so casually…or the fact that he actually did all of that,” Tim couldn’t understand why it unsettled him so much. “Alright, fine, the Zatanna part makes sense,” Jason continued, agitation creeping into his voice. “But if you were smart enough to figure all that out, why the hell didn’t you consider that the witch boy might not keep his end of the deal?!” His voice rose, rough and raw with emotion. “We thought we lost you, Tim! What the hell!”

Jason was almost snarling now, and a small, instinctive part of Tim wanted to shrink away from him, to curl into a corner and hide. But his face remained blank, his posture steady as he answered with quiet patience.

“Because it’s hard to know something was taken from you when you don’t remember ever having it in the first place.”

At his words, they all froze.

Tim could see it on their faces—regret, anger, something heavier that settled in their eyes like a storm about to break. Only Zatanna and Damian looked more composed, though judging by the way the green of Damian’s eyes had darkened, Tim suspected he was simply contemplating several methods of murder.

“I remember fragments,” Tim continued quietly. “But my mind was too unstable back then. And even now…everything feels messy and I don’t know what I miss.”

He gasped a sharp, ragged breath, jaw tight, as the tremor inside him threatened to break free. Every muscle in his chest quivered, his ribs feeling crushed beneath the relentless weight of the emotions clawing and pounding from within.

“When I made the deal, I was lost,” he said. “I was desperate.”

His gaze shifted toward Bruce and Dick.

“You hated me,” both of them flinched. “You were acting strange,” Tim added, his eyes moving to Jason and Damian. Then his gaze settled on Duke. “And Duke, I…I don’t remember you.”

There was grief in his voice as he spoke. But instead of hurt, he saw something else on Duke’s face—quiet support. And Tim let himself relax for a moment.

“I was alone,” he went on. “With no memory of what had happened to me. Ra’s was chasing me. And when I ended up in the cave, you attacked me. You locked me in a cell like a criminal,” his voice tightened. “And then I saw my own coffin. My empty coffin!” For a moment, his composure faltered. “So forgive me for making a deal just to figure out where—or when—the hell I had ended up!”

He wasn’t shouting. But he was close.

Then Tim looked up again, straight at Bruce. Tears stood openly in his eyes now, and when he spoke again, his voice dropped to a hoarse whisper.

“When I refused to give in the first time…they made me forget what happened. So, they could break me again,” his lips trembled. “I remember calling for you, Bruce.” The cave felt impossibly quiet. “I remember waiting for you every night. I thought you were looking for me. Every time I fell asleep, I hoped you’d come and find me.”

His voice cracked, barely audible now.

“But you never did. Because to you…I was already dead.” A weak and broken smile pulled at his lips. “I guess Ra’s succeeded in breaking me after all,” he paused. “Or maybe I’d been broken for a long time already.”

“No, Tim,” Bruce said, lowering himself beside him. Tim immediately caught the familiar scent of kevlar and cold metal, mixed with the faint, earthy warmth of Bruce himself.

Strong arms wrapped around his shoulders, pulling him against the solid wall of Bruce’s chest. For a moment, Tim allowed himself that weakness. He turned away from the others and pressed his face against Bruce, as if he could hide there.

“You’re stronger than all of us,” Bruce said quietly. “You survived something none of us could even imagine,” Tim felt Bruce’s arms tighten around him. “When you disappeared, I never stopped looking for you. But we had no leads. No trail to follow. Still, I kept searching—every night, every single day—praying that I would find you alive.”

Bruce’s voice trembled. Tim felt Bruce’s chin come to rest against the top of his head as the embrace tightened, completely surrounding him with warmth.

“And I did find you,” Bruce continued softly. “But it was already too late,” his grip on Tim’s shoulders faltered for a moment. “I couldn’t protect you. I betrayed your trust. I failed you.”

Tim could hear the weight of the words in his voice.

“I won’t ask for forgiveness,” Bruce went on, quieter now. “But I want you to know this—I never gave up on you, Tim.”

His arms tightened again.

“I would do anything to turn back time. Anything to keep you safe from all of this.”

“But you said…you regretted making me Robin,” Tim whispered, eyes closing as a single, traitorous tear slid down his cheek. He could hear Bruce’s heartbeat, feel his hand pressing gently against his back, and he couldn’t find the strength to pull away from the embrace.

“You’re wrong, Tim,” Bruce said firmly. “You didn’t become my regret because you failed me. You became my regret because I was the one who failed you, Tim. As Robin. As my son.”

Tim’s chest tightened, and he jerked back, staring in disbelief at Bruce’s eyes, brimming with pain.

“Jason was right. I taught you how to fight, but I never taught you how to be a son. And that’s entirely on me. I’m sorry, Tim. I’m sorry I was never the father you deserved.”

This time, Tim couldn’t hide his shock.

“We all failed you, Baby Bird,” Dick’s voice suddenly cut in, and he stepped beside Bruce. Tim turned his gaze at him, blinking uncertainly several times, opening his mouth to speak but finding no words.

“I was supposed to be the older brother,” Dick continued, voice heavy with regret, “but I let my own grief cloud my judgment. I’m not making excuses, but I want you to know, Tim—” He stepped closer, placing a hand gently on Tim’s shoulder, bending slightly to meet his eyes. “You’re the best Robin I’ve ever known. And you’re my family. No matter how long it takes, I’ll fight to earn back your trust. Even if you never forgive me.”

There was genuine sincerity in their eyes, and Tim wanted to believe it. But he had grown so accustomed to being alone, pushed aside, that he no longer allowed himself hope, because he knew that next time, he might never return as Robin.

“And speaking of that deal…” Jason’s voice cut through the heavy moment, and Tim was grateful for the interruption. He needed a pause, a moment to process everything that had been said. Dick released his shoulder but stayed nearby, turning toward Jason, who now fixed his gaze on Zatanna. “Why didn’t the witch boy simply honor his side of the deal? I mean, we probably wouldn’t be able to stop him if the deal had gone through.”

“I couldn’t have interfered with the deal,” Zatanna confirmed, tilting her chin thoughtfully. “Had the deal been valid, Tim’s fate would have been entirely different. But you planned for that too, didn’t you, Tim?”

Tim only shrugged. Zatanna smiled understandingly but spoke with authority.

“A soul’s bargain is a fragile thing. Even though it would have strengthened Klarion, you would have lost what he truly sought. A puppet without feelings isn’t much use. He restored your memories, Tim, letting you believe the deal had been completed, so that in the end, you would willingly hand over your soul. Only then would it be bound by his magic, while your mind remained untouched.”

“So it’s like a golden bird in a cage,” Jason muttered, clearly displeased, his hand instinctively brushing against his weapon. “He didn’t need Tim’s soul itself. He needed Tim.”

“Roughly speaking,” Zatanna nodded. “However—” she turned to Duke, who had been quietly standing to the side all this time—“what exactly made you realize that Klarion didn’t honor his side of the deal?”

“Tim told me,” Duke said simply, and a chorus of heavy exhalations rippled around the group. Tim caught the weight of their scrutinizing stares and, without thinking, bared his teeth in a tense, almost defensive scowl.

“I didn’t say anything.”

“Of course,” Jason replied seriously, crossing his arms over his chest. Duke chuckled softly, a playful glint flickering in his eyes.

“Not directly, no—but you gave me a clue the last time we saw each other,” Duke continued, and Tim’s attention sharpened, leaning forward in anticipation. “‘And if I cease to be myself, it means there’s nothing left of me worth saving.’ You could anticipate many things, Tim, but you couldn’t foresee how you’d return after death. Back on the rooftop, you said you would win this game. You asked me to trust you. And I did, Tim,” Duke’s gaze softened, the tension easing from his shoulders. “Just as you trusted me to understand. Because, even though you forgot me, you are still my brother, Tim.”

Tim felt a lump in his throat at Duke’s words, the weight of responsibility pressing on him—the knowledge that he had placed so much on his brother’s shoulders, only to forget him in the end.

“Zatanna, can you restore his memories?” Dick asked suddenly, and Tim turned his head toward the sorceress. But the hope in his chest crumbled the moment he saw her expression.

“I can’t return what I don’t have. I’m sorry, Tim.”

Tim lowered his head, nodding with understanding. He understood perfectly. And yet…he couldn’t help but feel the unfairness of it all toward Duke.

“Hey, Tim,” Duke called gently, sliding down to sit on the other side of him. Tim was now tucked between two large presences, with Damian in front, shielding him. But this time, he didn’t feel like a trapped animal in a cage. The sense of danger he had known before was gone.

“It doesn’t matter that you’ve forgotten me. We’ll make new memories. If, of course, you’re okay with being my brother again.”

And Duke’s smile—bright, warm, and unyielding—melted something in Tim. He let himself respond with a smile of his own.

“I’d like to be your brother again, Duke.”

“Well, I think it’s time for me to go,” Zatanna said abruptly. “I’ll deliver the good news to the League. They’ll want to hear about it.”

She pivoted on her heel, ready to leave the cave, but before stepping away, her sharp gaze lingered on Bruce, who visibly tensed beside Tim. “And Bruce,” she added, her tone softening slightly, “we’re all waiting for you to come back.” Her eyes swept over the others, gifting them a brief smile. “Each of you. After all, the League isn’t the same without the bats.”

“Wait!” Tim called out before she had a chance to leave. Zatanna paused, tilting her head questioningly at him. Desperation threaded through his voice. “Why…why did I forget the memories with Duke? Why that time?”

“Didn’t you figure it out yourself, Tim?” Zatanna replied, arching an eyebrow. “After what Duke said?”

Tim frowned, pressing his lips together in frustration. He couldn’t grasp what she meant.

“You trusted him, Tim,” she continued, her voice echoing slightly through the cavern. “That’s why those memories were dangerous for Klarion. His goal was to break you by taking away what gave you hope,” she moved toward the exit, her footsteps echoing sharply, but Tim caught the last words clearly.

“And that trust became your salvation.”

Tim stood in front of the mirror, watching droplets of water trickle from his hair. He looked exhausted. The dark circles under his eyes had grown deeper than usual, his face pale, and his body was covered in cuts and scrapes—wounds Alfred had carefully treated before insisting everyone change clothes.

He dried his wet hair with a towel, too drained to even reach for the hairdryer. He hung the towel to dry and stepped out of the bath, shivering slightly from the sudden change in temperature, but he stopped abruptly in the corridor, looking at Bruce who was leaning against the wall, phone in hand, his brow furrowed in thought. But the moment he saw Tim, that tension melted, and a warm, relieved smile spread across his face.

“Bruce?” Tim asked, confused. What was he doing here? Surely Bruce Wayne had more important matters than watching over him. But apparently, the man himself didn’t see it that way.

“Tim, are you done?” Bruce asked. Tim simply nodded, squinting slightly as he observed him. Bruce didn’t acknowledge the look, instead placing a hand on Tim’s back, guiding him down the corridor. “Good. Let’s go.”

Tim didn’t ask where. It didn’t matter. He was alone with Bruce, who wasn’t rushing to get rid of him, and Tim greedily soaked up the attention, afraid to lose it. Yet, one question still lingered in his mind—the same one that had gnawed at him ever since their conversation, refusing to be shaken free.

“Bruce, tell me… if you don’t regret that I became Robin, that I became Red Robin,” Tim began hesitantly, lowering his gaze, acutely aware of Bruce’s attention. It was…unsettling, almost overwhelming. “Then why did you remove my suit from the cave? Why did you just…forget about me?”

It might have sounded childish, but Tim was too exhausted to care about appearances.

“Oh, Tim,” Bruce sighed heavily, and Tim couldn’t quite place what it meant. “Come on. I’ll show you something.”

Bruce led him down another corridor, and Tim kept glancing over his shoulder, a knot of unease tightening in his chest. He knew exactly where Bruce was taking him.

It was the Wayne family wing—the part of the manor no one entered except Alfred or Bruce. Not because it was forbidden, but because everyone understood what that place meant to Bruce.

Stopping at the door that led to Bruce’s parents’ quarters, Tim couldn’t help but flinch. He could see how Bruce hesitated for a heartbeat, collecting himself, how his fingers brushed the handle. Tim wouldn’t have blamed him if he had turned away and left this wing entirely. But then Bruce flung the door open with a creak, nudging Tim gently inside, and Tim… Tim froze, his feet rooted to the floor, staring first at the portrait of Bruce with his parents hanging on the wall, then at the Red Robin suit displayed beside the photograph of two of them.

The Red Robin suit hung there, right next to the portrait of Bruce’s deceased parents.

“I wanted you to be with my parents, Tim,” Bruce whispered, not hiding grief in his voice, and Tim swallowed hard, trying to choke back the tears pooling in his throat. “I never forgot about you. I just…didn’t have the right.” A heavy hand settled on Tim’s head, and he lifted his gaze to meet Bruce’s glassy, but soft eyes. “You’re my son, Tim. And you’re home. You’re home, and I won’t let you go anywhere again.”

Tim didn’t argue. Because looking at the Wayne family portrait, and then at the photo of himself with Bruce, he finally understood, with a tremor of awe and relief, the weight of Bruce’s words.

Tim was home.

“What took you so long?!” Jason’s voice called from the hallway. Tim wiped the wetness from his eyes with his jacket sleeve and turned toward the door, spotting Jason’s figure stepping into view, his glare locking sharply on Bruce. “You promised to bring him to us immediately,” Jason added, frowning.

“Sorry, Jaylad,” Bruce replied calmly. “We were held up a little.”

Jason raised an eyebrow, but without pressing further, he grabbed Tim by the wrist and led him toward Bruce’s room. “You’re not allowed to be alone, so tonight we sleep together.”

Tim snorted but let himself be pulled along, watching Jason’s back and hearing Bruce’s footsteps behind them. When they reached the other room, Jason didn’t waste words—he swung the door open, pushing Tim toward the bed. “One baby bird delivered.”

Before Tim could even blink, he found himself enveloped in Dick’s arms, grinning down at him, mischief sparkling in familiar blue eyes.

“Careful, Todd. You might hurt Timothy,” Damian scolded, prompting Jason to roll his eyes as he joined them at the bed, waving at Dick. “Move, Dickwing.”

Soon, they were all sprawled across Bruce’s massive bed, Tim nestled safely among them, enveloped in a warmth and comfort he had only ever imagined. He began to drift toward sleep, the soft, familiar bickering of his brothers filling the room like a gentle lullaby. And just before darkness fully claimed him, Alfred’s calm voice echoed softly from the doorway.

“They’re all home, Master Bruce.”

“They’re all home, Alfred. My sons are home.”

“We meet again,” Tim turned to stare at the familiar smile reflected in the mirror—his own smile. In that instant, he relaxed, clasping his hands behind his back and letting out a quiet chuckle, squinting slightly.

“As if you didn’t plan this,” he replied cheekily.

“We did,” came another voice, sharper this time. Tim turned calmly, his gaze settling on the version of himself just a few months older—the months he had spent in Ra’s captivity the first time. And now he understood. Remembering everything that had happened to him, he recognized the coldness and the edge in that tone.

He was the same. They all had been the same when Bruce admitted that he regretted letting him become Robin.

But all of this—the sharpness, the boldness—it was merely a mask. The mask of Timothy Drake.

“We planned it,” the younger Tim repeated, stepping closer to his older self, whose gaze remained gentle as it met theirs. “We knew you would agree to the deal,” he said with calm certainty, furrowing his brows slightly in thought before nodding to his own reflection and adding, “That we would agree to the deal.”

Tim took a deep breath, closing his eyes for a moment and replaying their first conversation in his mind.

“But…weren’t you angry,” he began, glancing first at one, then the other, “that I agreed?”

The older Tim’s gaze changed into sadness, though his gentle smile never wavered. He tilted his head, letting strands of hair fall across his face, brushing close to Tim, who felt a little uncomfortable under the intimate attention.

“We were angry at ourselves,” older Tim said quietly, tilting his chin toward the younger Tim, who had crossed his arms over his chest, ignoring him entirely. “We both knew what you would do. Because we would have done the same.”

“Pretty two-faced of you,” Tim raised an eyebrow, earning only a quiet, knowing chuckle in response.

“Is it two-faced if there are three of us?”

“You said we’re the same person, didn’t you?” Tim pressed, still bewildered by how and why they were all here together.

“Tell me, Tim,” the older Tim said, his gaze calm yet edged with a quiet intensity, “can a person who has forgotten their past self ever truly be the same as before?” Tim lowered his eyes, unable to face those emotions reflected in a face that was his own. It felt unreal. Hollow. Just like him.

“Don’t worry,” the older Tim continued gently, “if everything goes according to plan, you won’t see us again. Your mind is still scattered. We are merely phantoms of the memories you lost—the lives you forgot. That’s all.”

Tim gave a measured nod, wetting his lips with his tongue, nervously fidgeting with his fingers before asking, “Then…what do you want from me?”

They looked back at him with expectant, questioning eyes, and he rolled his eyes, brushing a hand through his hair with a small snort. “Come on. You planned all this. So what did I miss? What did I forget?”

“That…we cannot tell you,” the older Tim said with that same infuriatingly calm smile that made Tim bristle for a moment, though he quickly suppressed it, crossing his arms over his chest in defiance. The older Tim just chuckled. “Don’t be upset. Some things we simply cannot say. Your mind must remember them itself.”

“But we do have a request,” the younger Tim interrupted, stepping forward. His confidence wavered slightly, as if he momentarily forgot what he wanted to say. The older Tim reached out, gently placing a hand on his shoulder, squeezing lightly, offering quiet reassurance—silent words of, it’s okay.

And Tim felt his throat tighten, bone-dry, because he recognized that mask. That damn mask he wore every time he wasn’t okay.

“If one day our…Bruce and the others find out about you,” the younger Tim began, voice hesitant, as though even the thought felt foreign to him. Tim understood. He could relate; he still couldn’t quite believe anyone had actually come for him. “If one day you see them, tell them…tell them…”

“…That they are not to blame,” the older Tim finished firmly, stepping decisively toward Tim and stopping just an outstretched arm away. But his next words seemed distant, carried from everywhere at once, through a wild storm of sound, and the figure across from him seemed to drift further away. No matter how hard Tim tried to reach for him, his limbs refused to obey. All he could do was watch that smile—so calm, yet carrying the weight of all the pain it had ever held.

“Tell them,” the voice continued, echoing from all sides, “…that the true writer of The Ballad of Dead Robin is

Timothy Drake.”
