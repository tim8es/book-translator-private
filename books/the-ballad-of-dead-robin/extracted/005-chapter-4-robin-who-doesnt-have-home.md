“I am Damian al Ghul Wayne, grandson of the Demon’s Head himself, Ra’s al Ghul. The true son of Bruce Wayne.”

Tim stared blankly into the venomous green eyes of the child standing before him. A child dressed in his colors, his armor, the mantle that once defined him. The Robin suit.

The katana in Damian’s small, sure hand gleamed under the dim light. He stepped closer. Tim didn’t move, not even when the blade’s tip kissed the hollow of his throat, biting into flesh until a thin line of blood traced a scarlet path down his skin. He simply stood there, watching, feeling something deep inside him shatter.

This wasn’t anger. It wasn’t fear. It was the quiet recognition that he’d been replaced — erased — and now stood face-to-face with the proof of his own obsolescence. Damian’s lips curved in cruel satisfaction; his eyes burned with inherited pride.

And Tim… Tim let it happen. Let the boy peel away what little was left of his identity, strip him bare of every illusion of belonging. Because he wasn’t family. Not really. He never had been.

“Batman doesn’t need a failed Robin,” Damian hissed. “I’ll be the one worthy to stand beside my father.”

In the half-light, those green eyes caught fire, bright, consuming, merciless, until every last flicker of hope inside Tim turned to ash.

“You don’t belong here, pretender.”

And the worst part was, Tim already knew. He had never belonged with them.

“Akhi?”

The word sliced through the silence like a whisper carried on cold wind.

Before him were the same green eyes, sharp and predatory, yet this time, they weren’t burning with hatred. No… this was something else. Something softer. A quiet ache that lingered in their depths—longing, mournful and heavy—and it made Tim’s stomach twist.

The man standing before him couldn’t be Damian. He couldn’t.

This wasn’t the boy who’d once pressed a blade to his throat.

The figure was taller now, broad-shouldered, his skin kissed by desert sun. Dark hair fell loosely around his face, the strands catching faint traces of moonlight as he moved. The green tunic he wore shimmered faintly with each breath, a shade that made his eyes seem almost luminous in the darkness. Those same eyes, fixed unblinking on Tim, like they were searching for something long lost.

He looked like Damian.

He looked too much like him.

But he couldn’t be. Even if Tim had fallen through time, even if this was another world entirely… Damian would never have called him that.

Akhi.

Brother.

No, this had to be a dream—a cruel, fevered dream born from exhaustion and nightmares.

Ra’s voice wouldn’t leave him.

Forgotten Robin. Unwanted. Abandoned.

The words echoed in his head like poison, seeping into the cracks of his mind.

When the figure before him took a step closer, Tim flinched instinctively, his body tensing as though struck. The movement made the other pause, and in those green eyes flickered something that shouldn’t have been there. Regret.

That alone proved this couldn’t be real.

“Akhi…”

The voice was deeper now, richer, but cracked around the edges, as if unsure of its own existence, like it was reaching through time or dreams to find him. It trembled with something raw, something lost.

Tim couldn’t move. Couldn’t breathe. He’d escaped one nightmare only to stumble into the hands of another.

The cold bit into his bare feet, spreading upward, sinking deep until it felt like frost was blooming in his veins. He could feel it, the numbness, the creeping stillness, freezing him from the inside out.

He stood there, caught between the pulse of his terror and the strange calm that followed. Tim Drake had always walked that thin line between reality and madness, between survival and surrender. Maybe Dick had been right all along.

Maybe there was no place for him among the sane.

“No,” he whispered, his own voice foreign, ragged, almost pleading. The fragile word desperately hung in the night air.

Across from him, Damian’s eyes narrowed, sharp as his katana. He lowered his hands slowly, his posture softening, but Tim knew better. He could read predators, the subtle quiet before they struck, the way calm was only another mask.

Because the first rule of the hunt was simple: Never let your prey see when you’re ready to tear out its throat.

“No,” Tim repeated, quieter this time, though he wasn’t sure who he was saying it to—Damian, himself, or his own demons that refused to let him go.

The other boy didn’t move. He just stood there, watching. Studying. And the longer those green eyes stayed fixed on him, the more Tim felt the cracks forming in his composure. A short, broken laugh slipped out of him, too raw, too desperate for the dead stillness of the night.

The cold settled on his skin like frost, the kind that bites through to the bone. The wind slipped beneath the oversized clothes he wore, and he shivered, realizing how small he felt. How utterly powerless he was beneath the weight of Damian’s gaze.

He wanted to turn away, to shut himself in his room and pretend this was just another nightmare he could wake from. But the voice stopped him.

“I’m sorry, akhi…” Damian whispered.

The word—akhi—hit like a blade. Tim blinked, confusion flashing in his eyes, disbelief tangled with something sharp and unnameable.

For a moment, everything tilted. The garden, the darkness, even the stars above seemed to blur together. Panic clawed at his chest, constricting his lungs as his vision began to swim. He could hear the pounding of his own heartbeat, could feel it, desperate and uneven, drowning out everything else.

“Sorry?” he rasped through clenched teeth. It came out half-choked, half-laugh—a sound that didn’t belong to sanity. And then came the laughter again, unbidden, tearing its way out of his throat like something feral that had finally escaped.

It was too sharp. Too broken. Too mad.

“For what? For the attempted murder?” Tim’s voice came out cold, jagged, his lips twisting into something that might have been a smile if not for how hollow his eyes looked. “Or for stealing my suit?”

He took a step closer, the words tumbling out like poison. “You know what… Forget Robin. I only regret that you didn’t finish the job back then.”

Damian’s head snapped up, his jaw tightening. His hands trembled, just barely, but enough to betray the violent instinct flickering beneath the surface. He looked ready to grab something, someone, but forced himself to stillness. A statue carved out of guilt and anger.

For a fleeting moment, Tim allowed himself to believe the lie his mind offered, that this wasn’t real. That the figure standing before him wasn’t flesh and blood, but a ghost conjured by his exhaustion.

“I never wanted to be Robin anyway,” Tim said at last, his voice low, frayed at the edges.

And that was it. No more words. No more laughter. Nothing left inside him to hold on to—nothing to anchor himself to the fragile thread of sanity that had been slipping through his fingers all night.

He turned, his body moving before his mind caught up, feet slapping softly against the cold floor as he stumbled back toward the manor. The corridor stretched endlessly before him, dark and silent, the only sound his uneven breaths and the faint echo of his own retreat.

That night, sleep never came.

And in the silence that followed, from the corners of the dark, he could still hear it—that quiet, mocking whisper, the one that refused to leave him alone.

“Akhi.”

Tim waited for the morning before daring to leave his room.  The smell of roasted beans drifted through the hall as he approached the kitchen, familiar and comforting, almost enough to steady him, until voices stopped him cold at the doorway.

Dick and Jason were deep in a heated discussion, their tones sharp enough to cut through the clinking of dishes. Bruce sat at the table, unmoving, a dark silhouette against the morning light bleeding through the windows. His hands were clasped, knuckles pale. He looked like a man deep in thought, or like a man holding himself together by sheer will.

Only Alfred seemed calm. But Tim saw the tremor in his fingers as he picked up a butter knife, saw the stiffness in his shoulders as he tried to make his movements look natural. It was a quiet kind of tension—habitual and restrained. The kind that came from years of holding grief close enough to make it invisible.

Damian was nowhere in sight. The realization brought Tim a flicker of relief, one that vanished the instant Bruce’s eyes lifted to meet his.

That cold look. Appraising.

Not angry—worse. Detached.

Tim froze under it, his fingers tightening on the fabric of his sleeve as if he could anchor himself there. He knew that stare. He’d lived under it once. It was the look that said you don’t belong here.

Because Tim Drake had been the one Bruce never chose.

He’d been Robin before he’d ever been a son. And maybe he never really was.

“Master Tim?” Alfred’s voice broke through the suffocating silence, polite as ever, but laced with concern that made everyone else fall quiet.

Dick and Jason turned sharply, their conversation dying mid-word. Their eyes landed on Tim, pinning him in place. The air in the kitchen thickened. And for a moment, just a heartbeat, Tim wanted nothing more than to turn around, retreat to his room, and close the door on all of it.

But he didn’t have the time, or the luxury, for childish hesitation. He needed answers. He needed to understand what had happened to him.

“Good morning,” he summoned what little courage he had left, stepping forward as though walking into enemy fire. Every motion in the kitchen seemed to stop; even the sound of the clock felt muted beneath the sudden, suffocating silence.

He could feel their heavy and dissecting eyes on him, but he kept moving, heading straight for the coffee machine. His movements were automatic. Open the cupboard. Take the mug. Pretend you belong here.

Nothing had changed. Everything was exactly the same. Unforgivably the same.

Even the new coffee maker stood in its old place, as if mocking the years that had passed.

Tim watched the dark liquid pour into his mug, the faint hiss and drip of the machine grounding him just enough to keep breathing. He lifted the cup with both hands, savoring the warmth against his skin before taking a long, deliberate sip. The bitterness hit his tongue, giving him the forgotten feeling of safety.

Only then did he turn. All three of them were watching him.

Dick stood with his arms crossed over his chest, his expression unreadable but his eyes predatory, scanning Tim from head to toe. His jaw was set, his lips pressed thin.

Bruce was worse. The same hatred from last night lingered in his face, but now it was colder, buried beneath a mask of icy restraint. The kind of anger that didn’t need to be spoken to be felt.

And Jason—Jason’s stare wasn’t cruel, but it was worse somehow. Judgment. It crawled under Tim’s skin like frost.

Tim’s grip on the mug tightened until his knuckles turned white. His jaw flexed.

He met Jason’s gaze with a piercing, venomous squint, a flash of fury that burned for half a heartbeat before the exhaustion swallowed it whole.

“What?” He tried to sound intimidating, but even to his own ears, it came out tired—threadbare.

Jason arched an eyebrow, looking unimpressed. He gestured impatiently toward Tim’s coffee cup, his voice rising with theatrical exasperation.

“Are we still going to pretend he’s not Tim?”

Dick’s lips pressed into a hard line, his eyes narrowing as he studied Tim. And Tim… Tim had no idea how he was supposed to act anymore.

They weren’t his family. Maybe they never were.  They could hate him. They could rage, accuse, threaten. It wouldn’t matter. Tim had seen it all before—their rage, their grief, their disappointment.

And this time, he had nothing left to lose.

“I need access to the computer,” he said flatly, ignoring Jason’s theatrics as his eyes locked on Bruce. The reaction was immediate. Bruce’s expression darkened, his voice dropping to that low, final register that could silence a battlefield.

“No.”

Tim frowned darkly, draining the last drop of coffee before setting the mug down with a sharp clack against the counter. He rolled his shoulders back, leaning casually against the bar as if he couldn’t care less, that same feigned indifference that always seemed to infuriate the adults around him.

His eyes, cold and flat, locked on Bruce.

“I need to go home,” he said, enunciating each word slowly, deliberately, almost mockingly, like he was explaining something to a child. Except the words tasted wrong. Because…where was his home, really?

Bruce didn’t answer; he just bared his teeth, the gesture too raw, too animal, to be mistaken for anything human. For a heartbeat, Tim was thirteen again. The memory slammed into him like a physical blow.

The smell of rain-soaked concrete. The sting of antiseptic. He remembered the grueling training sessions that left him bruised and battered, the patrols where he had to call ambulances over the pools of blood left in Batman’s wake. He remembered that cold, disgusted stare Bruce had cast at him one night, grief driving him to drain a bottle of whiskey and hurl it straight at Tim.

“Get out! You’re not Robin! You’re not him!”

The bottle had shattered against the wall before Tim even realized Bruce had thrown it. He’d ducked, but he was too slow, and a jagged piece had caught him across the eyelid, leaving a hot, wet line of red running down his cheek.

Bruce hadn’t even looked at him. Just stood there, breathing hard, eyes full of rage and hatred.

“You’re not my son.”

The words had burned worse than the glass. And standing there now, in the silence of the kitchen, Tim could still feel the echo of that night, that same ache pulsing behind his eye, the phantom taste of blood on his tongue.

Because Bruce’s son was dead, and all that remained of him lived only in the darkest corners of his memory. While Tim, piece by piece, had let his own soul rot away trying to drag Batman out of his personal hell.

And to do that, Tim had to befriend those same demons. Had to let them take root in his mind, one by one, until he could no longer tell where they ended and he began.

The magic of Robin was long gone.

“We have no reason to trust you,” Bruce said at last, but Tim’s answer was colder.

“And you have no reason not to.”

He could play this game forever. Tim Drake was the game, the master liar of his own theater, a boy made of masks and illusions.

“You broke into the cave. You hacked the code,” Bruce continued, his tone accusing. A muscle twitched in his jaw, and for a brief, electric second, Tim thought the mug in Bruce’s hand might actually crack.

There it was again, that flicker of madness behind his eyes. That wild, desperate gleam that Tim knew too well. It should’ve scared him. Instead, it made him want to laugh.

And lately, he’d been laughing far too often.

“Forgive me,” Tim said, voice dripping with mock sweetness, “for trying to come home.”

He didn’t mention Ra’s al Ghul. Didn’t tell them what he’d been running from. They already didn’t trust him, and Tim Drake wasn’t stupid enough to hand them another reason to.

He’d deal with Ra’s on his own, once he understood what had been done to him. Emotions could wait. Survival, gathering data—that was all that mattered.

“Or for planning to destroy us with a copy of the son I lost?”

Tim froze, staring blankly at Bruce as he rose from the chair, the groan of the wood echoing in the quiet kitchen. Every movement Bruce made seemed deliberate and calculated. He adjusted his collar, set down his empty mug, and walked away, leaving Tim feeling as though he was nothing more than a phantom haunting the edges of the kitchen.

Before disappearing, Bruce cast a brief, cutting glance at Dick.

“Do not let him into the cave,” he said firmly, leaving no space for arguments.

Hope crumbled in Tim’s chest. Every shred of it. Any thought that someone here might understand him, that someone might help, disintegrated in that single command. The void inside him expanded, pressing down on his lungs. Red Robin was alone. Tim Drake was nobody.

Jason’s voice tried to pierce the heavy silence. “Hey, Baby Bird… breakfast?”

Tim ignored it. Ignored everything. He wasn’t hungry. He barely noticed the cold metal of the mug in his hands, the ache in his bruised ribs and torn muscles that screamed with each step. Every movement hurt, every breath was a reminder of how fragile he was, but he didn’t stop. He fled.

Jason shouted in frustration.

“Tim—wait!”

Dick barked an order. “Stop!”

But neither of them followed. Neither of them really cared. Tim’s wounds, his staggered gait, his trembling hands—they could handle him like he was nothing. They had power, they had guns. They had each other.

He pushed the pain aside, forcing himself forward, tasting the bitter coffee, feeling it burn down his throat. His body screamed, but his mind was sharper than ever.

He would find the answers. He had always found them. Even when the whole world turned its back.
