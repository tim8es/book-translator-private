He slammed the door shut and collapsed against it, sliding down until he hit the floor. Fingers tangled in his hair, he tried—desperately—to stop the avalanche of thoughts pounding through his skull, refusing him even a moment of clarity. He needed to think.

Think, Tim. THINK.

“Damn it,” he hissed through clenched teeth, scanning the empty room with frantic eyes. They had made sure he was defenseless. No gadgets. No tools. Nothing but clothes and bed. Motion sensors glinted on the windows—there was no escape. The second he tried anything, they would know.

A soft knock fractured the stillness, jolting him. Tim scrambled backward across the floor, but remained seated, staring at the door like it might leap at him.

“Baby Bird…it’s me,” Jason’s voice, rough and muffled by the wood. Tim sealed his mouth shut, muscles locked in place. A weary exhale drifted through from the other side. “I brought breakfast. You didn’t eat a thing.”

Tim’s brows pinched lower. His fingers dug into the sleeves of his tunic, knuckles whitening as he waited…waited for the trap. The door was unlocked. He had no weapon. Nothing to defend himself. If they wanted to burst in—if they wanted to drag him out—there was nothing he could do.

He was completely at their mercy. Powerless. Cornered. Caged. Like an animal.

“You belong to me now, Timothy. Never forget that,” Ra’s voice oozed through his mind like poison, slick and sweet and suffocating. A violent shiver shot down Tim’s spine.

“Tim?” Jason called, and Tim’s stomach twisted hard at the fake concern dripping from his tone. Like Tim mattered to him. Like any of them ever cared.

“Baby Bird,” Jason tried again softly, almost pleading, and Tim bit down on his lip so hard that copper flooded his tongue.

It wasn’t Jason. It wasn’t Jason.

He had to stay focused. Figure out where he was. How he got here. How to get out.

“I’m not coming in unless you want me to,” Jason continued. “You’re safe in there, Tim.”

Safe. Tim almost laughed. He had never—not once—been safe near any of them.

“I’ll leave the tray by the door. Just…try to eat, okay?” A muted knock against the floor followed, and then heavy footsteps receded down the hallway, leaving Tim alone with the pounding of his pulse.

He let out a slow, shuddering breath, one he hadn’t realized he’d been holding, feeling it scrape through his chest like rough smoke, leaving a hollow ache in its wake. His lungs burned, straining to refill with air.

Plan. He needed a plan.

Think, Tim. Think.

He shot up to his feet and cracked open the door, checking it that no one lingered nearby. Then he grabbed the tray and slammed the door shut again.

Blueberry waffles. His favorite. The rich, buttery scent curled through the air, sticky-sweet with melted sugar and blueberries bursting like tiny jewels. It hit him like a punch, dredging up memories of quiet mornings he hadn’t felt in a while. His stomach twisted violently, as if rebelling against both hunger and the ache of longing that the smell unearthed.

He tossed the tray onto the bedside table and snatched the knife from it, slipping the blade into his pocket. A weapon. Finally…something.

His Red Robin gear was gone. Stripped from him. What little hadn’t been shredded or burnt during…during whatever happened—was nowhere in sight. Someone had changed him while he was unconscious.

Shaking his head hard, Tim began pacing the room in nervous, tight circles, his fingers dragging up toward his mouth, teeth gnawing at nails already bitten raw. His mother despised that habit. How many times had she slapped him for it? How many crescent-shaped cuts had her own perfect nails left on his skin?

But Janet Drake was dead.

And Tim was alone. He had always been alone.

A sudden spark of thought froze him mid-step.

His room. Timothy Drake’s room. There had to be something there.

He darted to the door, cracking it open slowly, eyes sweeping the empty hallway. No movement. No breath but his own. With silent, measured steps, he slipped out and headed toward where his room should be. Every shift of air, every creak of wood made his heart lurch into his throat. He didn’t want to run into any of Waynes.

Finally, he reached the door. The one that should’ve belonged to him. He froze, heart hammering, as if invisible hands had nailed his feet to the worn floorboards.

“Our Tim is dead,” that sentence crashed through his skull like a gunshot.

Dead. He was dead to them.

How desperately he wished that were true.

How easy everything would be if Timothy Drake had stayed dead.

Tim sucked in a sharp breath, his fingers trembling as they closed around the doorknob. With a twist, the latch gave way effortlessly, and a shiver ran down his spine. Darkness engulfed him instantly, the heavy curtains smothering the sunlight, pressing the room into a suffocating shadow. The air was cool, almost stale, carrying the faint scent of dust and something faintly familiar. He eased the door shut behind him, each movement measured, every step a cautious negotiation with the shadows. His pulse drummed in his ears, loud enough to compete with the whispering silence of the room, as his eyes darted from desk to shelf, from scattered objects to the corners that seemed to watch him back.

It was his room.

A corkboard dominated one wall, crowded with photographs pinned haphazardly. His computer. His scuffed and worn skateboard leaned in the corner. The desk groaned under the weight of overflowing folders, papers spilling like waves, while books lined the shelves in orderly chaos, their spines etched with titles he knew by heart. His camera and lenses neatly stacked beside it.

Just like his, Tim thought numbly. Exactly like his.

Everything looked untouched—preserved—as if the owner had only stepped out for a moment.

With every heartbeat, his chest tightened, breath stuttering, palms slick with cold sweat. His gaze darted from object to object, and horror crept over him like ice under the skin.

Everything was exactly the same.

But that was impossible. It made no sense. This wasn’t his home. It couldn’t be his home. So why—

WHY?

Think, Tim. Think.

He lurched to his feet, adrenaline snapping through his veins as he tore open the wardrobe, ripping clothes from hangers, sending them raining to the floor. His eyes caught a faint outline at the very bottom shelf, a spark of wild hope igniting in his chest.

With trembling hands, he reached down, fingertips brushing the familiar groove hidden near the back wall. Pressing firmly, he forced the false bottom open in one practiced movement.

There wasn’t much space inside. Just enough for one thing. A book.

His copy of Sherlock Holmes, borrowed from Bruce’s library and never returned. Tim always thought Alfred was the only one who noticed.

Snatching it up, he flipped straight to the back of the book, where the hard cover was sealed beneath a carefully glued sheet of paper. He dug the knife from his pocket and sliced along the edge, peeling the layer away in frantic, uneven strips until—

His breath caught.

There it was. The secret compartment. The hollow carved out by his hands.

And nestled inside—

A flash drive.

This wasn’t coincidence. There were too many parallels. Too many impossibilities lining up perfectly. Tim’s mind swung through every theory he had, every one breaking apart the moment it formed. Because occurrences like this—things this precise—they don’t just happen.

Tim Drake had always been several steps ahead. He’d been trained that way since childhood.

“Do you remember what I told you, Timothy?” His mother’s voice was cold and indifferent. She never looked him in the eyes, as though Tim wasn’t worth the effort.

“Yes, mother,” his gaze swept across the ballroom, a sea of wealth and arrogance glinting in gold and silk. Some guests already reeked of intoxication, their laughter sloppy and false, while others leaned in to whisper poison-laced gossip that twisted Tim’s stomach. The bitter revulsion churned in him, but he recognized it as a tool, a weapon he could wield if he played this room carefully.

“Don’t call me that,” she snapped, her lip curling with visible contempt. Her manicured hand landed on his shoulder, fingers digging in like talons through his jacket. “Repeat it, Timothy.”

He swallowed.

“Observe. Memorize. Weaponize.”

Her lips twitched—something that might have been a smile on anyone else. But her eyes, predatory and empty, stayed cold.

“Good. Don’t disappoint me, Timothy.”

Tim always watched. Always thought five, ten steps ahead. Always prepared for the worst.

This flash drive… It had to mean something. Because if Tim Drake hid it—if he hid it here—then there was a reason.

Snatching the flash drive, Tim shoved it deep into his pocket before sliding the book back into its hidden place and closing the shelf. Better to keep this secret, at least for now. Bruce and Dick already hated him enough. And Jason…Tim wasn’t sure what to think about him anymore.

His gaze drifted toward the corkboard, and suddenly, his blood turned to ice. Slowly, he approached it, each step weighed with dread. His chest tightened painfully as his eyes landed on the photographs.

Tim had always loved capturing moments, especially the ones that mattered to him. There were so many photos.

Alfred smiling softly as he held a plate of his traditional cookies. Bruce hunched over a computer, a mug of coffee in hand, brows knitted in focus. Dick sprawled across the couch, cheek buried in a pillow, and drooling in his sleep. Jason lost in a book, tucked away in the library. Damian standing before a canvas, paintbrush poised, expression uncharacteristically calm.

These were the frames of their lives. But not a single picture of Tim. Not a single trace that he had ever existed among them.

Because Tim Drake was never part of this family.

Alfred never let him into the kitchen, chasing him away every time Tim tried to help. Bruce looked at him like a subordinate. They discussed Wayne Enterprises during the day and crime cases during the night. Dick never invited him to the movie nights, preferring to spend time with Damian. Jason hated his company, and every encounter with him left new bruises on Tim’s body. Damian…Tim preferred not to be near him at all.

But despite all of that, he still saw them as his heroes. Because they were Gotham’s heroes. People needed them. And Tim accepted that he would always be the unnecessary Robin. The Robin who was never Batman’s son. And never would be.

The photos in front of his eyes flickered like a horrible reminder of that, but Tim tried to suppress those feelings, because the photos in front of him were the very same ones he had taken himself. Exactly the same. He remembered every one of those moments. Every sneer when they noticed him with the camera in his hands.

Tim tried to take a deep breath, but he couldn’t. He couldn’t breathe. He couldn’t think. He was lost. He was alone. Tim clenched his teeth, and, curling his fingers into a fist, struck the board, leaving a dent in it. But it wasn’t enough. Another hit. This time stronger. A third. A fourth.

In Tim’s ears there was only white silence. He felt nothing. Even when his knuckles were torn and bloody, the pain was muted. Or Tim was beginning to lose himself.

You were always a ghost to them.

Grabbing the board with trembling fingers, Tim ripped it from the wall as if trying to tear out the memories themselves. With a raw, broken sound escaping his throat, he flung it across the room. It slammed into the opposite wall, the sharp crack echoing through the walls. Photographs scattered into the air and rained down around him.

He grabbed the nearest chair, flinging it with the same desperate force. Papers tore, frames shattered, books flew from shelves, and shards of glass cut into his palms. Every movement was a violent punctuation of the anguish clawing through his chest, a silent scream against the world that had never let him belong.

Breath trembling, he seized the black lamp and hurled it with all the rage he never dared show. Glass exploded outward, shards catching what little sunlight filtered in, glittering mockingly as they fell.

The room was torn inside out, just like him.

His knees finally gave out, hitting the cold floor with a dull thud. Tim didn’t notice. His knuckles were torn open, dripping red onto scattered photographs that stared back up at him.

His chest ached with the force of the breaths he couldn’t seem to pull in, his body shaking as though trying to hold together what was already shattered beyond repair.

He felt everything. And yet, somehow…

He felt nothing at all.

The door slammed open, and Dick appeared in the doorway. His piercing gaze swept across the wreckage of the room, destroyed by Tim’s own hands. His lips curled into a snarl, a storm of anger and hatred etched across his face. Normally, Tim would have tried to shrink away from that storm in his brother’s eyes, but now…he didn’t care. He simply watched, hollow-eyed, as the man he had once considered a brother advanced slowly toward him, gripping the collar of his shirt and yanking him to his feet with brutal force.

“What the hell are you doing here?!” Dick hissed, his voice more predator than human, and Tim felt the pressure of the grip tighten. Somewhere deep inside, a twisted part of him hoped Dick would just slit his throat and leave him to rot. “You don’t belong in this room!”

“Do I not?” Tim said quietly, staring into those furious blue eyes, seeing the fire burning there. Dick didn’t answer with words. He shoved Tim back to the corridor with sheer strength, ignoring the fresh cuts and bruises covering him.

“I don’t ever want to see you here again!” Dick roared, slamming the door behind them and pointing a finger toward the hall. “Get out!”

Tim lingered for a moment, trying to process what had just happened. It all felt like a movie scene, surreal and distant, except for the blood caked between his knuckles, and the weight of the flash drive pressing heavily in his pocket.

He turned and walked away from the enraged figure, feeling Dick’s gaze burn into the back of his skull, searing through him like fire.

He needed access to the computer. But he doubted they’d ever let him into the cave now. Especially not after this.

There was only one way left.

“He destroyed his room, Jay!” Dick shouted, pacing through the living room, arms flailing. Jason exhaled heavily, rubbing the bridge of his nose. He had expected something like this. Tim had been teetering on the edge of losing control. Jason was almost relieved that it had ended with nothing more than a wrecked room.

But Dick saw no reason for relief.

“You know how Alfred tried to keep it in order! You know how Damian loved spending time looking at the photos Tim took! You know how—” Dick’s words faltered as he collapsed onto the couch, burying his face in his hands. His eyes, hidden behind his arms, glistened with unshed tears.

“You know how often I fell asleep on his bed,” he whispered, voice cracking, “hoping that it was all just a nightmare… that I’d wake up in the morning with Baby Bird in my arms.”

Jason swallowed a bitter lump in his throat, trying to keep the mask of composure intact. But through the cracks, regret seeped out, and he bit his tongue, silently listening to his brother’s confession.

“He’s gone, Jason… It’s been so many years, and we’ve tried to move on. And then he shows up. Isn’t that cruel, Jay?” The word was sharp, cutting. It was unbearably cruel to see their baby brother they had buried five years ago, knowing their Tim would never come back. “How can he… How can we just accept him?”

Dick’s eyes fixed on Jason, and Jason’s heart broke at the sight of the deep despair etched across his brother’s face. “How can you just accept him like that, Jay?”

“It’s not that simple,” Jason exhaled, running a hand through his hair, trying to hide the tremor in his fingers from Dick’s piercing gaze. But it was clear he hadn’t succeeded. Jason sank into the chair beside Dick, leaning back against the frame. A fragile, small smile tugged at the corners of his lips, but his eyes stayed fixed on the ceiling, tracing cracks and shadows, seeking somewhere safe to rest his thoughts. “But…maybe fate has given us a chance at redemption. Or maybe that’s just what I want to believe.”

“He’s not our Tim,” Dick muttered again, quieter this time.

“I know,” Jason simply nodded.

“He looks so much like him…” Dick’s shoulders slumped in frustration, and Jason exhaled with the same heavy sadness.

“I know, Dick.”

A deep silence settled between them. From the kitchen came the faint scrape of utensils and the soft shuffle of footsteps—Alfred, no doubt, busy with his cooking. The manor smelled faintly of wax polish and cold stone, a chill that seemed to seep into their bones. None of them spent time together anymore. Bruce was perpetually at work or holed up in the cave, consumed by the next case. Jason couldn’t even remember the last time he had seen Bruce without the cowl, except for this morning.

Dick spent time in the manor, but Jason couldn’t recall the last time he had seen his brother smile. Gotham’s streets had long noticed the change in Nightwing, and criminals trembled at the sight of him. His brother often returned from patrols with dark stains marring his suit, each step leaving a trail of crimson footprints across the polished floors, drawing Alfred’s disapproving frown and the quiet clatter of broom and mop as he scurried to clean up the mess.

Damian had almost stopped speaking to them. At first, he simply skipped dinner. Then lunch. Soon, he vanished entirely from their sight. Even Alfred sometimes didn’t know where he was gone. For a time, he would patrol the streets alongside Batman, but eventually, the Robin disappeared, leaving Gotham on edge. Only the katana, often vanishing from the cave, reminded Jason that Damian still walked the streets at night.

But Robin was dead.

And Jason hoped this time it was for good.

“What have I done, Jay… what have I done,” Dick repeated, looking haunted. They both knew exactly what he meant.

Dick blamed himself for Tim’s death, for he was the one who created the symbol of Robin. And yet now, his second brother had died in the suit that was meant to carry hope.

“It’s not your fault, Dickwing. We’re all to blame,” Jason said, his eyes vacant, staring at the same cracked ceiling. “We were terrible brothers. And Tim… Tim always put everyone else first.”

“If only I had been more attentive,” Dick murmured, and this time Jason found himself without words. Because that thought had haunted him for years too.

“We can’t change the past, Dick,” Jason said, leaning forward slightly, his gaze locking onto his brother. Dick turned his head toward him, and Jason saw the tears silently streaking down his cheeks. He tried to soften his expression, offering a sliver of comfort. “Some mistakes can’t be undone. All we can do is make sure we don’t repeat them.” Jason’s hand rested on Dick’s shoulder, firm but gentle, a fleeting attempt to bridge the gap of grief that separated them, trying to offer at least a fragment of the comfort they both needed. But Jason would never admit it. “I know it’s hard…but this Tim, wherever he came from, he isn’t responsible for what happened to our baby brother. For what happened to our Tim.”

Jason could see the storm in those pale blue eyes, the way doubt flickered, then grief took hold, and finally, a wave of aching sorrow. Dick felt everything deeply, letting the feelings overwhelm him. Only after they had buried Tim did Jason truly understand the depth of his older brother’s capacity for hatred and despair.

Though, perhaps he shouldn’t have been surprised. After all, Nightwing was the one who killed the Joker.

“I…” Dick began, his voice barely audible, but he was cut off before he managed to formulate his thought.

“Timothy is gone.”

They both turned toward the hall, where Damian stood, arms crossed over his chest, watching them with an unsettling calm. His face was a mask—empty, unreadable, and completely devoid of emotion.

The green eyes seemed to have dimmed, or maybe it was just the muted light. He stood perfectly still, shoulders squared, dark strands of hair slicked back with precision. The green tunic hugged his broad shoulders and defined muscles while covering his neck, giving him the appearance of a warrior more than a boy.

When did he stop being a child? Jason’s mind raced with indignation, noting that Damian was nearly as tall as he was now. But there was no time to dwell on that thought—the words Damian spoke struck his mind with the weight of steel.

“What do you mean, he’s gone?” Jason frowned, releasing Dick’s shoulder and rising from his seat, intending to check every room. Panic was slowly creeping in.

“Left…through the door,” Damian said in a hollow and flat voice. His green eyes bored into Jason’s with a predator’s intensity, freezing him in place. Slowly, they shifted to Dick, darkening like storm clouds, yet Damian said nothing. Jason could swear he glimpsed pure contempt flickering in the depths of those unyielding, almost demonic eyes, a silent threat that promised consequences without a single movement.

Damian began to turn away, as if the conversation were over, but Jason called after him.

“Hey…Demon Spawn, where are you going?”

“To find Timothy,” he said coldly, then resumed his stride, leaving the two brothers alone in the living room. Jason exhaled sharply, a mix of relief and worry tightening his chest.

“At least he warned us,” he muttered. It was a small victory.

Dick rose beside him, his gaze scanning the corridor where Damian had vanished. His brows were furrowed, eyes glassy with tears, but beneath it lingered the same readiness that Nightwing always carried into Gotham’s streets.

“He couldn’t just leave. The sensors should have gone off.”

“Don’t forget, we’re talking about Tim,” Jason replied with a shrug as he moved toward the cave’s way. “If he’s even a fraction like ours, he could have bypassed all of Bruce’s protocols and vanished without a trace. That’s what he does best.”

No one could stop Tim Drake if he wanted to disappear, and the thought gnawed at Jason like fire. He couldn’t lose Robin again.

“Hurry, Dickwing. Time isn’t on our side.”

He slipped into Gotham Academy without a hitch, pulling the hood of Jason’s stolen cardigan low over his face. The air buzzed with chatter, laughter, and the squeak of sneakers on polished floors. Lockers slammed, books thudded to the ground, and the faint tang of cafeteria food lingered in the hallways. It was passing period, and the corridors were crowded with students, perfect cover for someone who wanted to disappear into the crowd. Tim’s footsteps were careful, muted against the tile, his pulse quickening as he made his way to the computer lab, eyes flicking to every movement, though he knew it wouldn’t be long before anyone noticed he was gone.

They didn’t trust him, and he couldn’t blame them. But trusting them blindly? That wasn’t an option either. If they refused to help, he’d do it himself. He always did.

Sliding into a seat at the far end of the lab by a window, he quickly scanned the nearly empty room, then set to work. He didn’t risk using his old login credentials; Bruce could track him in an instant. Maybe it was just paranoia, but Tim wasn’t about to take the chance.

He hacked the system cleanly, opening a new server and embedding a code that ensured his actions couldn’t be traced. From his pocket, he pulled out the flash drive, plugged it in, and tugged on the headphones.

He glanced around once more, confirming he was alone, and opened the folder on the drive. Inside, there was only one file: a single video named 'D.R.R.'.

Taking a deep breath that scraped harshly down his throat, Tim clicked ‘play’. He pressed the headphones so tightly to his ears it almost hurt, as if afraid of missing a single sound. But when his own face appeared on the screen, his fingers went slack, slipping from the headphones as they fell against his neck, his hands landing heavily on his knees. He couldn’t look away, because it was him.

Timothy Drake.

The same dark hair, the same tired eyes, the same faint scar near his brow. The boy on the screen—Timothy Drake—sat in front of the camera with a small, unsettling smile, as if mocking him.

“The camera is rolling,” Tim on the screen said, and then his expression shifted. “Hello, I am Timothy Drake. And if you’re watching this video…” His smile twisted into something jagged, and Tim heard the tremor in his double’s voice. “…that means I am dead.”
