He was lying on something hard and cold. Something slick beneath him, leeching warmth from his back. Wind scraped along his neck and cheeks, biting and persistent.

Drip. Drip. Drip.

Raindrops splattered against his face, icy pinpricks against his skin, and Tim jerked his head weakly to the side, trying to shake the sensation away, trying to sink back into the dark where nothing hurt and nothing demanded anything of him. But firm hands caught him, pressing down on his shoulders, shaking him when he tried to curl inward.

“Ti—”

A familiar voice fought its way through the white noise ringing in his ears, distant and warped, but Tim didn’t have the strength to open his eyes. The world began to slip, his awareness unraveling as he started to fall back into his own mind, losing his grip on the sensations tethering him to reality.

“Timothy!”

Sharp nails dug into his chin, forcing his head up, turning his face back into the rain. The drops struck his skin again, cold and relentless. He tried to lift a hand, to swat the voice away, but even that was too much—his body refused to listen.

All he could do was whisper, barely shaping the word with his lips.

“Go away.”

For a brief moment, he thought they had finally left him alone.

The fingers withdrew from his face, and Tim was left with nothing but the cold—sharp, isolating, seeping straight into his bones. His whole body shuddered, teeth chattering faintly. His thoughts raced too fast to grasp, spiraling over one another, slippery and fragmented. He was disoriented. Exposed.

Then the same warm hands slid beneath his back and knees. The ground vanished.

Tim was lifted into the air, abruptly stripped of any solid surface, and his eyes flew open on instinct. Panic flared in his chest as he stared up at a familiar face.

Furrowed brows. Tawny skin streaked with rain. Sharp green eyes—predatory by nature, yet now clouded with unmistakable worry—locked onto him.

“Damian?” Tim breathed, blinking hard, as if afraid the image might dissolve.

Rain continued to patter against his face, tapping softly against his lashes and lips. His clothes were soaked through, heavy and clinging, fabric plastered uncomfortably to his skin. His hair hung damp and cold against his temples.

Damian started walking, carrying Tim as though he weighed nothing at all. At any other time, the indignity of it would have made Tim bristle, maybe even lash out, but now he was too drained, too empty to protest.

“Where…are we going?” he asked weakly. His eyes slid shut again as he leaned his forehead against Damian’s warm chest. Despite Damian’s steady stride, Tim could feel the gentle sway of each step, the rhythmic motion grounding him, gifting him comfort.

“Home, Akhi,” Damian replied. His voice was close, too close, almost intimate. Tim frowned, cracking his eyes open just enough to look up at him.

“Home?” he echoed slowly, testing the word as if it didn’t quite belong to him. As if it were foreign on his tongue. “I don’t have a home.”

The words slipped out before he could stop them, because they were true. At least, they felt true.

Damian clearly disagreed. His arms tightened around Tim, pulling him closer, holding him as though he were afraid Tim might simply fade away or be taken again.

“You have a home, Timothy,” Damian said clearly. He paused—just a fraction too long—as if deliberately giving Tim time to process the words. “You will always have a home with me.”

Tim let out a weak, humorless huff that barely qualified as a laugh.

“You can’t give me a home where everyone hates me,” he murmured. His voice sounded distant to his own ears, like it was coming from somewhere behind him. “That’s not a home, Dami.”

“I already told you, Akhi,” Damian said confidently.

He stopped beside the car. The click of the key fob cut sharply through the air, followed by the solid thunk of the door unlocking. Rain drummed steadily against the metal roof, louder here, enclosed, echoing like static in Tim’s skull. Damian opened the door smoothly and guided Tim down into the seat. The motion made the world tilt.

Damian didn’t move away.

He stood there, one hand gripping the top of the doorframe, the other braced against the car, leaning in just enough that Tim had to look up at him. Damian’s gaze was sharp, unyielding—anchored in a way Tim very much was not.

“One word,” Damian continued calmly, dangerously calm, “and I’ll deal with anyone whose name you say.”

Tim searched his face automatically, out of habit—looking for cracks, exaggeration, a tell.

There was none.

That realization unsettled him more than the threat itself. Because it meant Damian meant it. Completely.

Tim turned his head away, the movement slow and heavy, like he was pushing through water. He slumped back against the seat, letting it catch him. The leather was warm, and only then did he realize how deeply the cold had sunk into him. His fingers tingled faintly as sensation crept back in, pins and needles blooming under his skin.

His thoughts felt far away. Blunted. Like they were happening behind thick glass.

Damian was still standing there, waiting for his reply.

Tim didn’t have the energy to argue. Didn’t have the strength to explain the hollow ache in his chest, or the way the word ‘home’ felt like something he’d once owned and misplaced.

So he chose the easiest thing. The quietest thing.

“Let’s go home,” Tim said. The words felt strange in his mouth.

With warmth, his thoughts began to knit themselves back together, and with grim clarity, he realized the magnitude of his mistake.

He had agreed to the deal.

He had become a traitor to himself.

And time was not on his side.

Damian got into the driver’s seat without a word. The door shut with a dull, final thud, sealing them inside. The rain softened instantly, reduced to a muted drumming against the roof and windows. The engine rumbled to life, vibrating faintly through the frame of the car and through Tim’s bones.

Tim leaned his head back against the seat, staring at nothing. He drew in a deep breath, then let it out slowly, his chest rising and falling as if he needed to remind himself how breathing worked.

Why did you agree to the deal?

You both died…

Your mind is your greatest weapon.

The voices overlapped, out of sync, echoing against the inside of his skull like fragments of a broken recording.

A laugh slipped out of him. It startled even himself. It bubbled up from somewhere low and ugly in his chest, hard enough to hurt. The sound filled the car, bouncing off the glass. Tim laughed until his throat burned, until his stomach twisted unpleasantly, nausea creeping up his spine, but he couldn’t stop. It felt mechanical, wrong, like a reflex gone haywire.

“Timothy?”

Damian’s voice cut through it. In the rearview mirror, Tim caught the worried flash of green eyes, fixed on him instead of the road.

Tim forced the laughter down, swallowing it with effort. His lips stretched into a crooked, unpleasant grin that didn’t reach his eyes. His face felt numb, like it didn’t quite belong to him anymore.

“Oh, don’t look at me like that,” he said lightly, putting on a brightness that rang false even to his own ears. “I’m fine.”

The rain kept tapping against the windows. And somewhere deep inside, Tim knew he was lying.

“I don’t even know who I am anymore, Dami,” Tim said quietly, dragging a hand down his face. The smile vanished in an instant as if it had never been there at all. Because Timothy Drake was supposed to keep a clear head. Supposed to stay rational.

Memories began to strike his mind in sharp, fragmented flashes, each one sparking pain behind his eyes. The more he remembered, the worse it became—like pulling at a thread that unraveled the world around him. Reality grew thin, distant, as if he were slipping just a little out of phase with himself.

“Then trust me, Timothy,” Damian’s voice cut through the noise. It made Tim slowly lift his head, his gaze catching on those green eyes in the rearview mirror, watching him with an intensity that didn’t waver.

And as absurd as it sounded, right now Tim trusted Damian more than he trusted himself.

“You’re alive,” Damian continued, each word pronouncing slowly as to make sure Tim heard him. “And I won’t let anyone take you from me again.”

Except for a petty little witch, Tim thought grimly, but he didn’t say it out loud. Because neither of them could have stopped Klarion from taking him in the first place. That sour truth sat heavy in his chest.

Still, another question surfaced on Tim’s mind.

“Were you waiting for me at the grave?” he frowned, the image flashing back into his mind. The cold earth. The muddy ground. The empty coffin. The place Klarion had returned him to.

Damian pulled onto the street, pressing harder on the gas. The engine growled, the city lights streaking past as if the very hounds of Hell were on their heels.

“No,” Damian replied calmly. “I was going to find the witch and kill him.”

Tim felt his chest tighten, breath catching halfway in.

“But I didn’t have a lead,” Damian added, eyes forward, jaw set. “I didn’t know where to look.”

The rain hissed against the windshield. And Tim sat there, suspended between warmth and dread, knowing with painful clarity that Klarion had already been several moves ahead of all of them.

Tim frowned, leaning his forehead against the cold car window, the chill seeping into his skin as he tried to dull the pounding in his head. The glass vibrated faintly with the hum of the engine, grounding and grating all at once.

“So you went back?” he asked quietly.

“Yes,” Damian paused, his voice lowering, losing its sharp edge. “I did what the best Robin taught me,” there was something almost reverent in the way he said it. “I returned to the crime scene. When there’s no evidence, the place itself becomes the evidence.”

Tim’s eyes flew open. He stared at the back of the driver’s seat as if he could see through it—see then, see himself. “You taught me that, Timothy.”

“It’s a pointless waste of time!” Damian had shouted, arms crossed tightly over his chest, his glare sharp and venomous as it burned into Tim. Tim was already pulling on his mask, preparing to leave the Cave. “Richard and I checked that place. There’s nothing there.”

Damian’s hand had gone to the hilt of his katana as he stepped forward, teeth bared like an enraged predator.

“Or do you think we’re incompetent to follow the protocol?” His voice had risen, cracking with fury. “I’ll show you who’s incompetent here! I am the true Robin—you’re nothing but a parody!”

Tim hadn’t even raised an eyebrow. He’d grabbed the motorcycle keys, the metal clinking softly in his palm, and answered in that flat, maddeningly calm tone as he tossed the words over his shoulder.

“Criminals like returning to the scene of the crime,” he said. “It gives them a sense of invulnerability and control.”

He swung onto the bike, slotted the key into the ignition, grabbed the helmet and revved the engine. The growl echoed through the Cave, vibrating through bone and stone alike. Tim spared Damian a tired glance through the visor.

“If there’s no evidence,” he finished evenly, “you go back. Sometimes the place itself is the evidence.”

Bruce was moving quickly toward the door, forcing himself to stay calm, but the anxiety clawed at him all the same—relentless and overwhelming. Anxiety for his sons.

The moment Tim had vanished, Bruce had felt it as a second loss. As if history had looped back on itself and he had once again made the same mistake, walked straight into Ra’s snare. He no longer knew what to believe. His mind rejected hope on instinct, because Bruce knew—knew—that if that hope shattered again, he would not survive it.

And it was that very denial that had led him here now, into absolute helplessness.

He heard Alfred’s soft footsteps behind him, measured and quiet as ever, but Bruce didn’t turn to check whether Alfred was carrying a firearm. He couldn’t spare the attention. Every thought he had pulled in the same direction.

His son. Tim.

He was his son. And he was alive.

Bruce had to see it. He had to be sure, had to confirm that this wasn’t an illusion, not another cruel trick.

He didn’t even reach the door before it opened.

Damian stepped inside first, lifting his gaze to Bruce, like a predator marking its territory. His eyes burned an icy warning, impossible to ignore. Every line of his body was tense, coiled with readiness, and the faint drip of rain from his soaked cape traced cold rivulets down his shoulders. Behind him, Tim slipped in like a shadow, stopping just inside the threshold. He remained there, head lowered, not looking up.

His clothes were soaked through and smeared with grime. Damp strands of hair clung to his forehead, casting his face in shadow. Water dripped quietly onto the floor beneath him.

Bruce’s breath caught. He didn’t dare move.

“Oh my God,” Alfred breathed behind him, stepping around Bruce, who stood frozen, unable to tear his eyes away from Tim. He looked exactly like the son Bruce had lost. The son who might—might—be standing in front of him now. “You could use a hot shower, Master Tim,” Alfred continued gently, though worry creased his brow and deepened the lines in his face. “Come along. I’ll prepare dry clothes and a towel for you.”

Tim followed Alfred without a word. Alfred’s posture was composed as ever, but the tension showed—in the tight set of his shoulders, in the way his hands curled slightly as he walked. Bruce remained where he was, staring at the empty space Tim had just occupied, as though rooted there by something unseen, something spellbound.

“Father,” Damian’s voice cut through the stillness.

Bruce turned toward him, and in that instant, he felt like prey under a predator’s gaze, measured and merciless, as if Damian were deciding how best to strike.

“If you screw this up again,” Damian said coldly, “I will take Timothy, and you will never see either of us again. I promise you that.”

Satisfied that his words had landed, Damian turned and headed toward the kitchen, leaving Bruce alone in the hall.

Moments later, the door burst open with a sharp crack, and Jason was the first to stride in, his eyes sweeping the room in a quick, searching scan.

“Tim?”

“In the shower,” Bruce exhaled, rubbing at the bridge of his nose, his fingers pressing hard as if trying to keep himself together.

Jason nodded once and moved off briskly in Alfred’s direction. Duke followed with a quiet nod of his own, turning toward the kitchen. Dick, however, closed the door slowly behind him and stopped, standing there as if nailed to the floor.

Bruce understood him.

Because he felt exactly the same.

For a while, they simply stood there, not even looking at each other. Dick’s face was a storm of emotions—too many, shifting too fast—and it unsettled Bruce. It was a sign he knew all too well: his eldest son was no longer holding himself together.

The most dangerous beasts were mothers who would tear the world apart to protect their cub, even at the cost of their own lives.

“We really screwed this up, didn’t we?” Dick broke the silence at last.

Bruce clenched his jaw, feeling the tension lock into his muscles. Dick let out a short, humorless laugh, running a hand through his hair and tipping his head back to stare at the ceiling.

“It doesn’t matter if he’s from another universe or not. Doesn’t matter if he’s our Tim or not…” His voice faltered for half a beat. “We should’ve—no. I should’ve listened to him. I should’ve believed him. At least once,” he swallowed, his gaze dropping again. “But I kept pushing him away, telling myself he was here because of Ra’s plan. That he was just another weapon meant to finish us off.”

Bruce caught a dangerous flash in those blue eyes, a spark like lightning that sent a cold, electric shiver down his spine.

“But tell me,” Dick continued quietly, “was there even anything left in us that could be destroyed?”

“Maybe not in us,” Bruce answered darkly, turning toward the hallway where Tim had disappeared with Alfred. His voice felt heavy in his chest. “But maybe all this time…he was trying to break him,” Bruce’s gaze hardened. “And we were just the tools he used to do it.”

Hot water covered his body, but the cold had already sunk deep into his bones. The tremor started somewhere inside his chest, crawling outward like icy fingers, curling around his ribs, squeezing.

Or maybe it’s just your thoughts.

Tim clenched his teeth, shut off the water, and grabbed a towel. He didn’t have time for indulgences like this.

He knew what he had to do now.

He dressed quickly, movements automatic, then tossed the towel over the edge of the tub and stepped out into the hallway. The air felt cooler against his damp skin, raising goosebumps along his arms.

His gaze immediately caught on Jason, sitting on the floor with his back against the opposite wall, a book resting loosely in his hands. When Jason looked up, he snapped it shut and grinned, all teeth and sharp edges.

“So,” he drawled, “feeling warmer, Baby Bird?”

Tim frowned but didn’t answer. He turned and headed toward the kitchen, where voices overlapped with the dull clatter of dishes. Jason pushed himself to his feet and fell into step beside him, relentless as ever.

“The witch boy got lucky,” Jason continued casually. “Alfred already had his gun out. And Dick and I…” His tone shifted, darker. “We were gearing up to go after Ra’s head.”

Tim stopped so abruptly that Jason had to halt too.

“What are you trying to get at, Jason?” Tim asked coldly, not bothering to soften his voice.

They never believed you.

They never trusted you.

They never loved you.

The words landed like ice water, seeping into every crack Tim had been trying to seal shut.

Jason looked genuinely shaken by his words, but he recovered quickly. The half-smirk slipped from his face, leaving only stark seriousness behind. And in that moment, as Tim took in the familiar scars etched into Jason’s skin, the faint lines carved into his brow, he realized just how many years had been stolen from him.

Because Ra’s had taken everything he had.

“Except your mind. Your mind—your greatest weapon, Robin.”

“Back then… I couldn’t ask for forgiveness,” Jason started suddenly, and Tim’s eyes flew open as he took a step back, casting Jason a wary, disbelieving look. He wasn’t ready for this conversation, and there was nowhere to run. Jason, either not noticing or choosing not to stop, went on. “Even now, I still haven’t apologized, have I? Damian was right,” Jason continued, his voice husky. “All this time, I’ve been trying to buy my way out of my mistakes. I didn’t realize you never needed that. I was doing it for myself—to quiet the hole in my chest, at least a little.”

Jason’s eyes shone like open sky—clear, unguarded, painfully sincere. Tim couldn’t look away. Even if he wanted to. Even now, other people’s feelings mattered to him more than his own.

Pathetic.

“I can’t change the past, Tim,” Jason said, faltering as he drew in a deep breath, growing more uncertain by the second. “But I want to…” He stopped, swallowed hard. “I want to be here. For as long as you’ll let me. Not to earn your forgiveness. Not to ease my conscience. But because you’re my brother.”

His lips trembled into a small smile, and for a split second, a memory flashed through Tim’s mind—one of the old Robin photos he’d taken. A younger Jason, smiling the same way. It had become one of Tim’s favorites, kept hidden from everyone else.

“You’re my little brother, Baby Bird,” Jason said softly. “Will you let me be your big brother?”

There was emptiness in Tim’s head. He couldn’t believe what he was hearing. Everything felt unreal, as if he were still trapped in those hallucinations left behind by Klarion’s magic.

“You were my hero, Jason,” he said slowly. He noticed the light in Jason’s blue eyes dim, a faint green tint spreading across his irises. But Tim didn’t look away. “When I became Robin, the only thing I thought about was whether you would be proud of me. I don’t believe in God, but I imagined you watching me from above, smiling at every one of my victories. I never tried to be you, because I never thought I was a hero. You were real, Jason. You burned with purpose. You carried hope. Even Batman became softer when you were around.”

With every word, more pain showed on Jason’s face. They couldn’t fix what had happened. No one could.

“I remember…” Tim whispered, his chest tightening as his lips trembled. “…when Ra’s was torturing me. When I still believed someone would come for me, I called for Bruce. I begged him to save me. I begged, believing Batman would come.”

Jason closed his eyes, trying to hide the pain from Tim, but Tim saw it clearly.

“And in that moment, I thought only of one thing. How terrifying it is to die alone, looking death in the eyes, knowing no one ever came for you,” Tim’s gaze softened. “ I know your pain. I was never angry at you, Jason. I always dreamed of being worthy of calling myself your brother.”

“You were always worthy, Baby Bird,” Jason said through the tight knot in his throat, stepping closer and looking down at Tim, meeting his eyes head-on. “I’m the one who has to earn the right to be worthy now.”

A sharp stab of guilt pierced Tim at the thought of what he had agreed to in exchange for the memories he’d regained, but he pushed it aside. Because his Robin thought he was worthy.

“I don’t expect forgiveness, Tim. But I’m asking you for time—to prove to you that you’re my brother.”

Time Tim didn’t have. But he couldn’t say that.

Instead, he let a faint smile tug at the corner of his lips, turned sharply on his heel, and started toward the kitchen.

“You get one chance, Jason,” Tim said without looking back. “Because this is my last life.”

He hoped that would be the end of it. But Jason was stubborn, always had been, and he immediately latched onto the words.

“Last life?” Jason echoed. “What does that mean?”

Tim didn’t answer. He stepped into the kitchen, and everything came to a halt.

Alfred stood frozen with a kettle in hand. Damian held a single mug, mid-motion. Duke had been chopping vegetables but now stood still, knife suspended in the air. Dick and Bruce were seated at the table, clearly in the middle of a conversation before Tim entered.

Tim might have remained there, silently taking them all in, if not for Jason clearing his throat and giving him a light push between the shoulder blades, nudging him further inside.

“Before anyone else asks anything,” Jason said, stepping past Tim, crossing his arms over his chest and raising an eyebrow as he turned back to him, “I want an answer,” he tilted his head slightly. “So what did you mean, kid?”

Tim kept watching in silence.

Should he tell them the truth? Did the truth even matter anymore?

He licked his lips, fingers brushing the flash drive in his pocket, grounding himself in the familiar shape.

“I died twice,” Tim said at last, his voice returning to its cold, measured cadence. He could keep his emotions locked down. Feelings later. Right now, staying rational was what mattered.

Unlike him, terror froze the faces around him.

“Even if it was only the bodies that died,” he continued evenly, “the soul remembers the pain.” A pause. Flat. Controlled. “I’d rather not die a third time.”

He swallowed against the tightness in his throat, hearing the lie in his own words. They just needed to believe it. He just needed to stop standing in their way.

“What do you mean, only the bodies died?” Jason looked on the verge of breaking. His hands curled into fists at his sides, trembling—whether from rage or panic, Tim couldn’t tell. “Kid… you—are you really…?”

Tim lifted his chin, answering with deliberate indifference.

“I’m your Tim. I always have been.”

“But how…” Bruce whispered, rising unsteadily from his chair. Horror and grief warred in his eyes as he stared at Tim. “You were dead. I buried you.”

“You did exactly what Ra’s wanted you to do,” Tim said, giving a small shrug, his expression empty.

His body felt light, like it no longer belonged to him. As if he were already halfway gone. The room looked wrong, edges soft and distant, sounds dulled, like he was hearing everything through water. He kept talking anyway, words spilling out on autopilot, barely registering as his own.

“He replaced my dead body with another one. A body that was still breathing in another world—” Tim paused, eyes unfocused, drifting past Bruce’s shoulder, “—but whose soul had already left it.”

“Klarion…” Duke breathed, barely audible.

The name rippled through the room. Realization hit all of them at once. Dick’s eyes darkened, a storm gathering behind them, but Tim didn’t flinch. He stayed upright, detached, looking at Dick like he was watching a stranger react to someone else’s tragedy.

“Ra’s wants me,” Tim continued in a flat voice. “My mind. My intellect.” A beat. “But in order to control me, he needed to break me first.”

He didn’t blink as his gaze slid through Bruce, landing somewhere behind him—on Alfred, still frozen in place, a teapot clutched in his hand like he’d forgotten how to move.

“And he succeeded.”

Regret. Anger. Horror. Guilt. Every emotion flickered across the faces around him, but none of it reached Tim. It all felt distant, irrelevant—like it belonged to another version of himself.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out the flash drive, rolling it between his fingers. Then he locked eyes with Bruce.

Bruce had gone pale—ashen, like the blood had drained straight out of him. His chest hitched, breath shallow, uneven. His hands trembled at his sides, fingers flexing as if he were trying to hold onto something invisible. His mouth opened, but no sound came out.

Tim’s lips tugged into a crooked, hollow smile.

He flicked the flash drive toward Bruce, who barely reacted in time—caught it on instinct alone. He stared down at it in his palm, then back at Tim, eyes wide as if he didn’t believe what had just happened.

“You wanted so badly to know whether I was a threat to your family, didn’t you, Mister Wayne?”

Bruce flinched. He took a sharp step back and stumbled into the chair behind him; it screeched loudly across the floor as it slid away. The sound echoed, jarring and ugly, but Tim didn’t react. His gaze remained blank, stripped of any emotions.

“Enjoy the viewing, Mister Wayne.”

Tim turned his back on them and walked toward the Cave.

His steps were steady, mechanical. He didn’t look back; not at Bruce frozen in place, not at the others standing in stunned silence. The air felt heavier with every step he took, thick with things left unsaid.

He remembered exactly what was on that flash drive. Every frame. Every words. Every second.

And he knew, without doubt, that this was his only chance if he was going to follow the plan through to the end.

Even if it meant The Ballad of Dead Robin had to reach the end.

“The camera is recording,” Tim’s face filled the screen, but it wasn’t the Tim they knew. He was older. His hair hung longer around his face, matted with sweat and grime, strands sticking to blood-smeared skin. Fresh bruises bloomed dark along his cheekbones and jaw, cuts split his lip and brow. He was breathing hard, each inhale sharp and uneven, the sound crackling through the speakers as if he were running.

The image shook slightly with every step. Around him was nothing but darkness, swallowing the edges of the frame. No walls, no landmarks. Just shadow and the faint scrape of movement, like boots brushing against stone.

“If you’re watching this,” he continued, “then I’m still alive.” He swallowed, the camera dipping for a fraction of a second before leveling again. “Unfortunately…these might be my last words.”
