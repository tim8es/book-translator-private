“No one is going to save you, Detective,” Ra’s face flickered before his eyes, his voice slick and falsely gentle. “No one needs a dead soldier.”

Ra’s face vanished, and Tim stood exposed before Bruce, who kept looking at him with that same damned regret—regret that made Tim feel small and insignificant, and yet he could do nothing. He couldn’t even tear himself out of the arms still holding him captive, keeping him from reaching the knife Tim desperately wanted to drive straight into Bruce’s eyes.

“An eye for an eye, Detective.”

The mist-soft whisper drowned out everything in his ears. Tim tried to jerk free, desperate for even a shred of space, but was only crushed harder against the solid chest behind him. A ragged breath brushed against his ear.

“Easy, Baby Bird,” Jason’s voice was soft. Not like Ra’s—truly soft. As if he were afraid Tim might bolt if he talked too loud. His warm breath tickled the exposed line of Tim’s throat. “Calm down…”

“Calm down?” Tim couldn’t help the dark laugh that slid out of him, still staring at Bruce through the haze. In that fog, it felt like a pair of swamp-green eyes watched him with predatory patience. “Calm down?!” he barked louder, but for all the fury in his voice, he no longer fought the hold. Those warm hands gave off comfort so alien it terrified him—yet his willpower was bleeding out. Just a moment, something whispered in his skull. Just a moment he’d allow himself the illusion of safety. “You had no right, Bruce. No right…”

At the end, his voice wavered, and Tim pressed his lips tight, swallowing the burn of tears. He would not cry in front of them. And especially not in front of Bruce Wayne, who simply stood there, watching him in silence.

“…Is it really that hard to believe me?” Tim’s voice cracked again, thinner, rawer, words scratching his throat. “Just once, Bruce? Have I not proved I’m worth trusting? Was it still not enough?! Was I still not enough?!” He didn’t understand who he was seeing anymore. He understood that this wasn’t his Bruce. He understood they might have had no reason to trust him. But it didn’t smother the betrayal gnawing through him, chewing down to bone.

“Looks like in every universe, Tim Drake isn’t worth trusting after all.”

The words tore out of him with a sick, brittle laugh—sharp and venomous, like the beginning of a hysterical fit he didn’t have the strength to finish. A heartbeat later Jason let him go, and for a second Tim felt the phantom taste of freedom fizz in his chest only for those same hands to catch him again, looping around his shoulders and hauling him around, pressing him face-first against solid chest.

His nose hit Jason’s sweatshirt, breath snagging on the faint scent of his shampoo—spice and clean rain, something grounding, something horribly familiar. A broad palm settled on his back, moving in slow, deliberate circles. Despite the storm clawing up his ribs, Tim’s body betrayed him, muscle tension bleeding away inch by inch as exhaustion dragged its claws through him. He leaned forward, sinking into the warmth, hating how easy it was, how much he needed it.

“That’s it. Breathe, Baby Bird,” Jason murmured in a soft and soothing voice. The kitchen fell into a tomb-still silence—no one moved, no one even dared to breathe. Tim shut his eyes, forcing himself to pretend this was a dream—just another nightmare he could wake from. And for a moment, just one thin heartbeat of a moment, he almost did forget where he was and who crowded around him.

But reality has sharp teeth, and it always comes back biting.

“What is that, Bruce?” Dick’s voice sliced the quiet clean in half. Every head snapped toward Bruce. Tim turned too, just barely, cheek still resting against Jason’s chest. Bruce looked guilty. Pale and cornered, like a man who hadn’t decided whether to speak or run. Tim hoped—begged—that he would choose running. Just turn around and leave like he always did when Tim needed him the most.

But this time Bruce stayed. This time he didn’t hide his shame, didn’t dodge the weight of their stares. And that, somehow, made everything so much worse.

“A message from Tim,” Bruce finally said, voice low and strained. “Our Tim.”

Jason’s breath hitched, a jagged inhale that shuddered against Tim’s cheek. One hand froze mid-motion on his back; the other clamped down on his shoulder hard enough to make pain spark white-hot beneath Tim’s skin. A small, wounded sound slipped out of him before he could choke it down. Jason heard it anyway and his grip eased, fingers loosening but not letting go.

“And where did you get it?” Dick asked, tone level but cold and too controlled, as if he were holding his own rage by the throat. Tim glanced up. Jason’s jaw was clenched, a pulse ticking beneath stubbled skin, and his eyes glowed that sickly green Tim only ever saw in nightmares.

“It was with Tim,” Bruce answered honestly. Tim felt every gaze shift toward him, a prickle along his spine that made his stomach twist. Duke caught the reaction immediately.

“So let me get this straight,” Duke said, stepping toward Bruce, body angled protectively away from Tim. “You just decided to take something that belonged to him—without asking—and then you went through it. Without asking?” The silence that followed was answer enough. Duke exhaled sharply through his nose. “Bruce…you had no right. He hasn’t done anything—nothing—to make you think he’s a spy, or a villain, or—”

“I know,” Bruce said quietly, abruptly. Tim’s head snapped up, eyes narrowing with suspicion. “I know that now,” and just like that, whatever fragile, pathetic hope still clung to his ribs shattered on impact.

Bruce looked straight at him, stepped forward as if drawn toward him, and then halted, breath breaking on his lips as guilt twisted across his face.

“I’m sorry, Tim,” and then, softer, barely more than breath, almost ashamed to speak. “Forgive me.”

He shoved Jason away, catching him off balance with the sheer shock of it, and twisted to face Bruce, fists clenched so tight his nails dug into his palms. His teeth flashed in something between a snarl and a smile. His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird; any harder and it would tear its way out of his chest.

“Go to hell, Mister Wayne.”

Tim’s pace quickened, footsteps stuttering against tile as his breathing began to hitch, shallow and too fast. Anger and humiliation surged up his spine like fire; betrayal coiled tight in his throat, choking off air. The hall felt small—claustrophobic—walls closing in as phantom laughter flickered through his mind. Raspy, mocking, familiar.

Faces formed in the haze. Faces he knew.

“Timothy,” a voice called behind him, and then fingers hooked around his elbow. Tim yanked, vision swimming into black patches. Panic pressed against his lungs, clawing for escape. He spun, fist already coming up, ready to strike, ready to hurt—

He stopped an inch from Damian’s face.

Damian didn’t flinch. Didn’t even blink. He only stared down at him, expression blank and unreadable, as if daring Tim to keep going.

Tim froze, his fist still hanging in the air, knuckles aching with the force he’d almost unleashed. He stared at Damian in disbelief. Damian’s expression softened—only slightly—but he didn’t step back. Didn’t raise a hand to block or evade. He simply held Tim’s gaze, eyes holding danger, yet heavy with something painfully close to grief.

That look made guilt bloom in Tim’s chest, making him sick.

Slowly, he lowered his fist, muscles trembling as he tried to retreat, to put distance between them.

“I’m sorry,” he whispered.

Damian didn’t let him go. His fingers tightened around Tim’s elbow, grounding and unyielding, anchoring him in place. Tim didn’t know what else to say. There were no words left that hadn’t already broken him.

He turned his head away, hiding the tears gathering in his eyes. His lips quivered despite his effort to still them. A faint tremor ran through his body, as if his mind and body were pulling in opposite directions, like something inside him was coming apart at the seams.

“It’s alright, Timothy,” Damian said calmly despite the tension coiled through his frame. His grip didn’t loosen, but it softened, grounding rather than restraining

This couldn’t be real. It simply couldn’t.

Tim dragged in a deep breath, the air scraping his lungs raw as he swallowed the lump in his throat. He forced his face into blankness, locking every emotion behind glass.

But who was he fooling?

“Take me to the grave,” he said flatly.

The voice didn’t sound like his own. It felt distant, hollow, echoing from somewhere far away. The floor seemed to tilt beneath his feet, reality slipping, sliding, threatening to give out entirely. But he had to know. Had to be sure.

“To the grave?” Damian echoed, lifting his chin. His brows drew together, his gaze sharpening with wary thought.

Tim pressed his boots harder into the ground, as if anchoring himself to the world. The walls around him felt like they were slowly spinning, closing in, trapping him inside a narrowing spiral.

But he needed to see the truth.

“My grave.”

Dick, though—Dick was shattered.

He shouldn’t have.

And maybe Dick was standing on that same edge too.

Because beneath the guilt and the grief, something darker was taking shape—sharp and burning. A hunger for violence. For justice, soaked in blood.

Ra’s blood.

Because we all betrayed him!” Jason fell silent, but his words kept ringing in Dick’s head, looping like a broken record. “Where do you think you’re going, Jason?” That wasn’t Bruce Wayne. That was Batman. But no one cared.

They all knew what hid beneath that calm. The fury. The grief. The point of no return.

Normally.

Because Nightwing had no reason left for mercy.

He had been wrong. So so wrong.

He crouched, the dim light glinting off the fractured screen, then reached behind it. His fingers closed around the flash drive still lodged there.

Hope.
