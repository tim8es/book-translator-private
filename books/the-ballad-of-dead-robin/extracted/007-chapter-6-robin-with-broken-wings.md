“Well, well,” the boy said lightly, swinging one leg over the arm of Ra’s chair as he idly scratched the cat beneath its chin. “Fancy company.”

Slade stopped behind Ra’s, arms crossing instinctively, his gaze never leaving the Lord of Chaos. Something about the boy set his teeth on edge.

Ra’s merely raised an eyebrow and approached the desk. His steps were measured, almost hypnotic, each footfall silent on the marble floor. His robes trailing behind him in a slow, deliberate wave.

“I didn’t expect to find you here.”

Klarion hummed, as if considering the room rather than the man addressing him.

“Oh, I know,” he replied, voice bright and careless. “That’s why it’s fun.”

His eyes flicked up at last, something ancient in them, utterly uninterested in Ra’s authority. “Speaking of fun,” he continued, tilting his head, “where’s the little bird?”

“He flew away,” Ra’s replied evenly, his gaze fixed downward on the Lord of Chaos. Klarion blinked once, feigning confusion. Then he turned the chair lazily, spinning a half circle, before leaning forward and resting his elbows on the desk. His fingers laced together, chin settling on top of them. The cat was still on his lap. His smile didn’t fade, but in an instant, all traces of playfulness vanished, leaving behind something cold and calculating.

“So,” he said flatly, “you lost him again?”

Contempt bled through his voice. Ra’s brow twitched, his lips curling into a snarl.

“I would advise you to be careful about how you speak.”

“Of course,” Klarion replied, dripping with mock reverence. “How could I forget, your mighty majesty.”

Despite the sarcasm, his eyes remained ice-cold. He pushed back from the desk, gathered the cat into his arms, and rose to his feet.

“Still,” he added, circling the desk and stopping at the center of the room, his piercing gaze locking onto Ra’s, who maintained his composure despite the fury churning in his chest, “allow me to say one thing. If you fail again this time,” Klarion continued calmly, “I won’t keep playing these games.”

Ra’s lifted his chin slightly, studying him with open interest.

“Who would have thought,” he said with a hint of mockery, “that the Lord of Chaos would grow tired of amusement.”

The boy wasn’t fazed. He only shrugged, already turning toward the exit, as though the entire exchange had bored him. Slade stepped aside from the door, glaring at his back with distrust.

“Any game gets dull,” Klarion said lightly, “if you play it for too long.”

Bruce stared grimly at the monitor, fingers interlaced into a tight lock, eyes fixed on the feed as his sons disappeared beyond the manor grounds.

Because they had lost Tim.

He barely restrained himself from storming down to the Cave, from pulling on the suit and hunting that replica down himself, locking him away this time, sealing him behind reinforced walls. And then finding whoever had decided to play this cruel joke.

Bruce mourned. He had mourned for five years, waking up with his dead son’s name on his lips. A son whose face now belonged to another boy.

It didn’t matter where this child had come from. He was not Bruce’s son. Because Bruce’s son lay underground, with no future left to him. Bruce had seen it himself. Verified it more than once.

There could be no mistake. There simply couldn’t.

He would not repeat the same mistake he had made with Jason.

The door to his study creaked open, and Alfred stepped inside, carrying a tray.

“Master Bruce. Your coffee.”

He set the mug down on the desk with a soft clink. His expression remained composed, but Bruce sensed the tension beneath it. The past few years had been hard on all of them; their relationships had grown colder, and no matter how hard Bruce tried to be a good father, losing Tim had taken a part of him with it.

Bruce knew exactly what Alfred was about to ask.

He braced himself, fingers curling until his knuckles whitened, listening to the second hand of the clock tick forward, one measured beat at a time.

Tick. Tock.

“Master Bruce, don’t you think you’re being too harsh with the boy?” Alfred began when Bruce remained silent.

Tick. Tock.

“I respect your feelings, sir, but he is not at fault. He is still so young…”

Tick. Tock.

“…I am not asking you to close your eyes and trust him blindly…”

Tick. Tock.

“…but a measure of human compassion toward someone trapped in circumstances beyond his control would harm no one…”

Tick—

Bruce’s palm slammed against the desk. The sharp crack sent the coffee sloshing over the rim of the mug, dark liquid spilling across polished wood. Alfred fell silent at once. And perhaps, under different circumstances, Bruce might have felt regret.

But he could no longer contain the anger clawing its way up from his chest.

“Compassion?” Bruce snapped. “You’re asking me for compassion when a clone of the child I lost is walking through my house?!”

He surged to his feet, drawing himself up to his full height, one hand braced hard against the desk as he stared Alfred straight in the eyes. Alfred didn’t flinch, but his gaze went distant, shuttered, impossible to read.

“Where was that compassion when my son was being tortured?!” Bruce demanded. “Where was it when my child was murdered?! Where was that compassion when—”

He didn’t realize when his voice had risen, only that suddenly it broke. Bruce sagged, the fire burning out all at once, and he swallowed a bitter knot in his throat. He squeezed his eyes shut and turned toward the window, presenting his back to Alfred, no longer able to pretend he was strong.

“Where was it,” he said quietly, almost to the glass, “when my son was dying…thinking he was my mistake?”

“Are you disappointed in me, Bruce?” Tim’s voice was flat, stripped of emotion. His blue eyes were like ice, a dull light trapped beneath the surface. When he became Red Robin, Tim severed himself from Batman and Robin alike. He no longer relied on anyone, and that terrified Bruce. Terrified him to the point of madness.

In the time he’d been gone, Tim had changed.

“You almost killed him, Tim.”

“But I didn’t,” Tim replied in the same even tone, already moving toward the computer. His steps were light, unhurried, as he opened a report file. Bruce watched him grimly, a thick, clinging regret settling in his chest. “I calculated everything, Bruce. There’s no need to worry.”

“Calculated?” Bruce shot back. “Or were those calculations the reason Harkness is in the hospital?” Because Tim was too smart. Smarter even than Bruce. He never acted without accounting for every possible outcome, and Bruce wasn’t a fool—he knew Tim would never have let things spiral like that by accident. “Your decisions put others in danger. That’s not what Robin is meant to symbolize.”

“I’m not Robin anymore, Bruce.”

Bruce flinched at the sudden venom in Tim’s voice. Tim dropped into the chair and turned to face him, baring his teeth in something close to a snarl.

“Your real son wears that mantle now,” Tim went on. “Though there’ll be far more blood on his hands than on mine.”

“Tim,” Bruce growled, a warning lacing his voice as he stepped closer, jaw clenched while he forced himself to breathe. “Damian grew up in the League. He was taught to kill from childhood. We can’t expect from him what he was never taught.”

“Then maybe,” Tim said softly, barely above a whisper, yet the words rang through the Cave like an echo, “you shouldn’t expect from me what you never taught me either. Because everything you ever taught me was that I’m not your son. I’m just your subordinate. A soldier. A weapon. Anything, but not your son.”

Regret hit Bruce first. Then revulsion at himself. Then remorse.

“And that,” Bruce said at last, his voice low and broken, “was a mistake. I never wanted you to be my Robin.”

“I have a lot of theories about what might have happened to me.”

The voice in the headphones made Tim lift his eyes to the screen—to his own reflection. It was him. His voice, his cadence, the familiar rhythm of thought. That alone made something in Tim’s chest go hollow.

“But my money’s on Ra’s al Ghul getting to me after all. Maybe blowing up all of his League of Assassin facilities wasn’t the smartest move,” the Tim on-screen huffed, resting his elbows on the chair’s armrests.

Tim inhaled deeply, the air catching halfway down his lungs. His hands felt distant, like they didn’t quite belong to him, resting uselessly on his knees. Later, he told himself. Feel later. Right now, he needed to focus.

“Who would’ve thought arson would be my ticket to a promotion in Ra’s eyes?” the other Tim continued, shrugging lightly, that same easy, familiar smile fixed in place. “Still—what’s done is done.”

Tim realized his shoulders had begun to loosen, his body responding on instinct to the sound of a voice it recognized as safe. That frightened him more than the theories spiraling through his head. His thoughts felt detached, like they were happening several steps behind his body, delayed echoes instead of real words.

“I’m recording this because something feels wrong,” the video went on. “I keep noticing shadows that follow me. Feeling watching eyes. Every day they’re closer and closer, but I can’t catch them. Ra’s trains his people well.”

On the screen, Tim turned his head slightly, and the smile slipped. For just a second, his face went blank—empty in a way Tim knew intimately. A mask dropping when no one was supposed to be looking. Tim’s fingers twitched, numb, as recognition settled in his bones.

Then Tim in the video snapped his gaze back to the camera.

“And today, I received an envelope from him.”

He reached forward, movements slow and deliberate, and drew a sheet of paper into view, angling it toward the lens. Tim’s vision blurred at the edges as the words came into focus.

They were the same ones. The same promise Ra’s had whispered when he swore to make Tim his heir.

Tim felt himself drift, like he was watching all of this from several feet behind his own eyes, the room too quiet, the world slightly unreal, while his own face on the screen stared back at him, already knowing how this would end.

“—This is the beginning of The Ballad of Dead Robin, detective.”

The paper lowered out of frame, and that same faint smile returned to the screen, but the other Tim’s eyes remained hollow, glassy, stripped of anything alive.

“He always did love theatrics,” the Tim in the video added lightly. But they both knew it wasn’t theatrics. It was a game. Ra’s game. A predator circling its prey, tightening the radius slowly, and waiting patiently, until panic set in. Until mistakes were made. Until the victim did exactly what Ra’s needed. He didn’t want obedience. He wanted the mind. And the only way to claim a mind was to break it first.

“Tim,” his name landed like a crack of thunder. Those blue eyes, identical to his own, looked straight through the screen, straight into him. Tim’s breath stuttered. For a split second, it felt as though the room tilted, as if the voice had reached past the headphones and hooked directly into his skull. “I don’t know how much time I have before he makes his move,” the other Tim said quietly. “I don’t know how to stop him, because…”

He hesitated. Just for a heartbeat. As if the words themselves resisted being spoken. As if saying them would make them real.

“…when Bruce came back, I was no longer Robin,” Tim’s fingers curled against his knees. He couldn’t feel them properly—only distant and dull pressure. “And even so, Damian is still trying to get rid of me. Jason ignores my existence entirely. Cass is in Thailand. Stephanie, too, but she never believed in my words. Duke might listen to me, but he is still new. He would go to Dick first. And Dick…” A pause. A dark shadow crossed his face. “I can still feel his distrust. As if he thinks I truly belong in Arkham,” Tim on the screen let out a soft, humorless huff and lifted the paper again, holding it loosely between his fingers. “This time, I’m afraid he’ll actually send me there,” he continued. “Even if I bring him this. He’ll think my paranoia finally snapped. That Ra’s is just trying to scare me, and that I, like a child, fell for it.”

His voice sharpened, cracking through the practiced calm.

“But I know better! I know this isn’t a bluff! I know because Ra’s al Ghul never abandons his plans!” Tim swallowed. His throat burned. An eye for an eye, The Demon’s Head’s own words echoed in his mind.

The screen glowed softly, the other Tim staring back at him with the certainty of someone who had already seen the ending.

And somewhere deep inside, Tim felt the unsettling pull of recognition, like he was watching a memory he hadn’t lived yet, or a fate already set in motion, waiting patiently for him to catch up.

“Bruce regrets ever letting me wear the Robin mantle. He regrets it because I didn’t live up to his expectations,” Tim’s lips twisted into a crooked smile on the screen, and the fire that had flared moments earlier drained out of him all at once, leaving something flat and exhausted behind. “When the choice comes down to his first son…or his soldier, we both know who he’ll choose.”

Yes. Tim knew that truth intimately. He had known it from the very first day he became Robin—he had always known exactly where the line was drawn, and on which side he stood.

“Red Robin is no longer part of the bats. And the price of trust is too high,” the other Tim continued. “They will never accept us.”

Tim’s fingers clenched so hard his nails bit into his palms. His teeth began to chatter, sharp little clicks he couldn’t seem to stop. A tremor bloomed in his chest and spread outward like an infection, down his arms, into his legs, until his whole body vibrated with it. He felt distant from himself, as if he were watching his own shaking hands from somewhere just behind his eyes.

Truth is what holds the most power, detective.

Tim stood alone against all of them.

And he had no power.

“But you’re not alone, Robin, because I’m with you,” the smile on the screen returned. Thin. Ghostlike. “Think of me as a ghost risen from the dead.”

The other Tim leaned closer to the camera, his presence filling the frame, dark bangs falling on his eyes.

“I managed to dig up some of Ra’s plans, but in case this video leaks to the wrong hands, I uploaded the files to another flash drive. You’ll find it at the southern harbor,” and then his gaze softened, just slightly.  “I’ll be here. For as long as I can,” he said quietly. “I promise you that, Tim. So please—fight. Fight for both of us,” the screen seemed to dim around him, the room fading at the edges of Tim’s vision.

“Because we are all we have left,” the other Tim finished. “Everything else has already been taken from us.”

The video ended.

Tim sat motionless, the headphones heavy against his ears, his reflection staring back at him from the darkened screen.

He remained seated for several minutes, completely still. The flash drive was still plugged into the computer, but his body refused to respond, as if the signal between thought and movement had been severed. He couldn’t feel the floor beneath his feet. Couldn’t draw a full breath—each inhale caught shallow in his chest, like his lungs had forgotten what they were meant to do.

Think, Tim. Think.

This wasn’t the time for emotions. He needed the files. He needed answers. He needed to understand what had been done to him.

Tim’s memories ended the moment he brought Bruce home. After that—nothing. A blur where everything felt both real and unreal at once, as if the world had slipped out of alignment. And yet, fragments of the past haunted his sleep—memories that didn’t belong to him, or perhaps belonged too much.

Amnesia? Or had the other Tim’s consciousness bled into his own?

He had hoped, desperately, that Bruce would help him piece it together. But Bruce hated him. That much was painfully clear now.

He couldn’t rely on anyone but himself.

Drawing in a deep, shaky breath, Tim yanked the flash drive free and pulled the headphones from his ears. His hands moved on instinct as he wiped the computer’s history, erasing every trace of his presence.

Because if they found him—

if they caught him—

They wouldn’t let him out of that cell again.

Pulling his hood tighter around his head, Tim slipped toward the library’s emergency exit, the door leading to a dim corridor at the far end, and eventually out to the other side of the academy.

He forced down the gnawing anxiety curling in his stomach, replaying everything he had just heard. It was clear who was behind this. Ra’s. And it was just as clear that he would stop at nothing to claim Tim for himself. Lazarus’ influence had amplified his obsession, sharpening his desire to possess him.

The question wasn’t if—it was how. How exactly was Ra’s planning to do it? What was his game here? And why was Slade with him?

Lost in his thoughts, Tim didn’t notice the figure ahead until he collided headfirst with a solid chest, stumbling to the dirty floor in shock. He blinked rapidly, shaking his head to clear the sudden sting.

Standing over him was a silhouette in a yellow-and-black suit, a helmet concealing his face. He was tall, taller than Dick, but slightly shorter than Jason. But Tim’s attention wasn’t on his height.

It was the symbol emblazoned across his chest.

The unmistakable silhouette of the Bat.

Tim drew a sharp, ragged breath, digging his nails into the gritty ground. The figure across from him remained perfectly still, studying him through the glass of the helmet. It gave him the sinking, helpless feeling of a small animal caught in a predator’s gaze. Slowly, the figure sank to one knee, never breaking eye contact, and Tim mirrored the stare, rigid, afraid even to blink.

“Tim?” the vigilante breathed. Tim guessed it must be the new Gotham vigilante, though patrolling by day hardly fit Batman’s reputation. “Is that really you…?”

There was so much hope in the voice that Tim felt a flicker of guilt prick at his chest, but he couldn’t act on it, because…

“Who are you?” The sharpness of his question made the figure flinch. Shoulders tensed, the hand he had lifted—half to grab, half to help—froze midair. Inexperienced, Tim thought. The Gotham vigilante would never allow himself to appear this exposed.

“Tim? It’s me…Signal,” Signal? Tim had never heard of him. “You…really don’t know me?”

The fragile hope that had hung in the air moments before curdled into a heavy, viscous sorrow. Tim forced himself to recall the name his other self had mentioned. Duke. Probably it was him, considering he was new. But Tim pressed his lips together, silently observing the vigilante, and shook his head.

A heavy sigh followed. Then, a gloved hand gripped his elbow, carefully helping him to his feet, yet never releasing its hold. Tim narrowed his eyes, suspicious, but Signal ignored the glare entirely, reaching with the other hand toward a communicator.

And in that instant, Tim’s heart plummeted to his stomach.

“Signal here. Tim’s with me,” the voice crackled through. The price of trust is too high. “We’re heading to the cave…”

“Duke!” Tim shouted, straining his vocal cords. For a fraction of a second, the grip on his arm loosened. Just a fraction, but it was all the opening he needed. With a sharp slip beneath Signal’s hand, who lunged to grab him again, Tim bolted, sprinting toward the narrow alley, away from the watchful cameras.

“Tim, stop!” Signal yelled behind him, swearing under his breath, but Tim didn’t slow. He could feel the pursuit closing in. He was smaller. Weaker. Wounded, for God’s sake. But he had one advantage.

Gotham was his home. These streets had always protected him.

Turning down another street that ended in a dead end, Tim leapt onto the fire escape of a nearby building, the metal groaning under his weight. But instead of climbing to the roof, he vaulted onto the dividing wall between the streets, landing with a jolt that made his knees sting.

A flash of yellow emerged from the corner, a figure staring straight at him. Tim’s heart slammed in his chest. He dropped to the other side, tucking himself out of sight, scraping his jeans against the rough concrete. Pain shot up his legs, but he pushed through it, sprinting down the alley and cutting sharply left.

There it was. The door.

A heavy iron lock glinted in the dim light, eight buttons staring back like a puzzle.

“Please, please, please…” Tim whispered, his fingers trembling as they danced over the keypad. 1…6…0…8. His Robin’s date of birth. “Common.”

A soft click echoed through the alley, and relief washed over him in a hot, sudden rush. He slipped inside, slamming the door behind him.

Inside, a narrow staircase wound upward. Tim’s legs shook violently with each step, muscles screaming from the sprint, but he forced himself to climb. Step by step, he ascended.

Signal wouldn’t come here. Nobody would. Here, at least, he was alone.

This had been a residential building. But after the fire, when no one had bothered to repair it and the former tenants, having lost everything, refused to return, it had been put up for sale. That’s when Tim had bought it under his false identity. Bats knew all his usual hideouts, and Tim hoped—prayed—that this one would remain hidden.

No one would find it.

Some of the old residents had trickled back in; some new families had moved into the building. But among them, one apartment remained a secret refuge. Tim had neither the time nor the luxury to cross-check the intel. He could rely only on instinct, hoping that the other Tim had reasoned the same way he would.

Stopping at the door on the fourth floor, apartment sixteen, Tim drew a deep breath. Summoning all his willpower into a fist, he knocked lightly, listening intently for any sound from within.

Silence.

His throat tightened. He glanced down at the plain black carpet on the floor. Crouching, he lifted a corner and dug his nails along the edge, prying at the layers until a lock-picking pin fell into his palm.

Too many coincidences, he thought, a shiver running down his spine.

Unlocking the door, Tim eased it open, moving cautiously as his eyes swept over the empty space. No shoes. No coats. No traces of life. No props. No signs that anyone lived here.

Still, he stepped inside, every sense coiled tight. He closed the door behind him with deliberate care, then flicked the light switch. The bulb hissed and flickered to life. The apartment was washed in pale tones, white and clean, but it was obvious that no one had been tending to it. A small kitchen connected to the living room, furnished with nothing but an old, worn couch. One door led to the bedroom, another to the bathroom.

Everything was exactly as Tim remembered.

And that, somehow, made him uneasy and conflicted. It was too…right. Too orderly. He could have let himself slip into a simple time-theory explanation if it weren’t for the small details that gnawed at him, refusing to let him rest.

Despite all the similarities, the sequence of events in his mind kept breaking off. There wasn’t enough data, and without data, his theories remained nothing but theories.

But before starting to plan his next step, he moved to the kitchen, grabbing the kettle and filling it with water. At least the bills were paid. Though, knowing the other Tim, he had likely covered several years in advance, just as Tim would have himself.

He set the water to boil and reached for the coffee cabinet, pulling down a mug from beside it. Two scoops. Then, after a pause, he added two more. Fatigue was creeping over him, weighing down his mind and body. He watched the water bubble and hiss, focusing on the rising steam.

Tim Drake always had a plan. Tim Drake was always ready. Tim Drake was a perfect soldier.

The harbor. South.

A primitive code. But the kind of code no one would expect from Timothy Drake, the Red Robin. It wasn’t an algorithm. It wasn’t a program. Just one word.

Harbor.

The simplest single-word cipher, meaning coffee. But coffee in the south could only mean one place to Tim Drake.

Wayne Tower. The most exposed place.

“Figures. Hide it in plain sight, right?” Tim exhaled and grabbed the boiled kettle, pouring water in the mug. Turning his back to the cold, steel countertop, he leaned on it with trembling hands. The faint hum of the city seeped through the cracked window, distant but unnervingly loud in the tense silence of the room.

Then, in an instant, his body froze. Muscles coiled like springs, every nerve screaming.

Crouched on the windowsill, back slightly arched forward to make up for his height, hand gripping the edge of the open window frame, was Damian. The dim light caught the sharp angles of his face, and his bright green eyes locked on Tim. Relief tangled with rigid severity, and the quiet intensity made the air between them feel like a physical weight pressing down on Tim’s chest.

“Akhi…”

The word barely left Damian’s lips, but it was enough to shatter Tim’s focus. Instinctively, he grabbed the mug on the counter and hurled it. It clattered against the floor with a metallic ring, splashing some of the hot liquid, the smell of burnt coffee stinging his nose. On trembling knees, he lunged for the door only to slam into its unyielding surface. It was locked.

The faint creak of the floorboards behind him made him glance over his shoulder. The chill from the open window brushed against his neck, goosebumps rising, and for a heartbeat, time seemed to stretch.

He was trapped.

What do you think, Detective? Can a little bird without wings survive?
