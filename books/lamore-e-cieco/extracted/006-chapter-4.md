## Chapter 4: Bitten

Chapter Notes

See the end of the chapter for [notes](#endnotes4)

Dinner was served shortly after, though "served" felt like too gentle a word for the way the staff moved through the room—quick, silent, eyes down, as though the air itself had grown heavier since Arian had returned to his seat.

Caleb didn't seem to notice, or didn't care. Or pretended not to.

"Steak," he said, inspecting the plate set before him with a raised brow and barely surprised expression. "How delightfully on-brand. I did wonder if you'd serve something with a little more blood in it, Mr. Conti, given the company."

"Disappointed?"

"Devastated." Caleb picked up his knife and fork with theatrical precision, and cut into the steak, examined the color of it, and made a small, begrudging sound of disapproval. "Well. At least it doesn't move anymore. Which I suppose is the more accurate compliment."

Arian paused for a minute, not really rushing in to explain how expensive was that tenderloin cut. He was actually very sure Caleb was aware of that and simply decided to be picky out of spite. How cute.

"You're very free with your opinions, Your Highness. That’s a dangerous habit."

"I'm a Prince, Godfather. Freely offering opinions no one asked for is essentially my only official duty." Caleb picked a small piece of meat with his fork, considered, and put it back down. "That, and smiling in photographs. I'm told I do that rather well too."

"I've seen the photographs."

Something flickered across Caleb's face—cold amusement, quickly smoothed over.

"Have you. How thorough of you. Did you enjoy the one from Ascot? I'm told it's very flattering. Or the one from my cousin's wedding, where I fell into the fountain? That one's less flattering, but considerably more provocative. I'm not a shy maiden, after all."

Arian took a sip of the wine, glancing at the smirking man beside him.

"I preferred the one from your eighteenth birthday."

Caleb paused, wine glass halfway to his mouth.

"That's a strange one to remember."

"You looked uncomfortable in it."

"You have quite the eye for detail, Godfather. Should I be flattered or worried?"

"Both, generally, where I'm concerned."

"Noted." Caleb took a sip of wine, studying Arian over the rim of his glass with new interest, some calculation working behind his eyes. "You know an awful lot about me for a man I only met tonight."

"I like to be prepared."

"For what, exactly? Small talk? Seduction? Assassination?" Caleb smiled, sharp and easy. "Do give me some warning, if it's the last one. I'd like time to finish my dinner."

Arian said nothing to that, cutting his own steak, but with slightly more fervour than before.

Caleb set his glass down, and his eyes swept over Arian again, slower this time, more focused. There was a cold glint in those dark eyes that made Arian furrow his brows instantly. And he was right because—

"Speaking of dying—you're rather young for all this, aren't you? Godfather. Head of a Family. Terror of two continents." He gestured vaguely with his fork, encompassing the room, the guards, the weight of the title itself. "I confess I'm curious how a man gets that particular promotion so early in life."

Arian gritted his teeth as the conversation began to touch on topics he couldn't tolerate being touched on. No matter who touched them.

"That's not one of your concerns. Eat your food."

"Oh, but it is. I'm insatiably curious. It's one of my worst qualities, according to my tutors and the rest of the world, apparently." Caleb leaned back slightly, something reckless glinting behind his smile, the wine loosening whatever restraint he'd been holding onto since he sat down. "Let me guess. Someone died, and conveniently, it was you standing closest to the body when the succession needed sorting."

Arian's hand stilled around his glass. The music in the background faded into silence, as if sensing his tension. He turned his head slightly, eyes sharp and cold.

"I'd choose your next words carefully, Your Highness."

But Caleb didn't blink. He danced at the tip of the blade Arian just pointed at him.

"You're so young to already be a Godfather," he said, lightly, almost gently, as though he were discussing the weather. "I suppose there was blood involved. Was it your father? Or…your brother?"

The room went silent.

And something in Arian's chest didn't shift this time. It shattered.

He was on his feet before he'd decided to move, the chair behind him scraping back and toppling with a crack against the marble floor, and then his hand was around Caleb's throat—not crushing, not yet, but close, close enough that Caleb's breath caught audibly and his eyes went wide with something that finally, finally looked like fear.

"Don't speak of things you know nothing about," Arian hissed, and his voice didn't sound like his own. It came out low and raw, stripped of every careful, measured thing he'd built himself into over the years. "Tease me one more time and I'll teach you the Conti Family's rules myself.”

Caleb's hands came up instinctively, fingers curling loosely around Arian's wrist as though testing the grip, testing how much room he had left to breathe.

"Careful, Godfather," he managed, voice rough but steady, that infuriating, fearless glint still alive somewhere underneath the panic. "You're proving my point rather spectacularly."

Arian's grip tightened.

"You know nothing."

"Clearly." Caleb's eyes didn't waver, even as his knuckles whitened around Arian's wrist. "But I'd wager it's a good story. Everyone in this room's gone pale as a sheet."

Somewhere behind him, Arian heard his consigliere say his name, low and careful, a plea rather than a warning. He ignored it. His hand didn't loosen.

And then Caleb's fingers left his wrist.

It happened fast—faster than Arian expected from a man half-choked and unarmed, and by the time he registered the movement, the cold edge of a steak knife had already found its way to his neck held there with a hand that was shaking now; slightly, but Arian could feel it as clearly as the coldness from the silver against his skin.

For a long moment, neither of them moved. Arian's hand remained locked around Caleb's throat; Caleb's blade remained pressed to Arian's artery, close enough to draw blood if either of them so much as breathed wrong.

Around them, no one dared to interfere, but the clicks of gun safety catches being unlocked echoed through the great hall.

Arian looked at the knife. Then at the hand holding it. Then, finally, at Caleb's face—flushed, furious, terrified, and still, somehow, unwilling to look away. Proud and brave even when standing face to face with the devil.

Something in Arian—the part that had spent a lifetime punishing weakness wherever he found it—recognized, with a slow and unwelcome clarity, that he was looking at the strongest weak person he had ever met.

Slowly, he opened his hand.

The knife didn't lower immediately. Caleb held it a moment longer than necessary—not from malice, Arian suspected, but because his hand hadn't yet caught up with the fact that the threat was over. When it finally did lower, it wasn't graceful. It clattered against the table, and Caleb's fingers curled into his palm as though to hide how badly they were shaking.

No one moved to punish him for it. Arian made sure of that with a single glance around the room, and one by one, the safety catches clicked back into place.

He righted his fallen chair, sat, and said nothing for a long moment. Long enough that Caleb's breathing evened out, long enough that some color returned to his throat where Arian's hand had been.

Then Arian reached into the leather folder beside his plate, pulled out a thick stack of documents, and threw them onto the table in front of Caleb with far less ceremony than the moment probably deserved.

Caleb stared at the papers. Then at Arian. Then back at the papers.

"My goodness," he said, recovering his voice before he'd fully recovered his composure, that infuriating smirk back in place, "you are very thorough, aren't you? Do you always come this prepared to dinner, or am I simply a special occasion?"

"Read it."

Caleb picked up the first page with exaggerated caution, as if he was handling something that might detonate, which, in fairness, it very well might.

His eyes moved down the page. Then stopped.

"*Fake* marriage contract?!"

The words came out sharper than anything he'd said all evening—pure startled disbelief.

Arian watched him read it properly now, watched the Prince's eyes move faster down the clauses, his brow furrowing, his mouth opening and closing twice before he managed to speak again. It was fascinating to notice real emotions on the face of a performer.

"You're telling me," Caleb murmured slowly, "that you dragged me across a continent, destroyed my passport, threatened over dinner, and nearly had me shot by your very enthusiastic staff—for a marriage that isn't even *real*?"

Arian crossed his legs, leaning back.

“You sound disappointed. Why is that?”

Caleb glared at him.

“I might be an avid fan of having fun, but there are boundaries even I won’t cross. Fake marriage is one of them.”

"It's real enough for the purposes that matter."

"Which are?"

"The Carusos," Arian explained, even though he wasn't obliged to, "and the Conti Family have had a marriage alliance for years now. I’m expected to marry the Caruso daughter. Unless…”

Here Arian stopped and looked over at Caleb. The young Prince was still waiting for the continuity, a shadow of earlier offense and surprise still clinging to his pale skin and high cheekbones, and he reminded Arian of a startled deer. He almost smiled.

“Unless what?”

“Unless I’m not fond of women.”

Caleb inhaled sharply, blinking a few times.

“Oh.”

Arian reached out and picked up his wine glass, swirling the contents leisurely.

“I don’t intend to marry into the Caruso Family. So I've searched for a long time, looking for a solution. And then your brother crossed a line, and has given the Crown reason to want this scandal buried quickly and quietly. A public engagement between us solves both problems at once."

Caleb set the papers down, though his hand lingered on them, fingers drumming once against the table.

"How convenient. So, what am I? A shield?"

"A partner."

"In a marriage that doesn't exist, to a man I met an hour ago, who choked me at his own dinner table."

"You did put a knife to my throat."

"You put your hand around mine first. It was self-defense—"

"You provoked me."

"You're a mafia leader. Provocation should be an occupational hazard, not a personality flaw!” Caleb accused, but there was no real heat in it now—something almost like reluctant amusement had crept into his voice, the fear from moments ago folding itself neatly back beneath his usual armor of smile. He tapped the contract, and leaned back in his chair, studying Arian with new interest, the earlier fury softening into something more contemplative.

"And here I thought I'd have to earn your affections the old-fashioned way," he said. "Charm. Wit. Devastating good looks."

Arian couldn't help but step right into the honeyed trap and see what would happen.

"You may still have to."

"I do love a challenge." Caleb’s eyes flicked mischievously from the bottom to the top of Arian’s body, meeting his cold eyes in the end.  "Though I confess, Godfather, I'm struggling to see what my family imagines I bring to this arrangement, besides a face that photographs well and a talent for disappointing my father."

"You underestimate yourself."

"I assure you, I don't. I'm quite thoroughly aware of my worth to the Crown. It's roughly equivalent to a decorative pillow. Pleasant to look at. Easily replaced."

There was something underneath that—something quick and old and unamused, gone again before Arian could catch and inspect it.

"I don't share the Crown's assessment," Arian said quietly, and regretted it instantly.

"Well," Caleb said eventually, and picked the pen up off the folder, twirling it once between his fingers before setting the contract flat in front of him. "Since you've gone through the trouble of the paperwork."

He didn't sign it. But he didn't push it away, either.

"I have conditions," he added, glancing up.

"Of course you do."

"Naturally. I am, as established, a decorative pillow. Even we come with terms."

And for the first time since this Royal Headache stepped through the doors of the Conti estate, something that might have been the beginning of a real smile touched Arian's mouth.

"Let's hear them, then."

But the Prince only shook his head, and stood, hands sliding into his pockets with that easy, unbothered grace, as if he was on a stroll through the gardens with no care in the world.

Arian watched him rise, faintly thrown.

Caleb rounded the table slowly, unhurried, until he was close again—closer than propriety strictly allowed, close enough that Arian had to tilt his head back slightly to hold his gaze. With a flick of a hand he ordered his man to stay put, letting the Prince lean in.

Arian didn’t move, waiting. Caleb smiled, his white teeth showing.

 "About your offer, Godfather. I need to think it through."

"It wasn't an offer."

"No?" Caleb's mouth curved, slow and devastating, all traces of the earlier honesty folded away somewhere Arian couldn't reach anymore. "Well, then. I suppose I need to inspect the husband I'll be getting, don't I? That takes time. Due diligence, and all that. I wouldn't want to commit to fraud without doing my research first, and you strike me as a man with several skeletons and at least one dungeon."

Arian frowned, and Caleb smiled, entirely too pleased with himself. Then he traightened his jacket with two unhurried tugs, entirely unnecessary given the state it was already in.

"But, as you know," he added, already turning toward the door, "I'm a Prince, Godfather, and I do require my beauty rest. Even decorative pillows need fluffing."

Arian didn't respond, his teeth clenched as he watched him go. Something warm and unfamiliar settled deep in his chest, even though instinct told him it shouldn't.

But then Caleb paused in the doorway, glancing over his shoulder with the exquisite malice of a man who had never resisted the urge to say the last word in his life.

"Oh, and Arian..." It was the first time he'd used that name without a title, and Arian didn't like the sting he felt at the sound. "Next time, cook the steak longer. I don't do rare."

And with that, he departed, leaving behind the unsigned contract on the table and the Conti Godfather sitting alone at the head of the empty room, watching him with an expression his consigliere would later, very carefully, describe as murderous.
