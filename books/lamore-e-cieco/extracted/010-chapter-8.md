## Chapter 8: Betrayed

Chapter Notes

See the end of the chapter for [notes](#endnotes8)

Once Arian had made certain the estate was fully secured, he turned his attention back to Caleb.

"You. Go change."

Caleb glanced down at himself, apparently only now remembering the state of his clothes. Dust clung to his bathrobe, one sleeve was torn, and there were several dark stains across the fabric.

He looked back at Arian.

"How commanding."

Arian gave him a flat look.

"Change your clothes, Caleb."

"Yes, boss." 

The Prince took three steps before glancing back, catching Arian’s eyes.

"And you?"

Arian looked down at his injured arm.

"I'll deal with this. Go."

Caleb's expression sobered for just a moment.

"Make sure you do. Blood ruins good silk."

"For sure. Stay in your rooms until I come for you, Your Highness."

Caleb's eyebrow lifted, but he obediently made a turn towards his quarters. Of course, not without few last words:

“Who would have thought the head of a criminal family would be so good at giving orders? You'd make quite the nobleman, Godfather! Quite the nobleman…”

Arian watched until he disappeared down the corridor, a deep sigh escaping his lips involuntarily. How could he silence this madman for a moment without having to shoot anyone?

He headed for his office, his injured arm beginning to throb now that the immediate danger had passed.

"Captain."

One of his men stood waiting nearby, seamlessly following his steps. Arian didn’t slow down, taking off his cufflinks, already smudged with drying blood.

"Report."

"The first group breached the eastern perimeter shortly after sunset. Six confirmed attackers. Two more were spotted outside the grounds but fled before we could reach them."

Arian's expression hardened.

"Casualties?"

"Three wounded on our side. None critical. We've captured two of the attackers. The other four are dead."

"And the ones outside?"

"We're searching for them."

"Double the perimeter."

"Already done."

Arian nodded once.

"Good."

He walked toward his office, shrugging his shirt from his shoulders as he went. The moment the door closed behind them, he finally looked properly at his arm.

Blood had soaked through the makeshift bandage Caleb eventually improvised from his own bathrobe belt, tying it into a crooked, ugly bow.

"Perfetto…"

He sat down by his desk and began unwrapping it, pretending not to notice the slightest hint of guilt suddenly appearing at the sight of the bow disappearing under his rough tugging.. 

The wound wasn't deep, but it was messy enough to sting when he cleaned it. He worked quickly, efficiently, refusing to give it more attention than necessary.

His men continued the report from the doorway.

"We found evidence that the attackers had been watching the estate since His Highness Prince Caleb arrived. They knew the patrol rotations and location of his quarters."

Arian paused. That caught his attention.

"How certain are you?"

"Very. They avoided every regular checkpoint and targeted Prince Caleb’s wing directly."

Arian tied the fresh bandage tightly around his arm, not bothering with painkillers. He needed his mind clear. 

"Then, do we have identities of the attackers?"

"No, Boss. They are professionals—no ID’s, no symbols or insignias."

Arian leaned back slightly, eyes narrowing.

“What else.”

The soldier nodded, shifting his weight as he gathered his thoughts.

"The first breach happened shortly after sunset, on the eastern side of the estate. Our outer patrol noticed movement near the tree line, but by the time they reached the position, the attackers had already crossed the perimeter and split into smaller groups."

Arian listened without interrupting, his expression unreadable, although beneath the desk his injured arm had begun to throb again, the dull ache steadily worsening now that the immediate danger had passed. He kept his posture perfectly straight and his breathing measured, refusing to give the pain even the slightest acknowledgment.

"They were organized," the soldier continued. "They knew the grounds well enough to avoid the regular patrol routes, and they seemed to have a clear objective from the beginning. They weren't interested in taking anything from the estate, and they didn't waste time searching the rooms."

Arian's eyes narrowed.

He had already figured out the target of the attack, but hearing it confirmed only made the reality more unpleasant.

"How many?"

"We counted eight attackers in total. Two were captured, Four were killed during the fighting, and at least two managed to escape before the perimeter was completely sealed. We're still checking whether they had additional men positioned farther out."

Arian gave a small nod.

"And the prisoners?"

"One of the captured men appears to be a professional contractor. He’s refused to give us anything useful so far."

Arian's gaze sharpened.

"And the other one?"

"He's different."

"How?"

The soldier paused.

"He's the only one who is fully British. The others have no connection to Britain that we've been able to establish. More importantly, he's the only civilian among them."

Arian's expression changed almost imperceptibly.

"Name."

"The man is Paul Miller."

Arian didn't react.

The name was already familiar.

Paul Miller. British national. Prince Caleb's personal secretary. A man who had accompanied Caleb on diplomatic journeys, managed his correspondence and schedules, and had been close enough to him for years to know his habits, movements, and routines.

Arian saw him in the background of many of Caleb's photographs, the man’s stoic face always somewhere nearby the Prince’s mischievous grin. 

 His gaze lowered briefly to the blood-darkened bandage beneath his sleeve.

"Why was he there?"

"We haven't established that yet. He was carrying this."

The soldier produced a folded piece of paper and placed it on the desk.

Arian picked it up with his good hand and unfolded it.

***Make The Little Swan Fly.***

***Room 223, St Thomas' Hospital.***

His eyes remained on the sentence for a long moment.

The Little Swan. There was no mistaking the reference. But the hospital room?

Arian looked up at the man.

"Did Miller explain it?"

"No, Boss. He said only that he was following instructions."

"From whom?"

"He wouldn't say. He demands a conversation with Prince Caleb."

Arian folded the paper again.

His mind was already moving through the possibilities.

Someone had known where Caleb would be, and someone had been close enough to him to make certain the information was accurate.

And now one of Caleb's own people had been caught among the attackers with a written instruction identifying Caleb as the target.

There was a particular name that surfaced almost immediately.

Arian did not allow himself to dwell on it.

Suspicion was useful, but certainty was earned. And assumptions, especially where powerful families were concerned, had a habit of getting people killed.

"Where is Miller now?"

"Secured in the east holding room."

"Keep him there."

"Yes, Boss."

"No one questions him until I give the order."

The soldier nodded.

Arian looked at the folded note once more before slipping it into the inside pocket of his coat.

"Anything else?"

"Nothing that changes the situation at the moment. The grounds are secured, but we're continuing to sweep the perimeter in case anyone else is still hiding nearby."

"Good. Keep the patrols out until morning, double the eastern watch, and have someone quietly check every vehicle and person that entered or left the area during the last forty-eight hours."

"Understood."

"And keep digging into Miller. I want everything on him."

"Yes, Boss."

The soldier turned toward the door and left, closing the door behind him.

Arian remained seated for a moment, staring at nothing. His thoughts returned to the note.

Then to Miller.

Then, inevitably, to the complicated mess surrounding Caleb's family.

Arian's mouth tightened. There were several people who could have benefited from Caleb disappearing. There was one in particular who had reason enough to make the possibility worth considering. But the reason was not proof.

Until he knew who had given Miller the order, Arian would keep the suspicion where it belonged: inside his own head.

But then, he suddenly thought about the Prince. How will he react if Arian’s suspicions become the truth? Will he be disappointed? Will he cry? Will he be furious and hurt? 

He pushed himself to his feet. Pain shot through his arm, sudden and sharp.

His fingers gripped the edge of the desk. Only for a second. Then he straightened, his face once again perfectly composed.

He had blood on his sleeve, a fresh bandage beneath it, and an unanswered question sitting heavily in his mind, but none of that mattered now.

Arian left the office, paused only long enough to make certain the corridor was clear, and headed toward the Prince's rooms.

Men still moved through the corridors, boots striking against the stone as they continued their patrols, but the frantic energy of the attack had disappeared, replaced by the quiet efficiency and determination to bring back order and peace.

Arian stopped outside Caleb's door. He knocked once.

"Come in."

Arian opened the door.

Caleb had changed.

The torn, dust-covered clothes from the cellar were gone, replaced by a perfectly fitted cream-white suit that made him look as though he had spent the evening at some elegant diplomatic reception rather than hiding beneath an estate while armed men tried to kill him. His dark hair had been combed back into place, his face was clean of dust and grime, and there wasn't a trace of the frightened, breathless man Arian had held between his knees in the cellar.

Instead, Caleb stood by the window with his arms folded, looking entirely too pleased with himself.

His eyes flicked toward Arian. Then his mouth curved into a smirk.

Arian knew that expression. Trouble.

"You took your time, Godfather."

Arian closed the door behind him.

"I was dealing with the aftermath."

"Naturally." Caleb glanced at him, his eyes stopping at the hidden wound for a second longer. "You look terrible."

"I'm wounded."

"It was hard to miss, really."

"Then perhaps you could refrain from commenting."

Caleb's smirk deepened.

"I could. I simply don't see why I should."

Arian stared at him, frowning. And, to his own surprise, something inside his chest loosened. It was subtle. Almost imperceptible if he was stubborn enough to pretend it didn’t exist at all.

But it was there.

This Caleb—the smug, irritating, deliberately provocative Caleb—was familiar. Safe.

The man in the cellar, pale and shaking, had been real. Arian knew that. He had felt the tremor in Caleb's hands, had seen the fear in his eyes, had watched him struggle to breathe.

But this was real too. Or perhaps it was partly performative.

Arian knew Caleb well enough by now to recognize that particular brand of mischief for what it was: a shield he put on when he refused to let anyone see how badly something had affected him.

And, strangely, Arian found it soothing. After everything that had happened tonight, after blood and weapons and the knowledge that someone had deliberately come for Caleb, seeing him standing there in immaculate cream-white clothes, wearing that infuriating smirk as though he were already planning how best to irritate Arian, was perhaps the most grounding thing Arian had seen in a very long time.

Arian studied him for another moment before turning toward the door.

"Come with me."

Caleb raised an eyebrow, but followed anyway.

The walk to Arian's office was quiet, although Caleb made no effort to hide the occasional curious glance he sent toward him, clearly trying to work out why Arian had suddenly decided to summon him after spending the better part of the evening ordering him around.

Once inside, Arian closed the door. One of his men was already waiting there.

Arian moved behind his desk and reached into the inner pocket of his coat. He pulled out the folded piece of paper.

"Here."

He handed it to Caleb and the Prince took it without much thought, still wearing that faintly amused expression.

Then he unfolded it. And all of his amusement vanished.

His fingers tightened around the paper.

"Where did you get that?"

Arian didn't answer.

Instead, he gave the smallest nod toward the door.

The guard opened it and two of his people dragged in a man, with black sack over his head. And when they pulled it off, the colour drained from Caleb’s face.

"Paul…?"

Paul Miller stumbled forward between the two guards, his clothes dishevelled and his face bruised from the struggle. The moment his eyes met Caleb's, something desperate crossed his features. He scrambled to his knees, his bound hands raised in begging.

"Your Highness—"

"Why?”

Caleb's voice was quieter now, probably still shocked by the revelation of this betrayal.

"Your Highness, forgive me! I had no choice!"

Arian remained silent.

Caleb stared at the man who had served at his side for years, as though he were trying to reconcile the familiar face with the person standing in front of him.

"I had no choice," Paul repeated desperately, his reddened eyes shaking as he tried to catch Caleb’s gaze. The Prince refused to look at him. "It was your brother. He paid those men to kill you!"

For a moment, Caleb said nothing.

Then he gave a short, humourless snort. The paper trembled between his fingers.

Arian noticed.

"So framing me for his crimes wasn't enough for him, I suppose."

His laugh was quiet, almost disbelieving.

"Now he wants me dead."

"Your Highness, I swear—I had no choice! He threatened my family!"

"Does he honestly think I'm plotting against him for the crown?"

Caleb's voice sharpened.

"I told him I don't care for that!"

Paul shook his head desperately.

"I know, Your Highness. I know you don't. But he wouldn't listen. He said—he said you were deceiving him, that you were waiting for the right moment, and that if I didn't help him, my family would pay for it. I really had no choice—

Caleb looked away. His jaw clenched.

For a moment, the room was completely silent. And then, Caleb ripped the paper in his hands into shreds, letting it fall at Paul’s knees. 

“There’s always a choice, Paul. You just chose wrong.” 

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Paul remained between the guards, pale and visibly shaken, while Caleb stood amongst the scraps of his own death sentence. 

Arian watched him.

He watched the tension in Caleb's shoulders, the way his fingers tightened into fists, the way he deliberately kept his face composed despite the tremor that still betrayed itself in his hands.

There was anger there. Hurt, too. Perhaps even betrayal.

Arian wondered which one would win. 

He leaned back against the edge of his desk, his gaze moving briefly toward Paul before returning to Caleb.

"So," he said calmly, "what should we do with him?"

Caleb didn't answer.

"Bury him alive?"

Paul's head snapped up.

"NO! I beg you, Your Highness—"

It was almost enough to make Caleb flinch.

Instead, the Prince simply turned his head away.

He didn't look at Paul, he didn't look at Arian either.

His gaze settled somewhere in the evening behind the window, his jaw tight and his shoulders taut.

Arian watched him closely.

It was a test.

Arian wanted to know what lived underneath all that charm and deflection, whether the Prince was capable of hurting someone he cared about when given a reason to do so, whether betrayal would turn his compassion into vengeance, or whether there was a limit to the ruthlessness he was willing to embrace.

Arian already had his suspicion. He simply needed to see it for himself.

Caleb swallowed. Then, without looking at anyone, he said quietly,

"Lock him up."

Arian's eyes narrowed slightly.

"Very well."

He gave a small nod to the guards.

"Take him."

Paul looked at Caleb, desperation returning to his face. He started struggling, his expression twisted with fear and desperation.

"Your Highness, please! No!"

Caleb's eyes remained fixed on the wall. The guards forcefully pulled Paul toward the door, dragging his body without mercy.

"I'm sorry," Paul screamed again. "I swear to you, I had no choice! Your Highness!"

The door closed behind them and the silence returned.

Caleb remained where he was, still refusing to look at Arian.

Arian studied him for a moment, saying nothing, and then, quietly, he said,

“You could have asked me to kill him.”

Caleb’s eyes flicked toward him for a second, shining in the low light.

“I know.”

“But you didn’t.” Arian tilted his head slightly. “Why?”

Caleb finally looked at him. His eyes were steady again, though his hands were still trembling faintly at his sides.

“Because he was trapped.”

Arian said nothing.

He had expected compassion. He had expected restraint, perhaps even some lingering sense of loyalty toward the man who had betrayed him.

But he hadn’t expected Caleb to say it so simply.

As if that single fact explained everything. As if being trapped was enough to make a person worthy of mercy.

Arian’s gaze lingered on him for another moment, taking in the faint tremor Caleb was still trying to hide, the exhaustion beneath his carefully composed expression, and the strange contradiction of a man who had spent the evening being hunted in a place that was supposed to be safe, yet could still find enough room in himself to feel sorry for the person who had helped put him in danger.

As if Caleb himself weren’t trapped.

As if his own cage—built from royal blood, expectations, family, duty, and the decisions of people who had never bothered to ask what he wanted—held no significance at all.

Arian looked away first, not entirely pleased with the complicated thoughts beginning to circle in his head.

“Go get some rest.”

Caleb blinked, returning to the present, and then tilted his head, the faintest smile already beginning to pull at his mouth.

“Oh, my. Is that a concern I hear in your voice, Godfather?”

“Don’t flatter yourself.”

“I absolutely will.” Caleb’s smile grew. “It’s not every day I hear tenderness from you, future husband. But I must admit, caring doesn’t suit you. I prefer you to be unapproachable—”

Arian’s mouth twitched.

“Don’t push your luck. Go.”

Caleb smiled faintly, apparently satisfied that he had managed to annoy him one last time.

“Yes, boss. Goodnight.”

Arian did not return to his own rooms after Caleb left. Instead, he remained in his office, standing behind his desk with one hand resting against the polished wood while the events of the evening continued to rearrange themselves in his mind, each new piece fitting into place with an increasingly uncomfortable precision.

The estate was secured; his men were competent, disciplined, and loyal, and Arian trusted them without hesitation when it came to protecting the grounds. No one crossed his perimeter without being noticed, and anyone foolish enough to try would find half a dozen armed men waiting for them before they got anywhere near the house.

But tonight had proven that the perimeter was not the problem.

The attackers had gotten inside.

Worse, one of them had been someone from Caleb's own household.

Arian's eyes narrowed as his thoughts returned to Paul, to the note found on him, and to the simple fact that someone who belonged within Caleb's immediate circle had been involved in an attempt to put him in the ground. He had already ordered the patrols doubled, the eastern perimeter reinforced, and every person connected to the attack investigated, but none of those measures addressed the most immediate problem.

Arian's jaw tightened.

He could station another man outside Caleb's door, of course, and that would protect the Prince from anyone attempting to approach through the corridor, but what good would another guard outside accomplish if the danger had already demonstrated that it could come from within?

The thought irritated him precisely because it was reasonable.

Someone needed to remain inside Caleb's rooms tonight, someone Arian trusted completely, someone who would notice the smallest movement, hear the faintest sound, and react before an intruder could get within arm's reach of the Prince.

The obvious solution was to send one of his best men.

Arian dismissed the idea almost immediately, not because his men were incapable—they were anything but—but because Caleb knew them, and they knew Caleb, which meant that if someone had already exploited the familiarity and access that came with his household, placing another man inside his private rooms might create precisely the vulnerability Arian was trying to eliminate.

Besides, Caleb would never sleep properly with a stranger stationed inside his bedroom.

Arian frowned.

That was a ridiculous argument. Caleb's comfort was irrelevant. Security was the priority.

He considered sending one of his most trusted men instead, someone who had served him long enough that Arian would trust the man's life with his own, but another possibility immediately presented itself, and once it did, he found it increasingly difficult to dismiss.

If there was another attack, Caleb might hesitate for even a fraction of a second upon seeing someone he did not immediately recognize.

A fraction of a second was enough.

Arian knew that better than anyone.

His expression hardened. He didn't like the possibility, and he certainly didn't like the fact that the solution had become so obvious.

He should go himself.

He knew the estate better than anyone, knew every corridor and entrance, knew exactly what to listen for and where to position himself, and if something did happen during the night, Caleb would recognize him immediately; there would be no hesitation, no confusion, and no wasted second while the Prince tried to determine whether the man approaching him was friend or enemy.

It was the logical choice. Entirely logical.

Arian stared at the closed door of his office and exhaled slowly.

He was going to spend the night in Caleb's rooms.

For security. That was all.

It had absolutely nothing to do with the fact that the thought of leaving Caleb alone after what had happened made something unpleasant twist beneath his ribs, nor did it have anything to do with the strange sense of relief he had felt earlier when he had found the Prince standing straight again, already back to being insufferable and determined to provoke him.

Arian reached for his coat, then paused when his injured arm gave another dull, persistent throb beneath the bandage, reminding him that he had perhaps had enough excitement for one night.

He ignored it, of course.

He had a Prince to protect.
