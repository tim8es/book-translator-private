## Chapter 7: Unmasked

Chapter Notes

See the end of the chapter for [notes](#endnotes7)

The cellar gave them silence; a little time to breathe.

It wasn’t safety, not yet. Arian knew better than to confuse the two.

Safety was something that could be assured by armed men and reinforced by locked doors, while silence was simply the absence of imminent disaster, a momentary pause granted by fate before it decided whether to send something worse to the door. Arian had spent too much of his life learning the difference to relax now just because a piece of wood stood between them and hell.

Still, for the first time since the beginning, they could take a breath.

They seemed to have taken refuge in one of the old underground warehouses beneath the east wing of the mansion, a place Arian remembered from his childhood as cold, unpleasant, and constantly reeking of stone and dust, but now, with a faint whiff of smoke still hanging in the air and the ceiling occasionally shuddering as something heavy collapsed somewhere above them, he felt strangely grateful for the thick walls of this place.

Arian stood by the door for a few seconds, listening.

No footsteps descending the stairs. No voices. No movement behind the old stone. Only the distant, muffled chaos of an intrusion into his home.

Finally, he allowed himself to turn around.

Caleb stood a few feet away.

Or rather, he was trying to stand. The prince pressed one hand against the wall, breathing so raggedly that Arian noticed it immediately, though that familiar look of irritation and composure had already settled on Caleb's face, as if the pursuit of armed mercenaries through the burning manor was simply another inconvenience the Crown hadn't warned him about.

Arian watched him for a moment.

The Prince was afraid. He could feel it. There was no mistaking the way Caleb's hand tightened around his own as the gunfire echoed through the hall, no mistaking the strength of that grip, no mistaking the way Caleb remained close despite all his previous reservations, and Arian found himself revisiting that memory with a focus he hadn't particularly appreciated.

Caleb trusted him.

Perhaps unconsciously, and perhaps only for a moment. Certainly, if anyone asked him, the Prince would deny it until someone dragged him before the King himself and confronted him head-on. But when fear stripped him of his jokes, his pride, and his absurd determination to turn every command into an argument, Caleb's first instinct was to reach for Arian.

The thought stuck in his mind. It stuck with him now.

"Are you hurt?" Arian asked, searching for any concealed wound.

Caleb looked at him. The question seemed to come from nowhere, as he frowned, thought about it and shook his head.

"No."

But then Caleb's gaze dropped, and Arian followed it.

Blood reached his hand. For a moment, he simply stared at it, registering its dark spread across his skin with something close to indifference. Then the pain made itself known. Not suddenly—that wasn't how pain worked; it had been there since the bullet hit him, hot, sharp, and persistent, but adrenaline transported him to a distant place while his mind was occupied with more important matters, namely keeping Caleb alive long enough to get out of the line of fire. Now, however, with the immediate threat temporarily averted and the Prince safely before him, his body seemed to decide that the moment was right to remind him exactly what had happened.

The wound stung like Satan's pitchfork stabbing him repeatedly.

Arian exhaled slowly, trying to control the grimace slowly creeping onto his face.

“Arian—”

"I'm fine."

Arian glanced toward the door again, listening to the distant sounds above them. When he looked back, Caleb had stepped closer.

"Arian."

The Prince said his name differently this time. There was no *Godfather* attached to it. No sarcastic emphasis. No amused drawl designed to make Arian regret ever telling him his title. Just his name, flat and serious, with nothing underneath it except the thing Caleb was trying not to show.

Arian disliked how immediately he noticed the difference.

"What?"

Caleb stared at the blood. Then he stared at Arian. Then, quite suddenly, he moved.

"Right. You’re fine." The words came out short and sarcastic, as though Caleb could barely hold his temper at bay. He was already searching the room.

Arian lifted an eyebrow. 

"What are you doing?"

"Looking."

"For what?"

"I don’t know—something."

Arian stared at him. Caleb opened another drawer, found nothing, and shut it. Then he moved toward an old shelf, pushing aside boxes and bottles with increasing impatience while muttering under his breath.

"Bandages, cloth, anything remotely useful would be rather welcome right about now."

Arian remained where he was. 

"Caleb, there’s no need—”

"Quiet."

Arian complied. 

Caleb continued searching, and Arian had a rare chance to closely observe him. 

At first, he had assumed the Prince was merely reacting to the injury in the way anyone trapped in an attack might react after watching another person take a bullet intended for them, but the longer he watched, the more something about Caleb's movements began to disturb him.

He was frantic. But not in a way Arian was familiar with—that would have been easier to understand. Scared people were loud and chaotic. 

Caleb's panic was quieter than that, hidden inside quick movements and clipped breaths, inside the way he opened one drawer and immediately abandoned it for another, inside the fact that his fingers slipped twice against the handle of a cabinet before he managed to pull it open.

His hands were shaking.

Arian's attention settled on them and stayed there.

He had seen men shake before. Fear was not unfamiliar to him. People feared him. People feared the Conti name. People feared the things Arian was capable of doing when pushed too far. But this was different in a way he found he could not quite dismiss, because Caleb was not afraid *of* anything in this room. Caleb was afraid *for him*.

The realization came with an odd weight.

Arian did not know what to do with it. He had been injured countless times throughout his life, some wounds far worse than this one, and no one had ever made the sight of his own blood feel particularly significant before; blood was simply blood, pain simply pain, and survival had always been a matter of dealing with whatever damage had been done and moving forward before anyone had the opportunity to see weakness in it.

Now Caleb was standing across the cold and dark cellar with his hands trembling. Because Arian had been shot. Because the bullet had been meant for him.

Something vicious and merciless tightened uncomfortably beneath Arian's ribs, making him shift to feel the pain. Pain was simpler. 

"Caleb."

The Prince opened another drawer. Empty. He turned away from it and pushed one hand into his hair with a kind of restrained desperation that Arian found, against his will, very difficult to look away from.

"Where the hell is anything useful in this place? Is there a hidden mechanism that doesn't hold a gun, do you suppose?"

"It isn't a hospital."

"No, clearly, because if it were, someone would have had the foresight to put medical supplies inside it. You're a bloody criminal family after all.” 

Despite himself, Arian almost smiled. 

"It looks worse than it is."

"That's an incredibly irritating thing for someone bleeding all over the floor to say. Have a conscience, would you."

"You're exaggerating, it's not that serious—”

Caleb looked down. Arian followed his gaze. A drop of blood fell from his fingertips and then another, landing dark against the stone, already forming a quite unsightly puddle. 

Caleb looked back at him with an expression that required no translation.

Arian paused. Then he shrugged lightly. 

"Christ."

The Prince turned away again, dragging both hands through his hair. Arian watched, and this time he did not find it amusing. Caleb's shoulders had tightened. His breathing had become shallow. And there was something almost helpless in the way he looked around the cellar, as though he could not accept the possibility that there might be nothing here capable of fixing the situation immediately.

Arian knew that feeling. Not from being Caleb—from being ten years old, from watching something terrible happen and understanding, with the particular clarity reserved for children who have already learned too much about the world, that sometimes there was nothing you could do except stand there and wish you were strong enough to change it. He had spent his entire life hating that feeling, and perhaps that was why he could not bear seeing it on Caleb's face.

"Caleb."

"I'm trying!” 

Arian caught his wrist. The Prince stopped immediately. His head turned. Arian held his gaze for a moment, then reached for Caleb's other hand and caught that one too.

The trembling was unmistakable now. 

For a brief moment, Arian said nothing.

He merely looked at him.

Caleb had not been like this when the first explosion tore through the manor. He had startled, certainly, and Arian had felt the fear in the way the Prince's fingers had locked around his hand when gunfire erupted ahead of them, but Caleb had still been able to move, to argue, to make those absurd remarks of his even while armed men were attempting to kill them.

He did not fear guns.

Arian had seen that much.

He did not even truly fear being shot at.

But blood—

The moment Caleb had seen the wound, something had changed.

His eyes kept returning to Arian's shoulder despite the fact that he seemed unable to bear looking at it for more than a few seconds, as though some instinct compelled him to check the injury while another, much deeper instinct recoiled from the sight of it, and Arian watched the conflict play across his face with a growing, quiet certainty.

This was not ordinary fear.

This—

This had roots.

Arian knew something about things with roots. He could have asked.

But Arian did not ask.

There were questions that became demands simply because of who asked them, and Arian had spent enough of his life being expected to tear answers from people that he knew precisely how to recognize the moment when silence was kinder.

So he waited.

His fingers remained on Caleb's hands, squeezing them gently.

Tight enough to stop them from shaking, but light enough that the Prince could take them away at any moment.

Caleb looked down at their hands, then at him, and then looked away.

Arian waited.

Above them, somewhere beyond the thick stone ceiling, another distant shot echoed through the mansion.

Caleb flinched, and instinctively his fingers tightened, nearly crushing Arian's.

Still, he said nothing. He simply waited. And maybe that was what finally worked.

Maybe Caleb had been expecting the question.

Perhaps he had expected Arian to demand that he explain himself, or mock him for the panic he had failed to conceal, or simply order him to pull himself together in that cold, merciless tone Caleb seemed so fond of provoking from him.

Instead, Arian only watched. Exceptionally patient. 

Caleb's eyes slowly returned to his.

"I—"

The Prince stopped, and looked down at their hands.

Then, almost angrily, as though he resented the confession before it had even left his mouth, he muttered, 

"I can't deal with gun wounds, alright?”

Arian's expression did not change, although a thousand questions popped in his mind at the revelation. Caleb gave a short, breathless laugh that held no amusement at all.

"I—I don't know why, but I just can't—"

His voice caught.

Arian felt Caleb's fingers tremble again.

The Prince looked toward Arian's shoulder and immediately looked away.

"I can hear gunshots," Caleb continued, the words coming faster now that he had begun, as though keeping them inside had required more effort than letting them out. "I can hold a gun, apparently I can even stand there while someone is shooting at me, but the moment someone gets shot—"

He stopped again, swallowing with difficulty. 

"I just can't."

Arian looked at him for a long moment.

*I don't know why. I just can’t.*

It wasn’t an explanation at all, but somehow it was enough for Arian to understand. Not every fear arrived with a name attached to it. Some simply lived inside you.

Arian knew that too.

He was not a man accustomed to comforting people. He did not know the correct words for it—had no experience with soft reassurances or carefully chosen phrases intended to make fear less frightening, and if someone had asked him, only an hour earlier, what he would do if Caleb Whitmore stood in front of him looking terrified, Arian might have answered that the Prince was perfectly capable of taking care of himself.

Now he found himself holding both of Caleb's hands between his own. The irony was not lost on him.

"Hey."

Caleb's eyes lifted.

Arian looked directly at him, and there it was again—those dark eyes, wide and worried and far too honest for Arian's comfort.

"Focus."

Caleb did, and Arian had no idea why that particular expression frightened him more than the gunfire above them. 

"I’m alright, it's really not that serious," he said firmly, and then glanced briefly toward his shoulder before meeting Caleb's gaze again. “Unfortunately for you, I'll still live for years to come. Sorry to disappoint you.”

For one second, Caleb merely stared at him.

Arian wondered whether he had said something wrong. Humour was not his territory—humour belonged to Caleb. And perhaps he had misunderstood how it worked.

But then Caleb let out a shaky breath that almost became a laugh.

"For years, you say? What a shame."

Arian's mouth moved slightly. 

"I understand you were looking forward to inheriting my estate. Try again."

Caleb blinked. Then the laugh came—small at first, a little shaky, but unmistakably real, and the sound of it freed something caged inside Arian's lungs, allowing him to finally breathe. 

"Oh, absolutely," Caleb muttered, lowering his head briefly. "That was the plan all along. Seduce a terrifying Mafia Boss, wait until someone shoots him, inherit the house. It failed, but come on, you have to congratulate me on my genius, Godfather.”

Arian felt his own shoulders loosen.

*There you are.* The thought arrived quietly, shamelessly. *There you are.*

Caleb looked up again, and this time his eyes were sober. The fear had not vanished—it couldn’t be so easy—but Caleb had found his balance again, rebuilding the familiar layers one joke at a time. Arian understood that more than he wanted to. He understood, suddenly, why the Prince talked so much when he was frightened. Why he smiled. Why he flirted. Why every serious moment seemed to make him reach instinctively for something ridiculous.

Caleb made fear laughable so that it could not consume him.

And Arian had just tried to do the same, offering him a shield. 

It wasn't like him. Not at all. 

The realization was strange enough that he almost released Caleb's hands. He did not. The Prince looked down at them, and Arian did as well, and only then did he fully register their position: he had moved closer while stopping him, and Caleb now crouched directly between his knees, close enough that Arian could see the uneven rise and fall of his breathing, while their hands remained caught between them and the narrow cellar seemed suddenly much smaller than it had a moment before.

Caleb's eyes lifted. Arian was already looking at him. 

He became acutely aware of Caleb's warmth. Of the fact that the Prince's hands were no longer shaking. Of the fact that, if either of them moved even slightly forward, the distance between them would disappear entirely.

His pulse gave one heavy, irregular beat.

Arian blinked, paralyzed. He did not like that. 

Something was wrong. Not with Caleb.

With him.

He had spent years mastering the art of keeping his instincts beneath his reason. Anger could be sharpened into something useful. Fear could be strangled before it reached his face. Compassion was a weakness if indulged too freely.

And yet Caleb had somehow slipped past every barrier Arian had built.

He could provoke his fury with a single word. He could make him reckless with nothing more than a frightened look. He could draw protectiveness out of him so instinctive and violent that Arian sometimes wondered whether it belonged to him at all.

And now—

Now his body had betrayed him for an entirely different reason.

Arian looked at Caleb's lips—they were so close, so vulnerable and infuriatingly curved. 

But they weren’t his. He immediately looked away, terrified. What in the gods' names was happening to him?

It was as though there were a magnet buried somewhere inside the Prince, something invisible and merciless that kept pulling Arian toward him no matter how firmly he planted his feet.

His thoughts. His anger. His compassion. His protectiveness. His fury. Everything seemed to lead back to Caleb.

And Arian hated that he could no longer tell where his own instincts ended and Caleb's influence began.

Then—

A knock struck the cellar door.

Both of them froze. Arian's head snapped toward the sound.

"Boss," a voice called from the other side. "It's us. Team Alfa 1-0-1. The grounds are secured. Enemies dealt with or captured."

For a moment, Arian didn't move.

Then he looked back at Caleb. The Prince was still crouched between his knees, looking up at him with those red ears and startled eyes—

Arian cut the thought off before it could finish. He rose first, then offered Caleb his uninjured hand.

"It's over," he said, feeling the pain slowly taking over his consciousness. "Let's get out of here." 

Caleb dusted himself off with his free hand, looking around the cellar. Arian noticed the few glances the Prince gave his injury, only to turn away and sigh deeply.

"What a shame."

"What?" Arian asked, only to regret it a second later.

"I was beginning to enjoy our little hiding place."

Arian's expression went flat. Caleb's smile widened.

"Especially the cramped space and my future husband’s tight embrace—"

Arian let go of his hand at once.

"You're walking by yourself."

"Ah." Caleb stepped past him toward the door, not even looking back. “And gone is my knight in shining armour. What a pity…Galantry really is dead, my goodness.”

Arian followed, scowling. He had absolutely no idea what was going through that royal brat’s head. 

“I’m seriously going to put a muzzle on you…”
