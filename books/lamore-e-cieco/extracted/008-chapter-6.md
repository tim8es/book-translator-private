## Chapter 6: Revealed

Chapter Notes

Arian did not knock.

The door opened with enough force to strike the wall behind it, and Caleb, who had apparently been expecting exactly this response, turned toward him with the infuriating calm as if greeting a guest rather than acknowledging the furious Godfather who had just stormed into his bedroom.

He was still standing beside the bed, his trousers discarded somewhere near his feet and his shirt hanging over the back of a chair, while one hand rested against the waistband of his boxers in a position that made Arian's expression darken immediately.

Caleb looked at him. Then, very slowly, his mouth curved.

"What?" he asked, his tone so innocent that Arian immediately knew the brat was enjoying himself far too much. "Can't take what you asked for?"

Arian shut the door behind him.

Caleb’s brows rose.

“Oh, dear.”

Perhaps he had even spent the last several minutes imagining precisely what he would say when Arian finally arrived, prepared with another collection of infuriating remarks and that bright, careless smile he used whenever he wanted the world to believe he was completely unafraid.

What Caleb had apparently not expected was for Arian to stand close enough that there was no longer a room, a camera or a screen between them.

"You should be more careful," Arian murmured quietly, his voice low and threatening.

Caleb lifted his chin.

"Why is that?"

"Because one day, you may provoke someone who does not find you amusing."

The Prince's eyes narrowed.

"And what a dreadful loss that would be for me."

And Arian moved.

Caleb barely had time to take a step backward before Arian caught his wrist, turned him away from the painting and pushed him onto the bed with enough force to stop him from moving without hurting him, following immediately on top when Caleb attempted to twist away and catching his other arm before the Prince could decide that punching him might be sensible idea.

Caleb made an indignant sound.

Arian ignored it and held both of Caleb's wrists above his head, not painfully and not tightly enough to leave a mark, but firmly enough that the Prince understood immediately that Arian could keep him there if he chose to.

And then Caleb went silent.

It was almost comical. His eyes went wide, as well as his mouth; pink lips parted and trembling.

Arian stared down at him, suddenly interested.

The Prince stared back but the transformation was remarkable.

Only seconds earlier, Caleb had been standing in front of a hidden camera, half-undressed and smiling like a devil who had finally discovered a way to irritate the gates of heaven themselves, but now that Arian was actually there, that confidence had disappeared with almost suspicious speed.

Not entirely. Caleb Whitmore was still Caleb Whitmore.

His chin remained lifted. His shining eyes still carried a challenge.

But his breath had changed, now heavy and harsh. His face had gone warm, adding some colour to the pale complexion.

But Arian's anger had not disappeared.

If anything, the Prince's sudden silence made him more aware of how furious he had been when he left his office, because Caleb had frightened him in a way Arian was deeply unwilling to examine, and the knowledge that the Prince had been willing to go that far simply to prove a point had struck some instinct inside him that did not respond well to recklessness.

If Prince did that in front of anyone else—

He stared down at Caleb, trying to keep his temper from becoming something sharper. And since he had the Prince right where he wanted him—what harm can a little scolding do?

"You're playing with fire, Your Highness. Behave."

Caleb swallowed.

Then, because apparently self-preservation remained a concept the Prince considered optional, he found his voice again. It was muffled and tight with an emotion Arian couldn’t really describe. He knew it wasn't an embarrassment, though.

"And you lose your control too easily, Godfather."

Arian's eyes narrowed. Caleb shifted beneath him, his cheeks still faintly warm despite the coolness he was attempting to put back into his expression.

"Get off me this instant."

The command would have been considerably more convincing if the Prince had not looked so visibly flustered by the fact that his own little game had finally been returned to him.

Arian almost smiled.

"Causing trouble and then playing coy about the consequences?"

Caleb's eyes flashed.

"Excuse me?"

"Very un-princely."

"I am not coy."

"No?"

"Oh, do shut up."

The answer came so quickly that Arian actually laughed, quietly and without much warmth, but it was enough to make Caleb's expression darken with renewed frustration.

"I should like it noted," Caleb muttered, "that you are in no position to judge me for inappropriate behaviour while sitting on top of me.”

"I am not the one who began removing my clothes in front of a security camera."

Caleb glared, then he tugged experimentally against Arian's grip. Arian did not release him.

"Spy on me again," Caleb seethed through clenched teeth, "and there is no telling what you will see."

Arian's eyes narrowed.

For several seconds, he said nothing. Then he leaned slightly closer, almost making their noses touch, keeping his voice low enough that Caleb had no choice but to listen.

"Take off your clothes again, and there is no telling what I will do."

Caleb's breath caught.

There.

Arian felt something dangerously close to satisfaction tingling the top of his head.

For all Caleb's fearless performances, for all the jokes and shameless flirting and deliberately outrageous behaviour, there was a limit to how far his confidence could carry him when he was confronted directly.

When he jokes, Arian will joke back.

When he's shameless, Arian will be even more shameless.

And since the Prince loved flirting so much, Arian will show him how to do it properly.

He finally found it—Caleb's weak spots. And he didn't dislike the discovery.

"You're impulsive," Caleb said at last. Helplessly and with a hint of complaint.

Arian almost smiled again.

"You deserved it."

Caleb glared at him.

"Get off."

Arian held his gaze for another moment, and decided it was enough for today. Then he released his wrists.

Caleb immediately pulled his hands free, rubbing it once before looking at Arian with suspicion.

Arian stood.

The sudden absence of his weight and presence seemed to surprise Caleb, and the Prince remained lying there for a second before pushing himself upright with considerably more dignity than his situation allowed.

Arian adjusted his cuff that got undone during the scuffle and turned towards the doors.

"Put your clothes back on."

He couldn’t see Caleb right now, but he was almost sure the Prince rolled his eyes at his back.

"Are you always this bossy?" Prince grumbled, dissatisfied.

"Yes."

"How exhausting for everyone around you."

"Goodnight, Caleb."

Arian walked toward the door. But then, behind him, Caleb spoke again.

"Godfather."

Arian stopped. He looked over his shoulder.

Caleb was sitting on the edge of the bed, his hair disordered, his discarded shirt still lying across the chair, while the faint flush had not entirely disappeared from his face despite the fact that he was clearly doing everything within his power to pretend otherwise.

Arian waited.

Caleb held his gaze.

And then, because apparently the lesson had lasted approximately three seconds, his mouth curved.

"You did run rather quickly."

Arian stared at him, deadpanned.

Caleb's smile widened.

"Just an observation."

Arian said nothing.

"Perhaps you were worried."

Still nothing.

Caleb leaned back on his hands, stubbornly pressed to push Arian out of his thinning line of patience.

"That's rather sweet, actually."

Arian's fists clenched tightly. He started to feel the dull headache coming in again. He seriously considered putting a muzzle on the royal brat.

"Goodnight, Your Highness,” he forced out through his teeth and stepped out of the door.

Then Caleb spoke yet again, in the most irritatingly cheerful voice imaginable, and Arian’s blood pressure skyrocketed,

"Sleep well, darling!"

Arian slammed the door behind him.

For several seconds, he stood in the corridor without moving, breathing harshly.

Then he looked toward the ceiling, begging the decorative tiles to give him strength.

"That lunatic," he muttered.

Somewhere behind the closed door, Caleb Whitmore began laughing.

“Dear God, help us all…” Arian muttered, running his fingers through his already messy hair. He made a step back toward his office, when—

*BOOM!*

The ground shook with the force of the explosion, shaking the glass in every window in the manor. The light above him flickered in warning and then went out, shrouding everything in darkness.

Arian froze, instinctively reaching to his belt for the gun, but his hand found only air.

Oh, yeah, right. He'd run out of the office to stop the British Prince from completely exposing himself, leaving his gun and phone on his desk.

Arian sighed, astonished at the lack of precision and discipline he seemed to show lately, and immediately retreated into the room.

He had to save his Prince.

When he reached Caleb's door, he kicked them open, not even bothering to press the handle.

The wood struck the wall.

"Caleb—"

The Prince stood near the shattered balcony doors.

For one fraction of a second, Arian saw everything.

The curtains moving violently in the night wind. Broken glass glittering across the floor. Caleb turning toward him, his expression still caught somewhere between confusion and irritation.

And beyond him—

Arian saw the faint flash of a weapon outside. The shot came almost simultaneously.

*"Down!"*

Caleb did not have time to react.

Arian crossed the distance between them and threw himself at him. The impact sent them both crashing onto the floor, Arian twisting instinctively as he fell so that his body shielded Caleb's from the direction of the balcony, one arm wrapping around the Prince's shoulders while the other struck the ground beside him.

Pain exploded through his shoulder. It was hot and sharp, but it didn’t alarm him. Arian had learned long ago that pain could wait.

It was not something he ignored, precisely, because men who ignored injuries tended to die from them in embarrassing circumstances, but he had spent enough years in a world where blood often arrived before explanations to understand the difference between pain that required immediate attention and pain that merely demanded patience.

The bullet had grazed his shoulder. But his arm still moved, his hand remained steady around Caleb, and there was nothing wrong with him that could not be dealt with after every armed men currently trespassing through his estate had either left or stopped being a problem.

Caleb was considerably less easy to dismiss.

Arian looked down at the Prince, who was still beneath him on the floor, his hair disordered from the impact and his face paler than it had been only minutes earlier, although he had already begun gathering the pieces of his usual expression, carefully rebuilding that familiar look of amused arrogance behind which he had apparently decided to hide every unpleasant emotion he possessed.

Arian knew better now. He had seen the fear in his eyes before—it was impossible to unsee.

"Can you move?" Arian breathed in, quickly scanning the quiet man's face.

Caleb looked personally offended. He blinked in shock and then laughed, but it was exceptionally hollow.

"What an extraordinarily rude question. You're crushing me at the moment, so it is rather hard to move."

Arian stared at him, angry. The Prince sighed.

"Yes. But—" he hesitated, and glanced at the blood already sipping in through Arian’s white shirt. “Are you alright? You are bleeding—”

“It’s just a graze. Come on, we need to go.”

Arian rose first, keeping himself positioned between Caleb and the shattered balcony doors.

But before they left the room, Arian turned toward the sideboard near the door and reached between the carefully arranged ornaments until his fingers found an inconspicuous button hidden among them.

A hidden compartment slid open from the side, revealing the gun concealed inside.

"Mafia in full swing, isn't it?" Caleb muttered from behind him, his attention fixed very obviously on the button rather than the gun. "Every room has a compartment like this?"

Arian checked the magazine, released the safety, and turned toward the Prince with a flat look.

"You truly believe I am going to disclose the locations of hidden weapons to you?"

Caleb considered this.

"Worth asking."

"No, it wasn't. They're hidden for a reason, brat."

Caleb opened his mouth, offended, probably with an already forming very British 'Excuse me?' or 'Honestly, Godfather, you're such a brute.", but there was no time for talking.

Arian moved, leading them away from the windows and toward the corridor, keeping his weapon raised while the Prince remained only a step behind him, close enough that Arian could hear his breathing even beneath the chaos spreading through the manor.

The corridor beyond Caleb's room was darker now.

Emergency lights flickered weakly along the walls, throwing broken stretches of red across the marble floor while smoke from the explosions had begun to drift through the upper level of the house, carrying with it the smell of dust, burning fabric and shattered electrical wiring.

Distant shouts and the sound of heavy boots clattering against the marble floor were audible. His men were already in action, so Arian could calmly focus on keeping his royal headache alive.

If gunfire, explosives, and assassins could be considered a part of peace.

Arian stepped into the hallway first, keeping his posture low and his eyes focused.

"Stay close."

The words had barely left Arian's mouth before Caleb gave a quiet, nervous laugh.

"I'll climb on your back if I get any closer than I already am."

Arian looked over his shoulder.

True to his words, Caleb was standing directly behind him. Far closer than Arian would expect him to.

The Prince lifted one eyebrow.

"What?"

Arian turned back toward the corridor.

"Nothing."

"That's what I thought."

Despite the situation, Arian almost smiled.

Then movement appeared at the far end of the hallway. His expression hardened immediately.

"Behind me."

Caleb did not argue this time.

Arian raised his weapon.

The first mercenary stepped around the corner.

Arian fired.

The shot echoed through the corridor with enough force to make Caleb flinch behind him, although the Prince recovered quickly, pressing himself against the wall while Arian moved forward and fired again when another figure appeared.

There was no hesitation in him now—there couldn't be.

The exhaustion of the council meeting had disappeared.

The irritation caused by Caleb's behaviour had disappeared.

Even the pain in his shoulder had become distant beneath the familiar clarity of survival.

Arian moved and

Caleb followed him with surprising obedience, although Arian could hear the occasional unevenness in his breathing and knew that the Prince was forcing himself to remain calm.

Another man appeared.

Arian fired twice.

The corridor fell quiet again.

For several seconds, there was nothing except the ringing silence left behind by gunfire.

"You're surprisingly competent at this," Caleb whispered behind him.

Arian glanced over his shoulder.

"Disappointed?"

"No, although I had rather assumed your entire terrifying reputation was achieved through aggressive eyebrow movements and expensive suits."

Arian stared at him.

Caleb smiled weakly.

"Humour helps."

Arian looked forward again.

"I know."

The answer was quiet enough that Caleb said nothing.

They continued.

The corridor eventually widened as it approached the central staircase, opening into one of the larger spaces of the estate where the narrow walls that had previously offered them cover gave way to a broad marble hall, exposed on several sides by doorways, balconies and open passages leading deeper into the house.

Arian stopped at the corner.

This was worse.

A narrow corridor could be controlled. An open space offered too many directions from which an attacker could emerge. He listened to the distant gunfire, calculating the possibilities, before glancing behind him.

He extended one arm backward without taking his eyes from the hall.

"Grab my arm."

Caleb stared at him.

For one moment, Arian assumed he had not heard.

Then the Prince looked at the offered arm with open disapproval.

"Absolutely not. I touch no man's arm."

Arian slowly turned his head, but Prince continued before he could speak.

"If you were an attractive lady, tall, slim, chesty—"

Gunfire erupted ahead of them.

Caleb made a sound that was somewhere between a startled curse and an undignified gasp before his hand immediately found Arian's and his fingers closed around it with astonishing strength.

Arian snorted,

*“‘I touch no man’s arm’, my ass.”*

Nevertheless, he returned the grip, tightly enveloping Prince’s hand. It was warm.

Caleb, meanwhile, must’ve finally realised what he had done and cleared his throat awkwardly.

"Temporary solution, don't get attached."

Arian's mouth curved. Caleb's grip tightened.

"And don't make that face."

"What face?"

"The smug one. I can see it on the back of your head."

Arian looked forward again, wanting to continue the childish exchange, when another burst of gunfire echoed from somewhere within the hall.

His expression changed immediately and Caleb's hand tightened again.

Arian raised his weapon.

"Stay behind me."

Caleb inhaled.

Then, with his fingers still firmly wrapped around Arian's hand, he answered in a voice that attempted far too hard to sound casual.

"Yes, boss."

Arian said nothing. They moved again, searching for any cover in the open space, when from somewhere up on the first floor, a low-grade grenade was thrown in their direction.

And there was no time to think. Arian grabbed the front of Caleb’s bathrobe and slammed them both through the cellar doors.

Without a single breath of hesitation, Arian covered the Prince's body with his own.

*BOOM!*

The world above them shattered into pieces.
