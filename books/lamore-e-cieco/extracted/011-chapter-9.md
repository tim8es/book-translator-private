## Chapter 9: Uncertain

Chapter Notes

See the end of the chapter for [notes](#endnotes9)

By the time Arian reached Caleb's rooms, the estate had fallen into that peculiar silence that came only after midnight, when the last footsteps of the guards had faded into the deeper corridors and the old stone walls seemed to draw the night's cold into themselves.

Two of Arian's men stood farther down the corridor, exactly where he had ordered them to remain, their silhouettes barely visible beneath the muted amber glow of the wall sconces, and both straightened when they saw him approach.

Arian gave them a single look.

"Stay here. No one enters this room unless I give the order myself."

"Yes, boss."

He stopped in front of the door for a moment, his hand resting against the cool brass handle as he listened.

Nothing.

Then, faintly, from somewhere beyond the bedroom, came the unmistakable sound of running water.

He opened the door without knocking.

The room was warm, carrying the faint scent of cedarwood and expensive soap, while the soft light from the bedside lamps spilled across the dark carpet and caught along the edges of the heavy furniture. One of the tall windows had been left slightly open, allowing a thread of cool Sicilian air to slip through the curtains, stirring them gently enough that their shadows moved like pale ghosts across the floor.

Caleb was nowhere in sight.

Arian closed the door behind himself and listened again.

The shower continued in the adjoining bathroom, steady and unhurried.

Good.

It meant he could prepare the room without Caleb hovering over him, asking questions, making observations, or finding some new and inventive way to irritate him.

Arian crossed to the small leather briefcase he had carried with him and placed it carefully on the table near the window, then he released the two clasps, opened it, and began removing its contents.

Five devices.

Each was small enough to fit comfortably in the palm of his hand, constructed from matte black metal with no unnecessary markings, their compact frames concealing automatic launch mechanisms designed to respond to movement, sudden entry, or other predetermined signs of danger.

They were not weapons in the conventional sense.

The sort of machines Arian preferred because they did not require a man to be awake, alert, or standing in precisely the right place at precisely the right moment.

So they could protect Caleb even if no one was around him at the moment. And as much as Arian already gathered about the Prince, he was not going to accept the 24/7 surveillance from a stranger.

He carried the first to the bedroom door and crouched, securing it discreetly beneath the edge of a nearby cabinet where it would remain almost invisible to anyone entering the room. His fingers moved with practiced certainty as he adjusted its angle, checked the mechanism, and activated the first layer of its sensor.

A tiny green light blinked once.

Arian moved on.

The second device went near the balcony doors, the third was placed near the connecting door leading toward the private corridor, and the fourth went farther inside the room, covering the approach from the bedside and balcony.

That one made him pause.

Arian glanced toward the closed bathroom door as steam began to creep beneath the narrow gap at the bottom.

He set the device down carefully.

There was something strangely disarming about hearing Caleb shower while he stood in the middle of the Prince's bedroom preparing a miniature defensive perimeter, as though the ordinary intimacy of the sound belonged to another world entirely, one in which tonight had not happened and Caleb had nothing to fear.

Arian's expression hardened.

He finished the installation.

The fifth device remained in his hand.

He looked around the room once more, calculating angles and blind spots, measuring the distance between the bed, the windows, the doors, and every possible approach someone might use if they somehow managed to bypass the outer security.

Finally, he placed it right above the bedpost frame.

Close enough that if something happened, the device could respond before anyone reached Caleb.

Arian straightened slowly.

The room was secure. Well, as secure as he could make it without turning the Prince's bedroom into a military bunker.

Right then, the bathroom door opened and Arian stiffened, as if he was about to face an enemy.

Warm steam rolled softly into the bedroom first, carrying the clean scent of soap with it, and then Caleb stepped through.

He was wearing nothing but a towel wrapped securely around his waist, his damp chestnut hair falling untidily over his forehead as he rubbed another towel through it, apparently entirely unconcerned with the fact that someone had just invaded his room.

Arian's thoughts stopped.

For one profoundly inconvenient moment, every carefully organized thought in his head simply disappeared.

Arian looked away. Immediately.

He became intensely interested in the briefcase.

His mind, the traitorous thing that it was, took several seconds to remember how to function normally.

By the time his internal crisis subsided enough for him to trust his own face again, Caleb had begun looking around the room.

His gaze travelled from the door to the windows, then to the walls, the bed, and finally to the five small devices Arian had installed.

One eyebrow rose.

A nervous little laugh escaped him.

"I am aware this is your house," Caleb said slowly, "but barging into your guest's room in the middle of the night uninvited is wildly inappropriate."

Arian gave him a lukewarm glance.

Without answering, he reached for the briefcase. He lifted it with one hand and let it drop almost theatrically onto the floor beside the table.

The heavy case landed with a solid metallic thud.

Caleb's eyes flicked toward it, then widened slightly.

"Is that—May I ask why, precisely, you appear to be turning my chambers into an arms depot?”

"No.”

Caleb looked back at the devices. His expression became increasingly suspicious.

"What are those?"

"Fully automatic launchers."

Caleb stared at him.

Arian continued calmly, as though discussing the weather.

"They'll protect you at night."

There was a long silence.

Caleb slowly looked around the room again, taking in the devices mounted discreetly along the walls, the one positioned near the balcony, and the small mechanism fixed beside the bedpost.

"Splendid.” His tone was perfectly dry. "If any of those little toys go rogue like Paul did, I will end up dead peacefully in my sweet dreams."

Arian's mouth twitched, and Caleb sighed dramatically, one hand pressing against his forehead.

"I wonder what they'll engrave on my sad, royal tombstone."

He paused, apparently considering the matter with great seriousness. Then his eyes gleamed.

"Here lies Prince Caleb. He survived assassins, family politics, and an extremely determined Sicilian Godfather, only to be murdered by his own bedroom furniture. Let his exhausted soul rest and boil in eternal fire of embarrassment."

Arian stared at him, actually half certain he didn’t hear that right. Caleb grinned cheekily at him.

"That’s quite a vision, don't you think?"

"No."

"I thought you'd appreciate the poetry, it’s a lovely night after all."

"I appreciate silence."

"Then you've chosen the wrong husband, dear Godfather."

Arian's eyes narrowed, and Caleb's smile widened. So with more smugness than necessary, Arian took out his pyjama set, and headed straight for the vacated bathroom.

Just as he expected and anticipated, the smile faded from the Prince’s face almost instantly.

“Why—What are you doing?”

Arian didn’t even turn, delivering the answer in the most flat and cool voice,

“The answer is simple. I’m sleeping here tonight.”

“Excuse me?!”

Caleb’s tone was so scandalised it made Arian almost happy.

“The best way to keep you safe is to stay next to me, so deal with it.”

And then he shut the door, no longer listening to the frustrated Prince’s spluttering.

But just then, Arian raised his head and saw his reflection in the mirror.

He froze at the sight, uncomprehending.

For a gentle smile played upon his lips.

It was neither the polite grimace he offered strangers nor the forced half-smile he gave the media. For the first time in days—years, even, or perhaps for as long as he could remember—Arian saw something on his face that he had long since written off as lost.

Tenderness.

Sincere, unfeigned tenderness.

The lines of worry smoothed out, and his eyes softened.

Arian blinked, as if the face before him were not his own. But the reflection in the mirror did the same, filling Arian’s mind with terror.

He shook himself abruptly, turning his gaze—and himself—away from the mirror.

This was no good. Not good at all.

He busied himself with the simple, familiar rituals of preparation, folding his clothes with unnecessary precision and arranging them over the back of the chair, giving his hands something to do while his mind stubbornly refused to settle.

The bathroom still held the lingering warmth of the shower, and the faint scent of Caleb's soap remained in the air, absurdly soft against the colder scent of metal and gun oil that clung to the room after Arian made his way in.

He should have been thinking about security. About Paul Miller and his purpose.

About the men still being interrogated downstairs, the two attackers who had escaped, the possibility that someone had been watching the estate for days, and the far more troubling question of who exactly had orchestrated an operation precise enough to reach Caleb from within his own household.

Instead, his mind kept returning to the Prince.

To Caleb standing barefoot on the carpet with nothing but a towel around his waist, looking utterly at ease in a room that belonged to Arian.

To the way he had smiled.

To the way he had called him future husband with that shameless little glint in his eyes.

To the way his presence had somehow made the estate feel less empty.

Arian closed his eyes briefly.

This was becoming a problem.

Somewhere along the way, without Arian noticing the precise moment it happened, the Prince had begun making himself at home in places where no one had ever been allowed to settle.

Not merely in Arian's estate, where Caleb had already managed to leave traces of himself everywhere despite having been there for such a short time.

He had begun making himself at home inside Arian. Inside the part of him that had been sealed shut long before Caleb ever arrived.

That realization unsettled him more than the attack had, and Arian could only tighten his fists, trying to calm himself down.

Because he had spent years constructing that fortress within himself, brick by bloodied brick, tearing away every weakness before someone else could find it and use it against him.

Affection was dangerous. Attachment was dangerous. Trust was dangerous.

And love, if the stories of men who had been ruined by it were anything to judge by, was perhaps the most dangerous thing of all.

In his world, anything a man loved could be held against him, and anything he could not bear to lose could become a weapon.

Arian knew that lesson intimately, so he had built his entire life around avoiding it.

And yet Caleb had slipped through the gates without permission, without strategy, without even seeming to understand that he had found them.

But the worst thing of it all was that Arian had never intended for him to stay.

The original plan had been simple.

The Conti Family's Council wanted him married to Caruso's daughter, and Arian had needed a reason strong enough to make them retreat from the idea without provoking an unnecessary conflict between families.

Caleb has been the perfect solution.

A politically advantageous marriage, a royal connection, and a distraction powerful enough to make the Council reconsider what they thought they could demand from him.

So once the arrangement had served its purpose, Arian had intended to let Caleb go.

Caleb would return to his world, and Arian would remain in his.

The Prince would become nothing but a strange, irritating chapter in a life that had otherwise been carefully controlled.

Arian had never considered the possibility that Caleb might become something else.

Something permanent.

The thought struck him with enough force that he went still, his blood turning cold.

Permanent.

No.

There was no forever in his world.

There were alliances that lasted only as long as they served their purpose, loyalties sustained solely by mutual benefit, and people who remained by each other’s side until circumstances gave them a reason to turn away.

That was how things worked. That was how Arian had survived.

Love had no place in it, no right to exist.

Because love meant vulnerability, and vulnerability meant leverage. If someone knew exactly who mattered to you, they knew exactly where to place the knife.

Arian had spent years ripping every weakness out of his life.

He would not allow himself to grow another.

Especially not one with Caleb's face.

His jaw tightened.

Caleb was dangerous in ways the Prince himself probably didn't understand.

Not because he was powerful, or because he was royal.

Not because he could provoke Arian into losing his patience with nothing more than a mischievous smile.

Caleb was dangerous because Arian had begun to care whether he smiled at all.

That was the thing Arian could not permit, because if someone ever discovered how much Caleb mattered to him, they would not need to threaten Arian's life.

They would simply threaten Caleb's, and Arian knew himself well enough to understand what that would make him capable of.

His eyes drifted toward the bathroom door.

Arian could still hear the faint movements on the other side as Caleb busied himself with whatever he was up to.

Arian exhaled slowly.

He had once believed that the greatest danger to him would be the man who could put a bullet through his chest.

Now he was beginning to suspect that the greater danger was the man who could make him care whether anyone ever did.

Arian straightened finally, forcefully correcting his thoughts back to the present, his expression settling back into its familiar composure.

He would deal with this.

Remember the arrangement, remember the plan.

Caleb would leave eventually, and Arian would be exactly as he had always been.

Except, somewhere beneath all those carefully constructed assurances, a quieter part of him had already begun asking a question he did not want to hear.

What if I don't want him to?

By the time they had finally negotiated who would occupy which half of the massive bed, the night outside had deepened into an impenetrable blackness, the last traces of twilight long since swallowed by the Sicilian sky, while the estate beyond the windows had settled into a guarded stillness broken only by the occasional distant footstep of a patrol.

Inside the room, however, peace remained stubbornly out of reach.

The only sounds filling the darkness between them were the faint static hum of the installed devices, the measured rhythm of their breathing, and the maddening rustle of the duvet coming from Arian's left.

For thirty minutes, Caleb had been tossing and turning.

He shifted onto his back. Then onto his side. Then onto his other side.

He tugged the blanket higher, kicked it down again, adjusted his pillow, sighed, shifted once more, and somehow managed to produce enough noise from a perfectly ordinary bed to make Arian wonder whether the Prince had secretly smuggled an entire cavalry regiment beneath the sheets.

Arian stared into the darkness.

His injured arm ached beneath the bandage, a dull, persistent throb that seemed to have spread from his shoulder into his neck and eventually settled behind his eyes, where it pulsed with every passing second.

He had survived bullets, assassins, and political negotiations with men who considered murder a perfectly acceptable form of punctuation.

Apparently, he was going to be defeated by a restless royal in his own bed.

Another violent rustle came from beside him, and Arian closed his eyes forcefully.

Counted to five.

Then ten. And finally couldn’t take it anymore. He whipped his head to the side.

“Could you stop—”

“Forgive me, Godfather,” Caleb interrupted immediately, as though he had been lying awake specifically waiting for Arian to reach the end of his patience, “but I find it rather difficult to fall asleep beside a man who keeps a loaded gun beneath his pillow, particularly when you have also installed a collection of miniature execution devices around the room, one of which appears to be positioned directly above my head.”

Arian blinked his eyes, prayed silently, and then turned back to the ceiling.

“If you cannot sleep,” he continued, his voice flat with exhaustion, “go read a book.”

His head was beginning to throb in earnest now, the pain from the graze having transformed into a steady pressure that seemed to reverberate through his skull, accompanied by an increasingly persistent echo that sounded remarkably like the voice of the royal prince beside him.

Apparently, his reply had provoked Caleb in some inexplicable fashion.

The shuffling stopped. For perhaps three blessed seconds, there was silence.

Then—

“You know what?”

Arian clenched his teeth. Here we go.

“We should have a little chat.”

“No.”

“We’ve become rather close. Close enough to share a bed, certainly.”

Arian's jaw tightened even more.

“So, Godfather,” Caleb continued cheerfully, apparently delighted by his own inability to recognize danger when it was lying beside him, “tell me, how do things stand with my future in-laws? Have they accepted me yet? Your Conti Family’s Council must have a thing or two to say about this marriage, don’t they?”

“This doesn’t concern you.”

“Ah, surely they're delighted. Who could resist this charm, this handsome face, and, naturally, this magnificent body?”

Arian's eyes closed. Caleb gave a quiet, satisfied hum.

“I rather think they must be terribly impressed by my royal background. Even you could not resist me, my dear future husband.”

Arian inhaled slowly through his nose. Even in the darkness, he could practically hear the smile in Caleb's voice.

“Go to sleep.”

“I am trying.”

“You have been trying for half an hour.”

“Perhaps I require conversation.”

“You require silence.”

“Silence is overrated, dear future husband. Humour me.”

Arian's fingers curled against the sheets.

He heard Caleb suppress a laugh.

Then, as though the previous exchange had not already exhausted every last fragment of Arian's patience, Caleb added,

“I suppose I ought to thank you, though. It is rather touching, in its own peculiar way, that you are willing to sacrifice your sleep merely to keep me safe. How noble of you.”

Arian looked to the side sharply, meeting Prince’s gleaming, smiling eyes.

“I swear, if you do not go to sleep, I will personally force you to—”

“Force me?”

Caleb's voice carried an unmistakable note of amusement.

“Godfather, aren't you being a little harsh on your guest? Ah, forgive me. I forgot that harshness is apparently an occupational hazard of being a mafia boss. Tell me,” the Prince murmured, “is it an innate trait, do you think, or something you had to cultivate through years of practice? Was your family the same—”

That was the end of Arian's composure.

He moved in a single fluid motion, pushing himself upright before Caleb could properly register what was happening, and then leaned over him, one hand planted firmly against the mattress beside the Prince's face.

The movement was so sudden that Caleb's next remark died somewhere between his lips and his throat.

For once, there was no immediate answer.

Arian's face was only inches away.

For one suspended moment, neither of them moved.

Caleb was still beneath him, his face warm with embarrassment and his eyes fixed on Arian's, while Arian remained braced above him, acutely conscious of the distance between them and of how very little of it there actually was.

Then—

***Beep!***

A sharp electronic sound cut through the room.

Arian's head snapped toward it.

The small device mounted near the bedpost flashed once. And then the mechanism fired.

“Watch out!”

Caleb moved before Arian could.

He caught Arian by the shoulder and shoved with his entire weight, throwing both of them sideways across the mattress as the small projectile tore through the space where Arian's head had been a fraction of a second earlier.

The bed gave beneath them.

Unfortunately, it was far softer than either of them had anticipated.

Caleb's momentum carried them both backward, the mattress dipping dramatically before springing them upward again, and Arian barely had time to register the sensation of Caleb's weight crashing against him before their faces collided.

Their lips touched.

For one utterly stunned heartbeat, neither of them moved.

The world seemed to stop, leaving only their eyes, their breaths and the small, warm point where their lips met.

Then Caleb jerked backward.

Arian stared at him and Caleb stared at Arian.

Neither appeared remotely capable of producing a coherent thought.

Caleb was still sitting squarely on top of him, his hands planted on either side of Arian's shoulders, his hair dishevelled from the sudden movement and his entire face rapidly turning crimson.

Arian, for perhaps the first time in his life, had absolutely no idea what to say.

His heart was pounding so violently that he could feel it in his throat.

Caleb's eyes darted away.

Finally, with a voice that sounded considerably less composed than he would probably have preferred, he asked,

“What was the thing that just flew past us, do you suppose?”

Arian blinked once. Twice.

Slowly, painfully, his mind returned to the room.

He glanced toward the device. Then back at Caleb.

“It seems even the machines agree that you're jeopardising your own safety tonight.”

Caleb's mouth fell open.

“What imbeciles designed that?”

Arian gave a short snort.

Then he shifted beneath him.

The movement sent a sharp sting through his injured arm, and his expression tightened before he could completely suppress it.

But, apparently still operating under the assumption that Arian's movement had something to do with closing the remaining distance between them, Caleb immediately placed one hand against Arian's chest and began backing away.

“Whoa! Don't come closer!”

Arian stilled his movements, and his eyebrows rose.

For a moment, he simply looked at Caleb. Then his gaze dropped meaningfully toward the very obvious position they were currently in.

“Let me remind you, dear Prince,” he said with remarkable calm, “that you are the one still sitting on top of me.”

Caleb went completely still, as his eyes widened.

He looked down, and then at Arian.

A horrified flush spread all the way to the tips of his ears.

“I—”

Arian waited.

Caleb opened his mouth but nothing came out.

Arian's mouth twitched as he barely kept himself from snorting in amusement.

Caleb finally scrambled backward, nearly tangling himself in the duvet in his haste to put some respectable distance between them.

Arian watched him retreat, his own composure slowly returning, although his pulse remained stubbornly faster than it had any right to be.

Caleb wrapped himself in the duvet and turned his back on Arian, hiding his red ears and the last remnants of his dignity.

Arian grinned wryly and looked toward the malfunctioning launcher that was mounted next to the bathroom door, vis-a-vis the bed.

At the same moment Caleb pointed accusingly at the device.

“That one is coming out!”

Arian glanced at the top of his still visible head, brown locks a mess.

“It is staying.”

“It tried to kill you!”

“It tried to protect you.”

“But it nearly shot my future husband!”

Arian paused.

Caleb seemed to realize what he had just said and froze under the covers, not even breathing.

Arian stared at him, surprised as well. Then, to give them both a little room to calm down, he turned around, no longer looking.

“Goodnight, Caleb.”

The Prince did not respond.

The room had returned to its previous stillness, although it no longer felt quite the same as it had before. The faint mechanical hum of the devices continued from the walls, the curtains stirred occasionally in the cool night air, and somewhere beyond the thick windows a distant guard called something to another before the estate settled back into silence.

Arian lay on his back, staring into the darkness.

He could still feel it.

The brief, absurdly soft contact of Caleb's lips against his.

It had lasted no more than a heartbeat, perhaps even less, and yet Arian's mind seemed determined to replay it with an irritating clarity that bordered on deliberate cruelty. He remembered the warmth, the startled stillness that had followed, the way Caleb had looked at him afterward with his face slowly turning red, as though the Prince himself could hardly believe what had just happened.

Arian closed his eyes.

It had been an accident.

Nothing more.

There was no reason whatsoever for Arian to still be thinking about it several minutes later.

He opened his eyes again.

Caleb was lying beside him.

That was, somehow, the more difficult fact to process.

The Prince had finally stopped fidgeting, apparently exhausted enough that even his endless supply of commentary had run dry. His breathing had gradually slowed, becoming deep and even, and the faint tension that had lingered in his face throughout the evening had disappeared completely.

Arian turned his head slightly.

In the darkness, Caleb looked younger.

Softer.

Without the smirk, without the clever remarks and theatrical indignation, without the polished mask he wore whenever he wanted the world to believe that nothing could touch him, there was something almost disarmingly peaceful about him.

Arian watched him for several seconds.

Then longer.

He had expected Caleb to be troublesome even in sleep.

After the performance of the last half hour, Arian had anticipated more movement, more restless shifting, perhaps an occasional complaint about the weapons surrounding him or some sleep-muttered insult directed at his host.

Instead, Caleb was an impossibly perfect sleeper.

He did not toss. He did not turn. He did not speak or snore.

He simply lay there, utterly still, breathing deeply and peacefully as though the evening had not contained an attempted assassination, a betrayal by someone he trusted, an accidental kiss, and a near-disaster involving one of Arian's security devices.

Arian frowned faintly. He had been expecting...

What, exactly?

He couldn't say.

Perhaps another restless night. Perhaps Caleb moving closer in his sleep.

Perhaps….something.

The thought made Arian's expression tighten.

*Something?* What sort of *something* had he expected?

For Caleb to seek him out unconsciously? For the Prince to somehow make his way into Arian's arms?

For him to wake and find Caleb's head against his shoulder, his breathing warm against Arian's throat?

Arian stared into the darkness wide-eyed, bewildered by his own thoughts.

This was absurd.

He had spent years training himself not to expect comfort from anyone, and now he was lying beside a sleeping prince and apparently feeling disappointed that the man was giving him precisely what he had asked for.

Silence. Stillness. Distance.

Arian turned his gaze toward the ceiling, frustrated.

He was being ridiculous.

Caleb was asleep.

That was good.

It meant he was finally resting, which was precisely what Arian had wanted.

And yet, for reasons Arian refused to examine, the space between them suddenly seemed larger than it had a few minutes earlier when Caleb still had something to say.

He shifted beneath the covers, careful not to disturb him, and settled back into the mattress.

He would sleep.

Eventually.

Preferably before his mind found another reason to make the night considerably more complicated than it already was.
