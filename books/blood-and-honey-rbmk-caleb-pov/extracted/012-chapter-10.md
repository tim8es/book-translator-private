## Chapter 10

Chapter Notes

Caleb had asked one of the guards in the hall near his room if they had equestrian jumps, and they’d told him to give them an hour to get everything set up for him. He tried not to pace, restless energy buzzing beneath his skin, so he stole a bottle of what he knew to be outlandishly expensive scotch from the bar and drank straight from the bottle as he waited. He didn’t want to get drunk — alcohol and horses didn’t mix — but he needed to take the edge off, at least. He just needed his racing mind to quiet for a while.

The guard from before found him and let him know the jumps had been prepared just as the sun dipped to kiss the horizon. He cut through the parlor and paused when he heard low voices on the terrace. He hadn’t seen Arian or Lucien since that afternoon, and he figured he should at least let Arian know where he was going.

He stopped in the doorway, face crumpling and pain lancing his chest, when he saw how closely they stood together. Arian was facing out over the balcony, and Lucien’s chest almost touched his shoulder, his hand resting on top of Arian’s on the bannister as he spoke directly into Arian’s ear.

Arian didn’t stop him.

Caleb knew that Arian didn’t like to be touched (he was usually the one doing the touching), especially not that intimately, so the sight threw him, and he felt his eyes burn. Before he could do something stupid like interrupt or make a rude fool of himself again, he spun on his heel and fled the room. He didn’t stop until he hit the stables, a chestnut brown thoroughbred already tacked and saddled and waiting for him. He untied the reins and guided the horse out into the ring before launching himself onto its back and urging the horse into a gallop.

He lost himself in the familiar ebb and flow of riding, trying not to focus on the twinges of his sore muscles or remember the reason for them. He pushed all of his anger and pain and shame aside and focused on the ring around him. It felt good to do something he loved, but he couldn’t fully enjoy it. Caleb was good at compartmentalizing when he needed to, but Arian had systematically broken down every barrier he had. He’d stolen Caleb, and then, in less than a month, claimed his heart, as well.

Caleb wondered if he was just a conquest to Arian, even as that small, stubborn voice called him a fool for daring to think it. He had no idea where they stood now. They’d slept together. Arian had killed for him. He’d saved his life more times than Caleb could count. He’d taken a bullet for him and shielded him from an explosion with his own body. But what did it all mean? Caleb was a means to an end, a way to escape a contract marriage with a woman Arian didn’t want. He was protecting his own interests, Caleb told his stubborn, bleeding heart, nothing more. The fact that Caleb had fallen for him, and fallen hard, was immaterial, and ultimately, not Arian’s problem. He didn’t owe Caleb anything, especially not his affections.

Caleb had decided that he could be what Arian needed him to be, a fake husband, and he cared enough for him to see it through. He didn’t care what it would do to his own heart, he lied to himself. He would stand at Arian’s side and play his role to perfection for as long as necessary. Perhaps they could even get past this and become friends, last night relegated to a heated memory, a one-time event, and nothing more. He just needed time to be angry and hurt, to put Arian back in his box in the very back of his mind. Part of him knew it was impossible. The rest of him stubbornly refused to listen.

He looked up as the horse whinnied loudly. Arian was sprinting towards him at full speed. He pulled the reins hard to the side, turning the horse so he wouldn’t ride headfirst into him. He was dragged out of the saddle and tumbled to the sand, trapped beneath Arian’s panting, shaking body with their foreheads almost pressed together. Arian stared at the horse for a long, tense moment, eyes blown wide, chest heaving, before his chin dropped down to Caleb’s shoulder and he let out a relieved sigh.

Caleb pushed until Arian backed off, and he scrambled to sit up, pushing his hands into the loose sand and glaring at Arian. What was he playing at? “What’s the matter with you?!” He demanded. They both could’ve been seriously hurt by his little stunt.

Arian shook his head, lips drawing into a tight line, before he reached out and pulled Caleb up onto his knees, his arms binding around him in a vice-tight embrace. Caleb had missed something; Arian was terrified. He clung to Caleb like he meant something, and Caleb felt vaguely like he was suffering from emotional whiplash. The intensity of the man crushing the air from his lungs now didn’t seem like the same man who’d been unwilling to answer him in the hall that afternoon.

Arian pulled back and rested his palms on Caleb’s biceps, eyes devouring every detail of Caleb’s face as if he might never see him again. “What?” Caleb asked breathlessly.

Arian brought his hands up to cradle Caleb’s face, kissing him, once, twice, slow and thorough. He was still trembling, breath unsteady and eyes wild when he pulled back to look at Caleb again. Caleb pushed against his chest, and Arian fell back on his knees in the sand. He needed space to think. Arian looked vulnerable and broken, terror tightening his face, but Caleb still had no idea what they were. Did it matter, he asked himself. He had feelings for Arian, potentially even loved him, but could he settle for this? This passion and need that drove them both to flirt and push and tease one another, that led Arian to kill and Caleb to lash out. Arian’s eyes shone with unshed tears, and Caleb knew they should talk. He knew he had missed something important.

He surged forward, hands grabbing either side of Arian’s neck and dragging him into a kiss that was rushed and messy and raw. There was so much going on in his head, too much, and the only thing that made sense was this, was them, burning in the space between their shared breaths. He shuffled forward on his knees, seeking to close the distance between them, and Arian kissed him back just as fervently, gripping at his coat and wrapping around his back until they overbalanced. Arian landed on his back in the sand, Caleb on top of him with one hand pushed into the ground to keep from collapsing onto him. They continued to devour one another, desperate hands and lips grabbing and pulling as they lay on the ground.

Arian rolled them over and went up to his knees, pulling Caleb with him before standing and helping him to his feet. The second Caleb rose, Arian was on him again, arms tight around his back and lips dragging the air from his lungs. They stumbled together toward the stables, never parting, until Caleb’s heel hit a hay bale and he fell back onto it.

Arian fell with him, crawling up between his legs, and Caleb hooked his heel behind Arian’s thigh. Arian latched onto Caleb’s neck, teeth and tongue mauling the skin and making Caleb groan. Caleb had to know, though. He needed answers before he gave himself fully over to the blinding need Arian drew from him. “Where’s your new best friend with benefits?” His voice was flayed open, the words rushed and a little angry.

Arian froze.

He pushed up onto his palms above Caleb, eyes dark but imploring. “There is nothing between us. He isn’t exactly the person I thought he was.” There was anger and betrayal in those green-grey eyes. At Caleb or Lucien?

The heat retreated at the thought of the blonde he’d seen brushed up against Arian’s side, and he grinned, dark and full of pain. Caleb was hurting, emotions spilling out of him to puddle at Arian’s feet, but he couldn’t stop himself from wanting to make Arian feel how he did. “Relax, you don’t owe me an explanation. The nature of our relationship is purely manipulative.” Even as the cruel words left him, he knew they were a lie. He could never see Arian as just a contract, not anymore, and after everything that had happened, he doubted that Arian saw him that way, either. “We use each other to get what we truly desire, which makes our contract marriage equally beneficial to both parties.” Lies, all of it. He couldn’t keep being hurt by this man, though, and his mercurial, ever-changing moods. He hated himself a little more with each word, but he had to protect himself. He couldn’t allow Arian to keep carving off vital pieces of his heart. “With whom you’re associated with romantically in your leisure time is no concern of mine.”

He’d hit his mark like the trick shot he was. Arian looked devastated. His eyes flicked back and forth between Caleb’s own. “Is that really what you think we are?” As if Caleb had any idea. Arian wouldn’t talk to him, wouldn’t answer when he asked that very question earlier. All he knew was that he had to push Arian back into the box in his mind, and if he had to be callous and cruel and lie to get his point across, he would, and then hate himself for it later.

“What else would we be?” Caleb scoffed. “Were you expecting us to fall madly in love?” Caleb hadn’t been, for sure. Hadn’t stopped it from happening, of course, but it certainly wasn’t the plan when he’d agreed. They’d even made rules. He’d made rules. And then he’d broken them. Now he was floundering to pick up the pieces of what was left.

“That night — everything that happened between us — means nothing to you?” Arian demanded.

“Oh, about that night.” The beauty of Caleb’s words were the truths that built the lies, making them hurt all the more. “Betrayals, gunfights, explosions. All adds up to some adrenaline rush. Besides, I'd never been with a man before.” He shrugged, tearing out the last jagged shards of his fractured heart and throwing them at Arian like daggers off his sharp tongue. “Curiosity got the better of me.”

Arian stood up, eyes dark and cold as he stared down at Caleb.* “‘Curiosity,’”* he repeated darkly.

Caleb sat up, adjusting the cuff of his coat so that he didn’t have to look into Arian’s eyes. “Pick up any tabloid, and you’ll find at least 20 stories about me.” Here he threw on his most dazzling, mischievous grin. “I’m *famously* mentally reckless; none of it ever *means* anything.” The grin fell as the truth of the words hit the air. Most of Caleb’s interactions were shallow, superficial. The power dynamic of him being a prince unbalanced any attempt he made at normalcy or intimacy. Nothing ever meant anything, except what he could do for the Crown.

Arian meant something, though. He meant more to Caleb than he’d ever thought possible, which was why he had to stop this from going too far. He wouldn’t survive if they did, and Arian pushed him away again. The grin Caleb offered up as he spoke was the most powerful weapon in the prince’s arsenal, flippant and unbothered, while hiding the turmoil of emotion he truly felt. He casually leaned against the wall beside the hay, crossing his ankles for good measure. For once, Arian’s emotions were written plain as day on his face. Caleb latched tightly to his mask in the wake of the pure devastation that paled Arian’s tanned skin. “But for what it’s worth, you are one hell of a kisser.” He offered as a parting shot, just to really drive the knife that much deeper and scatter the ashes of the bridge he’d just burnt to cinder.

There was cold fury radiating off of Arian, and Caleb flinched as his fist flew towards him. He heard the harsh thump of bones grinding against the wall beside his head, and a spike of adrenaline crashed through him. He’d done his job too well, it seemed. At least if Arian hated him, Caleb wouldn’t feel as bad about hurting him to keep them both from doing something they’d regret.

“I have zero tolerance when people play with my feelings,” Arian growled, voice cold and unyielding.

Caleb knew he was walking a dangerous line. If he pushed too far, Arian might just decide to kill him and be done with it, but he couldn’t hold his tongue now that he’d started. He couldn’t take it back, not now that the knife of his sharp tongue had left him bleeding out. “A man with blood on every finger talking about feelings. You do see the irony, right?”

His eyes tracked up Arian’s tense body, widening at the blood dripping into the hay from his shredded knuckles as his fist clenched at his side. Caleb knew that had to hurt. He’d done that to him. He’d pushed him to that point.

Arian gestured to a black stallion in a stall nearby. “That horse used to belong to my brother. Five years ago he betrayed the family, and it was my duty to take care of it. My father- because my father couldn’t do it, he loved him too much. And in that one moment of mercy, my brother killed him instead. So I shot my brother and became the youngest godfather the Conti family ever had.” Caleb’s breath caught. He’d known, on some level, that there was blood in Arian’s past that had led to him becoming godfather, but he never could’ve imagined what Arian had gone through, what he’d done. “You’re right. There is blood on my hands, family blood.” A cold smirk lifted one corner of Arian’s face. He was wearing the mask of the godfather now, untouchable and cruel, but the hurt still flashed in the depths of his eyes. “A man like me has no right to talk about feelings.”

Caleb dropped his eyes to the ground, ashamed. Arian shook his head and stormed off, leaving Caleb with his thoughts. “What have I done?” He whispered miserably to himself.

Caleb had no idea how long he sat there against the hay bales. He thought he was doing the right thing, pushing Arian away. They couldn’t get hurt if they didn’t get involved. They couldn’t get hurt if they kept things professional. They couldn’t get hurt if they followed the rules.

It was too late for that. Caleb knew it. And he’d hurt Arian anyway. He’d lashed out with fangs and daggers and torn any chance of whatever he possibly could’ve had with the man to bloody pieces. Now all that was left was a hole dented into the stable wall and blood drying in the hay.

Arian wasn’t the type of man to forgive, and Caleb understood it. He couldn’t afford the luxury of second chances; it was why he’d been so quick to reproach Caleb for sparing Paul the second time. Second chances got you hurt. Second chances got you killed. Arian was a mafia godfather. He had no choice but to be ruthless. It sounded exhausting.

He wondered if Arian might have found peace with him, someone far removed from the darkness of his mafia life, but that was nothing more than a dream now. He’d thoroughly and intentionally destroyed any chance of becoming Arian’s solace. He had no idea what would happen next, no idea how they would act going forward, but he knew the teasing smiles and flirtatious banter were a thing of the past.

“Your Highness,” came a voice from the shadows to Caleb’s left, and he briefly wondered if he was hallucinating.

Paul stepped into the light, and Caleb felt his heart rate pick up. Arian had let the man go, he knew, when Caleb had spared him from being shot in the paddock weeks ago. He looked healthy and whole in a navy blue suit.

“What are you still doing here?” Caleb asked, still not convinced he wasn’t imagining things.

Paul held out a small booklet. “Your passport.” Caleb took it, shock furrowing his brows as Paul explained. “Prince Andrew has made enemies over the years. I’ve secretly brought them together. They’re ready to welcome you home, Your Highness.”

A pang of longing sliced through Caleb’s chest.

Home.

A month ago, he would’ve described it as an opulent palace filled to the brim with his family and courtiers and guards. Now, he knew they’d betrayed him, sold him, and used him as a scapegoat for the sake of their own image. Even still, he missed his family. He missed his sisters. Andrew, and honestly, his Father, he could do without, but he couldn’t imagine that his sisters had anything to do with everything that had happened. He wondered what they knew, if anything. And now here was Paul, offering him a chance to return to that life, to the palace and the grandeur and the people he loved. His chest was already sore with emotion from his fight with Arian, and it gave a sharp twinge.

Arian.

What was Caleb to do about Arian?

“I know you have every reason not to trust me,” Paul said, cutting off Caleb’s musing, “but you spared my life. From now on, I swear I’ll serve you until my last breath.”

Andrew held the life of Paul’s mother in his hands, Caleb knew, and yet here he was, pledging his devotion to Caleb. “Why would you do this?”

“I’ve known you for years. You’re clever, decisive, you care about people. You’re the one who should be the next king.” Paul sounded so sure that it threw Caleb somewhat. To have that kind of faith in him, especially when he himself was currently so unsure of what he wanted and what to do, was overwhelming. “Andrew can threaten my life, but he doesn’t deserve my respect and loyalty. Tomorrow night, at eight, a ship will be waiting at the dock to take you home.” Caleb looked down at the passport in his hands and then back up at Paul. “Sleep on it, Your Highness. Tomorrow at eight.” His secretary bowed and then disappeared back into the shadows, leaving Caleb with his thoughts once more.

He didn’t even know where to begin.
