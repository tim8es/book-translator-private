## Chapter 4

The mask of the aloof, unaffected royal accompanied Caleb into the study half an hour later, a perfectly tailored, cream colored suit his armor. He leaned casually against the solid oak desk, Arian in a sharp, deep blue suit and matching tie at his side. The Arian that stood beside him, though, was not the same one that had dared and teased him in a broom closet that evening. This was the ruthless godfather of the notorious Conti mafia family.

Regardless of the tension buzzing through his veins with no outlet, Caleb knew he needed all of his born and bred tricks in hand. Someone had made an attempt on the life of a mafia godfather. Now, Caleb would bear witness to the consequences of that. He knew, logically, that Arian led a life of crime, but knowing there were skeletons in his closet and watching him put them there were two entirely different things.

Arian flicked his chin up and the doors opened, admitting two of his guards with a man being dragged, bound and hooded, between them. Caleb spared a moment to be grateful that his own abduction had been far gentler than the treatment of the beaten, bloodied man brought before them. Then again, he hadn’t attempted to murder anyone or blown up their house. “We found one alive.” One of the guards spoke as they deposited their prisoner on his knees before Arian. The other handed the godfather a slip of paper.

Arian’s expression grew taut, eyes like shards of ice as he glared down at the bound man. Caleb uncrossed his ankles and took a step towards Arian. “What is it?” He asked with a cajoling smile, the smile he usually offered the palace kitchen staff in exchange for sneaking him ice cream or extra pudding.

“I think… they came for you,” Arian told him, and Caleb’s smile fell to shock as he took the note Arian held out to him. His eyebrows furrowed as he read the three simple words written on the paper.

***Kill Prince Caleb***

He looked back up at Arian and tried to hide the instinctual fear roiling in his gut. He’d thought Arian had been the target and he himself was no more than collateral damage. In actuality, Arian really had taken a bullet meant for him. He swallowed down his panic and guilt as Arian spoke. “You have been exposed. Someone gave away your whereabouts.”

Arian snapped his fingers, and the hood was yanked from the prisoner’s head, followed swiftly by a kick to the shoulder by one of the guards. The man went sprawling onto his side, eyes darting around now that he could see. Caleb felt like he had been punched in the gut. How many betrayals could he handle in one day? “Paul?” The name fell from his lips in shock.

“Your Highness,” the brown-haired man gasped.

“Why Paul?”

“It’s your brother, sir. He paid those men to kill you.” Paul rushed to explain, his voice trembling with fear.

Andrew. The very same brother whose actions had led Caleb to be in Arian’s house to begin with. The aloof mask was in tatters at Caleb’s feet on the wings of another betrayal by someone he trusted, so he grasped for whatever he could to keep his hands from shaking. “Making his younger brother the scapegoat wasn’t enough, I suppose. Now he wants me dead.” His tone came out far steadier than he had expected, and he held tight to his rage to ground himself. He strode forward, leaning down into Paul’s space. “Does he honestly think I’m plotting against him for the crown?”

“Please do forgive me, sir!” Paul begged, eyes unable to meet the ire bright in Caleb’s own. “You know what your brother is capable of. I had no choice!”

Caleb stood back to his full height, and Arian stepped forward. “What about him?” He gestured to Paul, sobbing and curled into a ball on the floor. “Bury him alive?”

Caleb was surprised that Arian had stayed silent for so long, doubly so that he was asking what Caleb thought a fitting punishment would be for the betrayal. It touched something inside of him that Arian offered him that leeway and respect. He could’ve taken the attack against his home personally, regardless of the intended target, but he was granting the prince justice for the slight against him.

“Please don’t. Your Highness, I have family!”

Caleb scowled down his nose at his former secretary. He was hurt, he was surprised, but more than anything, he was pissed off. How dare Andrew try to have him killed? How dare Paul betray him and act as an accessory to his murder? He knew he would be justified, in the eyes of the people in that room, at least, if he did take Arian’s suggestion of burying Paul alive. And yet, there was history there.

Paul had served as Caleb’s private secretary for as long as he could recall. He was a constant shadow in Caleb’s life and served as a sounding board and an accomplice on countless occasions. He’d put up with the prince’s antics, even helped to mollify the damage of the more outlandish ones. Caleb knew he wasn’t a bad man. Andrew was a force of nature. He’d never seen anyone come out of a battle of wits with his brother unscathed. More than likely, Paul had been coerced. Still, he could have said something, warned Caleb somehow.

“Lock him up.” Caleb finally said, and Arian snapped his fingers. The guards flanking Paul picked him up, replaced the black hood over his face, and began to drag him from the study as he begged.

“Please, sir, please!” He yelled, but Caleb just watched. The worst of his immediate anger had burned off, but the betrayal seeped into his bones. He could feel Arian’s stare on the side of his face, but he didn’t meet his eyes. He didn’t know what Arian would see if he did.

What an absolutely hellish day, he thought to himself, staring out the window at the smoke curling into the night sky from the now-extinguished fire on the hillside.

As soon as he returned to his new room, Caleb went straight for the shower. He didn’t think he could wash away everything that had happened over the course of the day, but he could feel the itch of dust in his curls and tension coiled into his muscles from being thrown around so much. The shower was massive. Off-white marble swathed the floor and walls of the space, including a built-in bench along the back wall, and four massive rain shower heads angled down from the ceiling. He fiddled with the knobs for a moment to get the water set to the temperature he wanted before he stripped and tossed his clothes into a tall hamper by the sink.

Stepping under the almost scalding hot spray was enough to leak most of the physical tension from his overwrought muscles. He almost moaned aloud at the sheer pressure of the water beating down on his chest and back. It felt amazing, like the shower heads were massaging away everything that had happened that day. His mind, however, was not so easily mollified. He didn’t even know where to begin to process everything.

This morning he had been on the Crown’s private jet, with not a care in the world, ready to set Sicily ablaze with his particular blend of mischief and boyish charm. He’d known that Paul was more twitchy than usual that day; had his shadow known what was about to happen? He must’ve. He’d been gone in a blink after handing Caleb his passport in the terminal. He’d then been surrounded, blindfolded, and carted off to who knew where in the Sicilian countryside. He’d met Arian. And within two minutes of meeting, the man had firmly cemented himself in Caleb’s brain.

Arian Conti, godfather of the Conti crime family, Italian mafia royalty in his own right. At first, Caleb had thought him brash, unsociable, with no mind for how his actions would be received. Caleb realized now that Arian simply didn’t care what others thought. He was unapologetic, straightforward, and had a simple kind of honesty to him that Caleb hadn’t expected. Arian carried himself with an almost quiet air of power; he didn’t need to scream to be taken seriously. He also had a dry sort of humor to him that both shocked and delighted Caleb. Arian Conti was not afraid to spar back and forth with Caleb, quip for quip. He tested him, pushed him, dragged him out of his comfort zone, and peeled back his masks like it was effortless. Arian had somehow, with a single glance, found a chink in Caleb’s impeccably tailored armor, and sank his way into Caleb’s very soul.

He’d told him of the deal the Crown had struck and then given him time and space to process. Dinner had been… enlightening. Caleb was not a simpering damsel, willing and waiting for a dark knight to set her free. If he was to be stuck with Arian for the foreseeable future, he was going to do things his way. Arian had matched him every step of the way. Even when Caleb pushed too hard and Arian’s temper got the better of him, his grip on Caleb’s throat had been a warning, not a punishment. He’d allowed Caleb to press a blade to his throat without consequences.

Caleb felt free around him — free to be himself, to be charming and irreverent and mischievous and pushy and impulsive and even a little arrogant and selfish. Arian tested his wits and wasn’t afraid to push back against Caleb’s antics. He offered him honesty instead of hiding behind his title. He gave Caleb the facts and allowed the prince to form his own conclusions. He offered the marriage contract, a mutually beneficial agreement, and hadn’t pushed when Caleb had been unwilling to decide right that moment. He’d let him walk away and think.

Later, after Caleb’s striptease, he’d thrown him to his bed and wrapped a hand around his throat. He’d growled his annoyance in puffed breaths across Caleb’s lips. He’d tested Caleb’s resolve, teased him back with a sharp sort of edge, but not pushed too far.

He’d saved Caleb’s life. Thrice.

He’d taken a bullet meant for him. He’d guided Caleb through the house amidst a spray of gunfire without question. He’d shielded Caleb’s body with his own from a grenade to keep him safe. And when Caleb had joked and teased him to draw him back from the pain, he’d met him with fire and intensity that had Caleb ready to burn.

Caleb was quite certain that, if the guard had not interrupted, Arian was going to kiss him. He wasn’t sure why that thought didn’t have him panicking. Caleb had never considered himself to be attracted to men, had never even considered it, but he’d also never met a man like Arian Conti before. Under the hot spray of the shower, he let his mind wander.

Arian probably kissed like he did everything else: intense, unyielding, consuming. Caleb found part of himself wanting that, but he pushed that part far, far down. In reality, he’d known the man for less than a day; he couldn’t afford to catch feelings for him, despite the attraction he felt burning low in his gut. In all actuality, he knew very little about the man. That didn’t stop a bolt of desire from running up Caleb’s spine. He pushed that down, too, and tried to clear his mind. He needed to focus on a strategy going forward. If he was to survive this arrangement with Arian, he needed to keep his wits.

By the time Caleb stepped out of the shower, he felt more himself than he had all day. Some of the turmoil in his mind had settled, and he had a plan, albeit the vague idea of one, at best. He’d sleep on it and reevaluate in the morning. It wouldn’t do to be impulsive about these things. He had no idea where he stood with the Crown now, and if he acted too rashly, he may have no quarter to turn to after. He dried off and wrapped the towel around his waist, fluffing his wet hair back out of his face as he stepped out toward the bedroom.

And then he froze.

Arian was perched on the end of his bed, casually examining a gun, one leg hitched up on the duvet and the other anchored on the floor at the end of the bed. He looked the picture of dangerous ease. Fear and arousal quickly lanced through Caleb in equal measure, and he swallowed hard. Arian wasn’t going to shoot him, was he? No, Caleb concluded, not after everything.

He leaned his bare shoulder against the doorframe and prayed the towel slung low around his waist would stay in place. His arms crossed over his chest, and he let a small smirk curl onto his lips. “I am aware this is your house, but barging into your guest bedroom is wildly unacceptable.” Arian said nothing as he stood up and pulled several small objects from the open case on the bed behind him and affixed them to the walls around the room. Caleb watched him with carefully practiced ease. “What are those?”

“Protections.” Arian placed another near the window. “Fully automatic launchers.” He turned back toward the bed. “Make sure you sleep well every night.”

“Splendid,” Caleb quipped. “If one of those things goes rogue like Paul did, I will end up dead peacefully in my sound sleep.” There was a harsh edge to his voice at the reminder of the latest betrayal. Arian pulled a folded dark blue robe from the case and then latched it shut. “And why did you bring your pajamas, may I ask?” Caleb’s eyebrows furrowed tightly together in confusion and no small amount of dawning suspicion.

“The answer is simple: I’m sleeping with you tonight.”

“Excuse me?!” Caleb’s reply was indignant, the words forced out of him as he stomped down on the rush of heat that coursed through him at the mental images his brain supplied of them in bed together.

Arian couldn’t be serious.

“You are only perfectly safe under my watch,” Arian stated flatly, undeterred by Caleb’s tone.

“How presumptuous!” Caleb scoffed indignantly, taking two steps towards Arian, gesturing with his hands as he spoke. “Shouldn’t you at least consult my opinion first?”

“Unnecessary,” Arian said dismissively. He grabbed the robe from the bed and walked toward the bathroom, circling behind Caleb. “My house. My rules.”

Caleb tracked his movement in disbelief. The audacity of this man was astounding. “Hey!”

He lost.

Caleb probably should’ve known it was a losing battle before he even tried, but it was a matter of pride at the time. He didn’t need a babysitter. He figured that in a secluded estate patrolled constantly by a small army of Arian’s private guard, he was safe as he could be. The attack earlier was an anomaly, right? Or was Arian’s guard force truly that useless? Either way, his vehement protests fell on deaf ears until he found himself lying ramrod straight on his back on the bed, Arian mere inches to his right. His hands clutched the blankets as he tried to fight with his mind to not stray into the depths he’d spent so much effort pushing down. The sharp bolts of lust he felt earlier had mixed with his indignant irritation at being treated like he needed constant supervision and had him in a foul mood.

He felt like he had to say something, anything, to break the awkward silence, or neither of them were going to be getting any sleep, so he tried one last time. “I don’t feel comfortable sleeping next to someone I barely know.”

Arian didn’t rise to the bait. He just continued to stare at the ceiling above them, unbothered. “Then don’t sleep. Go read a book.”

Irritation burned through Caleb’s veins and loosened his tongue. “You must have been told this before,” he rolled until he was propped up on his elbow, facing Arian, “that you are one incredibly rude person. Or did your mafia family forget to teach you manners while busy slaughtering innocent people?” Even as he said it, he knew Arian was far more a gentleman than he was giving him credit for. He’d been a near-perfect host so far, but his determination to decide for Caleb what was in the prince’s best interest was too far. Caleb was a prince; he wasn’t used to being ordered around so freely. Not even the special agents specifically tasked with his personal protection had felt the need to be *in his bed with him* in order to do their jobs. It was absurd!

Arian took a deep breath like he needed it to keep his composure, eyes never leaving the ceiling. “I lost my family when I was young. One more word, I’ll send you to meet them.”

“I-“ Caleb cut off with a grunt as Arian surged up and rolled, hand once again tight around Caleb’s throat. One of his legs was between Caleb’s, trapping him in place, and he pressed him back down onto his back, eyes blazing dark. A beep from across the room distracted Caleb from freeing the moan that almost escaped at the press of Arian’s muscular thigh against him, and he realized it was the launchers Arian had installed earlier activating. “Watch out!” He yelled, pushing hard against Arian’s chest and rolling them both over as a projectile flew past the back of his head, too close for comfort.

They landed back in Arian’s original position, Caleb’s lips slanted down over Arian’s from the force of the quick maneuvering. His eyes flew open, a sea of dark green-grey eclipsing his vision. For one long second neither man moved. Caleb was pretty sure they weren’t even breathing. In the next second his brain caught up with what had happened, and he sat bolt upright, hand coming up to cover his mouth with a gasp. Arian’s eyes were wide, jaw dropped slightly in surprise. Caleb swallowed roughly around the sudden fog clouding his mind and tried to search for a way to break the tension before Arian thought better of his plans and decided Caleb was more trouble than he was worth. “What in God’s name just flew past us, do you suppose?” He was staring at Arian’s chest rising and falling, unable to meet the man’s eyes.

Arian blew out a breath through his nose in amusement, a close-lipped smile softening his features slightly as he chuckled darkly. “My apologies, but it looks like the launchers were activated for some reason.” Like Arian grabbing his throat and pinning him to the bed, *again*, Caleb thought bitterly.

“What kind of imbeciles programmed these?” His haughty tone made Arian’s eyebrows jump toward his hairline as he watched him. “They almost got me *killed!*” Had Caleb not pushed Arian out of the way, the projectile would’ve hit him, he knew, but even that thought just riled him up even more. How could the precautions put in place to defend Arian’s home not recognize the man himself, but rather see him as a threat?

“Well, the machines must think you’re jeopardizing your own safety,” Arian reasoned. He started to sit up, some of that intense, consuming hunger from earlier back in his eyes, and Caleb panicked.

He threw a hand up between them, his palm facing toward Arian like a barrier. “Don’t you dare come near me.”

Arian smiled, clearly too amused by everything, and it irritated Caleb’s panic even more. What exactly did he think was funny about yet another life or death experience? “Really? Want to hear a funny story?” He cocked his head just slightly to the side, eyes burning hot and intense. “You are still on top of me.”

Realization poured through Caleb like ice, and he looked down past Arian’s smirk, his bare chest peeking out of the folds of his robe, to where, yes, Caleb was sat astride his hips. Arian’s large hand rested over one of his thighs, and the heat of it felt like a brand through Caleb’s robe. The blush that Caleb had been fighting stole red across his face, making him feel overheated, as he realized what position he was in, and just how affected the kiss — which could barely even be considered a kiss at all, really — had made him. Here he was, sitting on top of Arian, half hard in nothing but his silk boxers and a robe, while in a bed he was meant to share with the man, while said man stared up at him with a knowing smile and heat dark eyes like he had absolutely not a care in the world. Caleb’s composure broke completely. With a huff, he threw himself back to his side of the bed and ripped the covers over top of himself, burrowing down beneath them with his back to Arian as his heart made a valiant effort to escape his chest entirely. So much for his plan. After a moment, he felt Arian roll to face away from him, their backs almost touching.

Without thinking, his fingers came up to his lips where they still tingled slightly from the scrape of Arian’s stubble rubbing across them. It wasn’t a kiss, not really, so why was it consuming his entire mind like a revelation? “Don’t overthink,” he ordered into the tense silence. He didn’t know if he was saying it to Arian or himself.

“Don’t overreact,” Arian’s smooth whisper replied.
