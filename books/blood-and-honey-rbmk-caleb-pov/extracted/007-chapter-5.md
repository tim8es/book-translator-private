## Chapter 5

Caleb slept restlessly for a few hours. It was late when they’d gone to bed to begin with, and the sun shining brightly through the large windows combined with the events of the night prevented him from relaxing enough to truly sleep. The phone Arian had given him buzzed on the side table, and he picked it up to find several notifications from an international news outlet.

***British Prince Caleb: Mentally Unstable and Caught in Gay Scandal***

Caleb jolted upright, breath coming quicker as he saw the candid picture of the night before. Whoever had taken it had managed to capture the exact moment of their lips colliding. It was definitely damning evidence, to be sure, and out of context it was even worse. Caleb had been the focus of his fair share of media scandals, usually involving alcohol or models or rumors about his relationship status if he so much as glanced at a woman, but this was new territory for him. The sound of Arian’s footsteps entering from the bathroom drew his attention.

He had gotten up before Caleb had awoken, and he was dressed in black dress slacks and a black button-up shirt. His hands were deep in his pockets, a carefully neutral expression on his face. “Have you seen the news?” Caleb asked, flipping the screen to show him.

“Way ahead of you. It was Paul. He escaped last night and took a photo of us together with a stolen phone. But luckily we have gotten him back into our control again just now.”

“We,” not “my,” Caleb noticed, as if Arian was including him in that statement. Caleb shook his head sadly. “He really is devoted to my brother.” The statement dug a little bit more at that pit of betrayal festering in his gut.

“Your brother is taking care of his mother’s medical bills,” Arian said, and Caleb’s hand tightened into a fist against the blankets. Leave it to Andrew to exploit those around him to get what he wanted, by any means necessary. “If Paul disobeys, his mother will die.”

Caleb briefly wondered how Arian knew this, before coming to the conclusion that he’d rather not know what methods were used to make Paul turn on Andrew. His brother was tireless in his relentless pursuit of Caleb’s demise, and it made his decision clear. The plan had started to form last night, but as Arian had already told him, he had but one choice. “Bring me the contract.”

“What contract?”

“I am about to make your dream come true.” He rose up onto his knees until he was eye to eye with Arian, a knowing smile curling across his lips as his voice dropped to a low purr. “We’re getting married.”

“Why the sudden change of heart?”

His smile grew, teeth shining brilliant white and dimples deepening. “It’s a real win-win,” he explained, taking a page from Arian’s book and leaning in until he could whisper the words right into the man’s ear. “You use my last name to escape the Caruso marriage.” He leaned back, sitting on his heels. “I exploit your mafia reputation to stay alive from my beloved older brother.”

A surprised smile revealed dimples of his own set in Arian’s cheeks, and he huffed out a breath. “Deal.”

A flash of realization stopped Caleb up short, and he raised a hand to stop them. “*But* we set terms. This stays strictly business. No catching feelings, and no sex.”

Arian’s smile only grew as he crowded into Caleb’s space. Caleb’s hands instinctively came up to brace against his chest, but he stopped, palms hovering in the air between them. “Relax,” Arian purred, “I’m not interested in your ass.”

Something about the flippant way he said it riled Caleb, as if the mere thought of Caleb’s ass was a joke. It wounded a bit of his pride, and he couldn’t stop the scowl that was bordering on a pout from showing, much to Arian’s apparent amusement. “Get dressed. We have business to take care of.”

+++

Crisp white pants and a thick camel brown suede sport coat over a light blue patterned button-up and beige paisley tie were Caleb’s armor of choice for the day. It was overcast and a cold wind had blown in, chasing away the sun and heat from the previous day. Arian led him down to a large fenced-in paddock toward the back of the property where a few of his men waited beside a chestnut brown thoroughbred. Paul was tied to the horse’s back, looking even bloodier and more beaten than he had the night before. A twinge of guilt hit Caleb. He’d once thought of Paul as a friend, or as close to a friend as a prince could have anyway.

“Please. Please don’t do this,” Paul begged Arian as he approached, before he turned his gaze to Caleb. “Your Highness! Please have some mercy! I have family!”

Caleb stayed silent, unable to meet Paul’s pleading eyes. “In a moment we’ll see what’s faster: my bullet or your horse.” Arian’s voice was cold, once again settled into the role of the ruthless godfather. Caleb was beginning to be able to tell the difference by ear alone.

“No. No, please! Prince Caleb, I’ve served you for my whole life! I swear, I won’t do it again! Please, say something! Tell him to stop!” Arian approached and slapped the horse’s hindquarter. It jolted and took off, Paul still screaming on its back.

Caleb watched, his breaths coming quick and ragged, and he felt Arian at his side. He looked down, and Arian was holding out a revolver. “This son of a bitch betrayed you twice. Finish him yourself,” he ordered.

Caleb looked back out at the man he’d once called friend and felt himself start to shake. When he looked back down, the revolver was in his hands, though he had no memory of reaching for it. He gripped it tightly to hide how much his hands were shaking, his breaths growing more ragged at the thought of what he might be about to do. The godfather had given him an order, one that Caleb knew instinctively was not to be ignored, but he didn’t know if he could. He pulled back the hammer, but he hesitated to aim.

Arian’s hand came to his side, his chest pressed close to the back of Caleb’s shoulder, as his other hand settled over Caleb’s own around the gun. “I’ll teach you how.” His low voice washed over Caleb as he steadied his hand. “Hold your breath. Arm, gun, eyes on the man. One straight line. Steady.” Caleb felt anything but. He was trembling in Arian’s arms, mind awash with anxiety and panic. “Tighten your finger and trigger pull.”

A sense of déjà vu hit Caleb. The last time he had held a gun with the intent to kill anything larger than a pheasant, there had also been a beaten, bloodied, screaming person involved. The paddock fled his vision, and he was transported back to a snowy forest. He couldn’t remember how old he was, maybe 10 or 11, but he could still smell the sharp bite of the cold pine-tinged air mixed with the metallic tang of blood and the sweet, cloying scent of honey.

His father would be furious if he knew Caleb had slipped his guard detail to explore the woods around the estate they were staying at alone. He’d been warned of wolves and bears, at the very least, but he was young and tired of his seemingly endless lessons. He craved the fresh air and adventure that the forest posed. He’d been smart enough to bring his rifle with him, though. He wanted adventure, not death. He’d seen his brother, Andrew, and another blonde boy he didn’t know go running from the woods, cackling to themselves, as he snuck closer, but he just hid so they wouldn’t see him. Andrew would certainly run straight to Father if he knew; he loved to get Caleb in trouble. Once they passed, he crept his way into the forest.

The cold wind blew past him, carrying the scent of pine and… honey? Caleb followed the smell, and as he got closer, he heard small grunts of pain, followed by a scream. He ran toward the noise and saw a young boy, his age or maybe a little older, bound on the ground in a clearing. His face was bloodied and caked with dirt, his clothes tattered and torn. Who was he, Caleb wondered, and then he heard it. Off to his right, a low grunt drew his attention, and his heart raced when he saw a large bear approaching from the trees.

It was headed straight for the boy.

Caleb didn’t think, he just brought his rifle around and took aim. He took a breath and held it, just as he’d been taught. His feet firmly planted on the fallen log, he released his breath and pulled the trigger. The shot echoed off the trees, sending birds flying and cutting off the boy’s terrified scream. The bear collapsed onto its side, and Caleb slung the strap of the rifle over his shoulder. Slowly, he trudged his way closer to the boy. When he was close enough, he knelt down and pulled his silver hunting knife from its sheath at his side. He used it to cut the boy’s hands free and smiled down at him. He set down the knife and reached out his hand.

Arian sighed in his ear, jolting Caleb back to the present, the scent of blood and honey still cloying in his nose. “Forget it. You’re not made for this.” He stepped away, and Caleb suddenly knew what he would do. The ghost of that day fresh in his mind, he stopped shaking almost entirely. His focus clarified, and he tightened his grip on the gun, planted his feet, led his target, and fired.

Paul fell to the ground, and Caleb lowered the gun. Across the paddock, Paul brought his hand up, gripping the frayed rope where the bullet had sliced cleanly through it and sobbed. “Thank you. Your Highness, thank you!”

Arian turned and rounded to stand a few feet behind the prince. “Nice shot. Faultless technique. Pity you have a soft spot for your enemies.”

Caleb turned to face him, surprised by the glint of pride that shone in Arian’s eyes. He tossed the gun back, and Arian caught it without breaking eye contact. “Being royal means one has to master pistol shooting before adolescence. I am better with rifles.”

Arian huffed out a breath, a small smile tugging at his lips. “I may start to find you less boring.”

Caleb saw the comment for the attempt to break the tension that it was, and he appreciated it. “Praised by my only Italian mob friend. I may start to find you less vulgar,” he countered.

Maintaining eye contact proved to be too much, so he dropped his gaze, allowing himself a moment to settle his mind, both from the events of the morning and the unbidden rush of memories.

One of the guards approached and held out a black card to Arian. “Boss, an invitation from the Caruso family.” Arian took the card and read it before glancing up at Caleb. Caleb met his gaze, wondering what he saw.

“There’s to be a gala in two weeks. Will you attend?” There was something carefully guarded in Arian’s expression as he asked.

Caleb offered a small lopsided grin as a token of peace and nodded. “Of course. What good is a fake husband if he doesn’t accompany you to fancy parties involving women you were supposed to marry?”
