A lightning bolt of confusion and shock blasted through the prince at that declaration, causing his breath to escape him in a huff. This was absurd and nothing like he expected or had mentally prepared for on the ride over. If he was being honest with himself, this entire experience was far… gentler than he expected. The hands of the men who took him were guiding instead of cruel. The ride was almost pleasant, lush even, in the luxurious seats of the Rolls-Royce. There was a very prominent lack of any kind of restraints used, no rope or cuffs having touched his wrists. Even his apparent host, despite the danger Caleb instinctively felt, had been controlled and relaxed. If Caleb was honest, he would’ve expected something a little more… brutal. Bloody.

That’s when the words finally hit him, a mere fraction of a second after Arian’s declaration, and a scoff wheezed from his lips. “Husband?” This had to be some elaborate prank; it just had to be. There was no possible way that an Italian crime boss was proposing marriage to the rebellious youngest son of the British royal family after having his passport destroyed and abducting him from the airport as soon as he landed. It had to be a setup. It was the only explanation. Whichever of his siblings was behind this had bragging rights for the next decade for this one, except…

That was annoyance flickering across Arian’s face.

He took a deep breath as if to center himself, eyes closing briefly, before he stepped away from Caleb and out the open doors he’d been facing when Caleb was brought into the room.

“Wait!” Caleb called out as confusion lashed through him. “Hey, wait!” He followed after Arian, onto a back patio space that was opulent and tasteful, with a long lap pool off to their right. Arian didn’t even slow his steps, causing Caleb to jog to catch up. “*Husband?*” He repeated, his tone this time more confused than disbelieving amusement. “Please tell me I heard that wrong. This must be some kind of a prank. I’m a man!”

Later, much later, in the isolated safety of his room, Caleb would wonder to himself why his gender was the first and most prevalent excuse that so readily came to him. Not the fact that he didn’t know Arian, or that the man had just told him he was a mob boss, or any of another thousand perfectly logical reasons. No, he couldn’t marry Arian because he himself was a man. Brilliant.

His jaw audibly clicked shut when his brain caught up to what his eyes were seeing. Arian, with his back to Caleb, was taking off his jacket.

And then his shirt.

The mischievous grin Caleb was wearing like armor during his tirade slipped from his lips as he stared in something like envy at Arian’s impossibly sculpted back. Caleb himself was in phenomenal shape, but Arian? The man was all lean, functional strength and sharp lines bound by smooth swathes of muscle. His broad, tanned shoulders sloped inward into a tapered waist, and his perfectly tailored black trousers did absolutely nothing to hide the musculature of his ass or thighs either. A surge of sudden heat shot through Caleb, and he couldn’t help but stare.

Arian turned his head over his shoulder to smirk back at the royal, and Caleb forced his eyes to flick up to meet his gaze, feeling caught. “I’m aware of that.” He said, simply.

A beat of charged silence hung between them as Caleb scrambled to reorder his thoughts, Arian’s back flashing like an afterimage in his mind’s eye and scrambling him. He fell back onto his most constant standby and slipped easily into the role of the self-assured, aloof noble. “I’m a member of the British royal family.” He countered, his trademark mischievous grin slicing across his face like armor.

“I’m aware of that, too.” Arian sounded nonplussed, almost playful, watching Caleb scrabble to keep calm.

Caleb switched tactics. He hated being put on his back foot — a lifetime of fencing had taught him to be quick on his feet, both literally and metaphorically, but he felt off-kilter and unsteady. Arian’s smirk certainly wasn’t helping things. He’d turned fully to face Caleb head-on, and it was a matter of stubbornness that prevented Caleb from glancing down at smooth, sculpted pectorals and crisply defined abs that matched the powerful lines and curves of Arian’s back. “Kidnapping a prince is a felony.”

At this, the smirk grew, and Arian stalked towards him. He didn’t stop until he was once again toe to toe with Caleb, even closer than before. He was a few inches taller than the prince, and it gave him the effect of looming over him as he leaned in, eyes intense and lips parting slightly before he spoke. Caleb’s eyes widened, but he otherwise didn’t move. It was unusual for the prince to be so off-kilter; he almost always had a quip or a one-liner, but now he was nothing more than a deer in headlights. He also found his eyes wanting to trace every plane and curve and sharp edge of the man’s face in appreciation, like one would a fine piece of art. This close up, he could see the grey-blue striations in Arian’s light-dark green irises as he met Caleb’s own hazel eyes.

“Who said this was a kidnapping?” His voice trickled over him like he knew some secret Caleb didn’t, and Caleb felt his breath hitch. He desperately hoped that Arian didn’t notice. The last thing he wanted to do was show the man any kind of weakness he could exploit. When no response came to the prince’s lips, Arian’s smirk grew into a dark mirror of Caleb’s own mischievous grin, and the sound of a belt buckle clicking loudly in the silence between them snapped his attention downward. Down past solid, smooth pectorals, past immaculately defined abdominals, past a trail of dark hair that almost pointed like an arrow to where the man’s hands were currently freeing his belt.

Caleb felt his heart in his throat, thoughts flying through his head as his heart fluttered hummingbird-fast in his chest. In this game of chicken they’d inadvertently started, Arian wasn’t backing down. The prince suddenly took several steps back, hands coming up in a placating gesture, but also as a barrier. “Whoa! Whoa! Whoa! Whoa! That’s too much!” He could feel his heart still flying too fast, but at least he felt more himself again, instead of caught in whatever tense middle space they’d been in a moment ago.

Arian said nothing, his smirk back on his face and in full effect. He turned slightly and leaned over to a deck lounger by the edge of the pool before facing Caleb again. He tossed a cell phone at him, and muscle memory alone allowed Caleb to catch it before it could crack on the stone beneath his feet. Confused, he glanced down at the screen, and a wash of cold dread poured over him. He didn’t even look up at the loud splash of Arian diving into the pool as he read.

Caleb felt his eyebrows draw tight as the news anchor told a story of how Caleb had been sent to Italy by the royal family following a brutal attack on a man. “A secure treatment facility,” the anchor had said before going on to explain that rumors were circulating that Caleb had long-standing mental health issues.

“They made me the scapegoat.” He murmured aloud. Icy betrayal sliced through his chest, and he looked up at the sound of splashing water as Arian stood upright in the pool and pushed his dripping, inky black hair back from his face.

“Your older brother Andrew beat a man almost to death while not quite being himself. You see, that’s a felony in Italy, and quite a scandal, concerning that unlucky man is associated with one of the most important Sicilian families.” Arian told him, confirming what he was already beginning to suspect. His father had called it an international incident, one that Caleb’s particular brand of mischievous, flirtatious attitude might keep out of the news, so he’d been put on a plane. Despite being several feet below him in the pool, Caleb had the distinct feeling that Arian was still towering over him. His voice was smooth, assured. He was not sharing his opinion, Caleb realized, he was relaying simple fact. Those intense green-grey eyes kept him pinned in place as he continued, “I’m the only person that can make it go away. And you’re the price.”

A desperate, disbelieving scoff escaped Caleb’s lips. “The Crown… *sold* me?”

Arian pushed himself up onto the edge of the pool and walked towards Caleb, trailing water, before he stopped less than a foot away. “You have one choice now.” A long finger came up beneath Caleb’s chin, tilting his face towards Arian’s own as his voice dropped slightly and he stared into the prince’s wide, hazel eyes. “Marry me. Be my partner in crime.” Some of the amusement was back in his eyes, and Caleb felt as if this was some kind of a test, one he certainly hadn’t studied for.

Another scoff, almost a laugh, left Caleb. “*Marry you?* I’d rather die.”

The cold metal of a gun barrel pressed into his forehead before he could blink. All traces of amusement left Arian’s face. *This* was the godfather of the Conti family: ruthless, cunning, unyielding. He stared straight through Caleb like he wasn’t buying the bravado the prince had displayed. “You think I won’t kill you?”

Unbidden, Caleb’s eyes followed a stray water droplet as it tracked down the side of Arian’s neck before cutting down his smooth chest. Caleb was hopeless to stop an instinctive swallow, his mouth suddenly dry at the threat of death looming close. (It certainly wasn’t because he had the absolutely insane urge to lean forward and lick back up the path the droplet had taken, definitely not.) Caleb was an impeccably educated prince, incredibly quick-witted, and had a particularly keen eye for strategy and manipulation, though, so as soon as his brain turned over the question, he brought his gaze up to meet icy green-grey and met Arian’s glare without hesitation. “Maybe you will,” he admitted, his smirk growing into a lesser version of his trademark charming, mischievous grin, “but I don’t think so.” The small smirk that had begun to tip up the corners of Arian’s mouth dropped into a tight frown. “At least, not after you went through quite a bit of trouble to get your hands on me.”

Arian’s silence remained unchanged, but it gave Caleb enough room for bravado, or stupidity – he’s unsure which caused his hand to slowly reach up and grasp Arian’s wrist. He barely pushed down before the gun dropped to Arian’s side, and the man let out a small huff of amusement. “You’re right.” He stepped into Caleb’s space until the royal had to clench one hand into a fist to keep himself from taking a step back to create some distance between them. His lip ghosted across Caleb’s cheekbone as he whispered low in the prince’s ear. “It would be a shame to kill you so soon.”

Caleb looked down as he felt a hand trace down the front of his torso, just barely hard enough to feel the pressure through the fine material of his suit. His lips parted slightly in shock, grin long gone. The hand dipped just below the overlapped folds of fabric at the center of his abdomen and then, quick as a viper, Arian grabbed him by the hips and tugged him into his bare, wet chest.  One of his thighs pressed hard against Caleb’s own until he was held tight against Arian’s water-soaked hip. A gasp rushed out of Caleb as their bodies collided and his hands flew up to grip tightly at Arian’s biceps, though to steady himself or push him away, he did not know. The harsh metal of the gun’s grip dug into the back of his left hip as Arian’s voice purred dangerously in his right ear. Caleb stayed rooted to the spot and held tighter to the man’s arms in a bid for some kind of anchor. The incredibly mixed signals and manhandling had Caleb’s body on edge, not knowing how to react, and wasn’t that a new feeling in and of itself. “I should lock you away in my bedroom first.” Arian’s nose brushed his cheekbone as he pulled back, barely. He looked up through his dark lashes and watched Caleb’s panting face as the prince floundered. “And make you suffer properly.”

And just as quickly as it had started, he was gone. Arian strode back towards the house, leaving Caleb panting and shaken on the patio.

+++

Caleb didn’t know how long he stood on the patio. He just stared off into the middle distance at the ocean on the horizon as his thoughts ran wild. What was wrong with him? And what was it about Arian that had him set so firmly on his back foot? He couldn’t deny the electric buzz under his skin at the dangerous game they’d played with each other. Caleb lived for mischief and pushing boundaries, and Arian? He was quickly becoming the focus of Caleb’s considerable intellect. He knew it was probably unwise to goad the man, lest he do something drastic like follow through on the threats he so freely threw Caleb’s way, but Caleb had never had an overabundance of self-preservation.

Eventually, someone cleared their throat, and he turned to see one of the men from earlier standing a bit of a ways away. The man escorted Caleb to a room on the second floor that was to be his. Before he left, he told Caleb that dinner was at 8 and his presence was nonnegotiable, per Arian’s orders. A quick glance at the ornate clock on the bedside table told him it was just after 5, so he waited until the door shut behind the guard and took a deep breath to steady himself. He could still feel the ache in his hip from where the grip of the gun had dug into him as Arian held him. He idly wondered if it had bruised. There was a nervous kind of hum under his skin, so he decided to explore. He had three hours to kill and no desire to spend it cooped up in a room — albeit luxurious and tastefully decorated without being gauche or overbearing — the entire time until his presence was required.

He had no destination in mind, besides a desire to get a lay of the space and begin to build a mental map. He wandered the halls aimlessly, appreciating the art and decor that was expertly arranged throughout the various spaces. Arian’s home was massive, a sprawling mansion estate on several dozen acres, but the palace was much, much larger, so Caleb found himself unintimidated by the sheer number of rooms and halls. He found the staples, of course: bathrooms, bedrooms, a parlor, a billiard hall, a bar lounge, an impressively large kitchen attached to an even larger dining hall right beside it, and there was even a study-slash-library complete with two-story-tall built-in bookshelves with two rolling ladders to reach the volumes stowed higher up. Arian had good taste, it seemed. Caleb continued to wander until he was stopped by a guard and informed that dinner was to be served in 15 minutes.

+++

When Caleb backtracked his way to the dining hall he’d found earlier, he was met with the soft sounds of classical music. He was shocked when he opened the door and found a live orchestral band set up before a wall of open-air arches that let in the cool, ocean spray-tinged evening breeze. Across from the instrumentalists, the long table was set for two, side by side.

Caleb crossed the room with slow, deliberate ease and slid into the seat to Arian’s left without a word. He’d noticed the man’s presence as soon as he’d entered, and pointedly ignored it just as quickly.

The moment Caleb sat down, waitstaff appeared from the kitchen and poured fragrant, sweet-smelling wine. They then placed steaming plates of food in front of each of them before they vanished back through the door adjoining the kitchen. A large grilled steak took up most of the plate, the juices of which flowed across the entire surface to mingle with the accompanying vegetables and pomme puree. With just a glance, Caleb knew it was grilled rare, and he didn’t even bother to pick up his utensils to verify. He had no interest in eating that. Arian, however, had no such compunctions. He sliced into his steak with practiced ease, and Caleb was unsurprised that he was good with a knife. He assumed he’d have to be in his line of work.

Minutes passed with nothing but the scrape of silverware against porcelain and the orchestra to fill the silence. Finally, without looking up from his meal, Arian spoke in a low rumble. “If you’re planning to starve yourself under my roof, don’t expect anyone to bury you.” He then glanced at Caleb, who sat back in his chair, watching Arian out of the corner of his eye, with his arms stubbornly crossed.

A small laugh escaped the prince. “Interesting. No matter how refined the music is, it can’t quite hide the smell of criminals.” Gun oil and smoke, leather and suede, just like in the car. He realized now that the smell permeated throughout the entire estate, or at least, what he’d seen of it, anyway. It was like a whisper of shadow under the natural notes of citrus and sea breeze. No place smelled quite as strongly of that particular blend, though, as the space directly beside him. He wondered if it was a cologne, mixed with work, or if it all came from Arian’s trade. It wasn’t an unpleasant scent, to be honest. It suited the man and added to the air of mystery and danger he carried.

“Get used to it,” Arian mumbled, slicing into the steak before him. “This is your future home.”

The reminder that Caleb would be staying there indefinitely, practically bartered away by the Crown to save face, made him brash, and he spoke without a single thought as to the consequences. “You became godfather awfully young. I assume there was blood involved.” Arian froze, knife hovering over the plate. He let out a harsh sigh in warning, but Caleb was undeterred. He could lash out rather cruelly when he was cornered, and he couldn’t remember a time when he felt that way more so than sitting at that table with classical music playing in the background and an Italian crime boss who claimed to be his future husband casually eating at his side like it was an ordinary day. “Was it your father? Or your brother?” There was a dark glint of malice narrowing Caleb’s eyes, darkening the hazel to chestnut.

Arian exploded.

Wine glasses rattled, and silverware clanged as Caleb was dragged to his feet by his throat. Arian sneered at him, and his grip tightened just below the prince’s jaw. A wide grin stayed plastered across Caleb’s lips. He couldn’t believe how much fun it was to rile the man up. “Tease me again, and I’ll show you the rules of this house myself,” Arian growled as his fingers clenched just enough to leave Caleb slightly breathless.

“Only if you get the chance.” Caleb purred in return. He struck out with his left hand and tossed the steak knife from his place setting into the air before he caught it deftly in his right. Arian didn’t so much as flinch when Caleb snapped the blade up to rest horizontally against his throat. They stared one another down, neither willing to give in to the other.

Several guards rushed in and circled them both, guns drawn and pointed at Caleb’s head. He was still grinning. He’d already proven on the patio that he was important to Arian’s plans, and he highly doubted that he’d let them shoot him. He felt adrenaline surge through him at the inferno burning in Arian’s eyes. He’d successfully gotten under his skin, recklessly and without a thought to the consequences. And it had been fun, more fun than it had any right to be. To prove his point, he carefully dragged the flat edge of the knife from pulse point to Adam’s apple, and Arian gave a quick jerk of his head into the blade. His men dropped their guns slowly, and a tense heartbeat later, both Caleb and Arian retreated. The steak knife was dropped back onto the table, and then Arian took a folder from one of his men and threw it in Caleb’s direction.

He glanced down at the plain cream colored folder. “What’s this?”

Irritation still burned across Arian’s features when he responded. “An agreement.”

Caleb took his seat and picked up the stack of papers from inside the cover. “You do love paperwork. Ever considered becoming an attorney? The career choice of joining a mafia seems like such a waste,” he drawled as he lazily flipped through the pages. His eyes widened as his brain caught up with the words he’d seen, and he reread the page again, flipping through to make sure he wasn’t misunderstanding. He stared up at Arian in dumbfounded confusion. “A fake marriage contract?!”

“The Conti family and the Caruso family have had a marriage alliance for years,” Arian explained, hands sliding calmly into his pockets as he towered over Caleb. “I’m expected to marry the Caruso daughter, unless… I’m not fond of women.”

“Oh,” Caleb responded intelligently. Some of the pieces were beginning to come together in his head. Arian seemed like a man who despised being told what he had to do, and Caleb could appreciate the creative way in which he sought to permanently make it clear that things were going to be done only on his terms.

A small smile lifted the corner of his mouth, and something settled inside of him. Deception and loopholes were games he knew well. Half of his role as the reckless, rambunctious heir was to cause scandal to distract from important matters. Half was to find the tight wire between acceptable and allowable and dance across it with practiced ease. He’d never be king, especially after half the antics he’d been party to, if not solely responsible for, over the years, but that suited him just fine. The burden of the crown was Andrew’s responsibility, and he had no desire to bear it; he had far more fun causing mischief and getting into things he shouldn’t. If this was the game Arian wanted him to play, Caleb would exceed his expectations in spades. He was practically born and bred for this sort of thing. “You went through all this trouble because you need me to play along?” He guessed, and a muscle in Arian’s jaw ticked once.

“I don’t need you to play anything. I need you to obey.”

Caleb chuckled to himself. Caleb rarely obeyed anyone — his father, his tutors, his siblings, his security detail — so what made Arian think he’d obey him? He dropped the file back to the table and stood to meet Arian head-on, his stance relaxed now that the thoughts eddying in his mind were clearer. He adjusted his cuff with all the charm of an arrogant noble and looked up at Arian through his lashes. “Then,” he started, and stepped just into the man’s space, “I’ll have to inspect you properly. See whether this ‘husband’ is worthy of my lifelong devotion.”

That small, secret smile was back on his face at Arian’s harsh inhale. The man had no idea who he’d chosen to play this game with. That suited Caleb just fine; it made the game more fun that way. At least his life for the foreseeable future would be anything but boring. He tilted his head, letting his loose curls flop over his forehead charmingly. “And next time, grill the steak longer. I don’t do rare.” Without waiting for a response, he took a half step back and turned to leave the room, a plan already half formed in his mind. How far could he push Arian? And how spectacular would the explosion be when he finally hit his limit? Caleb may not survive the consequences, but he couldn’t think of anything more fun than playing with fire.
