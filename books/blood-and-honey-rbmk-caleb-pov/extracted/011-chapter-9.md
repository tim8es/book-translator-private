## Chapter 9

Caleb woke in that groggy, lazy way of the well-rested. He could tell it was at least mid-afternoon by how bright the room was behind his eyelids, but he felt no urge to rush into consciousness. He felt Arian’s arm around him, his chest a wall of heat at Caleb’s back, and he curled his body forward in a languid stretch. He opened his eyes slowly and looked back over his shoulder. Arian was propped up on one elbow behind him, just watching him, and Caleb gave him an easy, relaxed smile.

“Morning, sunshine.” Arian’s voice was sleep-rough and subdued, his responding smile gentle. Caleb smiled brightly at him and grabbed his hand before curling back to face away, tucking the stolen hand close to his chest as he debated dozing back to sleep, eyes heavy. The sun had been playing at the edges of the horizon when they’d finally turned in, and he was warm and content and comfortable. Arian’s fingers played softly with his curls as his voice washed over Caleb like silk. “For someone who claims he can’t sleep next to anyone, you slept like a baby.”

Caleb could hear the teasing smile in his tone, and his own easy, content smile stretched further across his face. “Give me a break; I was exhausted. That was my first time with a guy.”

Arian brushed a soft kiss across Caleb’s bruised shoulder, and the prince rolled onto his back, surging up to claim a kiss. One of Caleb’s hands rested on Arian’s shoulder blade and the other came up to cup the side of his neck, thumb settling between the hinge of his jaw and his ear. Arian’s own large hand was warm against the side of Caleb’s face as they traded slow, indulgent kisses. Caleb could happily spend all day in bed with Arian doing just that. Everything was cottony in that sleep-hazy way that made everything seem gentle and unhurried. He leaned back into the pillows, Arian having shifted to hover above him, and smiled brighter.

“To be all honest, you were a really good kisser,” Caleb beamed, and Arian laughed. Caleb loved it. Arian’s face was open, all smile lines and easy warmth. He looked happy, and it made him look younger and more unburdened.

“I am quite sure we did a lot more than kissing,” Arian reminded him, and he felt his muscles clench once with a trickle of desire at the memories of last night.

“If Caruso’s daughter caught us right now, it would be so thrilling to see the look on her face,” Caleb admitted, and a cold glint crossed Arian’s features at the name.

“The Carusos have already paid for last night.” Arian’s voice had lost its humor, and that cold glint was a blade on his tongue, sharpening his tone.

Caleb’s eyebrows scrunched in confusion. “What did you do?”

“I burned two of their estates,” Arian explained, with all the care of one announcing the day’s weather report.

Caleb let out a disbelieving scoff. He burned down two of their estates? Just for taking Caleb. “That’s preposterous,” he thought aloud.

“Always have been,” Arian told him sincerely, eyes bright with that fire that made Caleb want to burn.

Caleb had nothing to say. The reality of Arian’s admission floored him. He’d attacked another mafia family, which Caleb had no doubt would have potentially severe consequences if mafia politics worked even remotely like royal politics, on Caleb’s behalf without hesitation. There was no regret in his eyes, just a cautious openness that softened his green-grey gaze.

“Have you ever been to Sicily before?” Arian suddenly asked, and Caleb saw flashes of snow-covered trees, a bound and bloodied boy, a silver knife, like afterimages as he blinked. He thought again about telling Arian of the boy, but it was his most closely guarded secret, and as a prince he had so few things that were solely his own like that, so once again, he deflected.

“That’s a spur-of-the-moment question.” There had to be a reason Arian was asking, though. Had they possibly met before? Caleb couldn’t think of where they’d have potentially crossed paths, but it seemed important to Arian somehow. Some of the intensity had returned to his eyes as he seemed to come to some sort of internal decision.

“Hold on. I want to show you something.” Caleb nodded, and Arian kissed his jaw before slipping from the bed. He grabbed his dark blue robe and padded toward the desk.

Caleb stretched languidly, his muscles deliciously sore in the best possible way. He stood from the bed and stretched again before donning his own robe. He went to the bathroom before meandering towards where Arian was staring into the open desk drawer. “What is this?” He asked with an easy smile as he got closer, hands stuffed into the pockets of his robe.

Arian looked up at him with something like anxiety warring in his eyes. Caleb watched him take a deep breath and steel himself before he reached into the drawer. At the same time, someone knocked on the door. He froze. “Who’s there?”

“Boss, we found him,” said a voice from the other side, and Arian’s breathing quickened, his eyes blown wide and lips parted in shock.

“Where?” Arian’s reply was choked, and Caleb could almost swear he was nervous, but he couldn’t believe anyone could make Arian nervous.

“We put him in the garden.”

“I’ll be right there.” Arian’s eyes flew up to Caleb’s confused face, guilt written into every line of the man’s posture as he shook his head to clear it. “I have to-“ He gestured toward the closet, and Caleb just nodded, stepping to the side to let him pass.

Caleb himself chose to stay in his robe. Whoever this mystery person was, he doubted his presence was required, and he selfishly wanted to cling to the hazy, relaxed feeling he’d woken up with for as long as possible. Arian’s sudden shift in mood wasn’t helping in that endeavor, but clearly this mystery man was someone important if he had Arian rushing around to get dressed as to not keep him waiting. An instinctual surge of jealousy caught Caleb off guard, and he struggled to push it down. Realistically, aside from a fake marriage contract, a massive, unexpected, potentially unrequited crush, and an adrenaline-filled night of mind-blowing sex, he didn’t have any right to be jealous over Arian; he wasn’t really, truly his, after all. Caleb’s gut gave an uncomfortable lurch at that thought. He was starting to (had been for a while) wish that he was.

Arian emerged from the closet in black slacks, a white shirt, a black vest, and a metallic bronze tie with thin, diagonal butter yellow stripes. Caleb almost drooled at the sight of twin garters banded around Arian’s biceps, but he licked his suddenly dry lips and pretended to be enamored with watching a flock of birds cross the sky outside the window. He couldn’t see the garden from Arian’s terrace — he’d checked — but the breeze was cool on his face, and it gave him an excuse to not meet Arian’s eyes. He had no clue what his face was doing at the moment, and he didn’t need Arian to see the turmoil and uncertainty projected across his expression, so instead he was being a coward and hiding.

*How princely,* Caleb mentally berated himself bitterly.

“I need to go.” Arian’s voice cut through his anxiety, and then he could feel his hesitation like a physical weight between them. “Feel free to stay here and rest, if you wish,” he finally said, and there was an underlying note of some emotion in his tone that Caleb couldn’t parse — maybe hope or concern or something like fond exasperation.

Caleb hated how unsure and hesitant Arian sounded, but he didn’t turn around or offer him any sort of reassurance. He nodded, and then after a moment of weighted silence he heard the click of Arian’s dress shoes crossing to the door. He waited until the sound retreated down the hall before he wandered out and looked over the hall balcony.

He watched as Arian emerged from the lower hall and stopped, regarding a blonde man in a loose-fitting embroidered beige shirt and cream linen trousers. Caleb couldn’t read Arian’s expression from his spot on the balcony, but Arian didn’t balk as the man approached him, standing much closer than was necessary, with an easy, almost playful smile. He held a hand out between them as he spoke before shrugging and tucking it back into the pocket of his trousers, while Arian’s hands never left his own pockets.

Caleb wished he could hear what was being said. He wished that the surge of jealousy burning hot in his gut would go away. He wished he could go down there and demand to know who the man was to Arian. Foolishly, he even wished he could stake some kind of claim on Arian and make it clear to the man that he was standing far too close to someone who was his. Caleb felt vaguely sick and selfish and guilty. He had no right, marriage contract or not. Just because they’d slept together didn’t mean that they were together, or that Caleb had any say in anything. He knew that breaking the rules they’d agreed upon would have consequences. He knew that falling for Arian would complicate things. His heart didn’t seem to care. Caleb had no one to blame but himself.

Lost in thought, Caleb hadn’t noticed Arian and the man leave the garden until he heard footsteps on the stairs a moment before they came into view. They stopped in front of Caleb, and he quickly took in both men’s body language. Arian was tense, as if he was watching two lions circle one another and waiting for the inevitable pounce. His hands were shoved deep into the pockets of his trousers, his shoulders stiff, and his face was a perfectly neutral mask, so unlike the open warmth of his expression while they were in bed a moment ago. The stranger was carefully at ease, but something felt off about it, almost performative, like he was purposefully making himself seem unthreatening and relaxed. The prince was intimately familiar with the many masks of courtiers and politicians, and this man, despite his casual air and tousled blonde waves, looked too perfectly comfortable. There was a sliver of darkness in his bright eyes that made Caleb instantly dislike him, despite having no logical, non-jealous reason to besides an instinctual gut reaction.

He forced himself to cover his scowl with the armor of his well-practiced grin. He felt suddenly underdressed in nothing but his robe, but he refused to allow this stranger to see him caught off guard. Arian needn’t worry about Caleb’s reaction; he could be civil. “A new acquaintance?” He asked, and despite his best efforts, a sliver of that dark jealousy sharpened his tone, his gaze.

The blonde smiled, offering a hand. “Lucien Windsor — the shipping Windsors.” Caleb tried not to openly glare at the proffered hand as he tried to place the name. “I believe we met at a White House reception a few years ago, Your Highness.”

His smile tightened, and he felt the strain as his mask tried to slip. He pulled one hand free of the pocket of his robe and grasped Lucien’s hand, almost too tightly. He knew there was a cold shadow darkening the hazel of his eyes to chocolatey brown, but he was barely maintaining the facade of the aloof royal, so he tried not to dwell on it. “Pleasure,” Caleb said, but his tone made it clear it was anything but. His voice was a sharp, posh drawl that he usually reserved for those who managed to truly aggravate him, mostly his siblings or overzealous nobles and courtiers, as they shook hands.

Arian looked between them before taking a deep breath and locking eyes with Caleb. If Caleb didn’t know any better, he would have thought that Arian looked anxious. He was regarding Caleb like he was concerned the prince would explode. “Lucien will be staying with us for a few days.”

Caleb dropped the blonde’s hand, glaring up through his eyelashes at Arian as white-hot ire shot through him like a shotgun blast. He couldn’t quite bring his grin back up, but he pushed everything down, trying to keep his voice calm and unaffected. “How nice. The House of Conti is certainly *renowned* for its hospitality.” He cocked his head to the side and stared directly into Arian’s eyes. The dig was unfair, he knew; Arian had been a consummate gentleman for the few weeks Caleb had lived in his home, aside from the occasional (mostly deserved) threat. He didn’t remember having taken that sharp, double-edged tone with Arian before, and he wondered what conclusions the man was drawing from his quiet outburst.

“Sorry,” Lucian interrupted in an effort to break the tension, “I need to use the restroom.”

“End of the hall,” Arian told him.

“Thanks.” Lucien walked directly between the two men, ignoring the palpable tension between them, and stared directly at Caleb as he brushed far too close to the prince before continuing down the hall.

Caleb blinked and dropped his gaze to Arian’s polished black shoes. What was wrong with him? He had no reason to be acting so hostile with Arian’s guest, despite his own baseless intuition. Arian let out an unsteady sigh, and Caleb turned to leave. Arian’s hands whipped out and gripped Caleb’s biceps. “I can explain this.” His voice was rough, pleading, and Caleb felt a sudden rush of annoyance cut through him.

Arian was treating him like a child. That small voice in his head told him to stop acting like one. A cold smirk curled up his lips, and he turned back to Arian. “I don’t think you owe me any explanations.”

Arian persisted, words rushed and eyes intense, and Caleb tried not to see the flash of hurt there. “20 years ago, Lucien saved my life.”

Caleb breathed out a huff through his nose, a self-deprecating smile making his teeth flash bright in the sunlight. He was being ridiculous. Clearly Lucien was important to Arian, and Caleb was being uncharacteristically rude. “Wow, how touching.”

Arian’s hurt was clear on his face now, and guilt curled low in Caleb’s gut. He felt like an ass. “I’ve been looking for him ever since. Never been able to track him down, and now he just shows up out of the blue.”

Caleb felt the words like a knife to the chest. Arian had told him that he’d never see the person he loved again, last night, before everything that had happened. It made sense now – Arian’s anxiety. Here he was pleading with his dubiously bartered fake husband to understand that he’d found the man he’d spent 20 years loving and searching for. Caleb felt like a fool. Of course Arian didn’t feel the same way he did. Of course he wasn’t breaking inside at the sheer *thought *of someone else sharing Arian’s bed, his heart.

Caleb lashed out when he was hurt, and right now, he felt like he was breaking. “So you’ve always been into men.”

“No,” Arian insisted, hands dropping from Caleb’s arms. “Before you, I never touched a man.” Caleb could read the truth of it in Arian’s eyes. For once, Arian wasn’t trying to hide, and Caleb didn’t know what to do with that kind of raw honesty coming from him.

It shocked him, flayed at the edges of his already shattered nerves. “Really?” The question came out far too vulnerable. “Then what are we?” He dared to ask, despite being terrified of the answer. He could feel tentative hope licking at his chest.

Arian’s eyebrows were drawn tight over his green-grey eyes. They shone with too much emotion, but Arian said nothing, mouth opening and closing like he couldn’t find the words. His silence was confirmation enough, and Caleb’s small, hopeful smile fell. He scoffed and turned, needing to escape before he made an even bigger fool of himself. He would not be anyone’s second choice, no matter what it did to his heart. He didn’t stop until he reached his room, hot, shameful tears burning his eyes. He would not cry, he ordered himself. He was a *prince*, for fuck’s sake, not some lovesick damsel.

He threw the robe from his shoulders and strode into the massive closet in his guest room. He needed his armor. He needed some sense of normalcy and control. His anger — mostly directed at himself — flared hotter when he looked around and saw all of the suits and accessories that had been carefully stowed onto the racks and shelves from their shopping trip. His eyes raked over the new suits, and he almost lost it. He felt like a petulant child, arguing with his sisters over a stolen toy. Arian wasn’t his, he reminded himself, and he marched past the new clothes until he found the ones he’d arrived with.

He felt a little more himself once he pulled on his chosen outfit. He’d decided that he wanted to go riding to clear his mind and try to stuff Arian back into the box he’d kept him locked away in. He wore knee-high brown leather riding boots, tight off-white trousers, a white and blue plaid button-up, and a patterned grey tie under his camel brown suede sport coat. His mind was still a whirlwind of emotions, but his face in the mirror as he tightened the tie at the base of his throat gave nothing away.

If Arian couldn’t decide what they were, Caleb would do so for him. He would maintain their agreement and be the consummate fake husband when needed, but behind closed doors he would remain apart, aloof, unaffected, even if it killed some integral part of him. He would not be a second choice. He would rather keep himself apart and suffer in his own chest than be a fallback. It may destroy his heart, shatter his soul, and darken his mind, but he’d do it, no matter how much it hurt. If Arian couldn’t decide what they were to each other, Caleb had to protect whatever scraps of himself he could.
