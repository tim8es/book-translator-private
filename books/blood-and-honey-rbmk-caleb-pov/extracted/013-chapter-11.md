## Chapter 11

Caleb had overheard from the guards on his way to the stables that there was to be a party hosted at the Conti estate the next day, and when he woke from a fitful night of restless sleep, the staff was already bustling around preparing tables and flower arrangements and setting out tableware and champagne flutes. The garden was a mess of activity. Caleb went unnoticed as he wandered through the halls, taking in the fresh, warm breeze blowing through the open windows, and trying to memorize everything he could.

He’d been undecided for most of the night on what to do. To be honest, he still didn’t know if he would pick the right thing, but he wasn’t going to be so scared of picking the wrong thing that he picked nothing at all. Decisive, Paul had called him; Caleb felt anything but.

Lucien was noticeably absent, and Caleb wondered what had happened between him and Arian the night before. Before their fight last night, Arian might have told him, had Caleb asked. Now, he hadn’t seen the man all morning. His chest still ached with guilt and shame from everything he’d said to the man in the stables, but it made his decision to leave easier, he supposed. Arian undoubtedly hated him now, just as Caleb had intended. There was no further reason for him to stay.

Once the guests started arriving, Caleb returned to his room and grabbed his suitcase, taking only what he had arrived at the estate with. He refused to take advantage of Arian’s generosity by absconding with any of the suits he had bought. He did run his fingers over the cream suit indulgently before he left it, though. It was probably his favorite, but Arian had bought it, so it stayed with the rest.

Caleb walked past Arian’s room, hoping to at least see him one last time, but he wasn’t there. He would’ve moved on, had a glint of light from the desk not caught his eye. He stepped into the room and placed his suitcase on the ground. An ornate silver hunting dagger was propped on top of a small leather case, and Caleb froze, reaching for it in disbelief.

“This is my dagger,” he whispered to himself, gripping the handle and inspecting it.

He hadn’t seen the dagger since-

Caleb gasped.

White snow.

The scent of pine, honey, and blood.

A boy in tattered clothes, bound and beaten.

A bear.

A shot.

Pulling out his knife to free the boy.

Caleb’s hand reaching out.

That boy… Caleb’s most precious secret. One that he never told anyone. Not his father. Not his siblings. His alone.

*Arian was the boy. *

It suddenly made sense. Arian had asked him if he’d ever saved anyone’s life. He’d asked him if he’d ever been to Sicily before. He’d told him he wanted to show him something.

He’d said he loved someone he’d never see again. He’d thought it was Lucien, had told Caleb as much. Had that been why Lucien was absent? Arian had said that Lucien wasn’t who he thought he was. Arian had been searching for the boy who saved him for 20 years. He had no idea that he’d been living with the owner of the dagger for almost a month now.

*“My goodness,” *Caleb gasped. His entire world shifted on its head in an instant.

And suddenly he knew. He knew what he wanted, knew it wouldn’t be easy, but wanted to try anyway. He had to fix things. He had to talk to Arian.

He had to find him first.

+++

Caleb ran through the house and out into the garden, dodging through the throng of people in search of that familiar head of inky black curls. People began to murmur ahead of him, and he turned to see what drew their attention.

Arian approached the crowd from the opposite end of the garden, face neutral and eyes flat, all of their usual fire absent. He looked immaculately put together in a navy blue suit, but Caleb could see just by looking at him that this was the Conti godfather, not the Arian he’d come to know. His smile fell when he saw a delicate hand wrap around Arian’s elbow. Valentina stood at his side, a massive diamond ring looping her finger, and she looked for all the world like the cat that got the cream.

“I’ve gathered you all here today because I’ve got an important announcement to make.” Arian’s voice was sturdy, flat, lifeless. All of his usual sly humor and quiet teasing was gone from his tone. His eyes scanned the crowd, face an unreadable mask, until he landed on Caleb. His eyes widened, lips parting on a harsh breath, as they stared at one another from across the space.

Caleb was sure his face was a wreck. He wasn’t even trying to hide behind his usual masks. Arian’s eyes grew glassy, his silence stretching as he continued to stare. Valentina jerked his arm, and he closed his mouth, looking away from Caleb with something like regret. “The Conti and Caruso families have valued our alliance for generations,” he said, voice unsteady, eyes darting anywhere but Caleb. “I’ve decided to continue that tradition by becoming engaged to Miss Valentina Caruso.”

Caleb felt his chest crack.

He was too late.

He knew his fight with Arian would have consequences. He knew that Arian had been hurt and was rightfully pissed off with Caleb. He knew Valentina had been dogged in her pursuit of him, regardless of his supposed engagement to Caleb.

He didn’t know his actions would drive Arian into her arms.

Caleb felt tears well up in his eyes. Arian couldn’t even look at him. Caleb had well and truly fucked up, and there was no coming back from this. He took a shaky breath to hold the rush of tears at bay and trudged back towards the house in a haze, his mind a blur, his thoughts scattered, his chest hollow and cracked.

He had no reason to stay now.

Arian had publicly announced the engagement Caleb was brought there to prevent. He had nothing left holding him to Arian, apart from sentimentality and his own broken heart.

He went back to Arian’s room and grabbed the suitcase he’d left there. He hesitated in front of the desk, staring at his dagger. He swiped angrily at the single fat tear that dared to fall and took a piece of paper from a stack on the corner of the desk, scribbling a note onto it and folding it up.

***Ciao ciao!***

***My dagger leaves with its rightful owner. ***

He stopped a guard he’d become friendly with and asked him to make sure Arian got the note before taking his suitcase and heading for the dock. No one stopped him. He wondered idly when he’d stopped being a stolen prize and became someone who could freely come and go as he wished.

He hesitated on the dock, staring back up the cliffside at Arian’s grand estate. He couldn’t stay, but he’d left his heart in that house, and he couldn’t quite take his eyes off of it. At some point, he’d begun to cry and he didn’t bother to force the tears to stop. No one was around to see him break.

He felt someone jog up behind him, and then Paul was taking his case from his frozen hand. “Your Highness!”

He dragged his eyes away and numbly followed Paul onto the yacht. Paul disappeared below deck to stow his bag, and Caleb stood at the rear of the boat, watching the life he could’ve had slip further into the distance as the tears continued to fall freely.

Part of him would always remain in Sicily.

Part of him would always wonder what if.

A dark-suited silhouette hit the dock at a run, and his eyes widened.

Arian.

Arian had come. But why? He owed Caleb nothing. His hot tears burned his eyes as he stared at the lone figure on the end of the dock.

He wished he could’ve at least said goodbye.

The yacht suddenly shuddered beneath his feet, and he was thrown from the deck as an explosion rocked through the air, tearing the yacht apart and launching him into the air. He was unconscious before he hit the water.
