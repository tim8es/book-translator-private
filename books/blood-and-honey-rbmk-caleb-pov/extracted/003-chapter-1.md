Caleb could hear the screams, high and excited, even before the private jet’s door unfurled onto the Sicilian tarmac. Paul, his secretary, looked vaguely as if he would throw up, his already pale complexion grey and papery, as he glanced out the windows to the source of the noise. His head whipped back to the man sat across from him, and his eyes were wide and pleading, his breath coming in short, staccato puffs.

Caleb laughed at the look on his face and donned his diamond-encrusted sunglasses before rising from the plush leather seat he was lounging in. “Relax, Paul. We’re in Italy! Once the whole ‘international incident’ business is sorted, we can have a little fun. You look as though you expect to be marched to the gallows!” A bright, teasing grin curved across Caleb’s face and Paul gulped. He’d been even more twitchy than usual today, the Prince had noticed, but Paul had never been one for the spectacle that Caleb himself usually basked in. “Cheer up. There’s beautiful women to meet!”

Paul gulped and gripped the arms of the chair before unsteadily rising to follow his prince to the cabin door and the ear-splitting screams beyond. Caleb smoothed his hands down the already immaculately pressed lapel of his suit jacket and ruffled his hair into an artfully messy halo of loose curls with practiced ease. He looked damn good, if he did say so himself. The suit was one of his favorites — royal blue jacket and light cream trousers with a matching blue and white diagonally striped tie over a crisp white shirt — and he knew it suited him perfectly. Of course it did; it had been tailored to his exact measurements, after all.

“Time to shine.” He whispered to himself and took a long breath before releasing it and stepping out onto the top stair.

The screams immediately rose in pitch and intensity, almost deafening him, and he felt the rush of adrenaline hit his system. He lived for this — the spectacle, the spotlight, the game. His trademark boyishly charming grin split his face, cheekily bracketed by his deep dimples and smile lines. Mischief was written in every line of his face, and he blew a kiss to the assembled crowd. It took a concerted effort to not allow the grin to deepen into a smirk at the sight of a woman fainting in response to his antics.

+++

Caleb was welcomed to Sicily with open arms and bright smiles, as befitting the rambunctious youngest son of the British monarchy, right up until he handed his passport to the customs agent and it was promptly ripped in half in front of him. The smile left his face in utter shock, and when he turned to ask Paul, his constant shadow for years, what was going on, the man was noticeably absent.

He was alone.

For the first time in probably as long as he could remember, he had no entourage trailing his steps. He let out a disbelieving laugh. Perhaps this was all an elaborate joke by one of his older siblings? The laugh quickly morphed into a small, tight scowl when the sound of several sets of footsteps echoed through the empty space. He was then surrounded by men in black suits that screamed private force over government agency. The prince wasn’t stupid; he knew that his ransom alone would fuel a small army for a good long while if he was kidnapped — which he now highly suspected was what was going on — and that several unsavory types would absolutely kill to get their hands on him for the leverage his capture posed to the Crown.

After his brief moment of shock, Caleb steeled himself and forced his shoulders to relax as he sunk his hands into his pockets and put on the mask of the aloof, unaffected royal. “Well, that’s certainly a dramatic way of saying hello.”

+++

Caleb didn’t bother putting up a fight against the half dozen men; he was unarmed and didn’t fancy getting shot. He maintained his aloof disposition as the blindfold was slipped over his eyes, as two of the men grabbed him by the upper arm and pulled him along, as he was guided to sit in the back of a car — a Rolls-Royce, he was positive; he’d recognize the console at his elbow anywhere. It was quite a drive, wherever they were going, through the winding Sicilian hillside.

The whole time Caleb stayed silent, mentally cataloguing everything he could about his situation. So far, all he knew was that his passport was gone, he had been kidnapped, and he was being transported to some undisclosed location with none of his support staff. He spared a moment to hope that Paul and William were okay and unharmed before focusing back on his surroundings. There was a faint, almost imperceptible smell of gun oil and smoke in the car, as well as leather and suede. It smelled like luxury, and well-maintained luxury at that. The prince had a pretty good nose for that sort of thing, given the nature of his upbringing.

Eventually the Rolls turned down a gravel drive and slowed to a stop. Caleb fought the instinctual desire to panic. (What was it they always said — don’t let them take you to a secondary location? As if he had any say in the matter.) A hand gripped his arm and pulled him from the car as soon as the door opened. A second looped around his other arm, and they guided him down a gravel path for a ways, blindfold still firmly across his vision. He tried to focus his energy into counting the steps he took — it might be useful information for any future attempt at escape — as the scents of fresh citrus and salty ocean spray filled his nose. His feet made contact with stone and then tile before the hands released him and he stumbled forward a half step.

One of them pulled the tie of the blindfold free and Caleb blinked against the sudden brightness. He brought a hand up to shield his eyes as he tried to take in his surroundings, but his gaze caught on the silhouette of a man facing away from him and held. From this distance, with the man backlit through the open doorway by the midafternoon sun, Caleb couldn’t make out very many details about him. He was tall, for sure, easily every inch of 6’2”, with broad shoulders and short, slightly curly black hair. He had a relaxed posture and his hands were casually in his pockets, but an aura of raw power radiated off of him. This man was dangerous. That much was obvious. He glanced over his shoulder at the sound of Caleb’s escorts’ retreating footfalls before fully turning to face the prince. He had richly tanned skin, the beginnings of a shadow of dark stubble lining a sharp, angular jaw, broad, solid shoulders, a deceptively muscular, lithe frame, and dark, intense green-grey eyes, Caleb noted quickly. The light behind the man hid his expression, though, and the uncertainty had Caleb’s mouth running before he could think better of it.

“Who are you?”

“Arian Conti. Godfather of the Conti family.” His voice was smooth, like silk drawn over rough stone, and his tone low but commanding. Godfather? He didn’t look much older than Caleb himself. To already be a godfather at that age? A surge of adrenaline hit Caleb’s system, his heart rate skyrocketing.

Arian wasn’t just dangerous.

He was deadly.

He took careful, measured steps towards Caleb, and despite every instinct, the prince was rooted to the spot, unable to move. Arian was an apex predator, and he was looking at Caleb like he was prey. Arian only stopped once he was mere inches from Caleb, and then he angled his head until his breath ghosted hotly across Caleb’s ear. “Your future husband.”
