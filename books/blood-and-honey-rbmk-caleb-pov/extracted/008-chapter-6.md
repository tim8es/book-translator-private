Two weeks later, Arian took Caleb into the city to get new suits for the Caruso gala. They had settled into a delicate truce following the events with Paul in the paddock. They shared meals (sans orchestra) at least twice daily and saw each other around the estate often, but Arian did have a criminal empire to run, and Caleb had an entire estate to explore.

The salesman was a short, rotund man with an outlandishly curled mustache and a midnight blue velvet suit. The jacket had hideously patterned lapels, and the offensively orange silk ascot did nothing to improve the look. “Mr. Conti, these are the latest designer pieces. I guarantee these gentlemen will turn every head at tonight’s party.”

Caleb looked at the rack of suits the man had pulled and had to bite down on his tongue to keep from laughing aloud. He pulled his most charming grin onto his face. “You’re sure these are meant to turn heads, not stomachs?” The man huffed and scowled at Caleb indignantly.

Before he could say anything, Arian stepped forward to stand alongside Caleb. “Then buy everything, except this rack.” Arian’s voice carried through the shop, and a stock person dropped his armful of boxes at the words, all eyes turning to stare at Arian’s declaration.

Caleb was surprised, himself. He was well versed in the quality of a good suit, and he knew the prices that usually accompanied them. To buy out an entire store simply because Caleb had made a joke was absurd, and yet something like satisfaction coiled low in his gut. “Your idea of spoiling a wife is spectacularly tacky,” he drawled, trying to sound disinterested.

Arian crossed his arms and raised his eyebrows, daring Caleb. The prince turned and glanced around the sales floor until his eyes landed on a cream colored suit in a glass display case. He turned back to Arian with a wide grin. “I want this one.”

Arian gestured the salesman forward, and he pulled the suit out of its case, his eyes still a little wide, before he directed Caleb to the dressing room. Everything was going fine until he slipped the button free on his pants and the zipper wouldn’t budge down more than an inch. The pants were tailored to his frame, so there was no way he was going to be able to shimmy them off if the zip was broken. He sighed and tried again, drawing the tab back up and down once more, just for it to catch in the same spot, and then refuse to even move at all. Another deep sigh and he slumped against the wall. “A little help? Anyone?” He called out and then continued to fuss with the fickle trousers.

The curtains pulled back, and he turned to see Arian standing in the open space between them, looking unimpressed. Caleb let out a self-deprecating huff and offered up a lopsided, sheepish grin. “Zipper’s stuck.” The sigh that Arian let loose sounded like it came from the very depths of his soul, and he walked into the small dressing room. Caleb’s back was to the wall, and Arian reached forward and grasped his trousers, making him gasp and step back until his back hit the wall.

“Don’t move,” Arian ordered.

That was easy for him to say, Caleb thought. He tried not to think about the heat radiating off of Arian’s body into the too-small space between them or the fact that he was shirtless with Arian’s hands alarmingly close to his bare skin. He could feel his heartbeat pick up speed as his breaths quickened and he tried not to move. He didn’t know where to look. All he could see was inky black hair and tan skin. The increasingly familiar scents of gun oil, smoke, leather, and suede began to surround him. He tried to avoid looking just slightly lower at Arian’s mouth. He’d had nearly two weeks to make peace with the fact that he was attracted to the man, but they had two rules, and he intended to stick to them. Anything else would just muddy the waters and complicate things. That didn’t mean his mind wasn’t racing with the sudden proximity.

Arian must’ve sensed his stare because he paused, looking up at Caleb through his eyelashes. Caleb forced himself to laugh, to break the tension, before his body got the wrong idea and tried to react. It would be easier to convince his body to behave if he and Arian could stop finding themselves in compromising situations, but it seemed to be a bit of a theme with them by this point. “Well, at least no one’s around to see this.”

Arian smirked and resumed his efforts, so of course when Caleb glanced past his shoulder, he locked eyes with the salesman from before, gawking at them through the gap in the still-open curtain. The jacket he was carrying slipped from his limp hands before he did a rushed Hail Mary and scampered off, almost forgetting the jacket before he turned and took it with him. Caleb’s head dropped back against the wall, and he scrubbed a hand through his hair a moment before he heard the zip finally give. His pants fell in a heap around his ankles. He looked up to thank Arian and saw him take in a shuddering breath, eyes still focused downward. There was no way that Arian was checking him out, there just wasn’t, but good luck convincing Caleb’s libido of that. He chuckled and gave his best thousand-watt grin. “You’re enjoying this way too much.”

That caused Arian to meet his eyes, a scowl on his face. “I’m not enjoying anything,” he protested.

“Then why are you still looking?” It felt good to settle into the teasing camaraderie they’d had almost immediately. He couldn’t keep the smile off his face as Arian glared at him.

“Put something on,” he groused before he strode out and shut the curtain.

Caleb allowed himself a moment to come back down from the rush before he started to get dressed. The suit fit him perfectly. Caleb knew what colors and fabrics looked good on him, and he’d done well. The cream of the suit made his fair skin almost glow with warmth, and his messy brown curls popped against the light color and brought out the hazel in his eyes. He’d paired it with a classic white button-up, as well as brown oxfords and a textured chocolate brown tie. He adjusted the tie and crossed the room to stand in front of Arian with a smile.

For his part, Arian looked floored. His eyes were wide, his mouth slack, his breathing shallow. “Am I that handsome, or that threatening?” He teased, pleased to get a reaction out of Arian.

He seemed to steel himself and drew up a mask of his own, features smoothing out, but his eyes were on fire. “Dressed like that, the Caruso daughter may not just call off the engagement. She’ll probably try to run off with you.” Something like jealousy flickered in Arian’s eyes before it vanished just as quickly. Caleb’s jaw was slack at the open compliment, and he didn’t move as Arian stepped toward him. “Hold on.” Arian reached up and adjusted Caleb’s tie, smoothing down his lapels, even though he was quite certain he’d settled the Windsor knot with the careful perfection of someone who’d been doing it for over two decades.

The charged moment made Caleb’s heart flutter as a surge of desire pulsed through him. Arian had looked at him like he was starving and Caleb was a feast. “Even though we have an agreement, you should avoid gestures that could be misunderstood.” Caleb knew his eyes were darting anywhere but at Arian. He couldn’t seem to help it.

Arian’s eyes flickered down to Caleb’s mouth before he cleared his throat and looked back into Caleb’s hazel eyes. “Have you ever saved anyone’s life?”

A snowy forest, blood and honey, tattered clothes and sticky black hair, a gunshot, a bear, a scream, and then a silver knife and an outstretched hand. His father’s men dragging him away as the small body in the snow disappeared into the mist. The images flashed through Caleb’s mind, and he thought about telling Arian, but he hesitated. He’d never told anyone about that day, not even his father. Surprisingly, at the time anyway, the guards had never told the king, either, though Caleb knew now that was probably out of self-preservation for having allowed the young prince to give them the slip in the first place, let alone end up in that kind of danger. He’d looked for the boy, of course, in the days following their interaction in the woods, but he’d been unsuccessful.

“I asked you a question.” Arian’s voice cut through his reverie. “Have you ever saved anyone’s life?” His expression was open, almost pleading, eyes searching Caleb like only he held the answers to some great mystery.

Choosing to keep that memory to himself, Caleb deflected. “I’ve saved plenty of people. War zone children, a girl trying to jump off the Thames, you know how it is.” He counted off on his fingers with a shrug. “Royals need a few good deeds a year. And once, I drilled a hole in a fish tank for a man who got his head stuck in it.” He didn’t bother to tell Arian that the fish tank was empty and that the man had been one of his attendants, William, who he’d dared to see if he could fit his head into the bowl in the first place.

Arian simply stared blankly at him for a moment before turning on his heel and heading for the dressing room to change into his own suit.

The Caruso estate was just as vast and stately as the Conti one, but instead of rolling hillsides and vineyards, the yard overlooked a cliff and the ocean. Personally, Caleb thought, as they walked through the house to the back patio space where everyone had gathered, Arian had better taste in decor. The Carusos seemed to err on the side of more is more, whereas Arian chose the understated, elegant approach. He stuck close to Arian as they cut across the grass, people beginning to notice them in the form of gasps and murmurs.

He imagined they made quite a striking pair. Arian’s three-piece suit was black with a subtle tone-on-tone pinstripe, as his usual preference demanded, with a burgundy tie and white shirt to add some color, and it contrasted against Caleb’s cream suit perfectly.

Caleb reached forward and looped his arm into the crook of Arian’s elbow, causing him to stop dead in his tracks to stare at him. “If we’re putting on a show, let’s commit,” he purred mischievously, low enough for only Arian to hear. “Today, I’m breaking your engagement curse.”

Caleb gave him a cheeky smirk and winked. It didn’t take long for the whispers to start. Hushed murmurs and shock rippled through the assembled guests like wildfire catching. He leaned up until his lips were next to Arian’s ear, one hand resting on his lapel. “I think they’re starting to get the message,” he purred, tone far too amused. These kinds of games were what he lived for. Scandal and gossip were old friends of his, and he’d been playing the rebellious, irreverent royal for most of his life. It always made it more fun to have someone to play off of, and he had a feeling that in this particular dance, he couldn’t ask for a better partner than Arian Conti. “Looks like I’m public enemy number one.”

Arian smirked, his expression distinctly pleased and almost as mischievous as Caleb’s. Both of their heads whipped to the side as an enraged voice rose above the crowd. “Get your filthy hands off of my man!” A woman’s voice bellowed, and the crowd parted to reveal a petite woman with carefully styled brown hair pulled up into an artful chignon in a form-fitting metallic gold dress that flashed brightly as she stomped their way. Every line of her body radiated anger. She didn’t stop until she was firmly planted just in front of them, her hands on her hips and the large collar of diamonds around her neck glinting in the sun with every harsh breath she took.

All amusement had left Arian’s face, leaving him stoic and icy at Caleb’s side. He had not removed Caleb’s hand from his arm, though, so Caleb maintained his persona and simply waited. Arian’s arm casually came up and around his shoulders, palm resting between his shoulder blades and drawing him into his side like he’d done it a million times before. Caleb looped his own arm around Arian’s back and eased into him with a wickedly charming smile. He understood that his role there was mostly decorative, so he was more than willing to let Arian take the lead and offer quiet support.

“Allow me to introduce,” Arian began, and turned to stare into Caleb’s eyes with no small amount of mischief lighting up his face, “my fiancée.”

The smile on Caleb’s face fell slightly as an unbidden rush of longing surged through his veins. He searched Arian’s eyes and found mirth and humor there instead of unyielding ice. They were indeed playing this game together, it seemed. The gasping crowd around them was nothing compared to the palpable wall of incandescent fury coming from the woman in front of them. “You can’t be serious. He’s a *man!*”

Caleb looked down at their host with a private smile. Hadn’t that been his excuse that first day when Arian had called himself Caleb’s future husband? “Miss Caruso, true love doesn’t care about gender.” The statement came from the bottom of his heart. Caleb had long known that love knew no gender; his youngest sister, Cecily, had confided in him that she was a lesbian years ago, and he’d been secretly helping her hide her relationship with one of her ladies’ maids for a few years now. He and Arian shared a private smile before she spoke again.

“Arian, our families had an agreement first,” she glared at Caleb, head whipping to Arian next. “You’re throwing that away for this *slut?!*”

Arian’s fingers clenched against Caleb’s shoulder as his expression hardened to brimstone and glaciers. “First of all, you and I never had an agreement. Our families talk. That’s not the same thing. And second, he is *not* a slut.” He turned back to look at Caleb and his entire face softened. His low silk-over-stone voice flowed over the prince like mist on a hot summer day. “He is the one I love.” Arian sounded so sincere, so honest, that Caleb had to see what emotion was in his eyes. The green-grey was subdued and dark, anger still tightening his pupils until the irises were practically shining pools of misty jade. He had no idea if the shock he felt was telegraphed across his own face or not, but those words, in that same tone that Arian used to tease and push him, had shattered his carefully constructed walls to rubble.

“*No! *You cannot possibly be gay!” Valentina protested and then turned her vicious anger on Caleb. “You pathetic little *freak!* You bewitched him! *What did you do to him?*”

Before Caleb could respond, a group of reporters surrounded the three of them with cameras and microphones all pointed their way. “Mr. Conti, is this just for show, or are you two actually involved?” One of the reporters shouted at them.

“You’ve refused the Caruso alliance for years. Is being gay just your excuse?” Another asked quickly after.

“My feelings for Caleb are not fake,” Arian announced clearly. His voice was strong, sure, and left no room for argument or interpretation. “I love him.”

Caleb’s mask truly fell then, his lips parting just slightly as he got lost in the magma-hot inferno of green-grey staring down at him. “Prince Caleb has a reputation, Mr. Conti. Scandal after scandal, the disappointment of the royal family. What *do* you actually see in him?” A third reporter questioned, and Arian didn’t even look his way as he continued to smile at Caleb softly.

Arian’s answer was quick, like he didn’t even have to think to know what he would say. “His honesty. His kindness. The way he refuses to do what anyone expects.” All elements of his personality that Caleb would’ve thought most got under Arian’s skin, especially the last. They were straying once again into something a little too real, but they were committed, and Caleb couldn’t back down now, so he tried to keep as much off of his face as possible despite his heart pounding away in his chest.

“Fine,” Valentina scoffed, crossing her arms in challenge. “If this is real, *prove it to me. *Right here in front of everyone.” Her smirk said that she’d won, and part of Caleb wondered if she had. Proof?

“Great. What now?” He murmured low to Arian.

A beat of silence passed. Arian’s eyes darted between his. Before Caleb could process the resolve that he saw there, Arian’s right hand came up and cupped the side of Caleb’s neck as the left slid up from his shoulder to cover his nape, drawing him in. At the same time, he leaned in and slanted his lips over Caleb’s. For a single moment, Caleb’s eyes flew wide open, and then they fluttered shut. Even if he wanted to fight the kiss, he couldn’t — not without destroying the lie they were working to build — but he found that he didn’t really want to. His hand came up to grip Arian’s forearm as he thawed from his shock and began to kiss him back.

Caleb felt like he was on fire. Arian kissed like a man starved. Caleb had to hold back a moan when one of Arian’s hands found his ribs, the other still branding the back of his neck with heat. He brought his own hand up to rest against Arian’s neck, holding him to him. The sounds of the shocked crowd around them faded away until it was just Caleb and Arian. Arian kissed him like he wanted to consume him, but his hands held him like something precious, his thumb brushing across Caleb’s cheekbone as they kissed one last time. They pulled apart slowly, the moment stretching until the sounds around them filtered back into Caleb’s awareness and he tensed. He had no idea how long they’d been standing there, lost in their own little world. Arian slowly lowered his hand, both of them breathing noticeably harder. Caleb’s eyes darted around, taking in the shocked expressions of too many faces, before he licked his kiss-tender lips, chasing the lingering feeling there. He’d never been kissed like that before, like he was the only person in the world and nothing else mattered. He’d never been kissed so well that he forgot where he was and what was happening around him for a moment.

Arian squared his shoulders and faced Valentina head-on. “Would you like more proof?” His voice had a low rumble to it that it hadn’t before, his words clipped like he was losing his patience.

“If you want to make an enemy of the Caruso family, *you just got your wish!*” She spat at him before storming off.

He turned back to Caleb, and Caleb couldn’t stop a small, nervous swallow. He had the sinking thought that they’d just made things so, so much worse. “Come, I think we’ve outstayed our welcome.” Arian offered his arm and led him back out the way they came.
