## Chapter 12

Caleb gasped awake in a cream colored room with high, intricately molded ceilings and tapestries and reliefs lining the walls. His eyes quickly darted around the familiar architecture, taking in as much as he could.

Every inch of him ached.

He tried to remember why. He’d been on the yacht, watching Arian’s figure shrink on the edge of the dock, his chest aching and tears in his eyes.

He’d been blown up.

“Where am I?” He asked aloud, struggling to sit up against the aches and pains. He hissed, looking down at the IV in the back of his hand.

“Welcome home, my dear brother,” a rich, posh voice drawled from in front of the windows taking up most of the wall to the left of the bed Caleb was in. A man with shoulder-length, straight blonde hair in an impeccably tailored black three-piece suit was smirking down at Caleb. There was a vicious glint darkening his sky-blue eyes to cobalt.

“Andrew,” Caleb acknowledged, gingerly touching his head with a pained groan as he tried to sit up and his temples began to pound with an oncoming migraine. His breathing grew ragged as the pieces began to click together in his mind. “You blew up the yacht, didn’t you?”

“I certainly did,” Andrew smirked.

Caleb pushed aside the pain and forced himself to lean back on his hands as nonchalantly as possible. “I appreciate the honesty. And yet, I *am* still breathing.” Caleb put on his trademark mischievous grin. “Quite disappointed.”

Andrew chuckled, his smirk growing into a sinister grin. “I didn’t destroy that ship to kill you,” he told Caleb as he paced toward the bed. “Quite the opposite; I did it to bring you back.” He leaned over the bed, his thumb tracing a cut along Caleb’s cheekbone with the barest pressure. Caleb couldn’t stop his body from shaking, his eyes wide, his mask gone in the shock of Andrew’s confession. “The demolition must be extremely precise so I can have you back safe and sound, barely a scratch. But your Arian must think you’re dead.” His voice was low, nearly a whisper, but it was anything but comforting. The smile on his face was cruel as he continued to brush at the cut on Caleb’s cheek.

Caleb’s face hardened, jaw clenched tight, lips pursed, as he pushed Andrew’s hand away from him. Andrew scoffed, standing up and adjusting the cuffs of his shirt. “I really should thank Paul. If it weren’t for his sneaky plot against me, I wouldn’t have the faintest idea how to separate you from your protection in Sicily. He just delivered you right to me. Pity Paul had to die for it.” Caleb’s breathing grew more ragged at the news. Paul was a good man, in the end. “Oh, well. He did betray me, so he’s where he belongs now.”

Caleb was shaking harder in rage instead of terror, a sneer pinching his face. He launched himself forward, aiming his fist at Andrew’s smug face, but Andrew easily deflected the blow in Caleb’s weakened state and held his arm out to the side, unbothered. “You monster!” He growled into his brother’s face. “Why? You tried to have me murdered again and again. Why do you want me back now?” It didn’t make sense to Caleb. He and Andrew had never gotten along, even before everything that had happened the past month, but he couldn’t fathom a single reason why Andrew would go through all the trouble of bringing Caleb home unless it was to kill him himself.

Andrew glanced out the window at the palace courtyard. “My coronation is in one week; you are no longer a threat to me. I have won the game of thrones.” He turned back to smile down at Caleb, still clutching his arm. “But in a rather different game…” Andrew trailed off, leaning forward and forcing Caleb to sink back. He ended up almost pinned to the mattress with Andrew looming over him, his Cheshire Cat smile wide and dangerous.

“What different game?” Caleb dared to ask.

“In the game of *love*.” He whispered, and Caleb felt like he was going to be sick. “Never thought I would stand a chance until recently. It never occurred to me that you are into men, Caleb.” Andrew gripped Caleb’s chin and used the almost painful pinch to pull him up until he was sitting up again, their faces far too close together. His words didn’t make sense, and yet a creeping sense of understanding had Caleb’s stomach roiling. “A low-life mob scum bag doesn’t deserve to be with you.” Caleb jerked his head, but Andrew grabbed his chin tighter, forcing him to meet his eyes. “You’re mine and mine alone.” He stared down at Caleb with those cruel blue eyes.

Caleb’s sneer returned with a vengeance, and he pushed hard against Andrew’s chest, falling to the bed in the process. His chest rose and fell in great, heaving breaths. “Angry? My little Caleb.” Andrew’s face was full of amusement, and it only served to make Caleb see red. “Don’t be so difficult, as you were always.”

“You’re disgusting! We’re *brothers*, you *sick fuck!*” Caleb poured every ounce of revulsion into his tone. If he hadn’t just been blown up, he’d probably try to kill his brother where he stood. He couldn’t believe Andrew’s nerve.

Andrew turned and walked to a small desk near the end of the bed before returning with a royal blue folder. He threw it onto the duvet on Caleb’s legs. His voice was harder now, the humor stripped away to reveal the pure malice beneath. “In three days there will be a press conference. At which you will openly accuse Arian Conti of kidnapping and numerous other crimes under coercion, and make it clear that there is nothing romantic between the two of you. Then, you can move on and still be welcomed by our royal family.” The easy, relaxed smile on Andrew’s face sharpened. “But if you have a second thought, I promise you, there will be *endless* ways to make new life here a living hell.” He leaned over Caleb again, face inches away. “Memorize the speech, and don’t you *dare* change a word.” Andrew’s eyes bored into Caleb’s, and he chuckled to himself. “I can’t wait to see Arian Conti’s face when he hears your little speech…” he drawled, strolling back towards the windows, “with his newlywed wife.”

Caleb’s rage was a palpable thing. He was shaking violently; all thoughts aside from the desire to kill his brother had fled his mind.

Andrew was a monster.

How *dare* he use Caleb to hurt Arian.

How *dare* he blow him up and then claim to love him.

How *dare* he expect Caleb to fall in line and bend to Andrew’s every sick, twisted whim.

Caleb was quite finished playing the good little obedient prince. If Andrew wanted him to give a speech, Caleb would do just that. Andrew could drop him into whatever hell he could dream up afterwards. It wouldn’t matter. Caleb knew how to play the game, and now? Now he was out for blood.

+++

Three days after his return to London, Caleb was dragged into the back of a car by his brother’s personal guard and carted to Elizabeth Tower. His armor of choice was a cream suit with a light blue button-up and a royal blue tie with thin, white, diagonal stripes; Caleb knew he needed to look his absolute best to do the most damage. Most of his injuries from the explosion had healed over the past few days, and a stylist met him in an office that had been set aside for Andrew for the day. She diligently covered the yellowed bruises that remained visible and hid the dark circles under his eyes until he looked almost radiantly healthy. Caleb asked her to leave his hair alone, and to his surprise, she did. He ran a hand through it to tousle his curls in the haphazard, charming way he preferred and left it at that.

Andrew strolled in as she was collecting her things. His hands were in his pockets, and he moved with a confidence that spoke of his utter assurance that he had won. It made Caleb want to punch him. His hand clenched into a fist on his thigh, but he made no move to stand.

“Leave us,” Andrew ordered the stylist and guards, and they all bowed and shuffled out. As soon as the door shut, Andrew leisurely paced around Caleb in a circle until he stood directly in front of him. “My dear Caleb. I have such plans for you, if you behave.” He ran the back of one knuckle down the side of Caleb’s face, and Caleb jerked his head away with a sneer. The acidic ache of bile burned in his throat.

“*Don’t* touch me,” he growled, eyes almost luminescent with rage.

“Caleb, Caleb, Caleb.” Andrew sighed, his face pinching in irritation. “Need I remind you what is at stake? If you don’t go out there and give your little speech, exactly as written, I will give one of my own. And trust me, brother mine, I will be *far* more cruel about it. Give your speech, and you can come home. Refuse, and I will introduce you to a world of pain unlike anything you could ever imagine, *starting *with making you watch as I kill that low-life mobster for daring to touch you. It’s your choice.” Andrew tapped his palm against Caleb’s cheek twice before he spun on his heel and left.

+++

Twenty minutes later, Caleb walked to the podium in the press room of the parliament building, clutching the blue folder with his speech in it tightly in his fist. His steps were slow and measured, his eyes downcast, his shoulders hunched forward. He placed the folder onto the podium and took a deep breath, drawing together all of his courage before he raised his eyes to the back of the room where Andrew stood, watching with a smug, vicious smirk. Caleb’s grip tightened on the edges of the podium, and he swallowed roughly to clear the lump of anxiety from his throat.

“Morning, ladies and gentlemen. Many of you have heard about certain rumors circulated about my family and my personal life, and this is exactly why today I stand before you and call for this press conference.” He looked down at the folder, eyes skimming the words Andrew had written for him. “About one month ago, I took a trip to Sicily. As soon as I arrived at customs, my passport was torn up. Before I realized what was going on, I was kidnapped.”

Gasps filled the room, and Caleb’s eyes scanned the journalists’ stunned faces. “Kidnapping a prince is an international felony! Who did it, Your Highness?” One journalist yelled out.

“Was it a terrorist organization?” Another stood and asked just after.

Caleb’s chest rose and fell quickly. This was the moment. This was what Andrew was waiting for. He met Andrew’s cruel ice-blue eyes before dropping his gaze back down to the speech. His fingers were gripping the paper almost hard enough to tear it, his eyes tracking back and forth without seeing the words. He could hear his heartbeat in his ears. “No, it was not by any terror group. The man who kidnapped me was the godfather of the Conti family, Arian Conti.” Caleb looked straight ahead into the crowd and watched as they reacted to his words. There was shock, disbelief, outrage. Behind them all, Andrew huffed out a silent laugh and nodded before he turned and walked through the open doors toward the hall. As soon as Andrew turned his back, Caleb squared his shoulders, smirking down at the folder in his hands before flicking it closed. “*But*,” he said, voice calm and controlled, “there’s more to this alleged kidnapping. The royal family sold me to Arian. They also made me the scapegoat for the horrible crime committed in Sicily by my older brother, Andrew.”

The flash of cameras grew almost blinding as he spoke, and he smiled. Andrew had wanted spectacle, finality, with his little power play. Caleb made sure that it would backfire on him in the most explosive way possible, and he had played his part beautifully for three painfully long, torturous days. Caleb’s giddy grin was relaxed and natural. He had chosen his side, and it was the man who had chosen him, not the family that had sold him. His worst mistake had been trying to be something he was not; he had been trying to be the unobtrusive, agreeable guest, thinking that’s what Arian would want. How foolish. Arian had told the reporters at the Caruso gala that one of his favorite things about him was his inability to do the expected. Clearly Caleb hadn’t been paying enough attention. He was paying attention now, though. It wouldn’t change everything that had happened, but he could at least go down fighting instead of just allowing things to happen to him.

Caleb kept talking, knowing that sooner rather than later he would be dragged from the stage and face whatever hell Andrew had in store for him. “Of course, knowing my brother, he was not going to stop there. He made a few unsuccessful attempts on my life.” He watched with smug satisfaction as Andrew spun to face him, almost shaking with rage that Caleb could feel from across the room. “Arian Conti, however, was the one who saved my life over and over again.”

Andrew was powerless to stop him. Caleb would exonerate Arian, save *his *life this time, and go headlong into his fate with a wide, satisfied smile. “And somewhere down the road, as we spent more time together, we developed affections for each other.” The crowd was chattering loudly now, processing everything that Caleb was telling them. “Don’t look so surprised. What you heard and read is true: I’m gay.” He was roughly taken by the arms by two royal guards, and they began to drag him from the podium. “Though I never thought of myself that way until Arian made himself quite impossible to resist!” Caleb yelled back as he was dragged off the stage. The assembled crowd was going absolutely mental. Caleb felt smug satisfaction curl through his chest like a balm. Arian may still hate him for what he did, but at least he’d be safe from Andrew’s wrath, for a while, at least.

The chandelier in the center of the ceiling shattered, the lights going out as smoke filled the room, and Caleb stumbled. The guards dropped his arms and put their backs to him in a defensive stance as Caleb spun in place. He brought one hand up to cover his nose from the thick smoke. The room became utter chaos. People ran around trying to find their way in the low glow of the emergency backup lights amidst the thick white smoke filling the space. Screams filled the air. People shouted and tripped over one another in their panic.

A hand flashed out and latched around Caleb’s arm, and he startled. His heart leapt into his throat at the sight of Arian in a black leather jacket and a black ball cap pulled low over his face, standing beside him, his grip tight on Caleb’s arm like he might run or disappear if he let go. For a long moment, the world seemed to stop until it was just the two of them. Then Arian pulled and started to drag him toward a side door. He guided Caleb through side halls and service corridors until they emerged into an abandoned alley on the side of the building, his hand never leaving Caleb’s arm as if he needed the reassurance that Caleb was really there. They ran down the alley, hands clasping together as they dove out into the crowd and kept running. There was a bright, joyous smile lighting up Caleb’s face as he kept pace. Sirens pierced through the air all around them, but they kept moving.

Arian had come for him.

Despite everything. Despite Caleb intentionally hurting him. Despite announcing his engagement to Valentina. Despite spending who knows how long of the past three days thinking that the prince was dead.

Arian had come for him.

It made Caleb feel like he was flying.

They didn’t stop running until they hit the edge of the Thames, where two of Arian’s men waited at the bottom of a ramp leading into a yacht. Caleb and Arian slowed to a jog as they boarded the boat and finally to a halt below deck in a seating area overlooking the Thames flowing behind them. Caleb took a second to catch his breath and take in his surroundings as the familiar scents of gun oil, smoke, leather, and suede grounded him.

On the bar behind Arian’s shoulder was a vase overflowing with vibrant red roses and a bottle of champagne. Arian hadn’t planned on leaving without Caleb; that was clear. Caleb’s smile was unstoppable. He stepped forward into Arian’s space and slowly pulled off the cap. Arian was panting heavily, eyes locked on a spot near Caleb’s tie. Slowly, his eyes traced up to meet Caleb’s own. He almost looked like he couldn’t believe that Caleb was standing there before him. “You have surpassed my wildest imagination, godfather. Stirring the pot at the very heart of my nation. Maybe you did associate yourself with a terrorist group.” Caleb teased, dimples shining in his cheeks.

Arian let out a sigh that sounded equal parts exasperation and relief before surging forward into Caleb’s space. “Be quiet,” he whispered a moment before he pulled the prince into his arms and kissed him.

His hands held Caleb’s face like he was the most precious thing in the world as he devoured him. Caleb held tight to Arian’s sides, fingers pressed tightly into the supple leather of his jacket, and he kissed him back just as hungrily. They had made it. They were safe, at least for now. More importantly, they were together.

And most importantly to Caleb, Arian didn’t seem to hate him.

His heart pounded in his chest from more than just their run through the streets of London. He couldn’t contain everything he felt.

“Was it true?” Arian gasped as soon as they parted, foreheads still close together as if neither could bear to part. “What you said just now, that you have affections for me?” There was heat and fire lighting up those green-grey eyes, and Caleb smiled softly, matching the vulnerable expression haunting Arian’s face with honesty on his own.

“That may have been an act of hyperbole,” he joked, his smile growing until his dimples lit up his face, emotion shining bright and open for Arian to see, “but I do love you.”

“I love you too,” Arian’s responding whisper was soft and fragile. Caleb could feel tears pricking at the backs of his eyes, and he rested his hand on Arian’s chest so that he could feel the steady thrum of his heart. “Just like the day you pulled me out of the dirt, hold onto me. I won’t let go again, Caleb. Even if it means standing against the world-“ Caleb cut him off by wrapping his arms around his shoulders and pulling him into a tight hug. Arian’s arms wrapped around his back, and he squeezed him just as tightly.

*This, *Caleb thought, *this was home.*

And he would never give it up again. He would fight, with charm and skill and all of his varied talents to keep true to Arian’s request. He’d hold on until his fingers bled and his strength failed. Never again would he walk away.

Someday, probably soon, there would be a reckoning. Andrew would not take Caleb’s betrayal lightly. He wasn’t scared, though. He had everything he needed right here in his arms.

And he was never letting him go.
