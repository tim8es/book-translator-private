## Chapter 7

In the car, on the way back to the estate, Caleb couldn’t stop running his finger over the curve of his lip, lost in thought. How could he go back to normal after this? Now that he knew what Arian tasted like — rich coffee and smooth liquor — he didn’t know where they stood. He couldn’t quite decide if it had all been for show, the proof the mafia heiress had requested, or an inevitable breaking of the tension between them. It felt like maybe it was both, or had started as one and became the other. He couldn’t imagine that anyone could kiss someone like that, with that much raw hunger and passion, and not mean it. He knew he certainly couldn’t.

The feeling of Arian’s eyes burning into him drew his gaze, and he took a deep breath, aiming for a teasing tone. “Next time you use me, I’d appreciate a warning.”

“The more you wipe, the more it looks like you’re trying to remember how it felt.” Caleb felt caught out; that was pretty much exactly what he’d been doing while silently musing. Arian looked out the window at the passing streetlights, voice still carrying that unidentifiable rumble. “Careful, don’t catch feelings.”

With the question of where they stood answered for him, Caleb locked away everything rolling around in his mind and latched onto the mask of the aloof noble with both unsteady hands. “Funny,” he breathed out, leaning over the console toward Arian. “That kiss came so naturally I’m starting to wonder whether you prefer men or women. Are you deeply closeted, Mr. Conti?”

With an amused huff, Arian leaned into his space, face just a breath away as he purred, low and heated, “If you enjoyed it that much, I don’t mind kissing you again.”

Caleb’s eyes dropped to his lips before meeting his intense green-grey eyes. He was teasing him, he knew. Part of him wanted to reach across the car, drag Arian into him by his tie, and finish what they started at the gala. A larger part of him knew that the consequences of following that train of thought could be more than he could handle. For a long moment neither of them moved, waiting to see how the other would react, and Caleb finally conceded. He leaned back into his seat and adjusted his tie, desperately trying to keep a blush from coloring his cheeks.

They rode in silence for a while until Arian’s low voice cut through it, no louder than a whisper. “I’ll never see the person I loved again.”

Caleb felt the words like shards of ice in his chest. Arian loved someone, and it wasn’t him. He wasn’t sure why that made him hurt so much. He knew that nothing he had with Arian was real. It was a mutually beneficial arrangement, nothing more. He forced a grin onto his face and hoped it showed no sign of the grimace he felt. “Tell me her name,” he offered desperately, partially to put himself out of the misery of wondering who the mystery person was, and partially out of a genuine desire to help Arian. “I know everyone worth knowing. Maybe I can find her for you.”

Arian didn’t get a chance to respond as the front car in their motorcade exploded. The sound of glass cracking drew their eyes forward just as their car skidded to a halt, their driver slumped over the steering wheel with blood trickling down his back from a wound in his neck. “Stay in the car,” Arian ordered, and Caleb nodded, knowing better than to argue. He was a liability at the moment. He was unarmed and had no idea where they were or what was going on.

No sooner had Arian pulled out his gun and stepped from the car than Caleb’s door was being yanked open from behind him. He didn’t even get a chance to call out before he felt a sharp pinch in the side of his neck and everything went dark.

A blinding light woke Caleb up, and he blinked away the spots from his vision. His body was sore, like he’d been in the same position for too long, and his wrists and ankles hurt. He smelled the heavy, cloying scent of cigar smoke and stale sweat. The din of several overlapping voices all shouting at once assaulted his ringing ears. Where was he? The last thing he remembered was the explosion.

He tried to move his hands and couldn’t. Purple ribbons were tied tightly around his wrists to the point of being painful, and he could feel similar bonds trapping his ankles. He couldn’t see much past the light and the smoke surrounding him, but he saw bars of a gold cage around him in a circle, smoke-hazy figures moving around just outside of it. His breathing grew rough as he started to panic, his breaths restricted to just his nose because of a long piece of tape he felt pulling at his lips. He couldn’t stop himself from shaking in fear.

A face came into view just outside the bars: a goateed man with slicked-back hair looked down at him and then turned to address the crowd he could barely make out. “One full night with one of Europe’s oldest bloodlines!” The man announced, causing cheers to go up throughout the crowd and cold dread to pour through Caleb’s veins. “Let’s start at ten big ones!”

Caleb threw his shoulder hard against the bars. That couldn’t mean what he thought it meant. This couldn’t be happening. The smoke had cleared somewhat, and as his eyes adjusted to the spotlight illuminating him and the surrounding darkness, he started to be able to make out some more of his surroundings. It looked like a club of some kind, dark and full of men in all different variations of dress. Some wore suits, others wore leather, and others still wore nothing more than chains draped across bare skin.

“Twenty!” Someone quickly shouted from far too close, and the announcer grew visibly more excited. Caleb shrunk further against the bars, as far from the voice as possible.

“Twenty big ones goes to the gentleman in the front!” He called out.

“Thirty for that royal ass!” Another voice yelled.

“Thirty! Going once!” The announcer crowed.

“Forty!”

“Fifty!”

“Sixty thousand! Do I hear more?”

Caleb bashed his bound hands against the bars, struggling harder as the numbers climbed higher and higher, but the metal didn’t budge. Hot tears escaped down his face as he realized he was trapped. Bound, silenced, humiliated, and being sold to the highest bidder. People began to crowd around the cage, sneering and laughing. Some were catcalling, others saying things that made his blood run cold. The bids were still climbing higher, but he couldn’t focus.

He couldn’t breathe.

He couldn’t think.

Movement near the back of the room caught his eye, and he saw a man standing in the doorway in a black suit, staring right at him. His heart leapt into his throat at the instinctual relief he felt, but he couldn’t be sure it was Arian. He couldn’t see the man clearly from this distance in the low light with everyone crowding around the cage. The man did nothing for several long moments, and then Caleb blinked, and he was gone.

“A hundred and fifty thousand! This is un-*fucking*-believable!” The announcer screamed, drawing Caleb’s attention back to him. “Going *once*,” he paused, but no one bid. “150k, going *twice!*”

Caleb had really thought that he could trust Arian. He wasn’t even sure the man he’d seen had been him, but it had to be, right? The man was the right build, and there was a certain way Arian carried himself that Caleb felt like he recognized. Fresh tears burned down his face. “*Sold* to the gentleman in the next-" the announcer declared, and then cut himself off. “*Wait! *A new bidder?” Caleb tried to look past the announcer, but he was standing too close to the side of the cage. “This gentleman here, are *you* placing a bid?”

Several seconds of silence followed, and then a low rumble cut through the room.  “No.”

“Sir, you’re not bidding?”

“No. A better offer, though far more reassuring and tantalizing.” The voice was unyielding and final. It sounded like the rumble of a lion, just before a roar, all tightly coiled power and deadly promise.

A gunshot rang out like a sudden crack of thunder, making Caleb jump and curl his body tighter into the curve of the cage. Deafening screams filled the room as people started running. Several more shots rang out, and Caleb saw bodies drop, swirling smoke through the stale air.

Finally, the crowd parted, and Caleb drew in a sharp breath. Arian was walking toward the cage, steps even and measured, face a tight mask of permafrost and promised death, gun smoking in his hand. He stopped a few feet away and glared down at the announcer, who had hidden on his hands and knees at the foot of the raised platform the cage sat upon.

“Open it,” Arian growled, and the man scrambled to obey. He fumbled with the keys and scurried away as soon as the lock clicked open, but Arian didn’t even glance at him, his eyes cataloguing every square inch of Caleb from his bound ankles and hands to the shallow cut across the bridge of his nose to the fresh tears wetting his face to the tape across his mouth and the unstoppable tremble of his body.

He stepped up to the open cage door, and Caleb tipped his head back to meet his eyes, tears still falling. Arian had come for him. The thought played like a mantra through his mind, tattooing itself into his chest with every erratic beat of his terrified heart. Arian stowed his gun and stepped forward, dropping into a crouch at Caleb’s feet. Carefully, he untied the ribbons around Caleb’s ankles and then his hands before gently peeling the tape back from his mouth. Caleb gasped in a lungful of air, eyes sliding shut for a moment as the sudden rush of oxygen made the room spin. One large hand came up to gingerly cup the side of his face, Arian’s thumb swiping through the tear tracks on his cheek. He then wiped the ones on the other side away with the back of his knuckles.

Arian’s face was calm, but his eyes were impossibly bright. There was both fire and ice there, and it was so intense that Caleb almost had to look away. He looked positively shattered. “Valentina orchestrated the attack,” Arian told him, still crouched down in front of his trembling body. “By the time I arrived, this shit show had just started.”

Caleb startled. “Just started,” Arian had said. “Hang on a second. You’ve been here the whole time?”

“Yes.”

“Every second of it?” Caleb clarified in disbelief, watching the ice harden across Arian’s features at the reminder of all that had just happened.

His voice was a lethal blade, cold and unyielding, and promised vengeance. “Every second.” The two words were growled with so much venom that Caleb let out a harsh breath.

“And yet you did nothing.” Caleb’s smile was a fragile, broken thing. He had watched Arian buy out an entire store that morning on a whim, but outbidding the creeps that were tormenting and willing to buy Caleb was apparently a step too far. He scoffed bitterly. “Just sat there and let it all happen while watching me in agony.” Betrayal burned like hard liquor on his tongue.

“I stalled to memorize everyone that raised their hands and placed a bid on you,” Arian argued, eyes boring into Caleb’s soul and freezing him in place. “They thought they could tease you, humiliate you, with no consequences. They were all wrong because I was about to single-handedly take them all out.”

Caleb shook his head in disbelief, breath stolen by the declaration. Arian had killed a dozen men, if not more, for daring to bid on Caleb. That intensity was unlike anything he’d ever known. He knew he was important to Arian’s plans, but he didn’t know he would go to those lengths for him. He felt fresh tears track down his face. “You’re insane,” he whispered. He searched Arian’s eyes for any sign that he was teasing and found nothing but burning sincerity.

“Probably,” Arian admitted flatly, as if Caleb had told him the sky was blue. “Come on.” Arian extended his hand, palm up. “I’m getting you out of here.”

Caleb slowly reached out and placed his hand in Arian’s, whose grip tightened almost immediately. His thumb rubbed soothingly across Caleb’s knuckles. A small, teasing smile curled onto Arian’s lips, some of the haunting ire disappearing from his eyes. “No flinching this time?”

Caleb’s breath hitched, and he stared into Arian’s face, heart beating wildly. He came to several conclusions between the span of one breath and the next. First, on some level Arian cared for him, or at least his well-being. So much so that he had walked into a club alone and killed several men without a second thought for humiliating the prince. Second, he was drawn towards the intensity of Arian, not scared of it. Yes, the man was dangerous, and yes, he could kill Caleb easily if he really wanted to, but he was also deceptively charming and playful. He made Caleb feel more alive than he ever had. Third, Caleb had spent the better part of the past two weeks trying to convince himself that he could stick to the rules he had set in place for them. Two simple rules to keep their fake marriage contract strictly professional and uncomplicated, and yet it felt like a Herculean task to abide by them, particularly the first. Finally, Caleb admitted to himself that he had feelings for Arian that went deeper than simple attraction or lust. The man was an inferno and an ice storm all rolled into one and shackled inside of a crisp black suit. He felt deeply and hid most of it from the world, but the more Caleb saw him, the more he noticed. Arian’s emotions didn’t shine openly on his face like Caleb’s own had a tendency to, but rather hid in minute shifts of his mercurial eyes and the set of his eyebrows or the curve of his mouth.

Caleb was done fighting it.

He surged up onto his knees and pulled Arian into a kiss.
