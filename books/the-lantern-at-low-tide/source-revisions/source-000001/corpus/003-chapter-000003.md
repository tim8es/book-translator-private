<!-- bt-block:000002 -->
The Letter in the Glass

<!-- bt-block:000003 -->
✦

<!-- bt-block:000004 -->
Every morning, Ada walked the narrow beach before the fishing boats returned. She collected what the tide left behind: green stones, pieces of rope, and once, a silver spoon with a bent handle. On the first cold morning of October, she found a bottle sealed with blue wax.

<!-- bt-block:000005 -->
Inside was a letter, folded into a square no larger than a matchbox. Its paper was dry. The ink, though faded, was still readable: *If the lantern goes dark, look beneath the last step.* There was no signature, only a drawing of the old lighthouse on the eastern cliff.

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
The lighthouse had been empty for twenty years. Its keeper had vanished during a storm, and the town had agreed never to speak of him again. Ada knew this because her grandmother always changed the subject whenever the lighthouse appeared through the kitchen window.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
That evening, Ada put the bottle in her coat pocket and climbed the cliff path. Wind pressed against the grass. Above her, the dark lantern room looked like a closed eye. She counted the stone steps leading to the door. There were thirty-seven. Beneath the last one, something small reflected the moonlight: a brass key.
