*2025, Le Mans*

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
Alex made his way out of the pre-race conference room, slowly walking towards his motorhome. He had Practice in a few hours, so he had to get ready, go over the track data once more, fine-tune the setup.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
His thoughts strayed back to the conference.

<!-- bt-block:000008 -->
He’d gotten asked many questions, this time. About how well he was doing, how strong the Ducati was, how good Gresini was functioning as a team. And of course, he’d gotten questions about his race last week at Jerez.

<!-- bt-block:000009 -->
Jerez, where Alex had scored his first win.

<!-- bt-block:000010 -->
In the end, Alex had to admit— to himself, he’d never say as such to reporters— that it had felt just as good as he’d ever imagined it could, and yet not nearly enough at the same time. A relief, more than anything.

<!-- bt-block:000011 -->
He was a race winner, finally, after five years. Five long, hard years, of working as hard as he could and never giving up.

<!-- bt-block:000012 -->
But, it was hard, too. To know that it had taken him five years to achieve what it had taken Marc one month.

<!-- bt-block:000013 -->
Alex tried not to think about it too much. He’d known, even before starting MotoGP, that his chances of ever coming close to equaling what his brother had achieved were negligible. Winning even one world championship was already more than most riders could ever dream of, so who did Alex think he was, to want something more?

<!-- bt-block:000014 -->
But even with that knowledge, five years for one win was a bitter pill to swallow. Reality crashing down on him yet again, as though it hadn’t done enough of that already.

<!-- bt-block:000015 -->
Alex shook his head, kicking at a rock on the ground and watching it bounce along the asphalt.

<!-- bt-block:000016 -->
The day after his win, Marc had dragged him out to a celebration with friends and family, though Alex himself had said it really wasn’t necessary. And it had been delightful, really, but . . .  Well. It had also felt a bit demeaning, was the thing.

<!-- bt-block:000017 -->
It was hard to be proud of one win, with Marc at his side. Alex tried to be lighthearted about it, tried to joke around, tried to be happy for himself. He really was doing well this year; the bike felt good, and Alex had been getting podium after podium. But Marc had been getting win after win. He barely even celebrated them anymore, beyond the actual podium itself. Sometimes not even then.

<!-- bt-block:000018 -->
And it wasn't Marc's fault, really, that wins were so common to him that they weren't really worth celebrating anymore. It just made it a bit hard for Alex.

<!-- bt-block:000019 -->
Alex had also called Valentino, the day after the win.

<!-- bt-block:000020 -->
Valentino had congratulated him, of course, but then he’d gotten down to business. Dissecting Alex’s race, his pace, his tyre management, his line choices. They’d spent an hour just going over his braking points with regard to his bike setup, making tiny potential tweaks here and there for him to try out next weekend.

<!-- bt-block:000021 -->
It had been mentally exhausting, but it had also been . . . nice. Really nice.

<!-- bt-block:000022 -->
Really nice of Valentino, of course, in spending all night helping Alex with his racing. But also really nice for Alex, who finally had a chance to properly decompress. He wasn’t really a partier, not really someone who loved going to clubs. He’d drink here and there, but if given the choice, he’d rather stay in. Watch a movie. Play a video game. Go over racing lines for the Le Mans MotoGP circuit for four hours. Play with his dogs.

<!-- bt-block:000023 -->
Compared to the dinner with Marc, his talk with Valentino had been far more relaxing. Had even felt like a more sincere celebration of his victory, in a strange way— had, at the very least, felt less demeaning.

<!-- bt-block:000024 -->
That thought brought Alex back to the earlier press conference, when he’d been talking about how well his year had been going. There was one point on which his mind was still caught. One annoyance that still rubbed him the wrong way, a splinter digging into his skin.

<!-- bt-block:000025 -->
They’d asked, naturally, about what exactly had changed this year. Alex had, naturally, replied that if he knew that, he’d change it every year. After a round of polite and insincere laughs, Alex had leaned on the obvious answer; that his team had been working hard to make both the bike and its rider better, and this was just the fruits of their labor.

<!-- bt-block:000026 -->
Then came the setup question: who has helped you most? And Alex, like a stone rolling downhill, had only one answer: Marc.

<!-- bt-block:000027 -->
Of course it had to be Marc. He could name any number of other people— his trainer, his analysts, his mechanics, his parents, his friends, his dogs— but none of it really mattered. Marc was the one who had made all the difference. Always had, always would.

<!-- bt-block:000028 -->
But that was the thing. It hadn’t all been Marc.

<!-- bt-block:000029 -->
A lot of it, in fact, had been Valentino.

<!-- bt-block:000030 -->
During the day, he’d train with Marc. Go riding, on flat-tracks, on circuits, going over his set-ups and his lines and his movements. Going again and again, until his arms wouldn’t hold him, until his legs burned and his chest ached.

<!-- bt-block:000031 -->
But during the night, he’d call Valentino. They’d talk about what he’d done that day, what he’d done in the last week. Alex would explain his thought processes, and Valentino would offer his opinion. Alex would reveal his ideas for how to improve in the future, and Valentino would reveal things he’d done to improve in the past. Alex would talk, and Valentino would listen. Then Valentino would talk, and Alex would listen.

<!-- bt-block:000032 -->
And in the mornings, Alex would arrive on track with Marc, full of ideas and motivation to put those ideas into practice. Once in a while he’d do something Valentino had recommended, and Marc would ask how he’d come up with it. Alex would tell him it came to him in a dream— half as a joke, half as a blatant lie. Marc, thankfully, never pushed. Maybe he really did think Alex had the most helpful dreams in existence, when what Alex really had was Valentino’s assistance.

<!-- bt-block:000033 -->
But, Alex could never say that. He had to keep his thoughts behind gritted teeth, had to bite his cheek and his tongue as he smiled for the cameras, thanking his team and his family and his brother, but never, ever Valentino. No matter how much help he’d been, on and off track, with Alex’s riding and Alex’s emotions and Alex’s doubts and Alex’s fears, he couldn’t say anything about it. As far as the rest of the world was concerned, they could never be anything to each other, not even for an instant. And Alex understood why that was true. He knew why the secrecy was necessary.

<!-- bt-block:000034 -->
He knew why it was necessary, and yet frustration still curled at the base of his neck, hot and sharp and prickling, whenever he thought about their situation lately. Lately, and lately more, because the entire thing was slowly becoming unbearable to him.

<!-- bt-block:000035 -->
Alex didn’t just want to talk to Valentino in 3am phone calls. He didn’t just want to see him hidden in garage motorhomes, in liminal moments that felt as fragile as they were meaningful. He didn’t just want to smile at him in fleeting meetings, to hug him once every two months. He didn’t want to have to avoid him like the plague on race weekends, to pretend he didn’t think anything of Valentino, didn’t care about him beyond being a legend of the sport, if even then at all. He didn’t want to have to veer in the other direction whenever he randomly ran into him in the paddock, he didn’t want to have to look away.

<!-- bt-block:000036 -->
It hurt, to have to look away.

<!-- bt-block:000037 -->
No, Alex wanted to be able to walk up to Valentino anywhere. He wanted to be able to chat with him, to smile with him, to laugh with him. He wanted to be able to touch him, to hug him, to hold his hand. He wanted to be able to go to his house, to his ranch, to ride motorcycles with him, to just spend time with him. He wanted to be able to eat dinner with him at their homes, to stay up talking all night afterwards— to wake up the next morning with a crick in his neck and a low pain in his back from sleeping on the couch, both of them together, and then go to their shared kitchen to make coffee. Alex would cook them breakfast while Valentino chatted, about anyone and anything, and Alex would listen and smile and laugh, and they would eat together, and joke together, and be happy together, as they always were when they were together—

<!-- bt-block:000038 -->
Alex froze. Went over his thoughts again.

<!-- bt-block:000039 -->
Realization sunk into him like a ship’s anchor into the arctic ocean, icy and inescapable.

<!-- bt-block:000040 -->
Oh, no. Oh, no, no, no.

<!-- bt-block:000041 -->
He couldn’t— no. He couldn’t. He shouldn’t. He didn’t.

<!-- bt-block:000042 -->
. . . Except, he did, didn’t he?

<!-- bt-block:000043 -->
Alex closed his eyes. Pressed a hand to his forehead.

<!-- bt-block:000044 -->
Alex had known he was attracted to Valentino for a long time. He had been attracted to him since he was quite young, actually, though in a more passive sort of way at that age, the same sort of attraction that he felt when he saw models on a magazine. But getting to know the older rider personally had done nothing to lessen that feeling— had, in fact, made it bloom into something far warmer, far sharper, growing teeth with age, as many of Alex’s desires had.

<!-- bt-block:000045 -->
But, that had never really bothered him. Certainly not like this did.

<!-- bt-block:000046 -->
Because it was one thing to be attracted to Valentino— everyone who knew Valentino had a crush on him at some point in their lives, Alex was convinced of that, or at least every MotoGP rider did— but it was quite another to actually have genuine feelings for him.

<!-- bt-block:000047 -->
And, oh, Alex realized in that moment. He definitely had feelings for Valentino. Not the friendly kind, either.

<!-- bt-block:000048 -->
“Fuck,” he murmured under his breath.

<!-- bt-block:000049 -->
The day was dreary, cold and overcast, a perfect backdrop for the emotional turmoil Alex was now going through.

<!-- bt-block:000050 -->
Because there was no way this would end well. The best Alex could hope for was that he’d eventually just . . . get over his feelings. Get over Valentino.

<!-- bt-block:000051 -->
But it didn’t feel good, to think about getting over Valentino. It meant not wanting the smiles anymore, not wanting to spend the night talking anymore, not wanting to wake up next to him anymore. And sure, friends could want to do that every once in a while, too— but Alex wanted it all the time. And he didn’t want to stop wanting it.

<!-- bt-block:000052 -->
He wasn’t sure how he’d even go about it, was the other issue. Alex didn’t want to stop talking to Valentino, didn’t want to draw any sort of line between them. Valentino would have no idea what Alex was going through, and so he’d just take it as a personal slight. Knowing Valentino’s personality, that would go terribly, especially since Alex couldn’t, of course, tell him the truth—

<!-- bt-block:000053 -->
Alex froze again, as he felt his phone vibrating in his pocket. Hesitantly, he brought the device out. Swallowed, as he saw the contact name on the screen.

<!-- bt-block:000054 -->
One ring. Two. Five.

<!-- bt-block:000055 -->
At the seventh ring, Alex finally pressed ‘Accept’.

<!-- bt-block:000056 -->
“Ciao, Alex,” came Valentino’s voice from the other side.

<!-- bt-block:000057 -->
“Ciao, Pipa,” Alex said quietly, relieved that his voice sounded relatively normal. “What’s up?”

<!-- bt-block:000058 -->
“Just making breakfast. Felt like talking,” Valentino replied, easy as anything. “Are you getting ready for practice?”

<!-- bt-block:000059 -->
“In a bit,” Alex said. Closed his eyes. “What are you making?”

<!-- bt-block:000060 -->
Alex remained mostly quiet after that, letting Valentino’s voice wash over him. The softness of it, the warm domesticity of the conversation. He could hear the sound of chopping in the background, the boiling of water, the sizzling of a pan. He could almost smell the coffee, the heating oil, taste the morning dew on his tongue.

<!-- bt-block:000061 -->
The yearning felt like a living thing in his chest, wailing and thrashing about. Want coated his throat like oil, making it hard to breathe.

<!-- bt-block:000062 -->
In his ear, he could hear Valentino making a low humming noise, quiet and relaxed and pleased. The pans sizzled in the background; the coffee machine hissed.

<!-- bt-block:000063 -->
Alex scrunched his eyes tightly.

<!-- bt-block:000064 -->
He hadn’t realized it could hurt so much, to want so much.

<!-- bt-block:000065 -->
“I’m sorry, Vale, but I need to go,” he said. Paused. Opened his eyes, panicked, looking around. No one was there. He exhaled shakily. “Sorry, I mean, Pipa. I— I need to go.”

<!-- bt-block:000066 -->
Valentino was quiet for a second. “Careful,” he murmured.

<!-- bt-block:000067 -->
“I know,” Alex replied, quiet, ashamed. “I’m sorry, I got distracted. But no one’s around, it was fine.”

<!-- bt-block:000068 -->
“If you say so,” Valentino said. “Ciao, Alex. Talk later.”

<!-- bt-block:000069 -->
“Yeah, talk later,” Alex said, and brought the phone away from his ear. Hesitated for a moment before ending the call. Took another deep breath, as he leaned against the garage wall, raising his eyes to the grey sky above.

<!-- bt-block:000070 -->
The yearning was still there, curling around his lungs. The want seeped into his mouth, oily on his tongue.

<!-- bt-block:000071 -->
Alex took another deep breath. Another.

<!-- bt-block:000072 -->
Grabbed the yearning, grabbed the want, and crushed them into the depths of his chest. Locked them into the deepest parts of himself, where no one would ever see them, where no one would ever know they even existed.

<!-- bt-block:000073 -->
Alex, of course, would know.

<!-- bt-block:000074 -->
But, there was nothing he could do about that now.

<!-- bt-block:000075 -->
Now that he’d realized what the yearning was, what the want entailed, he’d never be able to look away from them entirely. Never be able to ignore that they existed, no matter how hard he tried to pretend.

<!-- bt-block:000076 -->
But, no one else would know. And that was what really mattered.

<!-- bt-block:000077 -->
After all, there was no possibility of the yearning ever being reciprocated. No possibility of the want ever being appeased. So all Alex could do was keep them locked, keep them hidden, and wait until they passed away.

<!-- bt-block:000078 -->
The sky was overcast, dreary and grey.

<!-- bt-block:000079 -->
Alex hoped, selfishly, that it would rain.

<!-- bt-block:000080 -->
*2025, Misano*

<!-- bt-block:000081 -->
Alex quietly, carefully closed the motorhome door behind him. Made his way inside the darkened garage, until he saw a light peeking out near the VR46 bikes, reflecting against the neon yellow paint and bathing the motorhome in a warm glow.

<!-- bt-block:000082 -->
Leaning against the counters was, as always, the person Alex was there to meet. Eyebrows furrowed, head tilted, as he poured over telemetry documents for . . . Ducati, if Alex wasn’t mistaken. So, he was focused on helping Pecco for today.

<!-- bt-block:000083 -->
That was fine, Alex thought. It was Misano, so it made sense for Valentino to be focused on his academy riders rather than Alex.

<!-- bt-block:000084 -->
Alex paused. Went over his thoughts again.

<!-- bt-block:000085 -->
Hm. No, that wasn’t right. Valentino should be focused on his academy riders in general. That he was spending time with Alex was very nice of him, really, but he didn’t owe Alex anything.

<!-- bt-block:000086 -->
“Ciao, Vale,” he greeted softly, not wanting to startle Valentino.

<!-- bt-block:000087 -->
Valentino still jumped slightly at his voice. Alex tried not to smile. From the glare Valentino shot him, he hadn’t quite succeeded.

<!-- bt-block:000088 -->
“Ciao, Alex,” Valentino said, turning the papers over— a bit uselessly, Alex thought, since he’d already seen them, but he let him have his illusion of privacy. “How are you doing? Ready for the race tomorrow?”

<!-- bt-block:000089 -->
Alex shrugged, leaning against the counter by Valentino’s side. “For the most part, yeah. There’s still some things I’m not sure about, but I don’t think going over them again will help, either.”

<!-- bt-block:000090 -->
“Are you sure? We can at least talk about it, if you like.” Alex glanced at the papers in Valentino’s hand. Valentino followed his gaze. “No, I can’t give you Pecco’s data. Corporate espionage is not part of my friendship, sorry.”

<!-- bt-block:000091 -->
“As if I’d want Pecco’s data, have you been watching the races?” Alex scoffed. Paused, at the dry glare Valentino gave him in return. “Sorry. Uh. Sure, I’ll take his data?”

<!-- bt-block:000092 -->
Valentino’s glare immediately loosened into a laugh. “No, you’re right. You wouldn’t want it. He’d want your data, if anything.”

<!-- bt-block:000093 -->
“Corporate espionage isn’t part of my friendship either, sorry.”

<!-- bt-block:000094 -->
Valentino let out a theatrical sigh. “I tried.”

<!-- bt-block:000095 -->
“What I was going to say was, I don’t mind talking about the race. But I don’t want you to feel like you have to help me, if you have other riders you’d rather focus on.” Alex gestured towards the stack of Pecco’s telemetry. “Like, you know. Your academy.”

<!-- bt-block:000096 -->
Valentino snorted. “Alex, please. Yes, they are my academy, but they are all professional riders in their own right. I can give pointers here and there, but if they still needed my help to improve at this point, that would be a bit disappointing.” He waved the papers off to the side. “Especially this one, with his two world championships. He’s going through a rough patch, yes, but he’ll figure himself out, I’m sure.”

<!-- bt-block:000097 -->
Alex hoped so, too. He didn’t particularly care for Pecco, certainly not like Valentino did, but his performance this year had been painful to watch, for all of them. “So, it’s just me that needs help.”

<!-- bt-block:000098 -->
Valentino rolled his eyes. “You do not need my help either, no. Especially not with your, you know. Rest of your team.”

<!-- bt-block:000099 -->
“Yeah, let’s call him that,” Alex said with a dry laugh.

<!-- bt-block:000100 -->
“My point is,” Valentino continued, ignoring Alex, “my help is not necessary. I am just having fun, helping you.”

<!-- bt-block:000101 -->
“Oh.” Alex squinted at Valentino. “Really?”

<!-- bt-block:000102 -->
“Yes, of course. I would not help, if it was not fun. Sorry, but is true. If you were annoying to teach, I would not.” Valentino grinned. “But you are not annoying, so all is good.”

<!-- bt-block:000103 -->
“Glad to hear that.”

<!-- bt-block:000104 -->
“But, if you don’t want to talk, I was actually going to suggest something else for today.” He gestured for Alex to follow him, and began walking back to the entrance of the garage. “Come on.”

<!-- bt-block:000105 -->
“Where are we going?” Alex asked as they reached the door, a little hesitant. They'd always met at the garages, because they knew they were relatively safe compared to the rest of the paddock. At the very least, they'd never gone anywhere else once they'd met there, so Alex wasn't sure what exactly Valentino was planning.

<!-- bt-block:000106 -->
“Not too far, don’t worry.” Valentino paused with his hand on the handle, turning back to Alex. “Do you trust me?”

<!-- bt-block:000107 -->
“Yes,” Alex replied immediately, and only a moment later realized that maybe he should have hesitated, just for a second.

<!-- bt-block:000108 -->
Valentino, too, looked a bit startled at the speediness of his answer, if not the answer itself. But he had to know that Alex trusted him, right? Surely, by this point, it had to be obvious. Or maybe he was joking?

<!-- bt-block:000109 -->
Alex had no time to contemplate the matter further as Valentino smiled at him— sweet and pleased, Alex observed, trying desperately to control his heartbeat— and then pushed the door open. “Okay, then follow me.”

<!-- bt-block:000110 -->
The paddock was smattered with darkness, a number of light posts keeping visibility to the minimum required for safety reasons. A familiar sight, to Alex, on these secret meeting nights— less familiar, however, was Alex's secret meeting companion walking around with him.

<!-- bt-block:000111 -->
“Are you sure about this? What if someone sees us?” Alex whispered, as Valentino led them through the patches where the light didn’t reach, hiding against garages and motorhomes as they made their way to the edge of the paddock. Alex was used to making the trek to the VR46 garage and back, sure, but this was far beyond that.

<!-- bt-block:000112 -->
“This is Misano. I know every nook and cranny of this circuit, every secret way and exit. No one will see us, if I do not want them to,” Valentino replied, as he clambered over a railing.

<!-- bt-block:000113 -->
Alex followed after him, adrenaline pumping through his veins. If we got caught like this, he thought, they’re going to think we’re trying to steal something. Whether that was better or worse than what they were actually doing, he wasn’t sure.

<!-- bt-block:000114 -->
Suddenly Alex was tugged to the side, and he barely managed to swallow back a yelp as he was unceremoniously shoved into the narrow space between two shipping containers.

<!-- bt-block:000115 -->
“Quiet,” Valentino hissed at him, and Alex pressed his lips tightly together to swallow back any other noise he might have made as he finally heard them— footsteps and voices, moving in their direction.

<!-- bt-block:000116 -->
Alex could feel his heart beating rabbit-fast in his chest as he pressed himself to the wall, Valentino pressed tightly against his side. Both of them held their breath, staying as still as they could while the group of people passed by the side of the container, never once looking in their direction.

<!-- bt-block:000117 -->
They remained there for a few seconds after the group was gone, just to make sure it was far enough away. The space was dark, and secluded, and the two of them were standing very close together, and Alex tried very hard not to think about other situations he’d like to be in that shared some of those characteristics.

<!-- bt-block:000118 -->
Thankfully, he didn’t have to try for too long, because Valentino finally pulled away and went to peer outside. Seeing the coast was clear, he motioned for Alex to once again follow him.

<!-- bt-block:000119 -->
“They almost saw us,” Alex hissed as they walked out— a bit accusing, though he could barely hold back his laugh.

<!-- bt-block:000120 -->
“But they didn’t,” Valentino sing-songed back at him, voice quiet, smile wide and eyes brilliant.

<!-- bt-block:000121 -->
Alex’s heart continued beating, rabbit-fast, against his chest.

<!-- bt-block:000122 -->
They didn’t run into anyone else the rest of the way— to Alex’s slight disappointment— and soon found themselves walking about the circuit perimeter. Then up, up the hill by turn 6, over another barrier, and then further up.

<!-- bt-block:000123 -->
“Here,” Valentino said, stopping at the crest of the hill, where a number of trees formed a somewhat secluded space— a facade of privacy, if not quite the truth of it. “This is my favorite spot in the circuit.” He looked around for a moment, nodding to himself as he watched the distant lights of the stands and further lights of the city. He paused, then, as he looked back at Alex. “You know, I’ve never actually brought anyone else here before.”

<!-- bt-block:000124 -->
Alex tried, desperately, to ignore the fluttering in his stomach. Seriously, what was he, eighteen, to fall for a line like that? More importantly, why was Valentino using a line like that on Alex?

<!-- bt-block:000125 -->
“I bet you say that to all the pretty girls,” Alex replied, clearly joking, and yet also slightly calling Valentino out for what had to be a blatant lie.

<!-- bt-block:000126 -->
Valentino laughed. “Only the prettiest ones,” he said, and again, Alex felt deeply ashamed at how a line like that could make him flustered. Yes, it was Valentino saying it, but still. “No, but, seriously,” Valentino continued, and he did sound a bit more serious, “I only really found this place after I retired. It is, you know, a recent favorite. So I really haven’t brought anyone else here before. Not on purpose, just never thought to.”

<!-- bt-block:000127 -->
“Okay, I believe you,” Alex said, still joking slightly, but he did genuinely believe Valentino was telling the truth now. Which, to his shame, only made the fluttering in his stomach get worse. He shoved the feeling down, pushing it aside as firmly as he could. “Why is it your favorite?”

<!-- bt-block:000128 -->
“It’s calm. Quiet. Perfect for the night before a race.” Valentino sat down on the ground, patting the space beside him as he looked up at Alex. “Come on, join me.”

<!-- bt-block:000129 -->
“I’m sure you can find other quiet places in the paddock,” Alex said, but he did join Valentino on the ground. From that perspective, the trees blocked most of the light from the paddock and the nearby town. The sense of seclusion was far more poignant, and Alex felt a sudden flicker of nervousness. He pushed it down as hard as he could. “Like, you know, the garage we just came from.”

<!-- bt-block:000130 -->
Valentino sighed, deep and long-suffering. “Shush, now. Lay down and look up at the sky. Look at the stars. Look at the moon.”

<!-- bt-block:000131 -->
Alex turned to find that Valentino was, in fact, already lying down on the ground. He didn’t care at all about getting his clothes dirty, Alex supposed— though, to be fair, Alex didn’t really care about that either. Not, especially, in this situation. So, with only a moment of hesitation, Alex also lay down. Keeping himself at Valentino’s side— not too far, but not too close, either, no matter how much part of Alex might want to move closer. His fingers tingled at his sides with the urge to reach out, to try and hold Valentino’s hand, if only for a moment.

<!-- bt-block:000132 -->
“See? Isn’t it beautiful?” Valentino murmured. His voice had gone soft, gentle like it only did when he felt truly relaxed. A rare occasion, and Alex’s urge to hold his hand only grew— to hold any part of him, whatever part Valentino would allow him. “Doesn’t it just make all your worries go away? Doesn’t just seeing this make you feel calm, happy?”

<!-- bt-block:000133 -->
Alex took a deep breath, slowly released it. Decided not to tell Valentino that, while he was indeed looking at the sky, at the stars, at the moon— and they were, indeed, beautiful— he was only thinking about the person lying beside him. There was no need, after all, to ruin the atmosphere with his own feelings, with his own yearning, his own rising greed.

<!-- bt-block:000134 -->
Alex already had all of this. He could not ask for anything more. No matter how much he might want it.

<!-- bt-block:000135 -->
“Yes,” he replied. Closed his eyes. “Thank you for showing me this. I am . . . happy.”
