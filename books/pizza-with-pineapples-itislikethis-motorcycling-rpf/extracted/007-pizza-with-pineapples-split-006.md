<!-- bt-block:000005 -->
Alex had realized it at some point: everyone thought he hated Valentino.

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
It wasn’t an unfair assumption, he supposed. Being Marc’s brother, the antipathy had a natural source— an obvious transversal, a probable conclusion. It would be perfectly normal for Alex to hate Valentino. Expected, really.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
But it was a bit of a funny assumption, too, because Alex didn’t think he’d ever said anything particularly negative about Valentino. Had generally been decisively positive, actually, at least as far as he could remember. And it wasn’t all PR pretense, either. Alex had always had genuine respect for the older rider’s accomplishments— some more than others, admittedly— and he saw no reason to deny that. It certainly wouldn’t help him to pretend otherwise, either.

<!-- bt-block:000008 -->
Now, admittedly, while he’d never said anything openly negative about Valentino, he’d also never actively corrected the misconceptions. Quite simply, it didn’t benefit him to do so. At least, not while Marc and Valentino still had their own little— hmm, game— going on.

<!-- bt-block:000009 -->
But, Alex wasn’t a part of that. Had stepped away from it, pretty much from the beginning, as soon as both Marc and Valentino made it clear it was an issue between them, and no one else. Alex was, at best, collateral damage, and he intended to keep his involvement at that level, if not below it.

<!-- bt-block:000010 -->
In any case, the point was, Alex didn’t hate Valentino. Hadn’t ever, really, even before they’d begun talking. Had disliked him for a while, yes, but even back then, it was hard to actually hate him when Alex knew that Valentino respected him as a rider, rather than as Marc’s brother.

<!-- bt-block:000011 -->
Alex wished he could say the same of everyone else in the grid, but, well. He couldn’t. He knew what they said about him, the moment his back was turned. How he didn’t deserve his position, how he wasn’t talented enough to be a factory rider, how all his accolades were Marc’s throw-aways, how all his results were due to Marc’s involvement.

<!-- bt-block:000012 -->
And then, of course, there was Marc.

<!-- bt-block:000013 -->
Oh, Marc.

<!-- bt-block:000014 -->
Alex wished he could believe Marc when he said he believed in Alex. Wished he could believe him when he said that all those criticisms aren’t true, when he said he respected Alex on his own talent, his own merits, but, well. He couldn’t.

<!-- bt-block:000015 -->
He loved Marc, truly, and he knew Marc loved him, too. But, that was the problem. Marc’s view of Alex would always be biased, would always be painted with a favoritism so blatant it bordered on delusional. Alex didn’t mind it, exactly, but he also couldn’t believe in it.

<!-- bt-block:000016 -->
And it was in that sense that Alex found comfort in Valentino’s respect.

<!-- bt-block:000017 -->
He’d heard, once, back in Moto3, about how Valentino had advised Luca to follow Alex in the practice session, to learn from him. He’d ridden that high all weekend, reveled in it every time he saw the FTR Honda bike in his peripheral, doing its best to follow his line without making it too obvious. He still caught himself reveling in it now, years and years later, whenever the number ten started tailing him, trying to catch a tow early in practice or near the end of qualifying.

<!-- bt-block:000018 -->
It was a bit malicious, the urge that arose within Alex in those moments. The desire to turn around, smile at Luca, and say, ‘learning anything?’.

<!-- bt-block:000019 -->
He thought Luca might actually try to kill him if he did. He’d seen how the VR46 rider reacted to any insinuations that he was using Alex’s data, his lines, defensive and offended and dismissive, all at once. He was never quite as angry when questioned about other riders, even Marc. Just Alex, specifically, seemed especially annoy him.

<!-- bt-block:000020 -->
He’d heard Luca joke, once or twice, about how Marc might as well be his brother, too, with how often he and Vale were compared to Luca. Alex wondered how Luca would react, if Alex made that same kind of joke. If Alex tried to claim Valentino as his own brother— one that had actually managed to follow in his footsteps, even. He had two world championships, after all. Had, in MotoGP, at least gotten a podium or two.

<!-- bt-block:000021 -->
Alex was certain Luca would actually kill him if he said that. So, he didn’t. But he did think about it.

<!-- bt-block:000022 -->
But aside from Luca, and as far as the rest of the grid was concerned, Alex thought it was a bit funny how insistent everyone was as to his hatred.

<!-- bt-block:000023 -->
However, while it usually was just funny, it did at times get a bit annoying.

<!-- bt-block:000024 -->
“But while your brother did spectacularly, our other veteran of this track did, uh. Not so much.” The interviewer smiled, a smarmy little thing that Alex instantly hated. And, well, he didn’t usually particularly like journalists, but this one gave him a particularly bad feeling. Not to mention, the way he kept slyly complimenting Marc while subtly disparaging Valentino certainly wasn’t helping. “But, well, he is getting up there in age. In his 40’s, now! Many people say he has waited far too long for his retirement, that he’s left it far too late in his career. What’s your opinion on that?”

<!-- bt-block:000025 -->
Alex blinked at the interviewer. Tried to gather himself. He wished he could be shocked at the question. Wasn’t.

<!-- bt-block:000026 -->
“Too late? I think Valentino can do whatever he wants with his career,” he replied finally. “I mean, this is Valentino Rossi we’re talking about. If he wanted to keep racing for four more years, he absolutely could.”

<!-- bt-block:000027 -->
The interviewer snorted. “But he’s 42 years old. 42! Doesn’t that seem like too much? I mean, especially with the kind of results he’s been delivering in the last few of those years.”

<!-- bt-block:000028 -->
Alex stared at the man, and tried his best not to snap.

<!-- bt-block:000029 -->
What did the interviewer want from Alex, exactly? For him to start ragging on Valentino, chastising him for daring to age? Piling on him for not delivering win after win after win, decade after decade after decade? And what had anyone expected anyway? Valentino was no longer on a winning bike, no longer on a factory seat. He was retiring next year, for fuck’s sakes. Even if they hated him, did they have to make it so painful?

<!-- bt-block:000030 -->
Worst of all, why the fuck did they have to try and use Alex to do it?

<!-- bt-block:000031 -->
“I mean, if I can still race in MotoGP at 42 years old, maybe then I can have an opinion, no?” he said with a laugh, trying his best to be genial, in spite of everything.

<!-- bt-block:000032 -->
“Well, sure, but most people would probably know better than to try and race at 42, right?” the interviewer laughed, sour and mocking. “It’s a bit arrogant, really.”

<!-- bt-block:000033 -->
Alex’s smile tightened, soon becoming a gesture that was no happiness, all teeth. “Most people don’t have nine world championships,” he said. “So I think most people can’t compare, really.”

<!-- bt-block:000034 -->
This whole humiliation ritual wasn’t really about Valentino, was the thing. It was about the sheer disrespect that was being shown to all riders, to all of MotoGP, through their disrespect of Valentino. Valentino was just an easy target, an easy excuse. And Alex, their mindless weapon of choice. Point him in the right direction, ask him to attack. Surely, Alex would comply. After all, he hated Valentino, didn’t he?

<!-- bt-block:000035 -->
But Alex was, unfortunately, not actually mindless. He was well aware that Valentino was today’s target, but he wasn’t the first, and certainly wouldn’t be the last. It had been Marc, once, who was the favorite chew-toy. Likely would be again, at some point— probably in the same way, with the same tools, the moment he was no longer delivering victories like he once had.

<!-- bt-block:000036 -->
His injuries had, at the very least, bought him some sympathy. That, Alex knew, wouldn’t last long. The moment Valentino was gone, Marc would become the newest, shiniest toy for the media hounds. Given the barest excuse, they would rip him to shreds, too.

<!-- bt-block:000037 -->
It didn’t matter that Marc had given MotoGP everything— his heart, his soul, his love, his limbs— just like it didn’t matter that Valentino had once given it everything, too. Had built MotoGP, from the ground up, turning it from what it was into what it had become, almost entirely by his sole influence, by his sole efforts, by his sole will.

<!-- bt-block:000038 -->
The moment Valentino showed himself to be mortal, to no longer be a god, he was stabbed. The moment Marc showed himself to be mortal, to no longer be invincible, he was stabbed, too.

<!-- bt-block:000039 -->
It was the way things were. It was the way things would always be.

<!-- bt-block:000040 -->
But Alex refused to be a part of that.

<!-- bt-block:000041 -->
“I have a lot of respect for Valentino,” he continued, before the interviewer could say anything more infuriating, “and all the things he has accomplished, in his career as well as for motorcycling as a whole. I think many of us would not be here, if it weren’t for him. And even if he is no longer competitive, his passion for racing has never dwindled. Even now, at 42, he is planning go out and try his hand at other motorsports, and I’m sure he’ll do well there, too. The rest of us can only dream of having a career like his.”

<!-- bt-block:000042 -->
The interviewer stared at him, looking a little baffled.

<!-- bt-block:000043 -->
Alex stared back, teeth bared.

<!-- bt-block:000044 -->
A moment passed, and the interviewer coughed.

<!-- bt-block:000045 -->
“Well,” he said. Laughed, the classic fake interview laugh. “Well! Ah. Noted! Um. Then, moving on to the next topic . . . “

<!-- bt-block:000046 -->
Alex slowly settled back into his chair, letting his expression shift back into something resembling a real smile.

<!-- bt-block:000047 -->
If they wanted a knife with which to attack Valentino, they would have to look elsewhere.

<!-- bt-block:000048 -->
“It’s not a problem for me, to be Valentino Rossi’s brother,” Luca said, with a self-assured grin and a glance at the roomful of journalists. “I actually think every rider would like to be in my place.”

<!-- bt-block:000049 -->
The journalists muttered among themselves at that, some agreeing, some not. Alex, in particular, felt a number of stares being shot his way, specific in their intent, if not in their allegiance. After all, surely, Alex wouldn’t want that, would he?

<!-- bt-block:000050 -->
And that was true, in some ways. Alex wouldn’t want to be Luca, wouldn’t want to be Valentino Rossi’s brother, because he was perfectly happy being Marc’s brother, actually. More to the point, he didn’t need to be Valentino Rossi’s brother to be close to him. Had, in fact, gotten close to him, without needing to be his brother. And wasn’t that even more meaningful, in the end?

<!-- bt-block:000051 -->
I talk to your brother every week, part of him wanted to say to Luca. He calls me when he’s bored, when he wants to distract himself, when he just wants to chat. He doesn’t do that with you, does he? Wouldn’t you like to be in my place?

<!-- bt-block:000052 -->
It was an unfair thought, Alex knew. Uncharacteristic of him, in how petty it was. How vitriolic.

<!-- bt-block:000053 -->
He shook his head, feeling a bit ashamed.

<!-- bt-block:000054 -->
The motion was, unfortunately, caught by one of the journalists.

<!-- bt-block:000055 -->
“A question for Alex. What is your opinion on the situation? You’re, uh, uniquely qualified to share your perspective, aren’t you?”

<!-- bt-block:000056 -->
Alex bit back a sigh. He should’ve known they would use any excuse to question him.

<!-- bt-block:000057 -->
“I’d like it if Marc gave me a bit more preference, actually,” he joked, and thankfully the reporters laughed along with him. He didn’t turn to look at Luca, though, not wanting the situation to escalate if the other took it as an affront. It was hard to tell, with Luca, what things would irritate him, what things wouldn’t. He was harder to read than most other riders, more controlled, less expressive. Even Alex, who prided himself on being pretty good at reading people, could never quite tell how any particular comment would land.

<!-- bt-block:000058 -->
Except maybe a comment about Luca following Alex’s racing line. Alex had some idea of how that one would go. He didn’t feel like getting into a fistfight in the back alleys of the paddock motorhomes today, though, so he resisted the urge.

<!-- bt-block:000059 -->
“I don’t think there’s anything wrong with supporting your family in their careers,” he continued. “So long as they earn their place, work hard, achieve results, I don’t see why it should matter who your brother is, or your father, or whatever. We’re all here because we were supported by those who believe in us. If we happen to be connected by blood, we can’t change that, either.”

<!-- bt-block:000060 -->
It wasn’t a perfect answer, but Alex didn’t mind. It wasn’t like he had to go too far in his own matters, after all— he was Marc’s brother, everyone knew that, and everyone knew he had his own advantages because of it. Surely no one expected him to repudiate Luca’s statements. Yes, at times the shadows cast by their older siblings could be a bit stifling, but their shade provided cover and protection more often than not. Alex wasn’t stupid enough not to see that. And, of course, neither was Luca.

<!-- bt-block:000061 -->
Speaking of Luca, Alex could feel the other rider’s icy stare on the side of his neck. Not in a negative way, necessarily, but the piercing aspects of Marini’s gaze were undeniable. Alex tried not to tense, not wanting to give the roomful of vultures in front of them any more meat to sink their beaks into.

<!-- bt-block:000062 -->
“So you wouldn’t mind being Valentino Rossi’s brother?”

<!-- bt-block:000063 -->
Alex blinked at the reporter who’d asked the question, somewhat surprised that she’d actually been brave enough to ask it directly. Brave, stupid, rude. It often looked the same, from where Alex was sitting.

<!-- bt-block:000064 -->
She smiled at him, proud of her own cleverness. He smiled back at her, faint and mocking.

<!-- bt-block:000065 -->
“I think the real question is, who would Valentino rather have as a brother?” Alex didn’t look in Luca’s direction. Could feel the stare into his neck. “But, you’d have to ask Valentino that to know the answer.”

<!-- bt-block:000066 -->
The reporter laughed, low and subdued. Brave enough to go after Alex and Luca, not nearly brave enough to go after Valentino. Seems she wasn’t that stupid, after all.

<!-- bt-block:000067 -->
Alex sat back in his seat, watching as the next journalist prepared to launch their own questions.

<!-- bt-block:000068 -->
Felt Luca’s stare into his neck, all throughout the rest of the press conference.
