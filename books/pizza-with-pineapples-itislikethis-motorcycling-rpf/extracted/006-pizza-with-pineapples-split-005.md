<!-- bt-block:000005 -->
Alex closed the door behind him, and tried not to cry.

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
He was very good at taking criticism, usually. Very good at being yelled at, even in aggressive, frustrating situations. Alex had built up a number of staunch defences against getting too emotional, getting too hurt, by the anger of the people around him. When your passion lay in an environment as dangerous, as competitive as motorcycle racing, it was inevitable that you’d quickly develop a thick skin. That, or you simply didn’t succeed.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
But it was different, when it was Marc. Alex had no defences when it came to Marc.

<!-- bt-block:000008 -->
He’d initially tried to take it like he took Marc’s racing feedback. Sharp and blunt, merciless in its analysis, not a shred of sympathy spared for Alex’s emotions when it could mean a tenth lost on the track. Alex was good with that, actually, had long learned to leave his pride at the door when it came to Marc and racing.

<!-- bt-block:000009 -->
But this had nothing to do with pride, and there were no tenths to be gained on track. Just Marc, and his arm, and his pain.

<!-- bt-block:000010 -->
And Alex, who could do nothing about it.

<!-- bt-block:000011 -->
He hated it, being useless. He tried, as much as he could, not to be. He took Marc to the doctor, to his physiotherapist, to any and all appointments he had, needed, took. He helped him with his exercises, his medications, his diet. Kept him distracted, kept him interested, in whatever topic took Marc’s fancy, whatever game he could find, anything to take his mind off the pain.

<!-- bt-block:000012 -->
But Alex could do nothing about the racing.

<!-- bt-block:000013 -->
He knew, viscerally, how much Marc struggled with not being able to race. The pain, fine. The surgeries, fine. The medication, fine. The doctors, rehabilitation, exercises, diet, fine, fine, fine.

<!-- bt-block:000014 -->
But the lack of racing?

<!-- bt-block:000015 -->
That was not fine.

<!-- bt-block:000016 -->
And Alex tried not to take it personally. Knew, genuinely, that it wasn’t about him. That he was only ever trying to help, and Marc knew that, too. Alex did his best to remember that Marc wanted him there. Was grateful to him. Loved him.

<!-- bt-block:000017 -->
But, sometimes, it was hard.

<!-- bt-block:000018 -->
Because, sometimes, when things got really bad, Marc looked at Alex like he hated him. Yelled at him, like he hated him. Jerked away from him, like he hated him.

<!-- bt-block:000019 -->
Alex tried, as best he could, not to take it personally.

<!-- bt-block:000020 -->
But, sometimes, it was hard.

<!-- bt-block:000021 -->
Alex went over to his his bed, sat down. Pulled out his phone, and pressed call.

<!-- bt-block:000022 -->
“Ciao, Pipa,” he whispered, as soon as the call connected.

<!-- bt-block:000023 -->
“Ciao, Tola,” Valentino replied. “I only have a few minutes.”

<!-- bt-block:000024 -->
Alex took in a shuddering breath. “Okay.”

<!-- bt-block:000025 -->
A moment of silence, and then— “Are you alright?”

<!-- bt-block:000026 -->
“I . . . “ Alex’s voice caught in his throat. “I’m . . . “

<!-- bt-block:000027 -->
“Are you injured?” Valentino asked, concerned, and Alex hurried to reassure him, not wanting the other rider to misunderstand his upset.

<!-- bt-block:000028 -->
“No. No. Just . . . “ He took a deep breath. Released it. “Can you just . . . distract me?”

<!-- bt-block:000029 -->
He didn’t want to talk about his situation. There was nothing Valentino could do to help, regardless, and Alex already thought about the whole thing far more than was probably healthy.

<!-- bt-block:000030 -->
No. What he needed to do was not think about it for a few minutes.

<!-- bt-block:000031 -->
“Sure,” Valentino said, thankfully not asking any more questions. Alex wondered if he’d realized what was going on, if he’d so easily figured it all out. “Allora, let me tell you about what happened this morning. So I was outside, setting up the ranch for training later. Normal things, you know, setting up the bikes, making sure the leathers are clean. I have some people to help, of course, but I like to make sure things are okay, so I am there sometimes, too. Anyway, all of a sudden Pecco comes running up to me. But the thing is, he had told me he was going to the zoo that morning, so this is very weird. Then I notice behind him is Bez, and behind him Luca, who were also going to the zoo, so now I am thinking, what is going on? Then I notice—”

<!-- bt-block:000032 -->
“Alex?”

<!-- bt-block:000033 -->
Alex jerked towards the door, where the quiet voice had come from. The rhythmic sound of Valentino’s chatter had lulled him into a peaceful sense of calm, but he already could feel his heart-rate picking back up again.

<!-- bt-block:000034 -->
Well. It had been a good few minutes.

<!-- bt-block:000035 -->
“—then the monkey runs into the room again, so now Luca is screaming, but also I hear yelling from outside, so I think Pecco found the—”

<!-- bt-block:000036 -->
“Sorry to interrupt,” Alex said, reluctant not only because it meant he had to face Marc again after their earlier fight, but also because whatever story Valentino was telling sounded genuinely interesting, if also incredibly confusing. Where had the monkey come from? “The story sounds really fun, but I actually have to go.”

<!-- bt-block:000037 -->
“No worry, I tell you another time,” Valentino said easily. Was quiet for a moment, before adding:  “Remember to take care of yourself, too.”

<!-- bt-block:000038 -->
Alex closed his eyes. So, Valentino had realized what was happening. Alex shouldn’t have be too surprised. He knew who he was dealing with, after all. “Thank you. I will.”

<!-- bt-block:000039 -->
“Good. Ciao.”

<!-- bt-block:000040 -->
With that, he hung up.

<!-- bt-block:000041 -->
So, Alex put away his phone. Stood and walked to his door. Took a deep breath. Pasted a smile on his face. Opened the door.

<!-- bt-block:000042 -->
Marc stood on the other side, expression taut and blank.

<!-- bt-block:000043 -->
Alex’s smile wobbled. “Can I help you with something?”

<!-- bt-block:000044 -->
Marc stared at him. Stared and stared and stared.

<!-- bt-block:000045 -->
Alex’s smile wobbled again. Faltered. “Marc? Is—”

<!-- bt-block:000046 -->
“I’m sorry.”

<!-- bt-block:000047 -->
Alex blinked at him. His smile dropped completely. “What?”

<!-- bt-block:000048 -->
“I’m sorry,” Marc repeated. His eyes closed. Opened again, as his expression twisted with anguish. “I’m sorry, Alex, I— I know you’re just trying to help, and I know this isn’t—But I’m just— It hurts so bad, all the time, and I can’t sleep and I can’t go racing and I can’t do anything and I’m just—”

<!-- bt-block:000049 -->
“Hey, hey, it’s okay,” Alex hushed him, all of his earlier frustrations and fears instantly dissolving in the face of Marc’s misery. “I know you’re not . . . yourself, right now. I know it hurts— I mean, I don’t really know, but I— anyway, I just mean, you don’t have to apologize. It’s okay.”

<!-- bt-block:000050 -->
Marc shook his head. Flinched, as the movement pulled at his shoulder. Alex reached out on instinct, and Marc jerked away, teeth bared in a snarl. Alex froze. Marc, too, froze, and his face scrunched. “Fuck. I’m sorry.” His eyes pressed together, and when they opened again, there was a wet sheen to them. “It just— it hurts so much. I hate this so much.”

<!-- bt-block:000051 -->
Alex wanted, desperately, to hug Marc. Knew he couldn’t, if the way Marc had reacted to the possibility of a touch was any indication.

<!-- bt-block:000052 -->
He felt his eyes prickling with tears.

<!-- bt-block:000053 -->
“I know, I know. It’s okay,” he said, as gently as he dared. He knew Marc hated being treated so gently, but Alex hated seeing Marc in pain. Had only seen Marc in pain, for almost a year now. “Is there anything I can do to help?”

<!-- bt-block:000054 -->
“Let me go racing?”

<!-- bt-block:000055 -->
Alex felt his heart breaking. “Marc—”

<!-- bt-block:000056 -->
Marc laughed, an astoundingly fake sound. “I’m just joking, it’s just a joke.” It wasn’t a joke, was the thing. And both of them knew that.

<!-- bt-block:000057 -->
“Asshole,” Alex replied, with a laugh that was equally as fake, because if he didn’t laugh he might start crying and he really didn’t want to do that.

<!-- bt-block:000058 -->
But what to do, then?

<!-- bt-block:000059 -->
“What about,” he said, “we go to the zoo?”

<!-- bt-block:000060 -->
Marc frowned. “The zoo?”

<!-- bt-block:000061 -->
Marc didn’t really care about animals, Alex knew that. But that also meant that they hadn’t gone to the zoo in a long time.

<!-- bt-block:000062 -->
And, well. If there was no way to help, might as well distract him, right?

<!-- bt-block:000063 -->
“We can walk around. Look at the monkeys. Or maybe the cheetahs. Watch them go fast.” Alex grinned. “Kind of like racing, right?”

<!-- bt-block:000064 -->
Marc seemed lost for a moment. “I don’t know—”

<!-- bt-block:000065 -->
“Come on, it’ll be fun,” Alex insisted. He wouldn't usually push, but staying in the apartment with nothing to do but go over Alex's telemetry for the hundredth time was driving them both up the wall, and Alex hated feeling useless, and he had to do something. “We can go for dessert, after. Get that chocolate cupcake you really like. I won’t tell your trainer if you don’t tell mine.”

<!-- bt-block:000066 -->
At that, Marc perked up slightly, just like Alex knew he would. “Okay,” he said, finally. “But just this once.”

<!-- bt-block:000067 -->
“Just this once,” Alex said, and felt the pressure of the day seeping away with the subtle ease in Marc’s shoulders, the tiniest glimmer in a gaze that had been dull and dark for far too long.

<!-- bt-block:000068 -->
It wouldn’t fix anything, Alex knew that. But, he also knew that nothing really would. Time was the only solution; the time it would take to heal Marc’s injury, and then to rehabilitate his muscles, all so he could get on a bike once more, go racing once again.

<!-- bt-block:000069 -->
They just had to survive until then.

<!-- bt-block:000070 -->
They had survived, and Marc was ecstatic.

<!-- bt-block:000071 -->
Alex, for his part, was happy, too. Not quite as ecstatic, however, when he saw Marc first get on that Honda, first set off onto the track in FP1 at Portimao.

<!-- bt-block:000072 -->
His arm was still bothering him, Alex could tell right off the bat. And the bike didn’t look too good, either, spitting and stuttering when he made a turn here, a push on the throttle there.

<!-- bt-block:000073 -->
Alex tried not to worry too much. Marc was an eight-time world champion, one of the best to ever do it, and he knew his way around a motorcycle better than pretty much everyone else on the circuit— with one notable exception, maybe, but that wasn’t relevant at the moment. He even knew his way around this circuit in particular, thanks to the pre-season tests he’d been allowed to take part in, through no small amount of pressure exerted by Marc himself, despite Alex’s concerns.

<!-- bt-block:000074 -->
And Alex did have concerns, was the thing. Despite knowing how accomplished his brother was, how skilled, how knowledgeable, how determined.

<!-- bt-block:000075 -->
Because Marc was, at his core, a little too determined. A little too willing to pick himself up, dust himself off, and put himself back on a bike that would as soon break him as let him ride it.

<!-- bt-block:000076 -->
Once, Alex hadn’t had a single thought like that.

<!-- bt-block:000077 -->
Now, as he watched Marc go around the track from the camera pit wall, from his own garage on the other side of the paddock, all too close and yet not nearly close enough, he had plenty.

<!-- bt-block:000078 -->
“Sorry about your race.”

<!-- bt-block:000079 -->
“Eh, it is like this.” Valentino tried to sound dismissive, but Alex could tell he was deeply frustrated. The older rider brought the cup of coffee to his lips, cradling it in his hands as he took a short sip. The pale garage lights shone down on him, highlighting the light bruising on his lower arms, making it look worse than it was. At least, Alex hoped so. “You did pretty good, though, no? Where did you end up, seventh?”

<!-- bt-block:000080 -->
“Eight,” Alex replied, realizing Valentino would rather just not talk about his own stint. Fair enough. Alex didn’t like to talk about his own DNF’s, either. “Marc was seventh,” he added, not really thinking about it. Bit his tongue, as he soon as he did.

<!-- bt-block:000081 -->
Valentino was silent for a few seconds, taking another sip of his coffee. Alex watched him warily, nervously adjusting his seat on the counter.

<!-- bt-block:000082 -->
It was only their third meeting in-person, after all, a tentative trial of privacy and secrecy that the both of them weren’t sure they could afford. But Alex had asked, at the beginning of the season, had needed the calm before it really sunk in that Marc was back, and with him came the storm.

<!-- bt-block:000083 -->
The thing was, Valentino’s presence provided a unique sense of security within the paddock, an undeniable blanket of assurance that was impossible to replicate. His academy riders knew that well; it was why they sought him out at any and every opportunity, why they’d made Vale’s motorhome their meeting place of choice. They liked talking to him, of course, but it was also just about being around him. Valentino’s mere presence was like a hearth, warm and comforting— assuming you were on his good side, of course.

<!-- bt-block:000084 -->
But Alex couldn’t do that, could never go visit Valentino in the comfort of his own motorhome. He could only request momentary clandestine meetings in closed-off garages, at indeterminate times of night, when no one would possibly suspect that anyone would still be awake, when no one would possibly come looking for them.

<!-- bt-block:000085 -->
Alex wasn’t sure why Valentino agreed to meet with him. Was immensely grateful that he did.

<!-- bt-block:000086 -->
“Good for him,” Valentino said finally. “But, anyway, I want to talk about you. What did you think about the medium rear? It kind of suck, no?”

<!-- bt-block:000087 -->
And that was the other thing. Valentino never wanted to talk about Marc. And Alex loved Marc, he truly did— But, god, he was so tired of talking about Marc. Ever since Marc’s return, everything Alex did, every interview, every press conference, every journalist he spoke to, they all wanted to talk about Marc. And Alex understood, really, he did, but—

<!-- bt-block:000088 -->
But Alex was getting really fucking tired of talking about Marc.

<!-- bt-block:000089 -->
“I had more problems with the fronts, actually,” Alex replied. He’d initially been a bit leery of discussing racing specifics with Valentino, before realizing that was stupid. There was nothing Alex could say that Valentino couldn’t easily figure out by himself. Any discussions they had were all for Alex’s benefit, really. “Especially on turn three, I could feel they were struggling with grip near the end of the curve, which was compromising . . . “

<!-- bt-block:000090 -->
As he spoke, Alex relaxed more and more, the stress of the day evaporating into the familiar ritual of race debriefing, if in such an unfamiliar setting— in a garage after midnight, sitting on a countertop, with Valentino beside him, peering up at Alex, wide-eyed and attentive.

<!-- bt-block:000091 -->
It was the one thing Alex was starting to miss most when they were calling as opposed to talking face-to-face; that expression Valentino always wore when he wanted you to know he was listening to you, that what you were saying was important to him. And Alex knew it wasn’t a personal thing, knew Valentino showed that face to everyone, but he wasn’t immune to its appeal. Far from it, he was coming to realize, the more and more he was exposed to it.

<!-- bt-block:000092 -->
Especially because, in these moments, it was all for him.
