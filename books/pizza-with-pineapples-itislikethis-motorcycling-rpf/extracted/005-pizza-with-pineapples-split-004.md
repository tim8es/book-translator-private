Chapter 3

<!-- bt-block:000002 -->
*2020, Austria*

<!-- bt-block:000003 -->
Alex pressed play on the video. Watched it. Rewound to the start. Watched it again.

<!-- bt-block:000004 -->
Glanced at the time. Bit his cheek. Went back to the video.

<!-- bt-block:000005 -->
He jerked as his phone buzzed with an incoming call. Looked to the side, where Marc was sitting, arm in a sling, eyes half-focused on the tv screen in front of them playing a movie Alex had not been paying any attention to. Some brainless action film. Or maybe it was romance. Alex really had no idea.

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
Marc, ever attentive, sensed his stare and looked back at him.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
Alex immediately declined the call.

<!-- bt-block:000008 -->
Marc blinked. Looked down at Alex’s phone screen. Alex fought the urge to hide it, knowing it would only arouse more suspicion.

<!-- bt-block:000009 -->
“Pipa?” Marc read, questioning tilt to his voice. “Who’s that?”

<!-- bt-block:000010 -->
Alex swallowed down a flare of nervousness. “A friend,” he said. Paused. “Actually, I should take this. I’ll be right back.”

<!-- bt-block:000011 -->
“Sure,” Marc replied, going back to watching the movie.

<!-- bt-block:000012 -->
Alex stood, trying not to look too awkward as he hurried out of the living room, down the main corridor of their shared apartment, closing the door of their shared bedroom behind him. The building was isolated enough that no neighbors would hear him, and the walls were soundproof enough that Marc wouldn’t hear him. Alex still felt a bit exposed, but it was the best he could do in this situation.

<!-- bt-block:000013 -->
He pulled out his phone, and called back.

<!-- bt-block:000014 -->
“Ciao, Pipa,” he greeted immediately upon connecting.

<!-- bt-block:000015 -->
“Ciao, Alex,” Valentino replied. He sounded more muted than usual, more tired. “I—”

<!-- bt-block:000016 -->
“Are you okay?” Alex blurted. “Sorry. Just, how are you? I saw the crash, and I— I wanted to call, but I didn’t know if you would be busy, and I didn’t want to bother you, but I was so worried, and—”

<!-- bt-block:000017 -->
“I am okay, Alex, I am okay,” Valentino interrupted, far more gently than Alex had. “I am okay. Nothing happened. Was scary, but nothing more. I am okay.”

<!-- bt-block:000018 -->
“Okay,” Alex repeated, half as a response, half just to reassure himself. “You’re okay. Okay.”

<!-- bt-block:000019 -->
They sat in silence for a few moments.

<!-- bt-block:000020 -->
“I was thinking about retirement,” Valentino suddenly said.

<!-- bt-block:000021 -->
Alex floundered a bit. Tried to make sense of the shift in topic. “Because of the crash?”

<!-- bt-block:000022 -->
“Kind of,” Valentino hummed. “It was more that I guess I realize, in that moment, that I could die anytime. No mistake I make, no issue with me, just random. Someone else crash, far away, and their bike fly into me and I die. Literally nothing I can do.” He paused. “And, you know. Is not like I am competing for championships anymore. So dying isn’t really worth it anymore, I think.”

<!-- bt-block:000023 -->
Alex tried not to think about that too much. Tried not to take it too seriously. “Well, you have been racing for over twenty years.”

<!-- bt-block:000024 -->
“Are you calling me old?”

<!-- bt-block:000025 -->
“You are old.”

<!-- bt-block:000026 -->
“Bastard,” Valentino scoffed as Alex giggled. “It is difficult, to leave. Even after twenty years. I still love racing. So much.” He paused. Sighed. “Aging fucking sucks.”

<!-- bt-block:000027 -->
Alex let out a slow breath. “I’m sure it does.”

<!-- bt-block:000028 -->
“Bastard. You will age, too,” Valentino retorted. Paused. Huffed out a laugh. “Hopefully.”

<!-- bt-block:000029 -->
Alex closed his eyes. “Too soon to joke about that.”

<!-- bt-block:000030 -->
“Allora, I am the one who almost die, I can joke if I want.”

<!-- bt-block:000031 -->
Alex flinched as if struck.

<!-- bt-block:000032 -->
“Can we—” he blurted out. Stopped himself. Steadied his breath, tried to keep his voice as calm as his emotions weren’t. “Can we meet? Next GP?”

<!-- bt-block:000033 -->
There was silence for a few moments.

<!-- bt-block:000034 -->
“You mean, in person?” Valentino asked, hesitantly.

<!-- bt-block:000035 -->
“Yes. Just for a little while. A few minutes. Or less.” Again, there was silence. Alex pressed his eyes shut, hard, until little lights were prickling the back of his eyelids. “Please.”

<!-- bt-block:000036 -->
Silence, still, for another few seconds, until—

<!-- bt-block:000037 -->
“Sure,” Valentino said, and his voice had grown soft. “We will have to be careful, but we are smart. We can figure it out.”

<!-- bt-block:000038 -->
“Thank you,” Alex breathed out. “I’m sorry. I’m not usually so— crashes don’t usually scare me, but—”

<!-- bt-block:000039 -->
“That was a bad one,” Valentino said, voice still soft. “Is normal, I think, to see that and be a bit scared.”

<!-- bt-block:000040 -->
Alex bit his lip so hard he tasted blood. “Were you scared?”

<!-- bt-block:000041 -->
Valentino was silent for a long moment.

<!-- bt-block:000042 -->
“Yes,” he admitted finally, in a low whisper. “Yes. I was scared.”

<!-- bt-block:000043 -->
Alex walked back into the living room to find Marc still watching the movie, but now while standing in front of the couch, doing measured leg squats. He stopped as he caught sight of Alex, smiling awkwardly. “I needed to move around a bit. Don’t worry, I’m not using my arm.”

<!-- bt-block:000044 -->
He’d already done three hours of physiotherapy that morning and had another two scheduled for the evening. Alex knew that Marc was about ready to chew off his own arm if it meant he could return to racing sooner. Too bad things didn’t work that way.

<!-- bt-block:000045 -->
“Better not be,” Alex replied, collapsing back onto the couch. Marc watched for a few moments, did a few more leg squats. Alex shot him a glare. Two more squats, and Marc begrudgingly joined him back on the couch.

<!-- bt-block:000046 -->
They were silent for a bit.

<!-- bt-block:000047 -->
“When do you think I’ll be able to ride again?” Marc asked suddenly.

<!-- bt-block:000048 -->
Alex closed his eyes. Opened them. “When the doctor says you’re healed.”

<!-- bt-block:000049 -->
Marc stared at the tv, eyes dark and unseeing. “You know I don’t have to be healed to ride. I just need to be capable of riding. And the rehab has been going really well, especially in the last week. I really think that I should be able to go back for the last two races, maybe even the last three—”

<!-- bt-block:000050 -->
“No!” Alex snapped, startling both himself and Marc with the vehemence of his answer. Marc stared at him, and Alex winced. “Sorry, didn’t mean to yell. I just . . . no. No racing until you’re properly, fully healed. We can’t have another accident, alright?”

<!-- bt-block:000051 -->
“‘We’, like you’re injured, too,” Marc said, and though he was clearly joking, there was also a clear edge of frustration to his tone.

<!-- bt-block:000052 -->
Alex bit his lip. Tasted blood, as the earlier cut split again. “It’s not just about injuries, Marc.”

<!-- bt-block:000053 -->
Marc opened his mouth. Paused, glanced at Alex. There was an unusual sharpness to his gaze, suddenly. “You’re thinking about the crash, aren’t you.”

<!-- bt-block:000054 -->
He didn’t have to specify which crash he was talking about. It was all anyone in MotoGP had been talking about, since two days ago. Even Marc’s own terrible highside had been all but forgotten in the wake of it, of what had almost happened.

<!-- bt-block:000055 -->
There was no point in lying, so Alex just nodded.

<!-- bt-block:000056 -->
He waited for Marc to say something. Glanced at him, when he didn’t.

<!-- bt-block:000057 -->
Marc sat, staring at the tv, unmoving. Alex watched the unblinking darkness of his eyes, the protruding jut of his jaw, the veins jumping on his hands as Marc tensed his fingers over his palms, scratching with his nails over the back of it, over, and over, and over again.

<!-- bt-block:000058 -->
He reached out, held Marc’s hand. Marc jerked to stare at him.

<!-- bt-block:000059 -->
“You’re going to hurt yourself,” Alex murmured. Marc froze, looked down. Forcefully released his grip on his own hand. Watched the red lines, the pinpricks of blood already rushing to the surface.

<!-- bt-block:000060 -->
“Sorry.”

<!-- bt-block:000061 -->
“He’s fine.”

<!-- bt-block:000062 -->
“What?”

<!-- bt-block:000063 -->
“Vale.” Alex stopped. Corrected himself. “Valentino. He was okay. After the crash.” Paused. “Shaken, but okay.”

<!-- bt-block:000064 -->
“Okay,” Marc said, and he sounded like he was half responding to Alex, half reassuring himself. “Okay. He was okay.”

<!-- bt-block:000065 -->
Alex held onto his hand, and felt Marc return the gesture.

<!-- bt-block:000066 -->
He was okay. They were okay.

<!-- bt-block:000067 -->
Everything was going to be okay.

<!-- bt-block:000068 -->
“Pipa?”

<!-- bt-block:000069 -->
“In here.”

<!-- bt-block:000070 -->
Alex followed the voice, turned the corner. Caught sight, finally, of the figure he’d been looking all weekend for.

<!-- bt-block:000071 -->
Except, he didn’t look quite like Alex had been expecting.

<!-- bt-block:000072 -->
He was leaning on one of the counters, long, spindly hands perched on the side as he crouched over what Alex guessed was probably some race telemetry documents. His hair was wild, curls everywhere, not a single attempt made to keep them in place that Alex could see. His clothes were loose, rumpled; shoes scuffed and worn.

<!-- bt-block:000073 -->
The light of the motorhome garage was stark and white, a pale daylight imitation that washed out every feature and liveliness from its subjects with an undeniable, ruthless efficiency.

<!-- bt-block:000074 -->
Alex felt, in that moment, that Valentino looked very, very mortal. Very subject to the rules that stretched across the rest of humanity, in a way he rarely otherwise did. The glitter of his charm stripped away, the armor of his charisma broken, leaving behind only a person.

<!-- bt-block:000075 -->
He stepped forward, and Valentino’s eyes snapped to him.

<!-- bt-block:000076 -->
“Tola,” he said, and just like that, the armor was back in place, the glitter in his eyes, the pull of his features— the inescapable aura that somehow melded into his messy hair and his rumpled clothes and his scuffed shoes and made every insignificant aspect of them look somehow interesting, somehow better, somehow more.

<!-- bt-block:000077 -->
The mortal man was gone, and in his place was Valentino.

<!-- bt-block:000078 -->
Or at least, Alex thought, a bit bitterly, one of his masks. Perhaps, even, his very favorite one.

<!-- bt-block:000079 -->
It wasn’t like Alex didn’t understand the purpose of masks. He used them, liberally, in front of cameras and fans and riders and journalists. He watched Marc use them too, since they were children, becoming more and more precise as they grew and their careers grew with them.

<!-- bt-block:000080 -->
They’d both watched Valentino, of course. The original performer. Had learned from him, as everyone had. The world’s teacher, when it came to personas, when it came to masks. No one had ever been able to match him, and Alex doubted anyone ever would. His talent was natural, his charisma innate.

<!-- bt-block:000081 -->
Even Marc, the best imitator Alex had ever seen, couldn’t quite recreate it, as much as he’d tried. Had veered off of it eventually, settled on something a little less bluntly likeable, a little more deliberately fragile.

<!-- bt-block:000082 -->
Not, Alex knew, that Marc was actually fragile. He’d bounced off enough crashes, had walked away unharmed from enough accidents, to prove that wasn’t true.

<!-- bt-block:000083 -->
But, Valentino had always been a unique wound to him. Not because Marc couldn’t close it, but because he didn’t want to. Alex had tried to help him as well as he could, as well as he knew how to. No longer knew how to. No longer knew if he could. But, Alex supposed, in the end, Marc was also a bit of a unique wound for Valentino, too. So there was that.

<!-- bt-block:000084 -->
Alex, for his part, was determined not to be a wound to either of them. And so he felt, perhaps a bit selfishly, that Valentino’s mask was just not necessary when it was just him.

<!-- bt-block:000085 -->
He took another step forward, drawing close to Valentino. Stopped, when he was an arm-length away, so as not to be overwhelming.

<!-- bt-block:000086 -->
Decided that was stupid a moment later, and stepped forward again to pull Valentino into a hug.

<!-- bt-block:000087 -->
Valentino, to his credit, barely hesitated before reciprocating the gesture.

<!-- bt-block:000088 -->
“Allora, is okay, I am okay,” he said, patting Alex’s back. Alex tightened his hold, pulling until Valentino let out a small wheeze. So theatrical, really.

<!-- bt-block:000089 -->
Valentino shifted, then, raising his head slightly to rest his chin onto Alex’s shoulder, and Alex suddenly realized that Valentino was actually slightly shorter than him. It was a strange realization, almost somewhat alarming. Valentino had always been one of the tallest figures in the MotoGP paddock, if not the tallest. But Alex had grown a lot over the last few years, shot up like a beanpole, as Marc was so— mm, fond— of reminding him. And now, he was taller than Valentino.

<!-- bt-block:000090 -->
Alex . . . wasn’t sure how he felt about that. So, he decided not to think about it.

<!-- bt-block:000091 -->
He tightened his grip one last time before finally letting go. Valentino huffed out another wheeze— yet more theatricality, Alex hadn’t hugged him that hard— before smiling. “You want to break all the bones the bike missed?”

<!-- bt-block:000092 -->
“Don’t joke about that,” Alex scowled, gently shoving Valentino’s shoulder as he did. Relaxed, as he felt the other rider’s body heat seep into him. The proof of his life, irrefutable. Alex took it in like a man starving, even as he tried his best to act casual. “Are you still thinking about it? Retirement, I mean.”

<!-- bt-block:000093 -->
Valentino hummed. “It wouldn’t be this year. Or the next.”

<!-- bt-block:000094 -->
“Or the next, or the next—”

<!-- bt-block:000095 -->
“Allora, that is what I want to think, too. But, it has to happen at some point,” Valentino said. He sounded reluctant, despite his apparent determination. “You know I almost retire like six times before?”

<!-- bt-block:000096 -->
“Really?”

<!-- bt-block:000097 -->
“Mhm. Back in 2003. And then 2006. And then 2010, and 2012, and . . . “ He paused, even as Alex tried not to react. Continued, a moment later. “2017, too. And 2018, when I win no races, even with a good bike. Was a hard year.” He paused again. “But then I settle down a bit. Think is just fun, so I continue until I can’t. Even if I can no longer win, I still love racing, so why not?”

<!-- bt-block:000098 -->
“Why not,” Alex repeated, not quite agreeing, but understanding.

<!-- bt-block:000099 -->
“But now I think, is maybe not so smart. I can race in other ways, without risking dying to a random bike flying in the air. Can maybe even win again, too. Would maybe be even more fun, to try new things.”

<!-- bt-block:000100 -->
“Like what?”

<!-- bt-block:000101 -->
“Other motorsports,” Valentino said. “Cars. Endurance racing. WEC, GTs, Hypercar. I’ve always wanted to try, you know? Have tried, actually, a few times. But to do it full time will be fun, I think.”

<!-- bt-block:000102 -->
“Yeah,” Alex murmured. “I’m sure it will be.”

<!-- bt-block:000103 -->
“Allora,” Valentino said. “You do not sound too happy.”

<!-- bt-block:000104 -->
Alex paused. He really wasn’t too happy, was the thing. “I’ll miss you,” he said plainly.

<!-- bt-block:000105 -->
Valentino blinked. Snorted. “As you say, I have been around for over twenty years. Is a bit boring, no, always me being there.”

<!-- bt-block:000106 -->
“Please, you?” Alex scoffed. “You could never be boring.”

<!-- bt-block:000107 -->
Valentino blinked again. Smiled, then, wide and sweet, his blue eyes crinkling at the corners as he looked at Alex with open fondness. “Thank you, Alex,” he said. “I think this is the nicest thing you ever say to me.”

<!-- bt-block:000108 -->
Alex stared at him. Realized he was staring. Forced himself to look away.

<!-- bt-block:000109 -->
“Yeah, well,” he said, trying not to let his embarrassment show. Embarrassment over what, exactly, he didn’t know, and had no intention of finding out. “I’ll be nicer when you deserve it.”

<!-- bt-block:000110 -->
Valentino laughed, and Alex felt him press into his side. Remembered, all at once, why exactly they were here, meeting secretly in the Yamaha motorhome garage, in the dead of night, in spite of all the risks that carried.

<!-- bt-block:000111 -->
He pressed back into Valentino’s side.

<!-- bt-block:000112 -->
Alright. Maybe, just maybe, Alex could afford to be a bit nicer.

<!-- bt-block:000113 -->
Just a bit, though.
