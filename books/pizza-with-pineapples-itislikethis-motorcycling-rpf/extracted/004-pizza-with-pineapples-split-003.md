Chapter 2

<!-- bt-block:000002 -->
*2020, Jerez*

<!-- bt-block:000003 -->
Alex collapsed onto the sofa, breathing shallowly as he gathered himself. He waited a few minutes, until he could be sure that no one would burst in, looking for him with more urgent news. Took out his phone, went to his contacts. Hesitated for another few minutes. Pressed ‘call’.

<!-- bt-block:000004 -->
It took five rings for the call to connect. Alex counted them, carefully, specifically.

<!-- bt-block:000005 -->
Then, the sound of breathing.

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
“Ciao, Vale,” he whispered.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
A moment of silence, then—

<!-- bt-block:000008 -->
“Ciao, Tola,” came from the other side, quiet and pressed to the microphone. Alex could hear voices on the other side, close but unrecognizable. “How are you doing?”

<!-- bt-block:000009 -->
It was a system they’d figured out, about a month into their— mm, acquaintanceship? Alex still wouldn’t call them friends, but they were something. Friends was too close, coworkers wasn’t close enough. Acquaintances it would have to be for now, though Alex didn’t think acquaintances treated each other like they did, kept each other secret from everyone else like they did. But it wasn’t like they spoke too often, either— four times, now, including their first chat and the ten-second call that had amounted to Valentino congratulating him for his championship victory and nothing else— so it couldn’t be anything else.

<!-- bt-block:000010 -->
In any case, the system went like this.

<!-- bt-block:000011 -->
It was a bit like a game. The caller would wait until the call connected. If it did not connect, they did not call again, as it meant the receiver was unavailable, and they would have to wait for a return call or message to re-initiate contact. If the call connected, the caller would greet first, using either the receiver’s name or their secret nickname. A name meant they could speak openly, being in private. A nickname meant they were in public, or might otherwise be overheard. The receiver then replied with their own greeting, using the same guidelines. At any point, either party could hang up without warning if they felt it was necessary. Neither would take offence to any part of the system, even without explanation. Alex was alone, in private. Valentino was not, and could be overheard. Thus, their respective greetings.

<!-- bt-block:000012 -->
Normally, Alex found their— mm, acquaintanceship— a bit bizarre, a bit fun. A game, really, if it could be anything else.

<!-- bt-block:000013 -->
But this situation was not normal, and Alex hadn’t known who else to call. Didn’t really have anyone else to call. Didn’t really have anyone else he could talk to, in any way, at all.

<!-- bt-block:000014 -->
It was a cold feeling, the realization of his isolation. Alex was generally aware of it, didn’t normally mind it, as he wasn’t normally much of a social person, anyway.

<!-- bt-block:000015 -->
But this situation was not normal.

<!-- bt-block:000016 -->
“I’m scared,” Alex said, pressing the device onto his ear, clutching onto it like a lifeline. He could still hear the beeping of the medical center monitors, like they’d become a part of his motorhome. Growing within the walls, maybe. With how the day had gone, Alex wouldn’t be surprised. “One of the doctors was saying he might never ride again.”

<!-- bt-block:000017 -->
He heard shuffling over the phone, and he curled into the sofa’s arm. The course fabric rubbed against Alex’s skin, shallow and rough. He was cold. He was sweating. He kind of wanted to throw up.

<!-- bt-block:000018 -->
“Allora, it is like this,” Valentino said plainly, and Alex almost choked on a startled laugh.

<!-- bt-block:000019 -->
“Asshole,” he murmured, as he rubbed at his eyes.

<!-- bt-block:000020 -->
He wasn’t sure what he’d expected, really, how he could’ve expected Valentino to react. Normally, he’d never bring up something like this, not into their tentative, fragile truce. But this situation wasn’t normal, and Alex didn’t know who else to talk to. Had no one else to talk to.

<!-- bt-block:000021 -->
But, Valentino had picked up. Was talking to him.

<!-- bt-block:000022 -->
No matter what else happened, Alex would not soon forget that.

<!-- bt-block:000023 -->
“I’m being serious,” he added.

<!-- bt-block:000024 -->
“So am I,” Valentino replied, and Alex couldn’t hear the background voices anymore. “If it is like this, you cannot do anything. Being sad will not help. It would make it worse for him too, no?”

<!-- bt-block:000025 -->
Alex took a deep breath. “I guess so.”

<!-- bt-block:000026 -->
“But is just one doctor, you say? So maybe he is okay,” Valentino continued. “You call your parents?”

<!-- bt-block:000027 -->
Alex tensed. Bit his cheek. “No,” he quietly admitted.

<!-- bt-block:000028 -->
“Is okay, I also would not call my parents,” Valentino said, and again Alex laughed.

<!-- bt-block:000029 -->
“No, I mean, I should call them,” he said. Sighed. “It’s just . . . “

<!-- bt-block:000030 -->
“Is difficult, when you are already stressed.”

<!-- bt-block:000031 -->
“Yeah.”

<!-- bt-block:000032 -->
“You worry, and they worry, and it become a whole big worry.”

<!-- bt-block:000033 -->
“Yeah. Exactly.”

<!-- bt-block:000034 -->
“So you call me instead,” Valentino concluded. “Because I do not worry, since I do not care.”

<!-- bt-block:000035 -->
Again, Alex laughed. He hadn’t thought he was capable of laughing at all, a mere few minutes ago. But something about Valentino made it so easy to relax, to forget, to be happy for just a moment.

<!-- bt-block:000036 -->
The thought filled Alex with guilt, and the laughter immediately stopped.

<!-- bt-block:000037 -->
Valentino, empathic as always, soon picked up on the shift in mood. “Allora, I am just joking.”

<!-- bt-block:000038 -->
“No, I know, I’m just—” Alex sighed again. “I feel bad. Laughing with you.”

<!-- bt-block:000039 -->
“Excuse you,” Valentino muttered, sounding deeply offended.

<!-- bt-block:000040 -->
“No, no, that’s—” Alex stopped. Took another breath. “Look, I— I really appreciate this, I do. I just . . . I don’t know. I don’t know how to deal with this.”

<!-- bt-block:000041 -->
“You deal with it by not thinking about it,” Valentino replied, calm and obvious.

<!-- bt-block:000042 -->
Alex blinked at the wall in front of him. Realized suddenly he couldn’t hear the beeping anymore. “I don’t know if avoidance is really the solution.”

<!-- bt-block:000043 -->
“Allora, who said avoidance?” Valentino tutted. “This is resting for your mind. If you go over a problem again and again when there is no solution, is like revving your bike with the brake on. You go nowhere and you kill the engine. So just get off the bike, and go for a walk. Go to a park, feed some ducks. Are there any ducks nearby you?”

<!-- bt-block:000044 -->
Alex blinked. “Uh. What are the ducks in this metaphor?”

<!-- bt-block:000045 -->
Valentino sighed. “I am telling you to take a walk, literally. Feed some literal ducks. Or play a game, watch a movie, whatever. Just don’t sit there and overthink. Is not good and will not help.”

<!-- bt-block:000046 -->
Alex considered the suggestion. “You learn that from experience?”

<!-- bt-block:000047 -->
“Something like that,” Valentino said. Paused, and Alex heard the voices in the background of the call growing louder once again. “Allora, I have to go now. But I hope I help a bit.”

<!-- bt-block:000048 -->
Alex pressed his eyes shut. Held the phone against his ear. Breathed in once. “You did.” Breathed out. “Thank you.”

<!-- bt-block:000049 -->
“No problem. Ciao,” Valentino said, and hung up.

<!-- bt-block:000050 -->
Alex took the phone away from his ear. Collapsed back onto the couch. He breathed slowly, in and out, letting his eyes trace over the blank ceiling of his motorhome.

<!-- bt-block:000051 -->
His muscles ached. He was tired. He was cold. He was shivering, a little bit. He kind of wanted to throw up.

<!-- bt-block:000052 -->
From the depths of his walls, there came the faint sound of beeping.

<!-- bt-block:000053 -->
“I really do need to get out of here,” Alex murmured, and rose from the couch.

<!-- bt-block:000054 -->
The night air was cool and refreshing, the light undercurrent of fuel and rubber bringing a comforting familiarity to the otherwise featureless scent. Alex breathed in deep. Let it out again.

<!-- bt-block:000055 -->
He walked, slowly at first, then more quickly. Kept going, until he spotted the distinctive neon orange vests of one of the track marshals, probably wandering back to their own lodgings after a long day.

<!-- bt-block:000056 -->
Alex paused. Sped up again.

<!-- bt-block:000057 -->
“Excuse me,” he called out, walking up to the marshal with a polite smile. “Sorry for the weird question, but, uh, do you know if there are any ducks nearby?”

<!-- bt-block:000058 -->
*2020, Andalucia*

<!-- bt-block:000059 -->
“Did you know,” Alex began the conversation, “that there’s a lake full of ducks about ten minutes from the circuit?”

<!-- bt-block:000060 -->
There was a few seconds of silence from the other end of the call, before Valentino finally replied, a bit hesitantly, “Is there?”

<!-- bt-block:000061 -->
“It’s inside a golf club thing, but yeah. It’s a huge lake, and there’s tons of ducks. Not all year long, maybe, but they were there when I checked last week. Do you play golf, by any chance?”

<!-- bt-block:000062 -->
Valentino snorted. “No, and I refuse to.”

<!-- bt-block:000063 -->
“Good. Anyway, the golf club let me in when I told them I was here for the GP, I’m sure they’d let you in, too. You being you and all that.”

<!-- bt-block:000064 -->
“Maybe.”

<!-- bt-block:000065 -->
“No, I’m sure they would. But anyway, the ducks were really interesting. I guess I hadn’t really noticed, but I haven’t really been around ducks since I was like, pretty young? In Cervera, near my parent’s house, there used to be this small creek where a lot of ducks and some geese would fly by while migrating, and we would feed them bread— but actually, I’ve heard you aren’t supposed to do that, it’s bad for them, something about too much sugar? Sad to be a bird and you still have to eat healthy, they aren’t even getting to ride motorcycles about it— but anyway, we used to feed them bread, cause we didn’t know, obviously, and they would fight each other for the bread. But the geese would always win. So I’d have to kind of trick the geese to go off to the side so I could then give bread to the ducks. And Marc liked to just kind of scare off the geese but I felt bad for them, so I didn’t—”

<!-- bt-block:000066 -->
“Hey, hey, Alex, can I interrupt a bit?”

<!-- bt-block:000067 -->
Alex bit his tongue, embarrassed. He hadn’t realized how much he’d been rambling. “Uh. Yeah. Yeah. Sorry. Didn’t mean to talk so much.”

<!-- bt-block:000068 -->
“Is okay. I mean this is all very, eh, interesting? I am learning a lot, really.” Valentino was being awfully nice about it, especially when Alex could hear his hesitation. “But, uh, I have to ask. Are you . . . okay?”

<!-- bt-block:000069 -->
Alex closed his eyes. Took a deep breath. “I don’t want to think about it.”

<!-- bt-block:000070 -->
A moment of silence.

<!-- bt-block:000071 -->
“Okay,” Valentino said, and Alex relaxed. “So, anyway, about the ducks. Did they attack you?”

<!-- bt-block:000072 -->
Alex laughed. “What? No, of course not.”

<!-- bt-block:000073 -->
“Really? I don’t believe you. They always attack me.”

<!-- bt-block:000074 -->
“That’s geese, Vale. Ducks don’t attack people.”

<!-- bt-block:000075 -->
“Sure they do. But, ah, what is the difference?”

<!-- bt-block:000076 -->
“Between a goose and a duck? What do you me— It’s a different animal!”

<!-- bt-block:000077 -->
“Allora, is all birds, no?”

<!-- bt-block:000078 -->
“No!”

<!-- bt-block:000079 -->
Valentino cackled, and Alex took a deep breath, begrudgingly amused as he realized he was being baited on purpose. But, it had worked perfectly to distract him.

<!-- bt-block:000080 -->
“Geese are much larger, that’s why they would always win against the ducks,” Alex continued, even though he half expected Valentino to just mock him again. “And so we had to trick them, so the ducks would get some, too.”

<!-- bt-block:000081 -->
“There were geese in the golf club?”

<!-- bt-block:000082 -->
“No, that was back in Cervera. There were no geese here. But there were a bunch of ducks. There were even a few families.”

<!-- bt-block:000083 -->
“Duck families?”

<!-- bt-block:000084 -->
“Yeah! With ducklings. They were really cute. But I was worried they’d be in danger. From, like, flying golf balls or something. I think there are also cats around, and they eat everything.”

<!-- bt-block:000085 -->
“I like cats, they’re cute.”

<!-- bt-block:000086 -->
“I don’t know, I’m more of a dog person.”

<!-- bt-block:000087 -->
“Really? I thought you were a regular human.”

<!-- bt-block:000088 -->
“Oh my god,” Alex groaned, as Valentino burst into giggles. “Don’t laugh, that was awful!”

<!-- bt-block:000089 -->
“It was funny!”

<!-- bt-block:000090 -->
“Yeah, okay, it was,” Alex admitted reluctantly. But he was smiling, so he had to give him that one. He relaxed back onto the couch. Grimaced, as he felt the rough, shallow texture.

<!-- bt-block:000091 -->
He really needed to get new furniture. His motorhome only had to be functional, but it didn’t have to be this uncomfortable.

<!-- bt-block:000092 -->
“Allora, Alex, this is fun, but I have to go soon.”

<!-- bt-block:000093 -->
Alex focused back on his phone. “Oh, yeah. No problem. Thanks for, uh. Listening to me ramble about ducks. And geese.”

<!-- bt-block:000094 -->
“Anytime. But, eh, one last thing, before I go.”

<!-- bt-block:000095 -->
“Yeah?”

<!-- bt-block:000096 -->
Valentino hesitated for a long moment. Alex felt the mood shift, ever so slightly, into something more serious. He shifted against the couch, trying not to tense.

<!-- bt-block:000097 -->
“You know I . . . “ Valentino stopped. Sighed. “Whatever, I do not care, anyway. And I am sure my advice is not needed.”

<!-- bt-block:000098 -->
Alex bit his cheek. Picked at his sleeve. A thread had come loose. He pulled on it, pulled and pulled until it caught. “What is it?” he gently prodded when the silence began to stretch on.

<!-- bt-block:000099 -->
Another few seconds of silence, and then—

<!-- bt-block:000100 -->
“ . . . They should not have let him ride again so soon,” Valentino said, and Alex froze. “I hear what happen, and I think, these doctors are crazy. He is crazy. He should not ride with a bone like this, it will not end well. And it didn’t.” He stopped. Sighed again. “Anyway. Is a bit useless for me to say this now. But also, I know he is really crazy, so I tell you, do not let him ride again. I think he will want to. Do not let him.”

<!-- bt-block:000101 -->
Alex stared at the walls of his motorhome. Felt them compressing into his chest, into his lungs, until he was struggling to breathe. “I . . . I don’t know if I can—”

<!-- bt-block:000102 -->
“You have to make him listen. Or he really will not ride ever again.” There was a moment of silence, a bit of shuffling. Another, quieter sigh. “Allora, this is my advice. But I am no, you know, real doctor. So maybe just ignore what I say. What do I know, really.”

<!-- bt-block:000103 -->
Alex pressed his eyes shut. Pressed the phone tightly against his ear. Took a deep breath. “I’ll try to tell him,” he said. “I’ll try to tell him not to ride. But I don’t— I don’t know if I can get him to listen to me, he’s so— Fuck, I don’t know how to—”

<!-- bt-block:000104 -->
He broke off. Breathed into the phone. Listened to the breathing on the other side.

<!-- bt-block:000105 -->
Silence.

<!-- bt-block:000106 -->
More silence.

<!-- bt-block:000107 -->
Alex realized, suddenly, that Valentino had nothing more to say.

<!-- bt-block:000108 -->
Had pushed, as much as he dared, as much as he cared, and now it was all up to Alex.

<!-- bt-block:000109 -->
So, Alex took another deep breath. Steeled himself, like setting up before a race, like turning into a blind corner, like knowing a crash was coming and preparing to bear the brunt of the asphalt.

<!-- bt-block:000110 -->
“I’ll figure something out,” he said, voice low and rough, thoughts spinning. “I could distract him. Lock him in his room. Take away his bikes— wait, no. Take away the wheels on his bikes. Then he really won’t be able to do anything. It’s not like he can put the wheels back on with a broken arm. And I’ll just make sure no one else helps him. Hide them, lock them away, somewhere he won't be able to get them.”

<!-- bt-block:000111 -->
“ . . . That’s a bit extreme, no?”

<!-- bt-block:000112 -->
“It’s necessary,” Alex retorted. “He won’t listen to me, I know he won’t. So rather than trying to prevent him from riding a bike, I’ll just make sure there are no bikes for him to ride at all. Then he won’t be able to do anything, and there’ll be no problems.”

<!-- bt-block:000113 -->
“Allora, you are a bit scary.”

<!-- bt-block:000114 -->
“What?”

<!-- bt-block:000115 -->
“Nothing,” Valentino said. “Okay, you have plan, so I go now. Ciao, and good luck.”

<!-- bt-block:000116 -->
“Ciao,” Alex replied, and the call disconnected. He leaned back into the couch, letting the unpleasant surface scratch into his skin.

<!-- bt-block:000117 -->
He lay there for a minute. Two.

<!-- bt-block:000118 -->
“Alright,” he breathed, bringing up his phone once again, scrolling through his contacts until he reached the name of Marc’s head mechanic. “Let’s get started.”
