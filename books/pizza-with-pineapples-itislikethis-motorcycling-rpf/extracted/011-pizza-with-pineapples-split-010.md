*2024, COTA*

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
Honestly speaking, Alex didn’t regret bringing Marc into Gresini at all.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
He knew it had been hard on Marc, leaving Honda, but Alex also knew it was the right thing to do. That bike would have killed Marc eventually, Alex believed it in his soul. Maybe not this year, maybe not the next one, but eventually. And as soon as that truth became apparent to him, Alex hadn’t been willing to consider the possibility of it actually happening. Not for a millisecond.

<!-- bt-block:000008 -->
So, he’d spoken to Gresini. Not too strongly, at first, not too obviously. But dropping hints here and there, mentioning Marc and his situation and his determination, making it clear that what Marc lacked was not the skill, but the bike. The hunger, at least, had always been apparent enough to everyone.

<!-- bt-block:000009 -->
Within a few months, Gresini had been ready to onboard Marc. And as soon as Gresini was ready, Marc had been ready, too. Well, not ready. But moderately willing. And then Alex had hauled him on, and he’d been a bit happier.

<!-- bt-block:000010 -->
Then he’d gotten onto the blue Ducati, and all doubts had immediately fallen off, just like Alex knew they would. And, for the first time in four years, Alex had looked at Marc, and seen him looking genuinely happy. Genuinely hopeful.

<!-- bt-block:000011 -->
So, genuinely, Alex didn’t regret bringing Marc into Gresini at all.

<!-- bt-block:000012 -->
But, as he looked up at the Practice timing tables, where Marc had come in P3 and Alex P13, Alex did sort of wish, just a little bit, that the direct comparison between them wasn’t quite so direct.

<!-- bt-block:000013 -->
But, there was nothing that could be done about that. So Alex just sat in his garage, taking a bit of a break while he waited for his analysts to prepare the debrief.

<!-- bt-block:000014 -->
His phone lit up with a message, so Alex, after taking a glance around the garage to make sure no one was looking for him, picked it up. It was a photo from Pipa, of a white-sanded beach. There was a small crab in the corner of the picture, and text on top of it that read ‘new friend’.

<!-- bt-block:000015 -->
Alex smiled. Sent back a picture of his dog that he’d taken earlier that week, one where it looked like his eyes were as big as his body. Titled it ‘very nice’. He received a ‘LOL’ for that one, so Alex counted it as a success. Then another message arrived: ‘hows practice?’

<!-- bt-block:000016 -->
‘Meh’ Alex replied. ‘Q1’

<!-- bt-block:000017 -->
‘Bleh’, Pipa replied, and Alex quietly laughed, perfectly able to imagine Valentino’s expression when—

<!-- bt-block:000018 -->
“Something fun?”

<!-- bt-block:000019 -->
“What?” Alex blinked up from his phone to find Marc peering over his shoulder. His helmet was at his side, water-bottle propped on his hip. So, he’d been there a while. Alex tried not to panic as he looked back down at his screen. Nothing incriminating, thank goodness. “Oh, uh. No, just messaging a friend.”

<!-- bt-block:000020 -->
“Who?” Marc leaned in a bit further, and Alex leaned his screen in his direction. “Oh, Pipa.”

<!-- bt-block:000021 -->
“Yep,” Alex said, popping the sound, and then turned his phone off, as casually as he could.

<!-- bt-block:000022 -->
Marc let out a low snort, but still walked around to sit next to Alex. “What about?”

<!-- bt-block:000023 -->
Alex shrugged, trying not to show just how relieved he was that Marc hadn’t seen anything revealing. He really needed to be more aware of his surroundings. That could have been bad. “Just chatting about the weekend. Random race stuff.”

<!-- bt-block:000024 -->
“Oh? Are they are MotoGP fan?”

<!-- bt-block:000025 -->
Alex couldn’t help but snort at the idea of labelling Valentino Rossi as a mere ‘MotoGP fan’. But, then again, he supposed it wasn’t exactly untrue, either. “Yeah, something like that.”

<!-- bt-block:000026 -->
“Why don’t you invite them to the paddock, then?”

<!-- bt-block:000027 -->
“Huh?”

<!-- bt-block:000028 -->
“You know, as a guest. We could get them passes,” Marc said, smiling encouragingly at Alex, as though he felt awkward in doing something like that. “If they’re a fan, I’m sure they’d love it.”

<!-- bt-block:000029 -->
“Oh, no,” Alex blurted out. Quickly tried to come up with a reasonable excuse. “I mean, they’re not, uh. Free. So they can’t really come to races.”

<!-- bt-block:000030 -->
“Ever?” Marc asked.

<!-- bt-block:000031 -->
“Their schedule is . . . complicated,” Alex said, trying not to fidget nervously. Shrugged. “They’re very busy.”

<!-- bt-block:000032 -->
“Still. Wouldn’t it be nice to go to a race? Even just once.” Alex shrugged again. Marc frowned. “You could at least offer.”

<!-- bt-block:000033 -->
“ . . . Sure,” Alex said, because he couldn’t reasonably keep denying the suggestion, although he knew he’d never actually go through with it. “I’ll, uh. Ask them if they want to, sometime.”

<!-- bt-block:000034 -->
“Okay.” Marc looked a bit confused, now, a bit concerned, like he was worried about why Alex wouldn’t want to invite his friend over to a race weekend. Was he worried Alex didn’t have enough friends? Alex hoped it was that simple.

<!-- bt-block:000035 -->
“You did really well out there,” Alex said, changing the topic very obviously, “mind giving me some tips?”

<!-- bt-block:000036 -->
Marc raised an eyebrow at him, noting the switch, but gracefully allowed it as he did, indeed, begin giving Alex some pointers.

<!-- bt-block:000037 -->
So, yeah. Alex really didn’t regret bringing Marc into Gresini at all.

<!-- bt-block:000038 -->
*2024, Sachsenring*

<!-- bt-block:000039 -->
“It’s not like the rest of us can compare to Marc and Vale, so—” Alex paused, an almost imperceptible moment of hesitation, before continuing his answer. The interviewer, thankfully, didn’t seem like he’d really caught on to what Alex had said, or at least had the grace not to push. He was recording everything, though, so Alex would likely still see this in a transcript at some point.

<!-- bt-block:000040 -->
Well. No matter. So long as no one else thought to make anything of it, it would remain as a small point in a small magazine, something no one thought about for longer than a second, and then only to wonder if Alex had been misheard. After all, why would Alex Marquez randomly refer to Valentino Rossi as ‘Vale’? It wasn’t like he personally thought of him that way, surely. Surely, it was just a mistake on the part of the interviewer.

<!-- bt-block:000041 -->
Alex sighed as he got in his car an hour later, closing the door behind him.

<!-- bt-block:000042 -->
Such a stupid, tiny slip-up, that likely no one would notice. Alex himself had barely noticed it, had thankfully barely reacted when he did. Still, it bothered him that he’d slipped up at all. What was next, mentioning ‘Pipa’ on live TV?

<!-- bt-block:000043 -->
Speaking of which . . .

<!-- bt-block:000044 -->
Alex pulled out his phone, scrolled to the contact labelled ‘Pipa’, and pressed the call button. A few rings later, the call connected.

<!-- bt-block:000045 -->
“Ciao, Vale,” Alex greeted, as he began the drive back home. “You got a minute to chat?”

<!-- bt-block:000046 -->
“Ciao, Tola,” Valentino greeted in return. The sound of pots and pans clinking could be heard in the background, along with a light sizzling. “Sure, while I make dinner. What is it?”

<!-- bt-block:000047 -->
“Just finished an interview. They asked me some questions about you, so I wanted to give you a heads up.”

<!-- bt-block:000048 -->
“Oh? What did you say about me?”

<!-- bt-block:000049 -->
“All bad things, don’t worry,” Alex said, laughing at Valentino’s huff. “No, it was pretty relaxed, actually. I talked about my time at Gresini, how bike development is going, that sort of thing. They brought up Marc, of course, but then kind of wanted to compare us to you and Luca, so that’s where you came up a bit.” He paused to look up directions, making sure he was going the right way. Continued talking once he made  sure he wasn’t getting lost. “We also talked a bit about the Yamaha contract. You know, the one I didn’t get.”

<!-- bt-block:000050 -->
“And? Did you blame me?”

<!-- bt-block:000051 -->
“Of course.”

<!-- bt-block:000052 -->
“Annoying bastard,” Valentino murmured, and Alex laughed. “But, eh, Gresini is looking good next year, and Yamaha is kind of shit. So really, I did you a favor.”

<!-- bt-block:000053 -->
“Of course you did,” Alex bit back a smile. “Who knows? Maybe next year, I’ll be competing for the championship.”

<!-- bt-block:000054 -->
“I know you will.”

<!-- bt-block:000055 -->
Alex groaned, even as the smile broke free. “Come on, don’t jinx me like that.”

<!-- bt-block:000056 -->
“Eh, sorry. I meant, you will be bad, so bad. Crash every race, actually.”

<!-- bt-block:000057 -->
“That’s more like it.” A beep came from his phone, and Alex chanced a glance down. Blinked as he saw another incoming. What poor timing was that? “Hey, uh, sorry. Getting a call from Marc. Talk later?”

<!-- bt-block:000058 -->
“Sure. Ciao.”

<!-- bt-block:000059 -->
“Ciao.”

<!-- bt-block:000060 -->
With that, Alex hung up his call with Valentino, and picked up the one from Marc.

<!-- bt-block:000061 -->
“Hey,” he greeted as the call connected.

<!-- bt-block:000062 -->
“Hey,” Marc replied. “You picked up quickly.”

<!-- bt-block:000063 -->
“I was already on my phone,” Alex replied.

<!-- bt-block:000064 -->
“While driving?”

<!-- bt-block:000065 -->
“Talking to someone else.”

<!-- bt-block:000066 -->
“Pipa?”

<!-- bt-block:000067 -->
Alex almost veered off the side of the road, he was so startled. “Ho— what?”

<!-- bt-block:000068 -->
“You know, Pipa,” Marc continued, blithely unaware that he’d almost caused a terrible accident. “That friend you’re always talking to on your phone.”

<!-- bt-block:000069 -->
“Oh. Yeah. Them.” Alex focused back on his driving, taking a deep breath. He shouldn’t have been so startled, Marc knew about Pipa, had known about them for a while— not who they were, of course, just that they existed. But it still made him jump a little whenever the name was mentioned. “Uh, yeah. Yeah. I was talking to Pipa.”

<!-- bt-block:000070 -->
“What about?”

<!-- bt-block:000071 -->
“Uh.” Alex wracked his brain. Tried not to sound as suspicious as he suddenly felt. “You know. Stuff.” Yep, not sounding suspicious at all. Great job, Alex. “So, uh, what were you calling about?”

<!-- bt-block:000072 -->
Marc, thankfully, decided not to push. “You finished the interview, right?”

<!-- bt-block:000073 -->
“Yeah, yeah, driving back now from there.”

<!-- bt-block:000074 -->
“How was it? Did they ask you about Valentino?”

<!-- bt-block:000075 -->
“What?” Alex, again, almost veered off the road. He wasn’t usually so jumpy, but the questions about Pipa earlier had set him on edge. “I mean, yeah, a bit. But why do you ask?”

<!-- bt-block:000076 -->
“I did an interview with them a month ago, and half the questions were about him.”

<!-- bt-block:000077 -->
“Oh, yeah, fair enough.” Alex took a moment to confirm his next turn. Not that one; good thing he checked. “Uh, yeah, they asked about Vale and Luca a bit. Not too much, though, so I didn’t mind.”

<!-- bt-block:000078 -->
Marc was quiet for a few seconds. “Vale and Luca?”

<!-- bt-block:000079 -->
“Yeah, you know, they wanted to compare us as brothers. That sort of thing.”

<!-- bt-block:000080 -->
“ . . . Right.” There was a slightly odd edge to Marc’s voice, but before Alex could figure out why that was, Marc spoke again. “Where are you right now? Could you drop by the grocery store, or is it too late?”

<!-- bt-block:000081 -->
Alex quickly took an early turn. “I can stop by the grocery store, yeah. What do you want?”

<!-- bt-block:000082 -->
“Some more bread, please, and we’re also running out of . . . “

<!-- bt-block:000083 -->
*2024, Misano*

<!-- bt-block:000084 -->
“Hey, Pipa just messaged you.”

<!-- bt-block:000085 -->
Alex fought not to react, turning around from where he’d been cleaning a few dishes in the sink, to find Marc waving his phone in the air. The notification for a message beamed, obnoxiously, from the top of the screen.

<!-- bt-block:000086 -->
“I’ll get it as soon as I finish this,” Alex replied, keeping his voice even. He was pretty sure he’d turned messaging notifications off a long time ago, but his phone had updated last night. Maybe it had reset the setting? Either way, it wasn’t like he wasn’t prepared for Marc randomly seeing his screen. He’d done it plenty of times before.

<!-- bt-block:000087 -->
Sure enough, Marc just hummed. “Sure. Better hurry, though, seems urgent.”

<!-- bt-block:000088 -->
Again, Alex tried his best not to react. Still, he scrubbed at the plate in his hand a bit more quickly. “Urgent? How so?” He was almost completely sure that his notifications didn’t reveal the actual messages, but if they did—

<!-- bt-block:000089 -->
“They’ve sent you five— no, six messages in a row. So seems a bit urgent.”

<!-- bt-block:000090 -->
“Oh. No, that’s normal, they just talk a lot,” Alex replied, relaxing slightly. Still hurried with the last cup. “You can ignore it, it’s fine.”

<!-- bt-block:000091 -->
“Is it?” Marc asked, a teasing tone to his voice. “You’ve got eight, nine messages now. They really aren’t afraid of double-texting huh? Or, uh. Nine-texting, I guess.”

<!-- bt-block:000092 -->
“They sure aren’t,” Alex replied, finally pulling off the dishwashing gloves and drying off his hands where some water had gotten onto them. He quickly made his way back to the living room, reaching over the couch. “Alright, I’m done, hand it over.” He blinked when, rather than handing him his phone, Marc instead moved it away. “Uh. Marc?”

<!-- bt-block:000093 -->
“Are you guys dating?” Marc asked bluntly, and Alex nearly choked on his spit.

<!-- bt-block:000094 -->
“Wha— No! Absolutely not, no. No. What the fuck?”

<!-- bt-block:000095 -->
“Just a question, relax,” Marc replied, eyeing Alex suspiciously from his place on the couch. “I mean, you’ve been talking to this Pipa person for years. And you never want to tell me anything about them. What secret are you keeping, if not that you’re dating them?”

<!-- bt-block:000096 -->
“It’s just a friend, Marc. What, I can’t have friends?” Alex asked, rolling his eyes as theatrically as he could to try and dissimulate his sudden panic. He hadn’t meant to overreact, but Marc’s question had thrown him for a loop. Alex and Valentino, dating? It was an absurd idea. Unthinkable. Ridiculous. Impossible.

<!-- bt-block:000097 -->
Alex firmly ignored the way his stomach twisted at that assertion.

<!-- bt-block:000098 -->
Marc, however, didn’t move. “I know all your other friends. You’ve never hidden a friend from me before. This is definitely something different.”

<!-- bt-block:000099 -->
Alex felt his heartbeat speed up. He tried not to react, again. “Marc—”

<!-- bt-block:000100 -->
“And if you never want me to talk to them, okay, sure— I mean, weird, but, I guess? But not letting me know anything about them is—”

<!-- bt-block:000101 -->
“It’s not like that,” Alex interrupted, doing his best to keep his voice level.

<!-- bt-block:000102 -->
“Then what is it like?” Marc asked, and Alex—

<!-- bt-block:000103 -->
Alex could do nothing but stare at him. Marc, too, stared back at him.

<!-- bt-block:000104 -->
Stared, until, finally, he sighed and reached out with Alex’s phone. Alex, warily, went to reach for it— grabbed it when he could, and gingerly took it back.

<!-- bt-block:000105 -->
“Marc?” he asked.

<!-- bt-block:000106 -->
“It’s fine,” Marc said. Continued, seeing Alex’s frown, “I just . . . I don’t understand. But . . . “ He paused, raked a hand through his hair. “I guess I don’t need to. You can have friends you don’t tell me about. It’s fine.”

<!-- bt-block:000107 -->
It clearly was not fine. But, what this clearly was, was Marc trying to give him an out, and Alex was gladly going to take it. He had no idea how else to deal with the situation, after all.

<!-- bt-block:000108 -->
“ . . . I’m kind of tired,” Alex said, though it was only 8pm, but he wasn’t sure he wanted to risk another fight, not when they both had a race the next day. “I’m going to go sleep early, if that’s okay . . . ?”

<!-- bt-block:000109 -->
He wouldn’t usually ask for permission, obviously. But Marc was upset, and Alex didn’t want to make him even more so. Not when he was, in the end, the reason himself.

<!-- bt-block:000110 -->
Marc, of course, recognized Alex’s attempts at being tactful. He smiled, a bit tightly, but earnestly all the same. “Yeah, don’t worry about me. Go sleep. Goodnight.”

<!-- bt-block:000111 -->
“Goodnight.”

<!-- bt-block:000112 -->
With that, Alex went to his room and locked the door. Waited until he heard Marc’s own door close before pulling out his phone to finally look at all his messages from Pipa. Thinking back to his argument with Marc, Alex briefly considered cancelling his usual Misano late-night meeting with Valentino.

<!-- bt-block:000113 -->
But, no. If he didn’t see him today, Alex wouldn’t be able to see him until late November. And, yes, he’d met with Valentino just two weeks ago, but . . .

<!-- bt-block:000114 -->
But Alex missed him already. And, he wouldn’t be able to see him again until late November! No, no matter how weird Marc was being, Alex still had to try and go. He’d just deal with his brother’s suspicions later, like he always had.

<!-- bt-block:000115 -->
He’d maybe, just maybe, have to talk to Valentino about sending so many messages at once. Alex honestly kind of loved that aspect of their system, as it always gave him something to look forward to when they couldn’t call, but if it was going to cause problems . . .

<!-- bt-block:000116 -->
No, fuck that. They already had to restrict their meetings, their conversations, their photos, their words. Alex hated the thought of having to restrict how much they could message, too.

<!-- bt-block:000117 -->
He would just have to be more careful. Yes, he’d be more careful, and that would be it.

<!-- bt-block:000118 -->
*2024, Thailand*

<!-- bt-block:000119 -->
Alex held the phone against his ear with his shoulder as he focused on mixing up a bowl of late-night cereal.

<!-- bt-block:000120 -->
It was Saturday night, and the race was tomorrow, which meant he probably should be asleep by now. But, he’d finished P15 in Australia, while Marc had won the race, so Alex wasn’t feeling great. So not great was he feeling, in fact, that he’d decided to call Valentino, despite Marc being asleep only a few rooms away.

<!-- bt-block:000121 -->
He hadn’t even called for racing advice, or anything else tangible. Just to feel a bit better, just to distract himself. And, sure enough, distracting himself he was.

<!-- bt-block:000122 -->
“So? Did you put in mango or not?” Valentino asked.

<!-- bt-block:000123 -->
“I told you, I don’t have any cut up,” Alex murmured into the phone, trying to keep his voice low, so as not to wake Marc.

<!-- bt-block:000124 -->
“And I told you to just cut one up,” Valentino sniffed. “You will regret if not.”

<!-- bt-block:000125 -->
Alex snorted. So serious about this weird food concoction, he thought fondly. But, Alex had asked for a late-night snack, and Valentino had provided an idea, so Alex had to at least try and go along with it. Even if it was a bit weird. “I can’t talk to you and cut a mango.”

<!-- bt-block:000126 -->
“Why not?”

<!-- bt-block:000127 -->
“Because I don’t have three hands, you asshole.” Valentino laughed, and Alex huffed in mock annoyance.

<!-- bt-block:000128 -->
“You should have planned ahead, then,” Valentino said, still chuckling. “Before you called me.”

<!-- bt-block:000129 -->
“Yeah, yeah,” Alex muttered back, though he did actually agree with what was being said, “just like you planned ahead before calling me yesterday while repairing that bike motor.”

<!-- bt-block:000130 -->
“I had no problems with that.”

<!-- bt-block:000131 -->
“No? So that fire alarm was just my imagination, then.”

<!-- bt-block:000132 -->
“Yes, and you should get it checked out. Very dangerous, to imagine fire alarms.”

<!-- bt-block:000133 -->
Alex laughed. “I told you already, I like da—”

<!-- bt-block:000134 -->
A quiet shuffle came from behind him, and Alex immediately spun around.

<!-- bt-block:000135 -->
Marc stood by the kitchen table, a hand against the chair closest to him.

<!-- bt-block:000136 -->
“Sorry, didn’t meant to startle you,” Marc said. Smiled. “I was just going to grab some water.”

<!-- bt-block:000137 -->
“Oh, yeah, sure,” Alex replied automatically, before quickly turning back to his phone. “I’ll call you back later.” He didn’t wait for a response before hanging up. Valentino would understand.

<!-- bt-block:000138 -->
“No need—” Marc fell silent upon realizing Alex had already hung up. His mouth pursed as he looked at Alex, and Alex looked away.

<!-- bt-block:000139 -->
They were both silent for a few seconds.

<!-- bt-block:000140 -->
“Alex—”

<!-- bt-block:000141 -->
“No,” Alex interrupted him. Sighed, as he turned back towards Marc. His brother was biting his lip, the only tell in his otherwise blank expression. It made Alex’s chest hurt. He didn’t like keeping secrets from Marc, never would otherwise, but this was— “I’m sorry, but . . . no. Anything else, fine, but not this, okay?”

<!-- bt-block:000142 -->
“I just don’t understand why,” Marc said, quiet and terse. “Did I do something? Say something, that would make you think I wouldn’t like them? Did I insult them, somehow?”

<!-- bt-block:000143 -->
“No, nothing like that.”

<!-- bt-block:000144 -->
“Then, why?” Marc’s eyes caught onto Alex, wide and pleading, as he took a step towards him. “Alex, please, you have to see how weird this is for me. I don’t— I’m trying to respect your privacy, but I’ve never hidden anyone from you. I’ve never really hidden anything from you, anything at all. And I thought you’d never hide anything from me, either. But now—”

<!-- bt-block:000145 -->
“It’s not like that!” Alex blurted out, because he couldn’t stand hearing this. “This isn’t— I’ve never hidden anything from you, either. You know that. You know that, Marc. Please.”

<!-- bt-block:000146 -->
“But you’re hiding this.”

<!-- bt-block:000147 -->
“I’m not really.” Alex could see that Marc didn’t believe him, so he tried to explain. “You know they’re my friend, you’ve known they’re my friend for a long time. I tell you when I’m talking to them. I’m not hiding them.”

<!-- bt-block:000148 -->
Marc pursed his lips. “You won’t even tell me their name.”

<!-- bt-block:000149 -->
“What do you mean?” Alex felt dread curl into his gut. Tried his best to ignore it. “You know their name. It’s Pipa.”

<!-- bt-block:000150 -->
“Sure it is.” Abruptly, Marc’s expression shuttered, and he turned away from Alex. “Whatever. I’m going back to sleep.”

<!-- bt-block:000151 -->
“Marc—”

<!-- bt-block:000152 -->
“I trust you, Alex. I really do.” Marc stopped at the edge of the kitchen. Didn’t turn around. But the set of his back was tight, tense, and his hand gripping the wall was shaking, ever so slightly. “I just wish you trusted me, too.”

<!-- bt-block:000153 -->
Then he was gone, and Alex was left alone in the kitchen.

<!-- bt-block:000154 -->
The half-finished cereal bowl sat next to him, but Alex no longer had much of an appetite. He ended up putting it in the fridge before going back to his room to sleep.

<!-- bt-block:000155 -->
He wouldn’t let it go to waste; he’d definitely eat it before the race.

<!-- bt-block:000156 -->
But, for now, he just couldn’t.

<!-- bt-block:000157 -->
*2024, Sepang*

<!-- bt-block:000158 -->
“Ciao, A—”

<!-- bt-block:000159 -->
“I feel really bad,” Alex said quickly, then: “Sorry for interrupting, sorry. Is it okay if we talk right now?”

<!-- bt-block:000160 -->
“Yes, is fine,” Valentino replied, only a little huffy. Softened, a moment later. “Why do you feel bad?”

<!-- bt-block:000161 -->
“I just—” Alex took a deep breath. He still felt bad about the argument he’d had with Marc last week. It had even affected him during the race, much as he had tried not to think about it. Even worse, Alex worried it might have affected Marc, too, as his results hadn’t been stellar either. But he just didn’t know what to do. “Marc’s been getting suspicious about Pipa lately. And I don’t know how to keep secrets from him. It makes me feel bad.”

<!-- bt-block:000162 -->
There was silence on the other end of the line. Alex could barely hear some shuffling here and there, some quiet breathing. He focused on the domestic sounds, letting them calm him as he leaned back into his motorhome bed. Marc was out, so Alex was alone. Still, he kept his voice low, just in case.

<!-- bt-block:000163 -->
“He needs to learn some boundaries,” Valentino said, after a few minutes. “It is good for him to learn some boundaries, especially from you. Is not healthy, for him to need to know everything you do.”

<!-- bt-block:000164 -->
Alex laughed, quietly, mirthlessly. “I know, I know. But it’s, you know. It’s not . . . “

<!-- bt-block:000165 -->
“Is not so simple, I know,” Valentino murmured, and Alex nodded, despite knowing Valentino wouldn’t be able to see him. “But is important. For him, for you. Like exercise, you know? You have to be sore today, to be strong tomorrow.”

<!-- bt-block:000166 -->
Alex closed his eyes. Took a deep breath. “Hey, Vale . . . Do you think you’ll ever . . . ?”

<!-- bt-block:000167 -->
“No,” Valentino replied, short and curt. Paused. Sighed. “Allora. I do not want to make this difficult for you—”

<!-- bt-block:000168 -->
“You’re not,” Alex interrupted.

<!-- bt-block:000169 -->
“As I say,” Valentino continued, nonplussed, “I do not want to make this difficult, but I will not pretend to get along with your brother. Not even for you. If this becomes too much of a problem for you, we will simply have to stop being friends. That is it.”

<!-- bt-block:000170 -->
Alex let out a long breath. He didn’t take Valentino’s threat seriously, not really, but it was still a bit unnerving to have that possibility laid out at all. “You know, he’s . . . changed, a lot, in the last few years. Calmed down. Matured. And—”

<!-- bt-block:000171 -->
“Alex,” Valentino said, a bit irritably. “I do not like your brother, and he does not like me. I do not want to—”

<!-- bt-block:000172 -->
“He does like you.”

<!-- bt-block:000173 -->
“This again,” Valentino muttered. Alex could practically see him rolling his eyes. “Doesn’t matter. I do not like him. And I will not pretend to. It is up to you to decide what you want to do. Ciao.”

<!-- bt-block:000174 -->
With that, he hung up.

<!-- bt-block:000175 -->
Alex pulled his phone away from his ear. Looked down at the ‘Call Ended’ screen.

<!-- bt-block:000176 -->
Sighed. Called back.

<!-- bt-block:000177 -->
“What?” Valentino greeted sourly.

<!-- bt-block:000178 -->
“I’m sorry,” Alex said. “I know you don’t like him. And you don’t have to. It’s your right. I just . . . I wish you got along. But I know that you don’t. And that’s okay.”

<!-- bt-block:000179 -->
Valentino snorted. “Is okay?”

<!-- bt-block:000180 -->
“Yeah. Yeah. I mean, I have to be okay with it, right?”

<!-- bt-block:000181 -->
