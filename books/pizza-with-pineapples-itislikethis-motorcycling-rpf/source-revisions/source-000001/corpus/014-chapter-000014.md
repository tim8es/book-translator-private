*2025, Valencia*

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
Alex absently wiped at his face, taking a long drag from his water bottle as he made his way back to his motorhome. The debrief after the sprint had been long, much longer than usual, and the sun had long descended past the horizon. But there had been a lot to talk about, and a lot to consider, a lot to celebrate in his victory and prepare for in tomorrow’s race. And, of course, there had been Marc.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
The aftermath of Marco and Marc’s crash had been unpleasant, to say the least.

<!-- bt-block:000008 -->
Not Marco himself, thankfully. He’d been perfectly behaved, as apologetic as anyone could be when he’d spoken to Marc after the race, deferential and properly chastised in every regard, even with Alex. He’d also taken full blame for the incident immediately, both in private and with the media. Really, no one could have asked for anything more of him.

<!-- bt-block:000009 -->
Except, of course, for him to not have crashed into Marc at all in the first place.

<!-- bt-block:000010 -->
But, Alex couldn’t stay angry for too long, looking at Marco’s teary eyes. The Italian rider really wore that kicked-dog expression like no one else.

<!-- bt-block:000011 -->
It had also helped that Valentino had called Alex a few hours afterwards and practically begged for mercy. Not in so many words, of course, but he’d sounded about as contrite as Alex had ever heard him over any incident Marc was involved in. It wasn’t because of Marc, of course, but because of Marco. Still, Alex understood, and he appreciated Valentino’s loyalty, just as Valentino appreciated Alex’s own. And in the end, it really was just an unfortunate accident. Nothing any of them could do about it, and no point in making any of it worse.

<!-- bt-block:000012 -->
But, it did mean that Marc was unable to ride for the rest of the season, which had left him in a right sour mood. He knew how to handle that sort of thing better nowadays, and the fact that he’d already won the championship certainly helped. But, Marc was still extremely irritable, and it was still up to Alex to keep him entertained by involving him in his own races. So in Valencia, Alex had Marc in his box more often than not, and they were once again sharing a motorhome. It made things easier, more simple.

<!-- bt-block:000013 -->
Well, in some ways. It did make Alex’s life a bit less simple, though, given that this was one of the races during which he’d have usually loved to sneak around and spend as much time with Valentino as he could. Especially given that they were now . . . together? Dating? Friends-who-kissed-sometimes? Whatever they were, Alex wanted to spend as much as he could with Valentino. But with Marc there, he couldn’t.

<!-- bt-block:000014 -->
Alex tried not to feel bitter about it.

<!-- bt-block:000015 -->
And, as he reached his motorhome and opened the door to find Marc by the dining table, poring over some of Alex’s telemetry, he really didn’t feel all that bitter at all.

<!-- bt-block:000016 -->
“Hey,” he called out, as he made his way inside and walked over his room. “Gonna shower and then we can figure out dinner, okay?”

<!-- bt-block:000017 -->
“Okay,” Marc called back.

<!-- bt-block:000018 -->
Ten minutes later Alex emerged, drying his hair with a towel and dropping onto the couch with a huff. “Okay. What do you want for dinner? I have my meal preps in the fridge, if you’re down for that.”

<!-- bt-block:000019 -->
Marc didn’t reply for a few seconds. Alex assumed he was just thinking, so he let him be.

<!-- bt-block:000020 -->
A minute passed, and then—

<!-- bt-block:000021 -->
“Alex,” Marc said, dropping onto the couch next to Alex with a low huff. “We need to talk.”

<!-- bt-block:000022 -->
That . . . didn’t sound ominous at all. But maybe it was just a telemetry issue, and Marc was being dramatic for no reason.

<!-- bt-block:000023 -->
“About what?” Alex asked, trying for casual as he wiped the towel over his hair. It was almost dry by that point, thankfully.

<!-- bt-block:000024 -->
“Pipa,” Marc said, and Alex sighed.

<!-- bt-block:000025 -->
“Marc, I’ve told you, it’s not—”

<!-- bt-block:000026 -->
“I know who they are.”

<!-- bt-block:000027 -->
The towel slipped from Alex’s frozen fingers, falling onto his lap. “ . . . What?”

<!-- bt-block:000028 -->
“Or, rather, I know who he is,” Marc said, tone rising as he regarded Alex with a knowing look. “Isn’t that right?”

<!-- bt-block:000029 -->
Alex stared back at him. Tried not to panic. Should he start apologizing? Explaining? Begging Marc to just hear him out, that this wasn’t what it looked like? Except, it was exactly what it looked like. Or, rather, it was worse.

<!-- bt-block:000030 -->
The other thing was— “How did you . . . ?”

<!-- bt-block:000031 -->
“It wasn’t too hard to figure out, once I really starting thinking about it,” Marc said, and in Alex’s opinion, he sounded far too calm about that fact. “The secrecy. The fake name. Being a MotoGP fan, but not being ‘free’ to come to races. How you’ve always absolutely refused to tell me anything about them.”

<!-- bt-block:000032 -->
Alex desperately wondered if he could still somehow salvage the situation. Marc hadn’t yet said an actual name, so maybe he didn’t yet know, just suspected, and was trying to trip Alex up into revealing himself. Alex wouldn’t put it past him. “That’s not really—”

<!-- bt-block:000033 -->
“But the first real clue was in 2023,” Marc interrupted Alex, as though he hadn’t spoken. “I remember how depressed you got after that crash in Le Mans. I thought it was a bit odd, how upset you were over it. But I assumed you just felt bad about the incident.” He paused. “Except, the incident itself wasn’t the issue, was it? It was the person involved in it.”

<!-- bt-block:000034 -->
Alex tensed.

<!-- bt-block:000035 -->
Okay. So. That was a lot more incriminating. But, it still could just be a coincidence—

<!-- bt-block:000036 -->
“There were other clues, too. But, what made me realize who it was . . . “ He paused. Held Alex’s eyes, as if waiting for Alex to say something. When Alex didn’t, Marc sighed and continued. “ . . . was Misano.”

<!-- bt-block:000037 -->
Oh.

<!-- bt-block:000038 -->
“ . . . Misano? What do you mean, Misano?” Alex tried, desperately, to delay the inevitable.

<!-- bt-block:000039 -->
Marc gave him a look, knowing and exasperated and pitying, all at once. “I saw you, Alex. I was walking around at night, and I spotted you two, skulking around in the darkness. You think I wouldn’t recognize you both, even in the dark?”

<!-- bt-block:000040 -->
Oh.

<!-- bt-block:000041 -->
Oh, he did know.

<!-- bt-block:000042 -->
Alex felt the blood drain from his face as panic bled into his stomach. “Marc, please, I—”

<!-- bt-block:000043 -->
“Listen to me first,” Marc interrupted again, although this time a bit more gently. “I’m not . . . I’m not too upset. I mean, I am upset, but I’m more upset over the secrecy than anything else. I honestly just wish you’d trusted me and told me the truth, rather than me having to find out on my own. I understand why you felt like you couldn’t tell me, but . . . I still wish you had.”

<!-- bt-block:000044 -->
Alex was still wary, could still feel the panic brewing in his gut. But Marc really didn’t seem that angry. Somehow, he really did seem to be okay with the situation. Upset, sure, but compared to what Alex had been expecting, genuinely relatively okay.

<!-- bt-block:000045 -->
Maybe that was why he’d waited this long to confront Alex about it. Maybe he’d been angry in the beginning, but after thinking it over for a few months, he’d decided it was . . . okay?

<!-- bt-block:000046 -->
No. That didn’t sound like Marc at all. But what other explanation was there?

<!-- bt-block:000047 -->
“Really? You’re not upset? About . . . him, I mean.”

<!-- bt-block:000048 -->
Marc shifted a bit, leaning back onto the couch cushions. “Not really. I’m just confused, mostly. Just, you know, how you two started talking.”

<!-- bt-block:000049 -->
Alex eyed him suspiciously. But, again, Marc really did seem to be telling the truth, so . . . maybe he really was okay with it? Truly, something Alex would never have expected of him. But maybe he’d completely misjudged his own brother in this context. Alex didn’t think he had, but, maybe? Well, apparently so, if this reaction was anything to go by.

<!-- bt-block:000050 -->
“It was . . . I mean, it kind of started randomly, to be honest,” Alex explained hesitantly. “We ran into each other a few years ago, and just . . . started talking. Kept talking.”

<!-- bt-block:000051 -->
“Hmm. Yeah, I guess that works,” Marc said with a shrug, and again, Alex was frankly baffled at how well Marc was taking this. Really, he kind of felt like he owed Marc an apology now, seeing as he’d been expecting Marc to take this in a completely— “The other question is, does Valentino know?”

<!-- bt-block:000052 -->
Alex’s thoughts immediately screeched to a halt. “What?”

<!-- bt-block:000053 -->
“Does Valentino know?” Marc repeated the question. Alex, meanwhile, could only stare at him in frozen confusion, because, what? What did he mean by that? Marc, seeing his obvious bafflement, frowned. “You haven’t asked?”

<!-- bt-block:000054 -->
Alex still had no idea what was going on. “Does Valentino know what?”

<!-- bt-block:000055 -->
“That you and—” Marc stopped. His eyes narrowed on Alex. “Wait a minute. What do you mean?”

<!-- bt-block:000056 -->
“What do you mean?” Alex asked, still not understanding what Marc was talking about. How would Valentino not know— what would Valentino not know—

<!-- bt-block:000057 -->
“Pipa is Luca, isn’t he?” Alex froze, yet again. Tried desperately to figure out what to say, how to react, how to salvage the situation now that he knew it wasn’t— “He isn’t.”

<!-- bt-block:000058 -->
The panic began to brew again in Alex’s stomach. “I— that’s not—I mean, it—”

<!-- bt-block:000059 -->
“But if it’s not Luca, then who . . . “ Alex watched with dawning horror as Marc put the pieces together, reassembled the puzzle anew, faster than Alex could react to try and stop him.

<!-- bt-block:000060 -->
“Valentino,” Marc finally said, looking nowhere near as calm and composed as he’d been at the beginning of the conversation. “Pipa is Valentino.”

<!-- bt-block:000061 -->
“Marc, I—”

<!-- bt-block:000062 -->
“Are you serious? What are you thinking?” Marc asked, voice climbing higher and higher in pitch with every word as he stared at Alex incredulously. “When did you— how did you— *Valentino*?”

<!-- bt-block:000063 -->
“It was an accident!” Alex blurted out, panic well and truly flourishing now. “I mean, not an accident, but a coincidence! As I told you, we ran into each other a few years ago. Starting talking. And kept talking. That’s it!”

<!-- bt-block:000064 -->
“That’s it. That’s it.” Marc repeated under his breath. He was silent for a long moment, and Alex could almost see the gears turning in his head, the calculations and recalculations and considerations, all of them combining with each other to reach one final, inescapable conclusion: “Alright, clearly you aren’t thinking. So I’m just going to tell you to stop talking to him.”

<!-- bt-block:000065 -->
“What?” Alex blurted.

<!-- bt-block:000066 -->
“Was I not clear? Stop talking to him,” Marc said, tone as solid as a stone wall. “It’s for the best, Alex. Just do what I tell you.”

<!-- bt-block:000067 -->
Alex tried his best to comprehend what was happening. Tried to comprehend what Marc was feeling, and what Alex himself was feeling, beyond just pure, blinding shock.

<!-- bt-block:000068 -->
“No,” he finally said.

<!-- bt-block:000069 -->
It was Marc’s turn to look shocked. “What?”

<!-- bt-block:000070 -->
“No, I’m not going to stop talking to him, least of all because you just tell me to,” Alex said, and, okay, what he was feeling was anger, he could tell pretty clearly now that the shock was waning. Anger, and indignation, and disbelief, and a thread of panic that yet remained buried beneath it all. “What do you think I am, your dog?”

<!-- bt-block:000071 -->
“I think you’re my brother,” Marc snapped. Stopped, then, and closed his eyes, only to open them with renewed determination. “Alright, look. I don’t know what he’s told you—”

<!-- bt-block:000072 -->
“We’ve been talking for years, Marc, he’s told me infinite—”

<!-- bt-block:000073 -->
“And frankly, I don’t care, because it’s not true.” Alex stared at Marc, blankly incredulous, and Marc seemed to realize the ridiculousness of what he’d just said because he added: “I mean, of course some of it has been true, but you know what I’m talking about. The actual important parts, none of those have been true.”

<!-- bt-block:000074 -->
“What are you talking about? What important parts?” Alex asked, because he still had no idea what Marc was talking about. Did he think Valentino had been feeding Alex lies about Marc himself? Trying to manipulate him into hating Marc, or sabotaging him in some way? The idea was patently ridiculous, but Alex couldn’t think of what else—

<!-- bt-block:000075 -->
“The parts where he tells you that he’ll help you. The parts where he tells you that you matter to him. The parts where he tells you that he cares about you,” Marc said, and, oh. “Alex, please. I was there, too. I know what it feels like. But this is Valentino, and you’re my brother. We both know what he’s really trying to get out of this. We both know who it’s really about.”

<!-- bt-block:000076 -->
Alex swallowed back a sudden lump in his throat. “This isn’t about you.”

<!-- bt-block:000077 -->
“Of course it is,” Marc said, and it was clear he was starting to get frustrated. “Look, I know it hurts to realize, but Valentino is not your friend, and he never has been. He’s trying to use you to get to me—”

<!-- bt-block:000078 -->
“This isn’t about you,” Alex repeated, a bit more firmly, because as much sympathy as Alex had for Marc and what he’d gone through with Valentino, if he continued saying things like that, no amount of sympathy would be quite enough to—

<!-- bt-block:000079 -->
“Alex, please. Do you really think he’d be pretending to be friends with you if it weren’t for me?” Marc asked, a tinge of something condescending and mean entering his tone. “I’m being serious, you can’t actually believe that he cares about you. Think about it, why would he ever care about you? You’re my brother, this is obviously about—”

<!-- bt-block:000080 -->
Alex stood, walking past Marc without a word. Without a word, he made his way out of his motorhome, across the paddock, over to Valentino’s motorhome, keeping just enough of a clear mind to make sure no one saw him.

<!-- bt-block:000081 -->
Valentino had barely opened the door before Alex pushed his way inside. Valentino locked the door, watching worriedly after Alex.

<!-- bt-block:000082 -->
“Alex? What—”

<!-- bt-block:000083 -->
“Fuck him,” Alex hissed, dropping down onto the couch. “He’s such a fucking asshole sometimes.”

<!-- bt-block:000084 -->
“Are you talking about who I think you are?” Valentino asked, somewhat hesitantly.

<!-- bt-block:000085 -->
“Of course I am!” Alex exclaimed. Took a deep breath, as he tried his best to steady his emotions. It was hard, though. He was so, so angry. “Fuck.”

<!-- bt-block:000086 -->
“I assume if you are here, you want to talk about it.” Valentino slowly walked over, sitting on the couch next to him. Paused, as he caught sight of Alex’s expression. “Or maybe not?”

<!-- bt-block:000087 -->
“Marc found out,” Alex explained, adding as he saw Valentino’s eyes widen: “About us being friends, not . . . not the rest.”

<!-- bt-block:000088 -->
“Oh,” Valentino said, and he didn’t look at all reassured.

<!-- bt-block:000089 -->
And, okay, Alex definitely was going to have to talk about it. No matter how mad thinking about it made him. “No, but listen. He was angry at me, and, like, that was fine, I get why he’s upset. But then he tried to convince me that the only reason you’re my friend— sorry, ‘pretending’ to be my friend— is because of him. That the only reason you’d ever give a fuck about me at all is because of him. Even though he knows we’ve been friends for years. He thinks it’s all some— some kind of secret plan by you to get back at him, somehow. That it’s all about him. That it’s got nothing to do with me at all.”

<!-- bt-block:000090 -->
Valentino’s mouth pursed, eyes narrowed as he considered Alex’s words.

<!-- bt-block:000091 -->
“That was . . . ” he began. Paused. Grimaced, as he continued: “That was very insulting of him. Towards you, I mean.”

<!-- bt-block:000092 -->
“Right?” Alex hissed. “It’s like, what he said about you, I get it— Sorry, but, I get it— but why the fuck would he say that about me? Making it sound like I’m not— like there’s nothing I could ever do that would make you—” He stopped. Took a deep breath, slowly released it. “And I know he wasn’t thinking. I know he didn’t realize what he was implying. He’s like that sometimes, you know? Just doesn’t think, especially when he’s angry—”

<!-- bt-block:000093 -->
“Oh, believe me, I know.”

<!-- bt-block:000094 -->
“Right, yes, you know. But, fuck. That was so—”

<!-- bt-block:000095 -->
“He’s a fucking asshole sometimes.” A laugh burst out of Alex at Valentino’s matter-of-fact tone.

<!-- bt-block:000096 -->
“Exactly! I thought he’d call me a traitor, or something like that.” Alex curled into himself, bringing his hands up around his arms. “But he didn’t. Called me worthless, instead.”

<!-- bt-block:000097 -->
A knock came suddenly from the motorhome’s front door. Alex and Valentino looked at each other, both knowing exactly who it must be.

<!-- bt-block:000098 -->
“I think,” Valentino said, eyeing the door with some trepidation, “if I answer that right now, he will actually kill me.”

<!-- bt-block:000099 -->
Alex sighed, rubbing his forehead. He couldn’t, however, completely refute Valentino’s statement in that moment. So he stood and, waving Valentino to the side, went over to the entrance.

<!-- bt-block:000100 -->
He took a deep breath before opening it. As expected, Marc was waiting on the other side, looking up at the door with a dark-eyed glare that instantly faded upon finding Alex on the other side.

<!-- bt-block:000101 -->
Alex couldn’t help but scowl at him. Of course he’d been expecting Valentino. Did he actually think Alex was an idiot? Or did he really think Alex would let Valentino fight his battles for him? Either way, deeply insulting.

<!-- bt-block:000102 -->
“Why are you here, Marc?” he asked, making sure to keep his voice quiet.

<!-- bt-block:000103 -->
“Where’s Valentino?” Marc returned the question, voice equally quiet. He was dressed in a nondescript, dark hoodie, too, so Alex felt secure at least in allowing the conversation to take place. Still, he kept a watch out for anyone who might come by at that moment. Late at night, no one should, but Alex knew well how carefully these secrets had to be kept.

<!-- bt-block:000104 -->
“This is his motorhome, where do you think?”

<!-- bt-block:000105 -->
“So he is here.” Marc’s eyes again narrowed, focusing on the space behind Alex, though Alex knew Valentino was smart enough to stay well out of sight.

<!-- bt-block:000106 -->
“Marc,” Alex snapped, and Marc’s expression shot up to him in surprise, “why are you here?”

<!-- bt-block:000107 -->
Marc stared at him. Took a deep breath. “I want to talk.”

<!-- bt-block:000108 -->
“No.”

<!-- bt-block:000109 -->
Marc stopped. Blinked up at Alex, scandalized. “What do you mean, no?”

<!-- bt-block:000110 -->
“I mean, no,” Alex said, enunciating each word very, very clearly. “If I try to talk to you right now, I’m going to say a lot of things I don’t mean. So, I’m actually going to ask you to leave.”

<!-- bt-block:000111 -->
Marc gaped at Alex. Tried to gather his bearings. “I’m not leaving you alone with him,” he said, and that was—

<!-- bt-block:000112 -->
It was such a hilarious statement that Alex couldn’t help but laugh, out loud, in Marc’s face.

<!-- bt-block:000113 -->
“I’ve been alone with him, many times, over the years,” he said once he found his voice again. “You just didn’t know about any of them.”

<!-- bt-block:000114 -->
Marc looked a bit like he’d just swallowed a lemon at that. “But I know now, so—”

<!-- bt-block:000115 -->
“What exactly do you think is going to happen here?” Alex interrupted him, his anger flaring again at Marc’s inability to listen, his unwillingness to understand, even when Alex was trying, he was trying so hard to be— “Do you think that he’ll make me angry at you? That he’ll convince me that you’re a giant asshole? That he’ll make me hate you? Because let me tell you, you’ve been doing a far better job of that tonight than he ever has.”

<!-- bt-block:000116 -->
He stopped short at the expression on Marc’s face, pale and frozen and hurt.

<!-- bt-block:000117 -->
“Fuck,” Alex breathed out, regret over his outburst making his anger immediately recede into guilt, into a bone-deep exhaustion that made him want to do nothing other than curl up and cry. “I didn’t mean that. This is what I meant. Please just leave.”

<!-- bt-block:000118 -->
But Marc didn’t move. “Alex, I need you to listen to me, he’s not—”

<!-- bt-block:000119 -->
“I think you’ve said enough for today, Marquez.” Alex startled, looking to his side to find Valentino standing at the door, peering down at Marc with a frown. “Go on, leave. Don’t make me call security.”

<!-- bt-block:000120 -->
Alex immediately looked back down, and Marc—

<!-- bt-block:000121 -->
For a split second, Marc looked like he’d just been gutted. Eviscerated, right there in front of Valentino’s motorhome, guts and blood spilling where anyone could see them.

<!-- bt-block:000122 -->
Then his expression darkened, emotions closing off with the finality of a guillotine, and he looked about ready to leap forward and rip Valentino’s head off with his teeth.

<!-- bt-block:000123 -->
Adrenaline flooded Alex’s system.

<!-- bt-block:000124 -->
“Marc,” he said, instantly moving to place himself between the two of them, watching with trepidation as Marc’s eyes didn’t stray away from Valentino’s for a second, even when Alex was directly blocking his way. For a moment, Alex feared Marc still wouldn’t listen to him. That Valentino actually might be forced to call security, if only to prevent a bloody assault in the middle of the Valencia circuit.

<!-- bt-block:000125 -->
“Marc,” he called again, tried again— again and again, though it never seemed to work when he really needed it, Marc never seemed to listen to him when he really needed him to, no matter how— “Marc, *please*.”

<!-- bt-block:000126 -->
His voice broke on the last word. And that, finally, seemed to snap Marc away from Valentino. He turned to Alex, and Alex wasn’t sure what expression he was making in that moment, but he watched as Marc’s face froze once more, shifting into something a bit scared, a bit panicked.

<!-- bt-block:000127 -->
A bit like he looked whenever Alex got hurt, really hurt, the kind of hurt Marc couldn’t easily fix with an apology or a bandaid. Alex hadn’t seen Marc look like that in a long time. But, Alex hadn’t gotten hurt in a long time.

<!-- bt-block:000128 -->
Not really. Not like this.

<!-- bt-block:000129 -->
“We’ll talk tomorrow,” Alex said, when it became apparent no one else was going to say anything. “After the race.”

<!-- bt-block:000130 -->
For a moment, Marc looked like he was still going to argue.

<!-- bt-block:000131 -->
But then, all the fight left his body.

<!-- bt-block:000132 -->
“After the race,” he echoed, and he sounded defeated. Looked defeated, too, as he took a step away from the motorhome. “Goodnight.”

<!-- bt-block:000133 -->
“Goodnight,” Alex replied, and closed the door in his face.

<!-- bt-block:000134 -->
It took him a few minutes after that to gather himself again. He realized at some point that Valentino had retreated back into the motorhome during his and Marc’s final confrontation, which was probably for the best.

<!-- bt-block:000135 -->
Alex released a deep breath and walked back inside. He found Valentino by the kitchenette, pouring himself some water. Alex watched him for a moment, letting his emotions settle. He then walked right up to Valentino and drew him into a hug.

<!-- bt-block:000136 -->
Valentino immediately returned the gesture, brushing Alex’s hair back as Alex buried his face into Valentino’s neck. The smell of him was familiar and comforting, a mix of sweat and petrol and leather, as well as something uniquely and undeniably Valentino.

<!-- bt-block:000137 -->
“So, that went great,” Valentino said after a long moment of silence, and Alex almost choked on his laughter.

<!-- bt-block:000138 -->
“He’s just worried about me,” he said, though he knew it wasn’t quite that, either. Valentino knew it too, if his doubtful hum was anything to go by. “But that could have gone better, yes.”

<!-- bt-block:000139 -->
Truthfully, Alex didn’t completely blame Marc for his anger. He knew it wasn’t just about Valentino, either— of course, Valentino was a big part of it, but not all. The secrecy over Pipa had bothered Marc for a long time, and it was what he’d been most upset over when he’d thought Pipa was Luca. Another issue was Marc’s injury; Alex couldn’t be sure Marc would have reacted better if he hadn’t been injured, but it certainly hadn’t helped.

<!-- bt-block:000140 -->
But, as Alex had told Valentino, what he was most upset about wasn’t Marc’s anger. It was how he’d directed it.

<!-- bt-block:000141 -->
Marc was far too perceptive sometimes. He had a talent for knowing, instinctively, just where to hit to make it hurt— that mean streak of his that he had, over the years, learned better and better how to hide, how to harness into a joke rather than a stab, how to tone it into something teasing rather than malicious.

<!-- bt-block:000142 -->
He usually made a concerted effort not to use that talent on Alex.

<!-- bt-block:000143 -->
He hadn’t, this time. Had aimed, in fact, right for the throat.

<!-- bt-block:000144 -->
Valentino was silent for another long moment. “Do you want me to try and talk to him?” he asked, finally. He didn’t sound enthused by the idea, but he sounded willing to try, if Alex needed it.

<!-- bt-block:000145 -->
And Alex appreciated that Valentino was willing to do that for him, truly he did. But, he knew it wasn’t possible.

<!-- bt-block:000146 -->
“No,” Alex said. “No. I have to do this by myself.” He steadied himself, holding onto Valentino even more tightly, taking what strength he could from their closeness. “I have to do this for myself.”

<!-- bt-block:000147 -->
“Yes, I think so too,” Valentino immediately replied, choking as Alex bit his jaw, none too gently. “Ow! Why? I was trying to be nice.”

<!-- bt-block:000148 -->
“You were,” Alex said, kissing the spot he’d bitten. “But I’m in a bad mood.”

<!-- bt-block:000149 -->
“Yes, this I can understand,” Valentino said. He was then silent for a long moment, and when he spoke again, his voice had fallen to a murmur, hesitant and unsure. “Alex . . . if this is too difficult, maybe I think it might be best if we—”

<!-- bt-block:000150 -->
“No,” Alex snapped, immediately softening his voice as Valentino’s expression tightened. “I’m sorry. I just, I know what you’re going to say. But no. I don’t want to do that.”

<!-- bt-block:000151 -->
“Even if you do not want, it may—”

<!-- bt-block:000152 -->
“No.” Alex pulled Valentino in closer, raising a hand to his jaw. Valentino wouldn’t meet his eyes, and Alex’s throat tightened. “Vale, please. Do you trust me?”

<!-- bt-block:000153 -->
Valentino looked down. Sighed. Finally looked back up to meet Alex’s eyes. “Yes,” he said, and Alex’s heart leapt in his chest, “but this sort of thing is not about trust. I do not want . . . “ He stopped. Emotions flashed across his face, open and honest and elusive all at once. A flicker of vulnerability, there and gone. “I know how important he is to you. He is you brother, your family. I do not want to be—”

<!-- bt-block:000154 -->
