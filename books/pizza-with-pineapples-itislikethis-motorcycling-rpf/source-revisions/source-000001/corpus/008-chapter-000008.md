*2021, Valencia*

<!-- bt-block:000005 -->
Alex scrolled through his Instagram page, liking almost every photo that crossed his feed. Every once in a while he looked up to take in the rolling hills of the countryside, to rest his eyes for a few seconds. Then, back to scrolling.

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
He’d nearly cried in Misano. Thankfully he hadn’t actually, or he would’ve had some explaining to do. He just hadn’t expected to get nearly as emotional as he had.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
Alex would’ve wanted to join the celebrations if he could. Knew he couldn’t.

<!-- bt-block:000008 -->
He sighed heavily, leaning his head against the cool glass of the car window. It was frustrating, how much he couldn’t do. It wouldn’t even have to necessarily have been a big thing, either. Just a short message. Just a small gesture. One ‘Grazie, Vale’, painted in light colors, tiny, where no one would notice.

<!-- bt-block:000009 -->
But it was too risky. It would only take one person noticing— only a random photo, aimed at something else, catching an edge of the paint, posted in ignorance— for the questions to start. And one question might be fine, two, three— but no matter how well he handled it in the moment, it would always remain there, on the edges of everyone’s consciousness.

<!-- bt-block:000010 -->
Alex, publicly supporting Valentino. The best they could hope for was everyone ignoring it. The more likely thing that would happen is that they’d try and use Alex as a knife. Again. Against Marc this time, perhaps, if the interviewer was feeling particularly vicious, particularly hateful.

<!-- bt-block:000011 -->
Not, of course, that they’d expect Alex to attack Marc. Rather, they’d expect him to rear his head, attack in the other direction in his anger. A mindless weapon. Just like always.

<!-- bt-block:000012 -->
Instead of that, Alex was stuck on his alternate, secret Instagram account, the one he used exclusively to interact with Valentino’s posts. Not that he interacted with them very often, but he was scared that if he used his official account, he might at some point accidentally like one of Valentino’s posts. Then, just like the tiny bit of paint, he’d be exposed.

<!-- bt-block:000013 -->
So, secret it was. And, just this once, Alex allowed himself to properly interact. Liking every post that referenced the farewell, no matter how small, no matter how removed from the event itself. Just liking, liking, liking. It helped settle some of the frustration, the helplessness of it all. Because Alex hated feeling helpless, even in something like this.

<!-- bt-block:000014 -->
He hadn’t really had a chance to talk to Valentino since Valencia had ended a few days ago, either. Not even just to say hi, or a single congratulation. Alex knew Valentino was probably extremely busy right now, taking care of everything that his departure from MotoGP entailed, and he didn’t want to bother him. So, he’d decided to wait until Valentino called him first, decided to give him this space for himself, decided to be selfless and patient, because as much as Alex wanted to talk to Valentino, this wasn’t about Alex at all.

<!-- bt-block:000015 -->
He just hoped he’d call soon.

<!-- bt-block:000016 -->
“We’re here,” the driver called out, snapping Alex out of his daze. He thanked the driver as he got out of the car, taking care to bring all his luggage with him. He made his way up to his apartment, letting himself in without incident.

<!-- bt-block:000017 -->
The light coming from the window draped the entryway in a cool ambiance, drawn and dark, with barely enough visibility to prevent Alex from tripping over the various chairs and baskets and drawers and boxes and medical equipment and exercise equipment that littered the apartment floor as he made his way across the living room to where their couch was. Even in the low light, Alex could easily pick out Marc, lounging on the furniture, with a swathe of bandages wrapped around his head. He seemed to be napping, but Alex doubted he actually was.

<!-- bt-block:000018 -->
“Marc?” he called out as he approached, wary of startling him, though obviously Marc would have heard the door.

<!-- bt-block:000019 -->
Marc turned to him. Alex couldn’t see his eyes from below the bandages, but the way the rest of his expression lit up in recognition was unmistakable.

<!-- bt-block:000020 -->
“Alex?”

<!-- bt-block:000021 -->
“Yeah, it’s me.”

<!-- bt-block:000022 -->
Marc immediately reached for his face, pulling up the bandage to reveal his eyes. Sure enough, they crinkled at the edges when they finally landed on Alex, pleased and soft and delighted all at once.

<!-- bt-block:000023 -->
Yet again, Alex hadn’t expected himself to get so emotional. Yet again, he barely managed not to cry.

<!-- bt-block:000024 -->
“Should you be doing that?” he asked, approaching the couch when Marc made little grabby motions with his hand, reaching out to hold it as soon as he was close enough. “Won’t it hurt your eyes?”

<!-- bt-block:000025 -->
“Hurts a little, but I don’t care, I want to see you.”

<!-- bt-block:000026 -->
“Marc—”

<!-- bt-block:000027 -->
“It’ll be fine, it’s just for a few seconds.” Marc tightened his hold on Alex’s hand reassuringly before letting him go.

<!-- bt-block:000028 -->
Alex made himself comfortable next to Marc, sighing as he finally allowed himself to relax completely. He was feeling somewhat exhausted, despite not really having done much all day, but travelling always—

<!-- bt-block:000029 -->
“What’s ‘Tola’?”

<!-- bt-block:000030 -->
“What?” Alex’s eyes snapped back open as he stared at Marc, who was looking down at his side. Alex looked down, realizing his phone had slipped out of his pocket, and the screen had lit up with a notification.

<!-- bt-block:000031 -->
“Your, uh. Instagram? I think?” Marc’s eyes flicked back up to him. “Sorry, didn’t mean to snoop. Just saw the screen light up with one of those, ‘recommendations for you!’ things.”

<!-- bt-block:000032 -->
Alex tried not to overreact. He’d forgotten to switch back to his regular account. How stupid, how stupid. Hopefully, Marc wouldn’t actually think anything of it. “Oh, yeah. No worries.”

<!-- bt-block:000033 -->
Marc, however, didn’t pick up on his hints to drop the subject. Seemed, if anything, a bit too curious. “So. Tola? New nickname?”

<!-- bt-block:000034 -->
“Oh. Um.” Seeing Marc’s expression, Alex knew he couldn’t evade answering entirely. “Not really. Just, you know. Don’t want to have everything on my main account. Get a bit paranoid that I might like something weird by accident.”

<!-- bt-block:000035 -->
“Yeah, yeah, of course. But I thought your private account had a different name.”

<!-- bt-block:000036 -->
“It does,” Alex said, a bit nervously. What to say? How to explain this? How to get Marc to drop the subject? “This one’s . . . uh, more private. Just for me. Just for fun.”

<!-- bt-block:000037 -->
“Oh, ‘private fun’!” Marc’s expression abruptly cleared with understanding. He wiggled his eyebrows, grin widening into something playful and knowing. “I see, I get it.”

<!-- bt-block:000038 -->
Alex almost choked on his spit as he realized what Marc was implying. “Not like that!”

<!-- bt-block:000039 -->
Marc laughed. “Hey, it’s okay if it is! Everyone does that sort of thing. Nothing to be ashamed of!”

<!-- bt-block:000040 -->
Alex groaned, trying again not to overreact. It probably would be better to pretend that was the purpose of the account. Then Marc wouldn’t ask questions, even if he accidentally saw it again. Alex would simply not think about the fact that he was now essentially treating Valentino as a hidden porn account. He would not think about that. Nope. Never. “ . . . Can we just. Not talk about this? Please?”

<!-- bt-block:000041 -->
“Sure, sure.” Marc eased back onto the couch, finally pulling the bandages back over his eyes. “So, anyway, tell me, how was your trip back? Anything fun happen?”

<!-- bt-block:000042 -->
It took three days for Alex’s phone to light up with a call. He declined the call under Marc’s questioning look, ignoring how his eyebrows twitched upon seeing the name at the top of the screen.

<!-- bt-block:000043 -->
“Just a sec, gotta take this,” Alex said, pushing himself up from the couch. He went to their room, closing the door behind him, and only after he was sure he was alone did he call back.

<!-- bt-block:000044 -->
“Ciao, Pipa.”

<!-- bt-block:000045 -->
“Ciao, Tola.”

<!-- bt-block:000046 -->
“I only have a few minutes, sorry. But I wanted to say congratulations.”

<!-- bt-block:000047 -->
Valentino huffed a laugh. He didn’t sound very happy. “Eh, thank you? I think?”

<!-- bt-block:000048 -->
“You successfully finished a MotoGP career. A pretty good one, I’d say.”

<!-- bt-block:000049 -->
“Pretty good?”

<!-- bt-block:000050 -->
“Okay, fine, it was kind of mediocre.”

<!-- bt-block:000051 -->
“Bastard,” Valentino huffed as Alex laughed. “Anyway, I’ll begin testing in a few days.”

<!-- bt-block:000052 -->
How very like Valentino, Alex mused, to move on from the past twenty years of his life with nary a flinch. He busied himself with rearranging the books on Marc’s side of the bookshelf, but soon realized there were just too many of them. Decided it was best to leave the organizing for another day. “Testing? Cars?”

<!-- bt-block:000053 -->
“Mhm. GT3, with BMW. I want to move on to Hypercar eventually, but that’s more difficult.”

<!-- bt-block:000054 -->
“Hypercars are cool,” Alex said. Jokingly added: “No F1?”

<!-- bt-block:000055 -->
Valentino snorted. “I’m too good for F1. It would be embarrassing for their drivers, so I can’t do it.”

<!-- bt-block:000056 -->
“Of course, of course,” Alex laughed. “With that neck of yours, who could compare?”

<!-- bt-block:000057 -->
“Exactly.” Valentino fell silent for a few seconds, then said, a bit more quietly, “I hope this can be a thing I do for a while. Maybe ten years.”

<!-- bt-block:000058 -->
“That’s a lot of time,” Alex murmured.

<!-- bt-block:000059 -->
“I plan for ten, we see how long it actually goes. Like my MotoGP career. Initially I plan for five, became twenty.”

<!-- bt-block:000060 -->
“What I’m hearing is, now you plan for ten, so you’ll be racing until you’re eighty.”

<!-- bt-block:000061 -->
Valentino laughed. “If it is fun enough, then yes, I would not mind that.”

<!-- bt-block:000062 -->
“Then I’ll be rooting for you from my retirement home.” Alex stopped as he suddenly heard a low shuffle outside his door. He lowered his voice to a whisper. “I have to go.”

<!-- bt-block:000063 -->
“Ciao, Tola. Take care.”

<!-- bt-block:000064 -->
“You too.”

<!-- bt-block:000065 -->
Alex hung up his phone and walked to the door, opening it to find Marc with his arm raised, ready to knock.

<!-- bt-block:000066 -->
“Hey,” Marc said, lowering his arm, a bit awkwardly. “Sorry, didn’t mean to interrupt. Just wanted to ask if you were hungry.”

<!-- bt-block:000067 -->
“No worried, I was already done,” Alex said. “I’m not super hungry, but if you are, I can make you something.”

<!-- bt-block:000068 -->
“Okay, thanks,” Marc said, as they made their way back to the living room. He made a small noise of annoyance as he bumped into the edge of a table, into a box on the floor. Raised one of his legs to get over a small trophy case. “How do you feel about pasta?”

<!-- bt-block:000069 -->
“What kind of pasta?” Alex asked, while moving aside to avoid a stack of resistance bands, only to almost step on a set of weights. That would have hurt.

<!-- bt-block:000070 -->
“I was thinking . . . “

<!-- bt-block:000071 -->
As they chatted, Alex found himself looking around the apartment. It was still kept mostly dark, to help with Marc’s eye recovery, but he’d gotten mostly used to it.

<!-- bt-block:000072 -->
Still, he kept almost tripping over stuff. Day in, day out, the floor was just . . . covered. Alex hadn’t really noticed, before, just how much stuff there was. But now that he had, he couldn’t unsee it.

<!-- bt-block:000073 -->
“Hey, Marc?”

<!-- bt-block:000074 -->
“Yeah?”

<!-- bt-block:000075 -->
Alex took another look around the apartment. At the endless shelves filled with endless knickknacks filled with endless dust filled with endless memories. The couches packed against the walls, the trophies packed against the cabinets, the tables and chairs and medical equipment and exercise equipment packed against the floors, the windows, the doors. Years and years of belongings that, left to their own devices, would grow to become years and years more.

<!-- bt-block:000076 -->
This is a fire hazard, Alex thought. Among other things.

<!-- bt-block:000077 -->
“What do you think about us getting a house?”

<!-- bt-block:000078 -->
“I hate him.”

<!-- bt-block:000079 -->
“Who?”

<!-- bt-block:000080 -->
Alex turned around. Looked down the wooden path behind him, making sure there were no other hikers on the trail that might overhear him. He’d come to the trail at a time when few others did, but it never hurt to make sure he was alone. Kept walking as soon as he was sure he was safe, but still lowered his voice, just in case. “Marc.”

<!-- bt-block:000081 -->
“Uh,” Valentino said, very eloquently. “I mean, I understand, obviously, but. Why?”

<!-- bt-block:000082 -->
“You know how we’ve been touring houses for the past month, trying to find a new place? Well, turns out, he’s useless. Like, last week, he wanted to buy a house that had three basements and an attached yard. An attached yard!”

<!-- bt-block:000083 -->
“Ah. Yes. That sounds. Bad?”

<!-- bt-block:000084 -->
“Don’t worry about it.” Alex mentally reassessed his estimation of Valentino’s knowledge of the subject. But, he was there to complain, and complain he would. “He also wanted to buy a house that had ten bathrooms. But, get this, it had two bedrooms.”

<!-- bt-block:000085 -->
“Okay, that is a weird ratio.”

<!-- bt-block:000086 -->
“Right? But he was all like, ‘no, it’s fine, more bathrooms, better for guests!’ What guests? Where are they staying, with our two bedrooms? Are they staying in the bathrooms? Is that what you want, Marc? Our guests sleeping in our ten bathrooms?”

<!-- bt-block:000087 -->
Alex could hear Valentino doubling over in laughter, and it helped ease some of his frustrations.

<!-- bt-block:000088 -->
“He’s so useless when it comes to taking care of the house, too. Like, he’d buy a hundred year old house because it ‘looks pretty’ but not care that it’s infected with mold. Or he wanted to get this one because it had a giant oak dining table, never mind that it was infested with termites. And one time, one of the houses had a bunch of raccoons in the attic, and he was like, oh, that’s okay, raccoons are cute! Like? What? What is wrong with him?”

<!-- bt-block:000089 -->
“I ask that every day,” Valentino said, continuing to laugh. “I do not envy you one bit, living with him.”

<!-- bt-block:000090 -->
Alex sighed, rubbing at his face. “It’s not that bad, really. It’s just, it’s his house, but for some reason, I have to be the one making sure his house doesn’t suck.”

<!-- bt-block:000091 -->
“Just let it suck.”

<!-- bt-block:000092 -->
“I’ll let you suck it,” Alex replied without thinking. Stopped, as he began thinking. “Uh, ah. Hmm. That’s not what I— Hmm. Please ignore that.”

<!-- bt-block:000093 -->
Thankfully, Valentino just seemed to find his slip-up hilarious, because he continued laughing even as Alex feared he’d get an actual fever from how hot his face suddenly felt.

<!-- bt-block:000094 -->
“Anyway!” He cleared his throat. “Point is, if it ends up being a great house, it’s because I’m the one in charge of finding it. If it ends up being a terrible house, it’s because Marc didn’t listen to me.”

<!-- bt-block:000095 -->
“Spinning the narrative already?”

<!-- bt-block:000096 -->
“I’ve seen what he can do, I know to be prepared,” Alex said, as Valentino laughed. “But yeah, I don’t have too much hope. I mean, don’t get me wrong, I’ll do my best. But I think eventually I’ll just have to make my own house.”

<!-- bt-block:000097 -->
“Eh? Haven’t even moved in yet, and already planning on moving out?”

<!-- bt-block:000098 -->
“Not for a few years, probably. But thinking about it helps me feel better.” Alex hummed as he absentmindedly kicked at a rock, watching it skip across the trail and smack into a fallen tree. He followed its lead, scampering over the obstacle and making his way forward. “Then I’ll invite you over, and you can tell me what you think. Judge who has better taste, though it’s obviously me.”

<!-- bt-block:000099 -->
There was silence for a moment.

<!-- bt-block:000100 -->
“Allora, you think so far ahead,” Valentino said, with a bit too much evenness in his tone. It brought Alex up short.

<!-- bt-block:000101 -->
“What do you mean?” he asked, a little nervously.

<!-- bt-block:000102 -->
“Allora,” Valentino repeated. Fell silent for a second. “Inviting me over, in a few years, to a house that doesn’t even exist yet. Is that not thinking far ahead?”

<!-- bt-block:000103 -->
“I guess so,” Alex replied. The explanation made sense, but he had a niggling doubt that wasn’t what Valentino was really talking about. “Would you not come? We’d be discrete, of course. I’m not talking about making it a public event or anything.”

<!-- bt-block:000104 -->
“I think I’ll forget, is what I mean,” Valentino said, and the ease had returned to his voice. “So you’ll have to ask me again when is closer to the actual date, okay?”

<!-- bt-block:000105 -->
“Oh,” Alex said, as he realized what Valentino meant. Not that he wouldn’t want to come, but that he wasn’t sure Alex would want him to come, still, a few years in the future. Which, Alex hoped he did. Certainly hoped nothing would happen between them that would make Alex want to rescind the invitation. Would try his best to ensure it wouldn’t be rescinded, in fact, if he had anything to say about it. “Sure, I’ll make sure to remind you.” He paused. “Old man.”

<!-- bt-block:000106 -->
Valentino let out a loud cough as Alex laughed. “Bastard,” Valentino said, but he sounded like he, too, was laughing. “Now I don’t actually want to go. Keep your ugly house to yourself.”

<!-- bt-block:000107 -->
“It won’t be ugly!”

<!-- bt-block:000108 -->
“Eh, who can say? I’ll never see it, so.”

<!-- bt-block:000109 -->
“You will, I’ll make sure of it.”

<!-- bt-block:000110 -->
“What, you’ll kidnap me?”

<!-- bt-block:000111 -->
“If I have to.”

<!-- bt-block:000112 -->
“Allora, you really are a bit scary,” Valentino said, but he still sounded amused, so Alex didn’t take him too seriously. “Okay, so I don’t get kidnapped, I promise to go see your house. When you have it.”

<!-- bt-block:000113 -->
“I’ll hold you to that.”

<!-- bt-block:000114 -->
“Just remember, I might forget.”

<!-- bt-block:000115 -->
“Oh, don’t worry,” Alex said. “I’ll make sure you don’t.”

<!-- bt-block:000116 -->
“Scary,” Valentino murmured. Paused, before adding, softly: “But, you know. I don’t mind.”

<!-- bt-block:000117 -->
Alex felt his cheeks warm up ever so slightly. Couldn’t possibly have said why. Maybe the day was too hot for hiking. He was wearing a long-sleeved shirt, and there were even a few patches of snow on the ground, but who was to say, really?

<!-- bt-block:000118 -->
But he bravely kept going, meandering through the wilderness, shifting the subject to what plans they each had for next week.

<!-- bt-block:000119 -->
For no reason, of course. Couldn’t possibly have said why.
