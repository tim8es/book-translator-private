Chapter 1

<!-- bt-block:000002 -->
*2019, Misano*

<!-- bt-block:000003 -->
Alex brushed his forehead, wiping away a bead of sweat that had been making its way down his face. He breathed in the cool night air, trying to slow his racing heartbeat. Behind him, the party music blared, loud and unrepentant in the otherwise quiet paddock, a bright neon sign of irreverence, of insolence.

<!-- bt-block:000004 -->
Alex turned away from it, a faint sense of guilt trailing behind him like a shadow, inescapable and foreboding.

<!-- bt-block:000005 -->
Marc wouldn’t mind that he was missing, likely wouldn’t even notice. He was having fun with his team, with his crew and his friends and his fans. Alex was his kid brother, old enough to drink but not quite old enough for anything else. He was there because Marc was there. That was it.

<!-- bt-block:000006 -->
Alex tried not to feel bitter about it. Managed it, almost. It wasn’t Marc’s fault that he was the superstar that everyone revolved around. It wasn’t Marc’s fault that Alex didn’t quite like always revolving around him.

<!-- bt-block:000007 -->
It maybe was a bit Marc’s fault, that he never quite noticed.

<!-- bt-block:000008 -->
Didn’t notice, chose not to notice. It was hard to tell, sometimes, with Marc, whether he was ignoring something because he genuinely didn’t realize it was happening, or because he just wished it wasn’t happening. Because he hoped that, if he just ignored it for long enough, it would go away.

<!-- bt-block:000009 -->
Marc wouldn’t hurt Alex on purpose, Alex knew. But he sometimes felt like Marc knew he had hurt Alex, and then chose to ignore it. Hoped that if he ignored the hurt, it, too, would go away. And Alex had learned over the years that, in many ways, it was easiest to just go along with whatever Marc wanted; certainly, it was easier than to try and go against him.

<!-- bt-block:000010 -->
Marc was an unstoppable force. Alex was not, in any sense of the word, an immovable object.

<!-- bt-block:000011 -->
So, when the bitterness got too sharp, and the pain got too sour, Alex just left the party. Went off to wander the streets of the Misano circuit, to delve into the darkness of the closed-down paddock, the blocked-off garages. If he walked for long enough, wandered far enough, the pain would, eventually, fade away. It was what Marc wanted, and Alex could give it to him. It was easier, that way, and Marc deserved to have some things be easy. Especially after how the last few years had gone.

<!-- bt-block:000012 -->
Alex sighed as he rubbed his arms. He wasn’t one to think about the past too much— not in a melancholic way, at least— but the last few years had been particularly formative. To Marc, to himself, to both of them, as brothers and riders— they’d learned a lot, about the world, about themselves, and about each other.

<!-- bt-block:000013 -->
Alex kept catching himself thinking about how things could have gone differently. If they could’ve, somehow, or perhaps not at all. It wasn’t Marc’s fault, Alex stood by that, but he also stood by the fact that it wasn’t entirely Valentino’s fault, either. They were both difficult people, with difficult lives and difficult goals. It was inevitable, he felt, that they would clash at some point.

<!-- bt-block:000014 -->
It maybe, just maybe, hadn’t had to happen in such a publicly nasty, hurtful way.

<!-- bt-block:000015 -->
A faint shuffling brought Alex out of his thoughts. He slowly turned in the direction of the sound, curious about who else would be out at this hour, awake and alert, yet not at the party.

<!-- bt-block:000016 -->
A tall silhouette stood off to the side of the road, half hidden under the shade of an overgrown, undertrimmed tree. Long arms were crossed tightly over a thin chest, back slouched, mouth tight, eyes distant, even as they flickered, intermittently, in the direction of the pounding, blaring music, the upturned lights— a glaring display in the otherwise dark, peaceful night.

<!-- bt-block:000017 -->
There was an uncharacteristic edge of vulnerability to the sight, especially coming from a being that Alex usually viewed as impenetrable, unreachable and dazzling as the midday sun and just as potentially blinding. But the sun had set a long time ago, and Valentino was just a human, and he had not noticed Alex’s approach at all because he, too, kept hearing the blaring music. Kept looking at the glaring lights.

<!-- bt-block:000018 -->
Oh, Alex realized in a sudden moment of clarity, of empathy, he’s hurt by all of this, too.

<!-- bt-block:000019 -->
And, well, why wouldn’t he be? The timing, the location, the race, the situation— It was so blatantly targeted that even Alex hadn’t been able to ignore it, as much as he’d tried to. He’d tried to find the party fun. He’d failed. That was why he’d left, why he was wandering the empty paddock, why he’d ventured out into the streets of Misano— why he’d ended up running into a Valentino that looked, for all intents and purposes, like he, too, was trying to walk away a hurt that would otherwise just be ignored.

<!-- bt-block:000020 -->
Alex swallowed his nerves, took a step forward. Felt the dry grass crunch beneath his heel, as Valentino’s head swivelled towards him, expression jumping at knowing himself observed.

<!-- bt-block:000021 -->
Alex took a deep breath. Took another step forward.

<!-- bt-block:000022 -->
“He’s being an asshole,” he explained into the silence.

<!-- bt-block:000023 -->
A startled laugh burst out of Valentino, who seemed as shocked by Alex’s words as Alex was by his reaction. Valentino bit back the sound a moment later, giving Alex a look that was less abjectly surprised, more wary.

<!-- bt-block:000024 -->
“What are you doing here?” he asked, not quite rude, but still accusatory.

<!-- bt-block:000025 -->
“Walking around,” Alex replied, trying for casual, failing miserably.

<!-- bt-block:000026 -->
Valentino’s eyes narrowed, flickering towards the music. “Didn’t like the party?”

<!-- bt-block:000027 -->
He obviously didn’t know what he was asking, didn’t know about Alex’s thoughts, but it still made Alex flinch slightly. “It’s . . . a bit too much for me.”

<!-- bt-block:000028 -->
Valentino continued watching him, and Alex could see him trying to put the pieces together. “He was being an asshole to you?”

<!-- bt-block:000029 -->
He didn’t have to specify who he was referring to.

<!-- bt-block:000030 -->
“Not me,” Alex said after a moment, biting his tongue because, well, that wasn’t quite true, but it also wasn’t not true, except— “To you.”

<!-- bt-block:000031 -->
That seemed to truly startle Valentino, who blinked at Alex a few times, like he couldn’t understand the words he’d said. “Me?”

<!-- bt-block:000032 -->
It wasn’t like he didn’t understand what Alex was referring to. Rather, his confusion clearly stemmed from Alex himself.

<!-- bt-block:000033 -->
“It’s a bit too much for me,” Alex repeated, not knowing how else to explain it, not wanting to throw Marc to the wolves, either, not even in this situation.

<!-- bt-block:000034 -->
Valentino’s head tilted as he visibly digested Alex’s admission, gentle as it had been. “Why not tell him to stop?” he offered.

<!-- bt-block:000035 -->
A scoff escaped Alex. “You’re joking.”

<!-- bt-block:000036 -->
A moment of stillness.

<!-- bt-block:000037 -->
Then, Valentino let out a low laugh of his own. “Yes, yes. Stupid question.”

<!-- bt-block:000038 -->
Alex released the breath he’d been holding, finally feeling his shoulders drop from where they’d been held, tense, since he’d first seen the shadowed silhouette beneath the tree.

<!-- bt-block:000039 -->
“It’s not like I haven’t tried talking to him about it,” he offered back, wanting to continue this bizarre moment of camaraderie, this liminal companionship, though it might be stupid, might be unwise— but Alex had never claimed to be the smartest, or the wisest. Didn’t think he’d be a motorcycle racer, if he were. “But it’s also not like he’s wrong. I mean, you went after him first.”

<!-- bt-block:000040 -->
Valentino shot him an annoyed, narrow-eyed glance. “You really want to argue about that now?”

<!-- bt-block:000041 -->
No, Alex really didn’t. He hadn’t meant to be accusatory either, just—

<!-- bt-block:000042 -->
He didn’t know what he was doing. He hadn’t planned to be here. He’d never really talked to Valentino like this before, didn’t know how to talk to him at all.

<!-- bt-block:000043 -->
“He really does like you,” he pivoted. “Even now. He’s just . . .  like that.”

<!-- bt-block:000044 -->
“Good for him,” Valentino retorted, mouth pursed. “I don’t like him back.”

<!-- bt-block:000045 -->
Alex paused. Bit his cheek. “Do you think you’ll ever—”

<!-- bt-block:000046 -->
“No,” Valentino interrupted blankly. Continued, seeing Alex’s expression, “Allora, why does this matter so much to everyone? We are rivals. Is normal if we do not get along, no?”

<!-- bt-block:000047 -->
Alex stopped. Sighed, as he rubbed at his head. What was he doing? He didn’t know. “Yeah. That’s true.”

<!-- bt-block:000048 -->
Valentino blinked. Turned to him more fully, brows furrowing, clearly having expected more of a fight.

<!-- bt-block:000049 -->
Alex shrugged, somewhat awkwardly. “He’s my brother, so of course I’d like it if . . . but I know you both are . . . “ He stopped again. Sighed, again. “Anyway, you don’t have to like him. Honestly, I don’t get why he likes you so much, either.”

<!-- bt-block:000050 -->
Valentino huffed, nodding in agreement. “Exactly. I wish he wouldn’t.”

<!-- bt-block:000051 -->
“Do you really?” Alex asked, half surprised, half not.

<!-- bt-block:000052 -->
“Yes, really,” Valentino raised an eyebrow at him. “Is not nice, you know? For him to focus on me so much. Always ends badly for me. Even here, today, he is doing all of this. It’s just because it’s me.”

<!-- bt-block:000053 -->
“Yeah,” Alex said with a wince. “Yeah, today was . . . yeah.”

<!-- bt-block:000054 -->
The celebrations, the party, the victory— it should have felt euphoric, but instead it felt petty, vitriolic. Marc had a mean streak in him, Alex knew that, accepted it, but it wasn’t usually so visible. Valentino brought out the worst in Marc, and Alex had once believed it was merited. Had struggled to hold on to that belief, the more time passed— the more it felt like picking at a festering wound, infecting it on purpose so that it would never be able to close, never be able to heal. It had somehow gotten even worse in the last two years— Argentina was one splinter, COTA another, Misano the latest. Alex used to feel annoyed by Marc’s narrow-minded focus, his implacable stubbornness, his need to always be right, to always win, in everything he did, be it games, races, or people.

<!-- bt-block:000055 -->
More and more, Alex just felt worried.

<!-- bt-block:000056 -->
“You are better,” Valentino said suddenly. “More bearable.”

<!-- bt-block:000057 -->
Alex blinked at him. “Thanks,” he finally said, only a little sarcastically.

<!-- bt-block:000058 -->
Valentino smiled faintly, expression loosening. “You are also a good rider,” he said. “This year, especially, you’ve been really, really impressive. Almost six wins in a row, eh? The championship is pretty much yours.”

<!-- bt-block:000059 -->
Alex startled. He hadn’t known Valentino paid attention to that— paid attention to Alex, or his racing, at all. Something proud and joyous bubbled up in his chest. He tried to push it down. Failed. “Don’t jinx me like that,” he complained, horrified to realize his cheeks were heating up.

<!-- bt-block:000060 -->
Valentino had the good grace to ignore his embarrassment. “Sorry, sorry. I just mean, you are good as a rider, not just as a person.”

<!-- bt-block:000061 -->
Again, Alex tried to fight back the embarrassing swell of pride. He wasn’t used to it, to this kind of— of respect. And from Valentino Rossi, of all people.

<!-- bt-block:000062 -->
Alex would’ve liked to claim he was immune to the whole circus. Above the adulation and admiration that followed the older rider like the gravity of the sun. But he wasn’t. Even after all these years, after everything that had happened, after everything he’d seen and heard and observed, Alex still wasn’t immune to it. As if, Alex thought somewhat bitterly, he could ever be immune to the gravity of the sun.

<!-- bt-block:000063 -->
It felt sincere, was the thing. And Valentino had no reason to lie to Alex about this. Perhaps worst of all, Alex wanted to believe him.

<!-- bt-block:000064 -->
“Not as good as Marc,” he said, trying to hide how much the comments were affecting him.

<!-- bt-block:000065 -->
Valentino’s smile turned sly. “Not as crazy.”

<!-- bt-block:000066 -->
Alex almost argued against that, barely stopping himself. Valentino didn’t seem like he was trying to pick a fight, but rather just making a joke. And, well. It was a fair point. “Not as good as you, either.”

<!-- bt-block:000067 -->
“Not as crazy,” Valentino said again, and, alright then, he really was just joking. Alex relaxed, only to tense again as Valentino continued, all too blithely: “I heard about the Yamaha contract.”

<!-- bt-block:000068 -->
“Oh?” Alex really hadn’t expected Valentino to open up that line of discussion. Had expected him to ignore it, and hope it would go away, much like—

<!-- bt-block:000069 -->
“They only tell me after,” Valentino said. “I have no idea, during.”

<!-- bt-block:000070 -->
Alex licked his lips. Valentino still seemed sincere, he really did, and a small, young part of Alex wanted to immediately believe him again. But, he couldn’t let himself be that naïve, not about this. “Really? None at all?”

<!-- bt-block:000071 -->
Valentino’s smile shifted, gaining a dry, bitter edge. “You see how they treat me with Lorenzo, back when I win everything for them. You think they care what I think, now I am losing everything, about what riders they sign?”

<!-- bt-block:000072 -->
Alex frowned. Considered the cynical truth of that argument. “But then, why . . . ?”

<!-- bt-block:000073 -->
“Maybe just using you to force Quartararo into a harder contract,” Valentino said, deceptively dismissive. Shrugged, at the wide-eyed look Alex gave him. “Eh, but what do I know? Don’t listen to me, just saying whatever.”

<!-- bt-block:000074 -->
Alex bit his cheek. Took a deep breath, released it slowly. Naïve, naïve, naïve. But maybe he was being naïve in the wrong ways. It was so hard to know who was lying, who was telling the truth— if anyone ever was. “Don’t worry, I’ll still blame you.”

<!-- bt-block:000075 -->
Valentino snorted. “Of course. Everything is always my fault, anyway.” His voice lowered, ever so slightly, as his eyes shifted towards the lights. “Always am a good excuse.”

<!-- bt-block:000076 -->
There was a flicker, again, of that fleeting vulnerability. The reflection of the sun in the moon in wide blue eyes— allowed, finally, to be seen, if only in a distant mirage. And in that moment, all Alex wanted was to make sure he could see it again.

<!-- bt-block:000077 -->
So with the same split-second determination that he used to overtake rival riders during races, he took yet another step forward.

<!-- bt-block:000078 -->
“We could exchange numbers,” he said, as Valentino turned to look back at him. “If you want.”

<!-- bt-block:000079 -->
Valentino blinked at him, the mirage of vulnerability gone from his gaze. Alex already missed it. “For what?”

<!-- bt-block:000080 -->
“I don’t know,” Alex said, again trying for casual, again failing. “Having someone to talk to?”

<!-- bt-block:000081 -->
“You don’t have friends?” Valentino asked, a bit meanly.

<!-- bt-block:000082 -->
Alex tried not to wince. He was failing a lot today, wasn’t he? “No,” he said, voice gone a little quiet. “Not really.”

<!-- bt-block:000083 -->
He half expected Valentino to mock him some more— to ask, maybe, why Alex was so isolated, as though he didn’t know, as though he weren’t a big part of the reason— but instead, he found Valentino’s phone being handed out to him.

<!-- bt-block:000084 -->
“Okay,” Valentino said, as Alex stared at him in surprise. “But you bother me too much, I block you.”

<!-- bt-block:000085 -->
Alex nodded, taking the phone before Valentino had the chance to change his mind. “Fair enough.” He sent himself a text, handed Valentino his phone back. Took out his own phone, created the contact. “Hm.”

<!-- bt-block:000086 -->
“What?”

<!-- bt-block:000087 -->
“Trying to think of a contact name.” Alex frowned down at his screen. “Can’t be your name, obviously.”

<!-- bt-block:000088 -->
“Hm.” Valentino looked down at his own phone for a few moments. “True. Then, I think you will be ‘The Tolerable One’.”

<!-- bt-block:000089 -->
“ . . . Seriously?”

<!-- bt-block:000090 -->
“Yes. Tola, for short.”

<!-- bt-block:000091 -->
That was kind of a cute nickname, actually, Alex thought. Immediately did his best to forget he’d ever thought that. “Okay,” he said, adding as another thought occurred to him, “then you’ll be ‘Pipa’.”

<!-- bt-block:000092 -->
Valentino blinked. “Pipa?”

<!-- bt-block:000093 -->
“Pizza with Pineapples,” Alex nodded, “Pipa, for short.”

<!-- bt-block:000094 -->
He laughed at the way Valentino’s expression twisted in disgust.

<!-- bt-block:000095 -->
“I take back what I said about you being the tolerable one, you are maybe even worse,” Valentino said, but his grin hadn’t completely faded, so Alex didn’t take him too seriously.

<!-- bt-block:000096 -->
“That’s the secret,” Alex said, tucking his phone away as soon as the contact was set. “I’ve always been the worse one.”

<!-- bt-block:000097 -->
He’d meant it as a passing joke, but no sooner were the words out of his mouth than Alex wished to take them back. He let out an astoundingly fake laugh to try to hide the biting truth of the joke, and desperately hoped Valentino wouldn’t notice.

<!-- bt-block:000098 -->
Valentino, of course, noticed. But rather than pitying Alex— or, perhaps more nicely, mocking him— he simply shrugged.

<!-- bt-block:000099 -->
“You aren’t,” he said, “not to me.”

<!-- bt-block:000100 -->
Alex opened his mouth. Shut it, when no sound came out. Stared at Valentino. Stared and stared and stared. Tried not to show how much the simple statement was affecting him.

<!-- bt-block:000101 -->
It was probably more of an insult towards Marc than a compliment towards Alex, Alex tried to reason desperately. Valentino had liked Marc well enough before 2015. Had certainly liked him more than Alex, back then.

<!-- bt-block:000102 -->
“I didn’t know either of you, back then,” Valentino said, startling Alex out of his thoughts. Valentino grinned at his wide-eyed disbelief. “I can tell what you are thinking. Is obvious. But as I say, I didn’t know either of you. I got along with Marc in the beginning, when I do not yet know what he is really like. Then I find out, and I do not like him so much anymore. Is a normal thing with people, no?”

<!-- bt-block:000103 -->
Yes, Alex supposed, that was true enough. “I used to like you more back when I didn’t know you, too.”

<!-- bt-block:000104 -->
Valentino did not rise to the bait. “No, you liked a person you imagined in your head, one that doesn’t exist. The person you dislike is closer to me, but even then is not really.” He paused, shrugged. “It is like this, always. I do not care, at this point. You can like me or not. You can hate me or not. No matter what I do, in the end, is all up to you.”

<!-- bt-block:000105 -->
Yes, Alex supposed, as he slowly turned over Valentino’s words in his head, carefully considered them— the bitter resignation of them, as well as the liberating decisiveness. That, too, was true enough.

<!-- bt-block:000106 -->
In the distance, the volume of the music rose, as did the brightness of the lights, in one glorious final crescendo.

<!-- bt-block:000107 -->
Alex did not look in their direction. Neither did Valentino.

<!-- bt-block:000108 -->
“I don’t dislike you,” Alex confessed, feeling like he was pulling teeth for what should have been an obvious admission after the conversation they’d been having. And yet, it wasn’t. “I . . . hope, you don’t dislike me, either.”

<!-- bt-block:000109 -->
Valentino's eyes narrowed. He considered Alex for a long moment, long enough that Alex began to feel regret pulling at his lungs.

<!-- bt-block:000110 -->
“Eh,” Valentino finally said, just before Alex could take back the implied offer, “maybe if you reconsider my contact name, I can consider this.”

<!-- bt-block:000111 -->
Alex stared at him. Caught Valentino's gaze. Burst out laughing, as Valentino followed soon after.

<!-- bt-block:000112 -->
In the distance, the lights went dark, and the music fell silent as the party finally drew to a close.

<!-- bt-block:000113 -->
Neither of them turned to look.
